Topic: childhood obesity
Subtopic: BMI classification and prevalence

Q: A nurse plots a 9-year-old's BMI at the 90th percentile for age and sex. How is this classified?
A) Normal
B) Overweight
C) Obese
D) Underweight
Answer: B
Rationale: In children, the 85th–95th percentile is classified as overweight. Obese is at or above the 95th percentile.
Trap: Adult BMI cutoffs (25–29.9 overweight, 30+ obese) do not apply to children, who are classified by percentile, not absolute number.

Q: According to Table 22.1, at what percentile is a child classified as obese?
A) At or above the 85th percentile
B) At or above the 90th percentile
C) At or above the 95th percentile
D) At or above the 99th percentile
Answer: C
Rationale: Obesity in children is defined as BMI at or above the 95th percentile for age and sex.

Q: What proportion of overweight children are expected to become overweight adults?
A) At least 30%
B) At least 50%
C) At least 70%
D) Nearly 100%
Answer: C
Rationale: At least 70% of overweight children will become overweight adults.

Q: Per the CDC's 2015–2016 prevalence data, in which age group was childhood obesity most prevalent?
A) Ages 2 to 5 (13.9%)
B) Ages 6 to 11 (18.4%)
C) Adolescents 12 to 19 (20.6%)
D) All age groups had equal prevalence
Answer: C
Rationale: Obesity prevalence increased with age: 13.9% in ages 2–5, 18.4% in ages 6–11, and 20.6% in adolescents 12–19.

Subtopic: obesity consequences and prevention

Q: Which physiological consequences of childhood obesity are correctly matched to body system? Select all that apply.
A) Cardiovascular — high blood pressure and high cholesterol
B) Metabolic — insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes
C) Respiratory — asthma and sleep apnea
D) Renal/hepatic — fatty liver disease, gallstones, and gastroesophageal reflux
E) Musculoskeletal — osteoporosis
Answer: A, B, C, D
Rationale: The chapter names cardiovascular (high BP, high cholesterol), metabolic (impaired glucose tolerance, insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes), respiratory (asthma, sleep apnea), and renal/hepatic (fatty liver disease, gallstones, GERD) consequences. Osteoporosis is not listed among the childhood obesity consequences.

Q: Per the Evidence-Based Practice finding (McKee et al., 2016), what was the strongest predictor of childhood obesity?
A) Low household income
B) Parental misperception of the child's weight status
C) Screen time exceeding 4 hours per day
D) Lack of access to a pediatrician
Answer: B
Rationale: Parental misperception of their child's weight was the strongest predictor of childhood obesity. More than two in five parents misperceived their child's weight status, and those who did were nearly 12 times more likely to have an obese child.

Q: Which family recommendations from Box 22.1 are correctly stated? Select all that apply.
A) Breastfeeding is associated with a lower risk for childhood obesity
B) It sometimes takes 10 to 15 tastes before a toddler learns to like a new food
C) Food should be used as a reward for good behavior to reinforce healthy choices
D) The television should be turned off during meals so children can attend to satiety cues
E) Appropriate beverages are milk and water; limit 100% fruit juice and avoid other sugary drinks
Answer: A, B, D, E
Rationale: Box 22.1 states breastfeeding lowers obesity risk, it may take 10–15 tastes to accept a new food, TV should be off during meals so children attend to satiety cues, and milk/water are the appropriate beverages while juice should be limited and other sugary drinks avoided.
Trap: The box explicitly says to AVOID using food as a punishment or reward and not to expect a child to "clean the plate" — both are associated with increased obesity risk. Using food as a reward is the inverted distractor.

Q: A parent asks why the family should not require a toddler to "clean the plate." What is the nurse's best response?
A) It ensures adequate caloric intake for growth
B) This feeding technique, along with using food as reward or punishment, is associated with increased risk for obesity
C) It teaches discipline and reduces food waste
D) It has no known effect on weight but wastes the parent's time to enforce
Answer: B
Rationale: Box 22.1 states that using food as a punishment or reward, and expecting a child to "clean the plate," are feeding techniques associated with increased risk for obesity.

Q: Screening for type 2 diabetes is recommended for a child with a BMI in which range, combined with two or more risk factors?
A) 5th to 85th percentile
B) 85th to 95th percentile
C) At or above the 95th percentile only
D) Any percentile if there is a family history
Answer: B
Rationale: Screening for type 2 diabetes is recommended for children with a BMI from the 85th to the 95th percentile who also have two or more of the named risk factors (family history, high-risk ethnicity, signs of insulin resistance, or maternal gestational diabetes).
Trap: Type 1 diabetes, by contrast, has no known prevention and is more common in Whites and males aged 10–14 — do not confuse the two diabetes patterns.

Topic: childhood injuries
Subtopic: leading causes by age group

Q: Which cause of unintentional injury death is correctly matched to the age group per Table 22.3?
A) Infants (0–1 year) — suffocation
B) Ages 1–4 — motor vehicle
C) Ages 5–9 — drowning
D) Ages 15–19 — suffocation
Answer: A
Rationale: The three to memorize: infants = suffocation, ages 1–4 = drowning, ages 5 and up = motor vehicle-related.
Trap: The other options each swap the leading cause with one that is present but not first in that age band — drowning is the leader for ages 1–4, not 5–9, and MVT is the leader from age 5 up, not suffocation.

Q: Which age group has the LOWEST injury death rate among children?
A) Infants
B) Toddlers and preschoolers
C) School-age children
D) Adolescents
Answer: C
Rationale: The school-age group has the lowest injury death rate of all pediatric age groups.
Trap: Infants have the second HIGHEST injury rate — an easy pairing to confuse with school-age children having the lowest.

Q: How do injury death rates compare between adolescent boys and girls?
A) Boys' rates are twice as high as girls'
B) Girls' rates are twice as high as boys'
C) Rates are approximately equal
D) Boys' rates are only slightly higher
Answer: A
Rationale: The injury death rates for boys are twice as high as those for girls, attributed in part to more conscious risk-taking among boys during adolescence.

Q: Which statement about adolescents and injury is accurate?
A) Adolescents have the lowest risk of any age group for motor vehicle deaths
B) Adolescents have the highest risk of any age group for motor vehicle deaths and fatal poisonings
C) Homicide is the leading cause of adolescent death
D) Suicide is the leading cause of adolescent death
Answer: B
Rationale: Adolescents are at the highest risk of any age group for motor vehicle deaths and fatal poisonings. Among US adolescents 10–19, homicide is the third leading cause of death and suicide is the second.

Subtopic: consent and mature minors

Q: A 16-year-old presents alone to a clinic requesting birth control. Under the mature minors doctrine, which is correct?
A) Parental consent is required in all 50 states regardless of the situation
B) All 50 states recognize the mature minors doctrine, allowing youths 15 and older to give informed consent when capable of understanding risks/benefits and the procedure is medically indicated
C) Only 30 states recognize this doctrine
D) The doctrine applies only to emergency care, not birth control
Answer: B
Rationale: All 50 states recognize the mature minors doctrine, which allows youths 15 years of age and older to give informed medical consent if they are capable of understanding the risks and benefits and the procedure is medically indicated.

Q: In which situations may adolescents be treated without parental consent? Select all that apply.
A) Emergency care
B) Substance abuse treatment
C) Pregnancy
D) Birth control
E) Elective cosmetic procedures
Answer: A, B, C, D
Rationale: Most states allow providers to treat adolescents without parental consent for emergency care, substance abuse, pregnancy, and birth control. Elective cosmetic procedures are not among the named exceptions.

Subtopic: child maltreatment and SIDS

Q: Under CAPTA, how are the following categorized? Select all that apply.
A) Physical abuse — an act of commission
B) Failure to provide medical or dental care — an act of omission
C) Exposure to a violent environment — an act of omission (failure to supervise)
D) Psychological abuse — an act of omission
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: CAPTA categorizes physical, sexual, and psychological abuse as acts of commission, while failure to provide (physical, emotional, medical/dental, educational neglect) and failure to supervise (inadequate supervision, exposure to violent environments) are acts of omission.
Trap: Psychological abuse is an act of COMMISSION, not omission — pairing it with the "failure to" category is the inversion to watch for.

Q: SIDS most commonly peaks at what infant age?
A) Birth to 1 month
B) 1 to 4 months
C) 6 to 9 months
D) 9 to 12 months
Answer: B
Rationale: SIDS peaks between one and four months of age, although it may occur up to 1 year.

Q: What is true regarding predicting SIDS?
A) A validated screening test can identify at-risk infants
B) No test exists to identify infants who may die of SIDS
C) Genetic testing at birth reliably predicts risk
D) An abnormal apnea monitor reading is diagnostic
Answer: B
Rationale: There is no test to identify infants who may die of SIDS, making it a frustrating clinical problem. The "Back to Sleep" campaign (1994) has dramatically decreased incidence since its introduction.

Topic: smoking, nutrition, immunizations, and environmental hazards
Subtopic: smoking and ETS

Q: What is the recommended teaching approach for antismoking programs directed at children and teenagers?
A) Emphasize long-term consequences such as lung cancer decades later
B) Emphasize short-term effects and cosmetic consequences, since youth cannot developmentally visualize future consequences
C) Focus exclusively on the financial cost of cigarettes
D) Rely on scare tactics depicting terminal illness
Answer: B
Rationale: Antismoking programs are more successful when they focus on short-term effects rather than long-term effects, because children and teenagers cannot developmentally visualize future consequences. Immediate health risks and cosmetic effects should be emphasized, along with how advertising pressures people to smoke.
Trap: The intuitive answer — teaching about lung cancer or emphysema decades later — is the wrong approach for this developmental stage.

Q: Which effects are associated with children's exposure to environmental tobacco smoke (ETS)? Select all that apply.
A) Increased middle ear infections
B) Increased asthma episodes
C) Increased upper respiratory tract infections
D) More missed school days
E) Decreased incidence of otitis media
Answer: A, B, C, D
Rationale: ETS exposure in children increases middle ear infections, asthma, upper respiratory tract infections, and missed school days.

Subtopic: immunizations and environmental toxins

Q: Which are named reasons that families decline immunizing their children? Select all that apply.
A) Not appreciating the seriousness of vaccine-preventable diseases because prevalence is low
B) Confusion from media misinformation, including a perceived link to autism
C) Concerns about the safety data for vaccines
D) Distrust of the agencies and companies behind vaccine recommendations
E) Religious objection is the single most commonly cited reason
Answer: A, B, C, D
Rationale: Four reasons are named: low perceived seriousness because prevalence is low, media misinformation (including autism claims), concerns about safety data, and distrust of recommending agencies/manufacturers. Religious objection is not named as one of the four.

Q: Which mechanisms explain why children are more vulnerable than adults to environmental toxins? Select all that apply.
A) Growing tissues absorb toxins readily
B) Developing organ systems are more susceptible to damage
C) Smaller size means an increased concentration of toxins per pound of body weight
D) Children occupy lower air spaces where heavy chemicals concentrate
E) Children's slower respiratory rate reduces the volume of pollutants inhaled during play
Answer: A, B, C, D
Rationale: Six mechanisms are named in the fact bank: readily absorbing tissues, susceptible developing organs, higher toxin concentration per pound, occupying lower air spaces where heavy chemicals concentrate, increased outdoor play/exertion increasing inhaled pollutant volume, and chewing/mouthing/floor play/rolling in grass increasing contact exposure.
Trap: Children actually run and breathe hard during play, which INCREASES the volume of pollutants inhaled — the opposite of what option E states.

Q: What is identified as the most common environmental health hazard for children?
A) Pesticide residue on produce
B) Lead poisoning
C) Poor air quality from vehicle exhaust
D) Contaminated drinking water
Answer: B
Rationale: Lead poisoning is the most common environmental health hazard for children. Pesticides and poor air quality are also named but secondary to lead.

Topic: adult health legislation and ethics
Subtopic: the five key acts

Q: Which act established the Administration on Aging (AOA) and state agencies for older adults' social service needs?
A) Americans with Disabilities Act (1990)
B) Older Americans Act (1965)
C) Patient Self-Determination Act (1990)
D) Family and Medical Leave Act (1993)
Answer: B
Rationale: The Older Americans Act, passed in 1965, established the Administration on Aging and state agencies, with the mission of helping older adults maintain dignity and live independently in their communities. It was reauthorized in 2020 and extended through 2024.

Q: Which statement about the Patient Self-Determination Act (1990) is correct?
A) It creates new patient rights not previously recognized
B) It reaffirms the common law right of self-determination under the Fourteenth Amendment and requires Medicare/Medicaid providers to ask about advance directives
C) It mandates that all patients complete a living will before admission
D) It applies only to patients in long-term care facilities
Answer: B
Rationale: The Patient Self-Determination Act does not create new rights for patients; it reaffirms the common law right of self-determination guaranteed by the Fourteenth Amendment. It requires providers receiving Medicare and Medicaid funds to ask whether patients have advance directives and to provide information about rights under state law.
Trap: "Creates new rights" is the direct inversion the chapter warns against.

Q: The Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA), as covered in this chapter, was amended in 2008 to do what?
A) Extend leave from 12 to 16 weeks
B) Increase entitlements for military families
C) Require paid leave for all eligible employees
D) Extend coverage to employers with fewer than 50 employees
Answer: B
Rationale: FMLA was initially passed in 1993, providing job protection and continuous health benefits for eligible employees needing extended leave for their own illness or to care for a family member. It was amended in 2008 to increase military family entitlements.

Q: The Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Reconciliation Act (1996), known as "welfare reform," did which of the following?
A) Changed AFDC to TANF, requiring women heads of households to find employment to retain benefits
B) Established Medicaid and CHIP
C) Created the Administration on Aging
D) Reaffirmed the Fourteenth Amendment right to self-determination
Answer: A
Rationale: This 1996 act targeted women receiving public assistance and changed AFDC (Aid to Families with Dependent Children) to TANF (Temporary Assistance for Needy Families), a work program mandating that women heads of households find employment to retain benefits.

Q: The Americans with Disabilities Act (1990) requires equal opportunity accommodations for which types of limitations?
A) Physical limitations only
B) Mental limitations only
C) Both physical and mental limitations
D) Only limitations acquired after age 18
Answer: C
Rationale: The ADA requires government and businesses to provide disabled individuals with equal opportunities for jobs, education, transportation, public buildings access, and other accommodations for both physical and mental limitations. One effect has been greater emphasis on community care rather than institutionalization.

Subtopic: advance directives and elder rights

Q: A patient wants to designate someone else to make health care decisions in the event she becomes unable to do so herself. Which document accomplishes this?
A) Living will
B) Durable power of attorney
C) DNR order
D) Values history instrument
Answer: B
Rationale: The durable power of attorney is the legal way for a patient to designate someone else to make health care decisions when he or she is unable to do so. The living will instead expresses wishes about the use of medical treatments in the event of a terminal illness.
Trap: Living will and durable power of attorney are easy to swap — living will = express your OWN wishes; durable power of attorney = designate SOMEONE ELSE to decide.

Q: Which are part of the legislated rights of the elderly? Select all that apply.
A) Freedom from physical and chemical restraint
B) The right to vote
C) Control of one's own funds
D) Automatic removal of self-determination rights upon admission to a nursing facility
E) The right to practice the religion of one's choice
Answer: A, B, C, E
Rationale: Legislated rights of the elderly include individualized care, freedom from discrimination, privacy, freedom from neglect and abuse, control of one's own funds, ability to sue, freedom from physical and chemical restraint, involvement in decision making, the right to vote, access to community services, the right to raise grievances/obtain a will/enter contracts, the right to practice one's chosen religion, and the right to dispose of personal property.
Trap: Self-determination rights can be removed ONLY if the person is declared incompetent — admission to a facility alone does not strip these rights.

Q: An older adult living in a nursing facility makes a choice that her adult children strongly disagree with. What principle governs whether her decision can be overridden?
A) Family members with power of attorney can override any decision they disagree with
B) Her right to self-determination can be taken from her only if she is declared incompetent
C) Facility staff may override patient decisions that conflict with facility policy
D) Any decision that increases fall risk can be overridden by the care team
Answer: B
Rationale: Older persons can make independent choices with which others may disagree; their right to self-determination can be taken from them only if they are declared incompetent.

Topic: adult chronic disease
Subtopic: hypertension and CVD

Q: Under the 2017 AHA reclassification, a blood pressure of 132/78 falls into which category?
A) Normal
B) Elevated
C) Hypertension Stage 1
D) Hypertension Stage 2
Answer: C
Rationale: Hypertension Stage 1 is now defined as systolic 130–139 OR diastolic 80–89. A systolic of 132 places this reading in Stage 1 even though the diastolic (78) alone would be in the elevated range — the systolic value governs here since either criterion met qualifies the stage.
Trap: Under the OLD classification, 132/78 would have been called "prehypertension," not hypertension at all — the reclassification lowered the threshold from 140/90.

Q: What is the key change in the 2017 AHA hypertension reclassification?
A) Normal BP is now defined as under 130/80
B) Hypertension now begins at 130/80 rather than 140/90
C) Stage 2 hypertension now begins at 130/80
D) The categories of normal, elevated, and hypertensive were eliminated in favor of a single "at risk" category
Answer: B
Rationale: The 2017 AHA reclassification created four categories: Normal (<120/<80), Elevated (120–129/<80), Stage 1 Hypertension (130–139 or 80–89), and Stage 2 Hypertension (≥140 or ≥90). The headline change is that hypertension is now 130/80, not 140/90.

Q: Which statement about hypertension prevalence and control is accurate?
A) About 3 in 4 adults with hypertension have their condition under control
B) About 1 in 4 adults with hypertension have their condition under control
C) Hypertension is more common in women than men
D) Hypertension always produces early warning symptoms
Answer: B
Rationale: About 1 in 4 adults with hypertension have their condition under control. Because hypertension does not have symptoms, one-third of affected people do not know they have the disease. A greater percentage of men (47%) than women (43%) have HBP, and it is more common in non-Hispanic Black adults (54%) than other groups.

Q: What is the leading cause of death in the United States for both men and women across racial/ethnic groups?
A) Cancer
B) Cardiovascular disease
C) Unintentional injuries
D) Diabetes
Answer: B
Rationale: Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death for both men and women, including African Americans, American Indians/Alaska Natives, Hispanics, and Whites in the United States.

Q: Per the 2018 physical activity guidelines, what is the recommended weekly aerobic activity for adults?
A) 60 to 150 minutes of moderate intensity only
B) 150 to 300 minutes of moderate intensity OR 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous intensity
C) 300 to 450 minutes of moderate intensity regardless of vigorous activity
D) 30 minutes of moderate activity per day only on weekdays
Answer: B
Rationale: Adults are advised to get at least 150 to 300 minutes of moderate intensity, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous intensity, aerobic physical activity per week.
Trap: This is the adult guideline; do not confuse it with the AAP recommendation of 60 minutes of moderate aerobic activity PER DAY for every child and adolescent (a different guideline for a different age group).

Subtopic: diabetes, cancer, and chronic disease framing

Q: What are the three most common, and also most costly and preventable, chronic diseases named by NCCDPHP (2019)?
A) Heart disease and stroke, cancer, and diabetes
B) Obesity, asthma, and arthritis
C) Depression, hypertension, and osteoporosis
D) Stroke, COPD, and Alzheimer's disease
Answer: A
Rationale: Heart disease and stroke, cancer, and diabetes are named as the most common chronic diseases and conditions, and also the most costly and preventable of all health problems.

Q: A nurse is teaching community screening for type 2 diabetes as a secondary prevention activity. Which intervention fits this level?
A) Educating adults about nutrition and the risks of obesity and smoking
B) Screening with finger-stick blood glucose or glucose tolerance tests
C) Activities aimed at reducing complications of established disease
D) Community exercise programs for weight reduction
Answer: B
Rationale: For diabetes, the stated levels of prevention are: primary — educating adults about nutrition and risks of obesity/smoking/inactivity plus community interventions; secondary — screening with finger-stick blood glucose or glucose tolerance tests plus history and physical; tertiary — reducing complications of the disease.

Q: Cancer is described as the second leading cause of death in the US, exceeded only by which condition, and what proportion of new cancer diagnoses are considered possibly avoidable?
A) Cardiovascular disease; about 20%
B) Heart disease; at least 42%
C) Stroke; about 60%
D) Diabetes; at least 42%
Answer: B
Rationale: Cancers are the second leading cause of death in the US, exceeded only by heart disease. The ACS estimates at least 42% of newly diagnosed cancers (about 750,000 in 2020) are possibly avoidable, including those from smoking, excess body weight, alcohol, poor nutrition, inactivity, and sun exposure.

Topic: women's health
Subtopic: eating disorders and reproductive health

Q: A patient with anorexia nervosa has lost a significant amount of weight but insists she is "still too heavy." Which statement about this presentation is accurate?
A) This is atypical; most patients with anorexia readily acknowledge their weight loss
B) Individuals with anorexia rarely complain of weight loss because they view themselves as normal or overweight
C) This presentation suggests bulimia rather than anorexia
D) This level of body image distortion is inconsistent with a DSM-5 diagnosis
Answer: B
Rationale: Anorexia nervosa involves a fear of gaining weight coupled with disturbances in perceptions of the body. Excessive weight loss is the most noticeable clue, but individuals with anorexia rarely complain of weight loss because they view themselves as normal or overweight.

Q: Which features distinguish bulimia from anorexia nervosa?
A) Fear of gaining weight with disturbed body perception, and rarely complaining of weight loss
B) Persistent concern with body shape and weight, recurrent binge eating with loss of control, and extreme methods to prevent weight gain such as purging
C) Exclusive occurrence in adolescent females
D) Absence of any psychological comorbidity
Answer: B
Rationale: Bulimia involves persistent concern with body shape and weight, recurrent episodes of binge eating with loss of control, and extreme methods to prevent weight gain (purging, strict dieting, fasting, laxatives/diuretics, or vigorous exercise). Anorexia is instead characterized by fear of weight gain and rarely complaining of weight loss.

Subtopic: HRT and cancer screening

Q: Based on the Women's Health Initiative trial findings, what is the current recommended use of hormone replacement therapy (HRT)?
A) Long-term use to prevent heart disease
B) Long-term use to prevent osteoporosis and fractures
C) Short-term use for management of vasomotor symptoms ("hot flashes") only
D) HRT is no longer recommended for any indication
Answer: C
Rationale: The WHI trials found HRT did not prevent heart disease and long-term use showed adverse effects including increased risk of stroke, coronary heart disease, and thromboembolism. HRT is still considered useful for managing vasomotor symptoms but only for a limited time to avoid these adverse effects.
Trap: The trap answer is prescribing HRT for cardiovascular or bone protection — the trials specifically disproved the heart-disease-prevention benefit.

Q: Regarding breast cancer incidence versus mortality by race, which statement is accurate?
A) Both incidence and death rate are higher in White women
B) Both incidence and death rate are higher in African American women
C) Incidence is higher in White women, but the death rate is higher in African American women
D) Incidence is higher in African American women, but the death rate is higher in White women
Answer: C
Rationale: Although the incidence of breast cancer is higher in White women, the death rate is higher for African American women, thought to be due to lower screening activity, social determinants such as low income, and poor access to care.
Trap: This incidence-versus-mortality inversion pattern repeats with prostate cancer (African American mortality nearly twice as high) and testicular cancer (White men 4–5 times more likely to develop it) — always check whether the question asks about incidence or death rate.

Topic: men's health
Subtopic: prostate and testicular cancer screening

Q: According to ACS screening guidance, at what age should an African American man or a man whose father was diagnosed with prostate cancer before age 65 be informed about prostate cancer screening risks and benefits?
A) Age 40
B) Age 45
C) Age 50
D) Age 55
Answer: B
Rationale: Men at average risk should be informed starting at 50; men at high risk — African American men and men with a father, brother, or son diagnosed before age 65 — should be informed starting at 45; men with several such family members diagnosed at an early age should be informed starting at 40.
Trap: The ACS recommends men be INFORMED about risks and possible benefits of screening so they can decide — not that screening automatically be performed.

Q: A 68-year-old man asks the nurse whether he should perform testicular self-examination. What is the best response based on current guidelines?
A) Yes, monthly self-examination is recommended for all adult men
B) The USPSTF (2011) recommends against screening by self- or clinical examination in asymptomatic males due to insufficient evidence, low incidence, and high cure rate
C) Self-examination is recommended only for men under age 40
D) Self-examination should be replaced by annual PSA testing
Answer: B
Rationale: Because painless testicular enlargement is commonly the first sign of testicular cancer, self-examination was traditionally recommended. However, the USPSTF's 2011 updated guidelines recommend against screening by self-examination or clinical examination in asymptomatic adult or adolescent males, citing insufficient evidence, low incidence rate, and high cure rate even with advanced disease. Box 22.4 lists testicular cancer screening for symptomatic males only.
Trap: This directly contradicts what many students were taught previously, making it a near-certain exam item.

Q: Why is the PSA test considered a limited screening tool for prostate cancer?
A) It is invasive and carries surgical risk
B) It produces many false-positive results because infections, ejaculation, exercise, and BPH can all elevate PSA
C) It cannot be performed in men over age 70
D) It requires general anesthesia
Answer: B
Rationale: The PSA blood test is not accurate in sensitivity or specificity, producing many false-positive results because many factors — infections, ejaculation, exercise such as bike riding, and benign prostatic hyperplasia — can elevate PSA.

Q: Which factors are named as contributing to poorer health outcomes for men? Select all that apply.
A) Socioeconomic status and access to health care
B) Male acculturation to health issues and harmful perceptions about masculinity
C) Lack of understanding of male health behaviors
D) Men seeking routine care earlier in the disease process than women
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: Giorgianni et al. (2013) name socioeconomic status, access to health care, male acculturation to health issues, harmful perceptions about masculinity, and lack of understanding of male health behaviors. Men are actually LESS likely than women to seek routine care early in the disease process — the opposite of option D.

Topic: frail elderly and community-based care
Subtopic: frailty, caregiver burden, and TLC

Q: How is frailty defined in this chapter?
A) A normal, expected consequence of aging requiring no intervention
B) A geriatric syndrome placing older adults at risk for falls, worsening disability, institutionalization, and death
C) A diagnosis synonymous with dementia
D) A temporary condition that resolves with adequate nutrition alone
Answer: B
Rationale: Frailty is a geriatric syndrome that places older adults at risk for adverse health outcomes, including falls, worsening disability, institutionalization, and death — a complex state of impairment involving physical functioning, physiological resiliency, metabolism, and immune response.

Q: In Eliopoulos's TLC framework for addressing caregiver burden, what does the "L" stand for?
A) Learning new caregiving skills only
B) Leaving the care situation periodically to obtain respite and relaxation
C) Locating community resources
D) Limiting the caregiver's other obligations
Answer: B
Rationale: TLC stands for Training (in care techniques, safe medication use, recognition of abnormalities, and resources), Leaving the care situation periodically for respite and relaxation, and Care for the caregiver (sleep, rest, exercise, nutrition, socialization, solitude, support, financial aid, health management).

Q: Which statement about family caregiving of the frail elderly is accurate?
A) Caregiving is universally experienced as a negative burden
B) For many families, the caregiving experience is positive, rewarding, and fulfilling, even though burden, strain, and stress are common
C) Male spouses represent the largest group of family caregivers
D) Caregiver burden only affects financial well-being, not emotional well-being
Answer: B
Rationale: While caregiver burden reflects stress, strain, and burnout involving the work itself, relationships, effects on others, and lifestyle, the chapter also states that for many families the caregiving experience is positive, rewarding, and fulfilling.
Trap: Female spouses, not male spouses, represent the largest group of family caregivers for the elderly who remain in the home.

Subtopic: community-based care settings

Q: How does adult day health differ from a senior center?
A) Adult day health is a social/recreational model; senior centers are a medical model
B) Adult day health is more of a medical model, and participants often return home to caregivers at night
C) Senior centers provide respite care; adult day health does not
D) There is no meaningful difference between the two settings
Answer: B
Rationale: Adult day health serves individuals whose mental or physical function requires more health care and supervision; it is more of a medical model than the senior center, and individuals often return home to their caregivers at night. Some adult day health settings also offer short-term overnight respite care.
Trap: Senior centers are the social/recreational model (developed in the early 1940s), offering exercise, education, hot meals, and case management — reversing which setting is "medical" versus "social" is the easy inversion.

Q: What population is served by rehabilitation as described in this chapter?
A) Only patients recovering from elective surgery
B) Debilitated persons typically needing physical, occupational, psychological, and speech therapy after a hip fracture, stroke, or prolonged illness causing serious deconditioning
C) Only patients with a terminal diagnosis
D) Exclusively pediatric patients with developmental delay
Answer: B

Q: What is stated about the need for personal care by age group (Jan–June 2018 data)?
A) Need for personal care decreases with advancing age
B) 20% of those 85 and older needed personal care, compared to 9% of those 75–84 and 4% of those 65–74
C) All age groups over 65 have roughly equal need for personal care

Q: Long-term care services and support (LTSS) is best defined as what?
A) Acute hospital-based care for functional emergencies
B) A variety of health and social services to assist individuals with functional limitations, with the goal of improving functioning
C) End-of-life comfort care exclusively
D) A federal cash benefit program for disabled adults
Answer: B
Rationale: LTSS is defined as a variety of health and social services to assist individuals who have functional limitations due to physical, cognitive, or mental conditions or disability; the goal is to improve functioning among those served.

Q: What complicates access to and financing of long-term care for the frail elderly?
A) Nursing homes and 24-hour skilled home care are among the least expensive long-term care options
B) Medicaid is administered by the states, creating variability in level of service, and eligibility depends largely on income and assets
C) Medicare fully covers all long-term nursing home costs
D) Long-term care insurance is legally required for all adults over 65
Answer: B
Rationale: Nursing homes and 24-hour skilled care at home are actually the MOST expensive types of long-term care, costing thousands of dollars a month. Medicaid, which many rely on along with personal funds and private insurance, is administered by the states, creating variability in service level, with eligibility largely dependent on income and assets.
