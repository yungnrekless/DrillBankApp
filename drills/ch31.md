# Chapter 31 drills — The Nurse in the Schools

Authored from `sources/Ch31_Fact_Bank.md` (Stanhope & Lancaster, 6th ed.,
printed pp. 552–576). Blueprint weight is 2 questions, so this is a narrow
chapter; coverage concentrates on the two highest-yield clusters the fact
bank names — federal legislation (with the IEP-vs-IHP distinction) and the
levels of prevention in schools — with a smaller set of items on school
nurse roles, confidentiality, and the WSCC model.

Topic: school nursing foundations
Subtopic: definition and scope

Q: How does the ANA/NASN (2017) definition characterize school nursing?
A) A specialty practice focused on providing health care and illness prevention to school-age children, with the goal of facilitating their participation in educational opportunities
B) A generalist practice providing acute care to children during school hours only
C) A subspecialty of pediatric nursing limited to students with diagnosed disabilities
D) A community health role focused exclusively on immunization compliance
Answer: A
Rationale: School nursing is defined as a specialty practice focused on providing health care and illness prevention to school-age children, with the goal of facilitating their participation in educational opportunities (ANA & NASN, 2017).

Q: School nursing is best described as which of the following?
A) Population focused, serving children, families, staff, and teachers in the school
B) Focused solely on individual students with chronic illness
C) Limited to students enrolled in special education programs
D) A service that ends once a child reaches adolescence
Answer: A
Rationale: School nursing is population focused: the population is all children, families, staff, and teachers in the school setting, not only individual students.
Trap: An option limiting the role to direct student care misses the population-level framing the chapter emphasizes.

Topic: federal legislation
Subtopic: sequence and content of the laws

Q: Which law first established the right of children with disabilities to a public education?
A) Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act
B) Education for All Handicapped Children Act (1975)
C) IDEA (1997)
D) Americans with Disabilities Act (1992)
Answer: B
Rationale: The Education for All Handicapped Children Act (1975) established the right of children with disabilities to public education, the earliest law in the sequence.

Q: A 1997 federal law (PL 105-17) required schools to allow more children with special needs to attend and to accommodate those needs through IEPs and IHPs. Which law is this?
A) No Child Left Behind Act
B) IDEIA
C) IDEA
D) Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act
Answer: C
Rationale: IDEA (PL 105-17, 1997) required schools to accommodate more children with special needs through individualized education plans (IEPs) and individualized health plans (IHPs).

Q: In 2004, IDEA was renamed and its coverage was extended. What did the renamed law, IDEIA, extend coverage to?
A) Until the child turns 18
B) Until the child turns 21
C) Until the child turns 22
D) Until the child completes a four-year degree
Answer: C
Rationale: The Individuals with Disabilities Education Improvement Act (IDEIA), the 2004 renaming of IDEA, extended coverage until the child turns 22.
Trap: 18 and 21 are the plausible-sounding distractors; the chapter is explicit that IDEIA's extension goes to age 22.

Q: Which 2001 law (PL 107-110) required a healthy environment in schools, and what later replaced it?
A) IDEA; replaced by IDEIA in 2004
B) No Child Left Behind Act; replaced by the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) in 2015
C) Section 504; replaced by the ADA in 1992
D) ESSA; replaced by No Child Left Behind in 2001
Answer: B
Rationale: No Child Left Behind (PL 107-110, 2001) required a healthy environment in schools. It was revised and replaced by the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA, PL 114-95) in 2015.
Trap: The ESSA/NCLB order is easy to reverse — ESSA came after and replaced NCLB, not the other way around.

Q: Which federal law prohibits disability discrimination and requires accommodations, distinct from IDEA?
A) Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act
B) FERPA
C) HIPAA
D) Child Nutrition Act
Answer: A
Rationale: Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act prohibits disability discrimination and requires accommodations.

Q: Place the following in the correct chronological sequence: ADA, Education for All Handicapped Children Act, IDEA, IDEIA.
A) Education for All Handicapped Children Act → ADA → IDEA → IDEIA
B) ADA → Education for All Handicapped Children Act → IDEIA → IDEA
C) IDEA → Education for All Handicapped Children Act → ADA → IDEIA
D) Education for All Handicapped Children Act → IDEA → ADA → IDEIA
Answer: A
Rationale: The sequence is Education for All Handicapped Children Act (1975) → ADA (1992) → IDEA (1997) → IDEIA (2004).

Q: The increased presence of children with HIV/AIDS, chronic illness, or mental health problems in general education classrooms, and the corresponding demand on the school nurse, is attributed mainly to the effect of which legislation?
A) The Child Nutrition Act and Healthy, Hunger-Free Kids Act
B) FERPA and HIPAA
C) IDEA and the ADA
D) No Child Left Behind and ESSA
Answer: C
Rationale: IDEA and the ADA meant more children with HIV/AIDS, chronic illness, or mental health problems are in classrooms, requiring more attention from the school nurse.

Subtopic: IEP vs. IHP

Q: A child's plan specifying classroom accommodations, instructional modifications, and educational goals is which of the following?
A) An Individualized Health Plan (IHP)
B) An Individualized Education Plan (IEP)
C) An emergency plan
D) A Section 504 accommodation log
Answer: B
Rationale: The IEP (Individualized Education Plan) is developed so a child's educational experience accommodates special needs.
Trap: Confusing the IEP with the IHP is the classic school-nursing trap — IEP is about education, not health care coordination.

Q: A plan developed so that a child's health care needs are met in balance with the school experience is which of the following?
A) An Individualized Education Plan (IEP)
B) An Individualized Health Plan (IHP)
C) A crisis team protocol
D) A 504 plan
Answer: B
Rationale: The IHP (Individualized Health Plan) is developed so a child's health care needs are met in balance with the school experience.

Q: Which statement correctly distinguishes the IEP from the IHP?
A) The IEP addresses health care coordination; the IHP addresses classroom accommodations
B) The IEP addresses educational accommodations; the IHP addresses health care needs
C) The IEP and IHP are two names for the same document
D) The IHP is required only for children over age 18
Answer: B
Rationale: IEP = educational accommodations; IHP = health care needs met in balance with the school experience. IDEA requires both for children with special needs.
Trap: Swapping the two, or attributing health-care coordination to the IEP, is the distractor the fact bank flags as the classic trap.

Subtopic: FERPA, HIPAA, and confidentiality

Q: In the school setting, a student's health record is protected primarily under which law?
A) HIPAA
B) FERPA
C) The ADA
D) IDEIA
Answer: B
Rationale: FERPA protects the privacy of student education records, and in schools, student health records fall under FERPA's protection. HIPAA protects health information generally but is not the operative law for school health records.
Trap: HIPAA is the plausible distractor because it is the health-information law students recognize, but FERPA is the one that governs records in schools.

Q: A teacher, without written authorization, asks the school nurse to see a student's confidential health record to better understand the student's needs. What should the nurse do?
A) Share the record, since teachers are part of the educational team
B) Share only a summary, since teachers have a legitimate educational interest
C) Decline to release the record; protected information cannot be released to anyone, including teachers or other health personnel, without authorization
D) Share the record only if the student's parent is unreachable
Answer: C
Rationale: The school nurse must maintain confidentiality. Protected information cannot be released to anyone, including teachers or other health personnel, without authorization — this is the FERPA principle in practice.
Trap: "Teachers have a legitimate educational interest" sounds reasonable but is not how the chapter frames the confidentiality rule; authorization is required regardless of who is asking.

Topic: school nurse roles
Subtopic: role identification

Q: A school nurse coordinates care for a child with complex medical needs and attends parent-teacher meetings to better know the family. Which role is this?
A) Consultant
B) Case manager
C) Community outreach
D) Researcher
Answer: B
Rationale: The case manager role coordinates health care for children with complex needs, including attending parent-teacher meetings to know families.

Q: A school nurse is asked by school administrators to provide health information to guide a policy decision. Which role is this nurse fulfilling?
A) Direct caregiver
B) Health educator
C) Consultant
D) Case manager
Answer: C
Rationale: The consultant role is the school nurse providing health information to administrators, teachers, and parent-teacher groups.

Q: Which of the following are recognized school nurse roles? Select all that apply.
A) Direct caregiver
B) Case manager
C) Consultant
D) Researcher
E) Community outreach
Answer: A, B, C, D, E
Rationale: Recognized school nurse roles include direct caregiver, case manager, consultant, researcher, community outreach, health educator, and counselor.

Topic: levels of prevention
Subtopic: applying the three levels in schools

Q: Which level of prevention represents the largest responsibility for the school nurse?
A) Primary prevention
B) Secondary prevention
C) Tertiary prevention
D) Quaternary prevention
Answer: B
Rationale: Secondary prevention — early intervention for children who need health care, including caring for ill or injured students and employees, screening and assessing children, and referral to appropriate health agencies — is the largest responsibility for the school nurse.
Trap: A stem asking which level occupies most of the school nurse's work is testing this fact specifically; primary (education/vaccination) and tertiary (managing existing conditions) are the distractors.

Q: A school nurse conducts vision and hearing screenings and refers a student with a suspected hearing deficit to an audiologist. This is an example of which level of prevention?
A) Primary
B) Secondary
C) Tertiary
D) Health promotion, not a level of prevention
Answer: B
Rationale: Secondary prevention includes screening and assessing children and referring them to appropriate health agencies.
Trap: Mislabeling a required school screening as primary prevention is a common trap — vaccination is primary prevention, but screening is secondary.

Q: A school nurse teaches a classroom unit on substance abuse prevention and administers required immunizations. This is an example of which level of prevention?
A) Primary
B) Secondary
C) Tertiary
D) Secondary, because substance abuse is already a risk
Answer: A
Rationale: Primary prevention in schools includes educating children and adolescents about substance abuse, injury prevention, and disease prevention, and providing vaccinations.

Q: A school nurse manages the daily care of a student with a seizure disorder and helps address the aftermath of bullying for another student. This is an example of which level of prevention?
A) Primary
B) Secondary
C) Tertiary
D) Primary, because the goal is preventing further harm
Answer: C
Rationale: Tertiary prevention is nursing care for children with existing conditions to limit disability and support function; it also addresses bullying and mental health sequelae.

Subtopic: immunization requirements

Q: What is true about immunization requirements for school enrollment across the United States?
A) All states require immunizations before enrollment
B) Only a minority of states require immunizations before enrollment
C) Immunization requirements vary such that several states require none
D) Immunization is recommended but never legally required for enrollment
Answer: A
Rationale: All states have laws requiring immunizations before enrollment; this protects the whole community and lowers costs, reflecting herd-immunity logic.
Trap: An option suggesting immunization is optional, or that some states require none, directly contradicts the chapter.

Topic: frameworks
Subtopic: WSCC model

Q: The CDC's Whole School, Whole Community, Whole Child (WSCC) model is best described as which of the following?
A) A federal law requiring individualized health plans
B) A framework that school health programs are encouraged to follow
C) A billing structure for school-based health centers
D) A screening tool for identifying students needing an IEP
Answer: B
Rationale: The Whole School, Whole Community, Whole Child (WSCC) model is the CDC framework that school health programs are encouraged to follow.
