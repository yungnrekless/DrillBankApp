# Chapter 2 drills — The History of Public Health and Public/Community Health Nursing

Authored from `sources/Ch2_Fact_Bank.md` (Stanhope & Lancaster, 6th ed.,
printed pp. 20–37). This chapter is almost entirely named people and their
"firsts," landmark dates, and key organizations, so the drills below lean
hard on the fact bank's own flagged confusable trio — Wald, Nightingale, and
Jessie Sleet — and on the dates/organizations tables.

---

Topic: key historical figures
Subtopic: Nightingale

Q: Florence Nightingale trained briefly at Kaiserswerth, Germany, in what kind of nursing?
A) Deaconess-run district nursing
B) Military field nursing
C) Hospital-based surgical nursing
D) Settlement house nursing
Answer: A
Rationale: Nightingale trained briefly at Kaiserswerth, Germany, with Lutheran deaconesses who practiced district nursing.

Q: During the Crimean War, Nightingale's population-based approach and use of simple epidemiology cut soldier mortality at Scutari from approximately what rate to what rate?
A) 415 per 1000 to 11.5 per 1000
B) 11.5 per 1000 to 415 per 1000
C) 250 per 1000 to 50 per 1000
D) 100 per 1000 to 15 per 1000
Answer: A
Rationale: Nightingale's population-based approach and simple epidemiology cut soldier mortality from 415 per 1000 to 11.5 per 1000 during the Crimean War (1854–1856) at Scutari.
Trap: The two figures are easy to invert. Mortality dropped sharply — the larger number is the "before."

Q: In what year did the Nightingale Training School for Nurses open, and where?
A) 1860, St. Thomas' Hospital, London
B) 1850, Kaiserswerth, Germany
C) 1873, Bellevue Hospital, New York
D) 1859, Liverpool, England
Answer: A
Rationale: Nightingale founded the Nightingale Training School at St. Thomas' Hospital, London, in 1860.
Trap: 1873/Bellevue is the first Nightingale-model school in the US, not the original Nightingale school in London — a different milestone entirely.

Q: What is Florence Nightingale most fundamentally credited with, per the fact bank's summary?
A) Being the first public health nurse in the United States
B) Providing the model of trained nursing and nursing education
C) Founding the Henry Street Settlement
D) Establishing the first US visiting nurse service
Answer: B
Rationale: Nightingale is the model of trained nursing and nursing education generally; she is British and predates the founding of US public health nursing specifically.
Trap: This is the core confusable pair from the fact bank. Nightingale founded trained nursing broadly; Lillian Wald was the first public health nurse in the United States. Do not credit Nightingale with the US "first."

Subtopic: Wald, Sleet, and the confusable "firsts"

Q: Who was the first public health nurse in the United States?
A) Florence Nightingale
B) Lillian Wald
C) Jessie Sleet Scales
D) Mary Breckinridge
Answer: B
Rationale: Lillian Wald was the first public health nurse in the United States and coined the term "public health nurse."
Trap: This is the single most common stem in the chapter. Nightingale is British and founded trained nursing generally; Jessie Sleet was specifically the first African American PHN. Neither is the plain "first US PHN."

Q: Who was the first African American public health nurse, hired in 1900 by the New York Charity Organization Society?
A) Lillian Wald
B) Jessie Sleet (Scales)
C) Mary Brewster
D) Lina Rogers
Answer: B
Rationale: Jessie Sleet (Scales) was hired by the NY Charity Organization Society in 1900 as the first African American public health nurse and studied TB among African Americans in Manhattan.
Trap: Wald is the first US PHN overall (not specifically the first African American one); keep the two "firsts" attached to the correct qualifier.

Q: With whom did Lillian Wald co-found the Henry Street Settlement in 1893?
A) Mary Brewster
B) Jessie Sleet Scales
C) Mary S. Gardner
D) Lina Rogers
Answer: A
Rationale: Wald and Mary Brewster founded the Henry Street Settlement (1893) in New York's Lower East Side, which became the Visiting Nurse Service of NYC.

Q: Which statements about Lillian Wald are accurate? Select all that apply.
A) Coined the term "public health nurse"
B) Arranged the first insurance reimbursement for nursing, through Metropolitan Life in 1909
C) Was the first president of the NOPHN
D) Studied tuberculosis among African Americans in Manhattan
E) Helped establish the Children's Bureau and school nursing
Answer: A, B, C, E
Rationale: Wald coined "public health nurse," arranged the first insurance reimbursement for nursing (Metropolitan Life, 1909), became the first NOPHN president (1912), and helped establish the Children's Bureau and school nursing.
Trap: Studying TB among African Americans in Manhattan is Jessie Sleet's contribution, not Wald's — a near-miss distractor built from the chapter's other flagged "first."

Q: A question stem describes a nurse who "used a population-based approach and simple epidemiology to reduce mortality in a military hospital." Which figure does this describe, and who is the easiest person to confuse her with?
A) Lillian Wald; easy to confuse with Mary Breckinridge
B) Florence Nightingale; easy to confuse with Lillian Wald
C) Jessie Sleet; easy to confuse with Florence Nightingale
D) Mary S. Gardner; easy to confuse with Jessie Sleet
Answer: B
Rationale: The Crimean War mortality statistic belongs to Nightingale. She is the figure most often confused with Wald, since both are described as working at the population level — but Nightingale is British military-hospital epidemiology, while Wald is the first US public health nurse working in a community settlement.
Trap: The fact bank explicitly pairs Nightingale and Wald as the two population-level pioneers most likely to be swapped in a stem.

Subtopic: other named firsts and founders

Q: William Rathbone established district nursing in which city and year?
A) Liverpool, England, 1859
B) London, England, 1860
C) New York, United States, 1893
D) Edinburgh, Scotland, 1850
Answer: A
Rationale: William Rathbone established district nursing in Liverpool, England, in 1859.

Q: Who became the first school nurse, in New York City in 1902?
A) Jessie Sleet Scales
B) Lina Rogers
C) Frances Root
D) Pearl McIver
Answer: B
Rationale: Lina Rogers, a Henry Street resident, became the first school nurse in New York City in 1902.

Q: Mary Breckinridge founded the Frontier Nursing Service in 1925 to serve which population, using what type of provider?
A) Urban tenement families, using settlement house nurses
B) Rural Appalachian Kentucky, using nurse-midwives
C) Homeless populations in New York, using visiting nurses
D) Southern African American communities, using public health nurses
Answer: B
Rationale: Breckinridge founded the Frontier Nursing Service (1925), using nurse-midwives to serve rural Appalachian Kentucky.

Q: Who was the first trained nurse hired to visit the sick poor at home, marking the beginning of organized visiting nursing in the US?
A) Frances (Francis) Root, 1877
B) Lillian Wald, 1893
C) Mary Brewster, 1893
D) Lina Rogers, 1902
Answer: A
Rationale: Frances (Francis) Root was hired in 1877 by the Women's Board of the NY Mission to visit the sick poor at home — the start of organized US visiting nursing, predating Wald's Henry Street Settlement by 16 years.
Trap: Wald's Henry Street Settlement (1893) is often mistaken for the true beginning of US visiting nursing, but Root's 1877 hiring came first.

Q: Who wrote the 1919 textbook Public Health Nursing?
A) Mary S. Gardner
B) Lemuel Shattuck
C) Pearl McIver
D) Mary Breckinridge
Answer: A
Rationale: Mary S. Gardner wrote the textbook Public Health Nursing (1919).

Q: Pearl McIver holds which distinction, achieved in 1934?
A) First public health nurse in the United States
B) First nurse employed by the US Public Health Service
C) First African American public health nurse
D) First nurse-midwife in the Frontier Nursing Service
Answer: B
Rationale: Pearl McIver became the first nurse employed by the US Public Health Service in 1934.

Q: Lemuel Shattuck authored which landmark 1850 public health document?
A) The Shattuck Report
B) The Flexner Report
C) The Sheppard-Towner Act
D) Public Health Nursing
Answer: A
Rationale: Lemuel Shattuck authored the Shattuck Report (1850), a landmark public health document.

Topic: landmark dates and organizations
Subtopic: landmark dates

Q: Put these in chronological order: (1) Henry Street Settlement founded, (2) Shattuck Report, (3) Frontier Nursing Service established, (4) NOPHN formed.
A) 2, 1, 4, 3
B) 1, 2, 4, 3
C) 2, 4, 1, 3
D) 4, 2, 1, 3
Answer: A
Rationale: Shattuck Report (1850) → Henry Street Settlement (1893) → NOPHN formed (1912) → Frontier Nursing Service (1925).

Q: In what year was the American Public Health Association (APHA) established?
A) 1850
B) 1860
C) 1872
D) 1893
Answer: C
Rationale: APHA was established in 1872.
Trap: 1850 is the Shattuck Report and 1860 is the Nightingale Training School — both close neighbors on the same timeline.

Q: The first Nightingale-model nursing school in the United States opened in 1873 at which institution?
A) Bellevue Hospital, New York City
B) Henry Street Settlement, New York City
C) St. Thomas' Hospital, London
D) Johns Hopkins Hospital, Baltimore
Answer: A
Rationale: The first Nightingale-model nursing school in the US opened at Bellevue Hospital, NYC, in 1873.

Q: What happened in 1909 that is described as a landmark for public health nursing financing?
A) The first nurse practice acts were passed
B) Metropolitan Life initiated the first insurance reimbursement for nursing care
C) The Social Security Act was passed
D) The USPHS established a division of public health nursing
Answer: B
Rationale: In 1909, Metropolitan Life initiated the first insurance reimbursement for nursing care, arranged by Lillian Wald.
Trap: The first nurse practice acts date to 1903, six years earlier — an easy date to swap in.

Q: Which pair of events both occurred in 1934 and 1935, respectively?
A) Pearl McIver becomes first USPHS nurse; Social Security Act passed
B) NOPHN formed; Frontier Nursing Service established
C) Jessie Sleet becomes first African American PHN; Lina Rogers becomes first school nurse
D) Shattuck Report published; APHA established
Answer: A
Rationale: Pearl McIver became the first nurse employed by USPHS in 1934, and the Social Security Act — which funded public health and expanded the federal role in health and welfare — passed in 1935.

Subtopic: key organizations

Q: The National Organization for Public Health Nursing (NOPHN), formed in 1912 with Lillian Wald as its first president, was later folded into which organization?
A) The American Public Health Association (APHA)
B) The National League for Nursing (NLN)
C) The American Nurses Association (ANA)
D) The US Public Health Service (USPHS)
Answer: B
Rationale: NOPHN (1912) was later folded into the National League for Nursing (NLN), formed in the 1952 consolidation.

Q: Which organization has roots in the 1896 Associated Alumnae and was renamed in 1911?
A) APHA
B) NOPHN
C) ANA
D) NLN
Answer: C
Rationale: The American Nurses Association (ANA) has roots in the 1896 Associated Alumnae and was renamed in 1911.

Q: The US Public Health Service (USPHS) established a division of public health nursing in what year?
A) 1900
B) 1912
C) 1918
D) 1934
Answer: C
Rationale: The USPHS established a division of public health nursing in 1918.
Trap: 1934 is when Pearl McIver became the first nurse employed by USPHS — a related but distinct milestone from the division's founding in 1918.

Q: Which organization ran rural and town-and-country nursing services that Lillian Wald helped establish?
A) American Red Cross
B) NOPHN
C) USPHS
D) AACN
Answer: A
Rationale: The American Red Cross ran rural/town-and-country nursing services, which Wald helped establish.

Q: Which statements about the American Public Health Association (APHA) and NOPHN are accurate? Select all that apply.
A) APHA was established in 1872
B) NOPHN was formed in 1912 with Wald as its first president
C) NOPHN was later folded into the ANA
D) APHA predates NOPHN by 40 years
E) NOPHN, not APHA, is a nursing-specific organization
Answer: A, B, D, E
Rationale: APHA (1872) predates NOPHN (1912) by 40 years; NOPHN was formed with Wald as first president and was a nursing-specific organization.
Trap: NOPHN was later folded into the NLN, not the ANA — a plausible-sounding swap among the chapter's four nursing/public-health organizations.
