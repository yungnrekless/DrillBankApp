# Chapter 3 drills — US and Global Health Care

Authored from `sources/Ch3_Fact_Bank.md` (Stanhope & Lancaster, 6th ed.,
printed pp. 38–57). This chapter's job on the blueprint is system structure,
the cost/access/quality problems, the federal/state/local organization, and
the global comparison. The payment mechanisms also named on the shared Ch3/Ch4
blueprint line — managed care, HMO, PPO, Medicare, Medicaid, CHIP — are
actually defined in Chapter 5, so they are drilled there, not here, to avoid
duplicating that content.

Topics are primary care vs. primary health care (the single highest-yield
item in the chapter), the three system problems, federal/state/local
organization, and global health.

Topic: US health care system
Subtopic: primary care vs. primary health care

Q: A nurse is asked to distinguish "primary care" from "primary health care" for a class of new students. Which statement correctly captures the difference?
A) Primary care is population-focused; primary health care is individual-focused
B) Primary care is an individual's first point of contact with the health system; primary health care is essential care made universally accessible to a community with its full participation
C) The two terms are interchangeable synonyms for the same delivery model
D) Primary care refers to hospital-based care; primary health care refers to outpatient care
Answer: B
Rationale: Primary care is the individual's first point of contact with the health system, provided by physicians, NPs, CNSs, PAs, and CNMs. Primary health care (PHC) is essential health care made universally accessible to individuals and families in the community, with their full participation, at an affordable cost — a population/community-level model.
Trap: The two terms are one word apart and are the most commonly swapped pair on this blueprint line. If the stem says "individual" or "first contact," it is primary care; if it says "community," "universal access," or "participation," it is primary health care.

Q: Which model of care traces its origin to the Declaration of Alma-Ata (1978)?
A) Primary care
B) Primary health care
C) Managed care
D) Public health surveillance
Answer: B
Rationale: Primary health care emerged from the 1978 WHO Declaration of Alma-Ata, which stated that health is a human right and introduced the PHC model along with the role of community health workers and traditional healers.
Trap: Primary care is a clinical delivery model with no Alma-Ata origin. Any stem naming Alma-Ata is pointing to primary health care.

Q: A community health nurse works with local residents to design and deliver essential health services that are affordable and universally accessible, with the community's full participation in identifying its own priorities. This best exemplifies which concept?
A) Primary care
B) Primary health care
C) Managed care
D) Public health assurance
Answer: B
Rationale: This scenario describes primary health care: essential care made universally accessible to individuals and families in the community, with their full participation, at an affordable cost — the Alma-Ata model.
Trap: "Community participation" and "universal access" are the scenario's tell. A first-contact clinic visit would be primary care instead.

Q: Which statements describe primary health care (PHC) as opposed to primary care? Select all that apply.
A) Population/community focus
B) Traces to the Declaration of Alma-Ata, 1978
C) Emphasizes prevention and equity
D) Delivered by physicians, NPs, CNSs, PAs, and CNMs as an individual's first contact
E) Built on full community participation
Answer: A, B, C, E
Rationale: PHC is population/community-focused, emerged from Alma-Ata, emphasizes prevention and equity, and is built on full community participation. Being an individual's first point of contact, delivered by physicians, NPs, CNSs, PAs, and CNMs, describes primary care instead.
Trap: Option D is a true statement about primary care, not PHC — it belongs in the individual-focused column of the confusable pair.

Q: Which professionals are described as delivering primary care as an individual's first point of contact with the health system? Select all that apply.
A) Physicians
B) Nurse practitioners
C) Clinical nurse specialists
D) Physician assistants
E) Certified nurse-midwives
Answer: A, B, C, D, E
Rationale: Primary care — first-contact, individual clinical care — is provided by physicians, NPs, CNSs, PAs, and CNMs. Advanced practice nurses (NPs, CNSs, CNMs) are described as vital members of primary care teams.
Trap: Do not confuse this list of individual-care providers with the population-level participation (community members, community health workers, traditional healers) built into the primary health care model.

Topic: US health care system
Subtopic: the three system problems — cost, access, quality

Q: US health care spending as a percentage of GDP is best described how, relative to other industrialized nations?
A) Comparable to the average of other industrialized nations
B) About 19.7% of GDP, the highest share among industrialized nations
C) Lower than most industrialized nations because of managed care controls
D) Roughly 10% of GDP, similar to most developed countries
Answer: B
Rationale: US health spending reached 19.7% of GDP, the highest share among industrialized nations, despite not leading on health outcomes.
Trap: The US spends the most but does not have the best outcomes — a question that implies high spending equals high quality is testing this exact mismatch.

Q: US health care spending was approximately how much in 2009, and how much is it projected to reach by 2026?
A) $1.2 trillion in 2009, projected to reach $2.5 trillion by 2026
B) $2.5 trillion in 2009, projected to reach $5.7 trillion by 2026
C) $5.7 trillion in 2009, projected to reach $8 trillion by 2026
D) $2.5 trillion in 2009, projected to remain flat through 2026
Answer: B
Rationale: US health spending reached $2.5 trillion in 2009 and is projected to reach $5.7 trillion by 2026, about 19.7% of GDP.

Q: Within US health care spending, which statement correctly ranks the shares of expenditure?
A) The largest share goes to acute care, with physician services next; public health receives the smallest share
B) The largest share goes to public health, with acute care next
C) Physician services receive the largest share, followed by prescription drugs
D) Acute care and public health receive roughly equal shares
Answer: A
Rationale: The largest share of expenditures goes to acute care, with physician services next; public health receives the smallest share. Other cost drivers include prescription drugs, technology, chronic/degenerative disease care, and the aging baby-boomer population swelling Medicare enrollment.
Trap: Public health is consistently the smallest slice of the pie — a distractor that puts public health spending on par with or above acute care inverts this.

Q: The US access problem is often described as a "two-class system." Which group is specifically identified as falling into the gap between the two classes?
A) Medicare beneficiaries
B) The working poor, who earn too much for public funds but cannot afford private insurance
C) Undocumented immigrants only
D) Retirees on fixed incomes
Answer: B
Rationale: The US is described as a two-class system — private and public. Those with insurance or ability to pay get superior care, while the working poor (who earn too much for public funds but can't afford insurance) and undocumented immigrants get lower-quality care.
Trap: Undocumented immigrants are also named as falling into the lower-quality-care group, but the working poor are the group defined specifically by being caught between the two classes on the basis of income.

Q: How did the number of uninsured Americans change between 2012 and 2016?
A) It rose from 27.6 million to 48 million
B) It fell from 48 million (pre-ACA) to 27.6 million
C) It remained essentially unchanged at approximately 40 million
D) It fell to zero following full ACA implementation
Answer: B
Rationale: Uninsured Americans fell from 48 million in 2012 (pre-ACA) to 27.6 million in 2016. The uninsured receive less preventive care, are diagnosed at more advanced stages, and receive less treatment.

Q: Which statements about federally funded community health centers are accurate? Select all that apply.
A) There are more than 1,300 nationwide
B) They serve medically underserved areas, both rural and urban
C) They serve people of all ages, races, and ethnicities regardless of insurance status
D) Staffing includes NPs, RNs, PAs, physicians, social workers, and dentists
E) They serve only patients who already carry private insurance
Answer: A, B, C, D
Rationale: More than 1,300 federally funded community health centers serve medically underserved areas (rural or urban), all ages/races/ethnicities, insured or not, using NPs, RNs, PAs, physicians, social workers, and dentists.
Trap: The centers exist specifically to serve people regardless of insurance status, including the uninsured — an option restricting them to already-insured patients inverts their purpose.

Topic: US health care system
Subtopic: federal, state, and local organization

Q: Which federal department is described as the agency most involved with US citizens' health and welfare, administering 100+ programs through 10 regional offices?
A) Department of Homeland Security (DHS)
B) US Department of Health and Human Services (USDHHS)
C) Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS)
D) US Public Health Service (USPHS)
Answer: B
Rationale: USDHHS is the federal agency most involved with citizens' health and welfare. It administers 100+ programs, maintains 10 regional offices, and contains the office of the secretary plus 11 agencies.

Q: The US Public Health Service (USPHS), a major component of HHS, comprises eight agencies. Which of the following are among them? Select all that apply.
A) CDC
B) FDA
C) NIH
D) SAMHSA
E) CMS
Answer: A, B, C, D
Rationale: The USPHS comprises eight agencies, including CDC, FDA, HRSA, AHRQ, ATSDR, IHS, NIH, and SAMHSA.
Trap: CMS is a separate HHS component that administers Medicare and Medicaid — it is not one of the eight USPHS agencies.

Q: Which federal agency administers Medicare and Medicaid?
A) US Public Health Service (USPHS)
B) Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS)
C) Department of Homeland Security (DHS)
D) Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA)
Answer: B
Rationale: The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) administers Medicare and Medicaid. (The details of what Medicare and Medicaid cover are Chapter 5 content — this chapter covers only which agency runs them.)

Q: The Department of Homeland Security (DHS), created in 2003, runs which community-level emergency preparedness program?
A) CERT (Community Emergency Response Team)
B) HRSA Emergency Response Corps
C) National Disaster Medical System
D) Strategic National Stockpile
Answer: A
Rationale: DHS, created in 2003, runs the CERT (Community Emergency Response Team) program, with goals of awareness, prevention, protection, response, and recovery.

Q: At the state level, which statement correctly describes the state health department's role and authority?
A) States administer Medicare and Medicaid directly, independent of CMS
B) State health departments handle licensing, health statistics, and communicable disease control, and hold police power to protect public health
C) State health departments deliver most direct clinical services, bypassing local health departments
D) States are the level at which USPHS agencies are organized
Answer: B
Rationale: State health departments handle licensing, health statistics, communicable disease control, and coordinate local health; states hold police power to protect public health. The local health department is where services actually reach the community, with functions varying widely by locality.
Trap: Direct service delivery to the community is a local, not state, function — states coordinate and regulate, localities deliver.

Topic: global health
Subtopic: WHO, Alma-Ata, and international goals

Q: What did the 1978 Declaration of Alma-Ata state?
A) Health is a commodity to be purchased through market competition
B) Health is a human right and should be the primary goal of every government
C) Primary care should replace primary health care as the dominant global model
D) Nations should adopt a single unified system of managed care
Answer: B
Rationale: The Declaration of Alma-Ata (1978), issued by WHO, stated that health is a human right and should be the primary goal of every government, and introduced primary health care along with the role of community health workers and traditional healers.

Q: How do the Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) and Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) compare?
A) The MDGs (17 goals, 2000–2015) were succeeded by the SDGs (8 goals, 2015–), with SDG 3 focused on health
B) The MDGs (8 goals, 2000–2015) were succeeded by the SDGs (17 goals, 2015–); SDG 3 is Good Health and Well-Being
C) The MDGs and SDGs ran concurrently, each covering different UN member states
D) The SDGs (8 goals) preceded the MDGs (17 goals)
Answer: B
Rationale: The Millennium Development Goals were 8 UN goals spanning 2000–2015. The Sustainable Development Goals succeeded them with 17 goals from 2015 onward; SDG 3 is Good Health and Well-Being.
Trap: The goal counts are easy to swap — 8 belongs to the MDGs, 17 to the SDGs, and the SDGs came after, not before.

Q: Which organization leads the "Leadership for Change" program, positioning nursing to take a leadership role in achieving health for all?
A) WHO
B) ICN (International Council of Nurses)
C) USDHHS
D) USPHS
Answer: B
Rationale: The International Council of Nurses (ICN) leads the Leadership for Change program, through which nursing takes a leadership role in achieving health for all.
