Topic: public health model of violence
Subtopic: socioecological model and CDC framework

Q: What are the four steps of the CDC's public health approach to violence prevention, in order?
A) Identify risk factors, identify the problem, disseminate strategies, develop strategies
B) Identify the problem, identify risk factors, develop and test prevention strategies, disseminate and implement
C) Develop strategies, identify the problem, disseminate strategies, identify risk factors
D) Disseminate strategies, identify risk factors, develop strategies, identify the problem
Answer: B
Rationale: The four steps in order are: identify the problem, identify the risk factors, develop and test prevention strategies, and disseminate and implement the strategies.

Q: A nurse observes that a neighborhood has high mobility, low cohesiveness, high population density, and a built environment with few green spaces. Which level of the socioecological model does this describe?
A) Individual
B) Relationship
C) Community
D) Societal
Answer: C
Rationale: The community level covers settings such as schools, workplaces, and neighborhoods, including considerations like high mobility, low cohesiveness, population density, built environment, and drug traffic.

Q: Which level of the socioecological model addresses a culture that accepts violence and perpetuates disparities between groups in society?
A) Individual
B) Relationship
C) Community
D) Societal
Answer: D
Rationale: The societal level covers broader factors contributing to a culture that accepts violence, reduces inhibitions against violence, and creates and perpetuates disparities between segments of society.

Q: Which factors are named at the individual level of the socioecological model? Select all that apply.
A) A history of abuse, including witnessing abuse
B) Substance use
C) Mental health disorders
D) Neighborhood population density
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: The individual level covers biological and personal history factors such as education, substance use, a history of abuse including witnessing it, and mental health disorders. Neighborhood density is a community-level factor.

Q: Which statements does the chapter make explicitly about the socioecological model? Select all that apply.
A) The presence of risks guarantees violence will occur
B) Protective factors do not eliminate the risk of violence
C) It is important to act across multiple levels of the model simultaneously
D) The presence of risk factors does not guarantee violence
Answer: B, C, D
Rationale: The chapter explicitly states that the presence of risks does not guarantee violence, protective factors do not eliminate risk, and it is important to act across multiple levels simultaneously.
Trap: Option A directly contradicts the chapter's own stated position — risk factors increase likelihood but do not guarantee an outcome.

Q: According to Sumner et al. (2015), violence tends to be higher in communities with which characteristics? Select all that apply.
A) Limited economic opportunities
B) High concentrations of poor and unemployed people
C) Frequent population turnover
D) Abundant civic and voluntary associations
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: Violence is higher in communities with limited economic opportunities, high concentrations of poor and unemployed people, frequent moving, limited public/mental health/social services, and fewer civic and voluntary associations — not abundant associations, which is protective.

Topic: homicide, assault, and suicide
Subtopic: homicide and firearms

Q: Which statement about intimate partner homicide (IPH) is accurate?
A) Women are nine times more likely to be killed by a stranger than an intimate partner
B) Women are nine times more likely to be killed by an intimate partner than by a stranger
C) Men and women face equal rates of intimate partner homicide
D) IPH rates are unrelated to prior domestic violence history
Answer: B
Rationale: Women are nine times more likely to be killed by an intimate partner than by a stranger, and the top risk factor for IPH is previous domestic violence.

Q: Which are named risk factors for intimate partner homicide? Select all that apply.
A) Access to guns
B) Nonfatal strangulation
C) Estrangement
D) Marital counseling attendance
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: Named risk factors include previous domestic violence (the top factor), access to guns, estrangement, threats to kill or with a weapon, nonfatal strangulation, and a stepchild in the home if the victim is female.

Q: What percentage of women killed by a husband, boyfriend, or ex-partner had been seen in a health care setting during the year before the homicide?
A) 25%
B) 50%
C) 75%
D) 90%
Answer: C
Rationale: 75% of women killed by a husband, boyfriend, or ex-partner had been seen in a health care setting during the year before the homicide — underscoring the importance of screening at routine encounters.

Q: What does the chapter say about a nurse's duty regarding severe abuse and homicide risk?
A) Nurses have no formal duty regarding homicide risk disclosure
B) Nurses have a duty to warn family members of the possibility of homicide when severe abuse is present, just as they warn of smoking hazards
C) This duty applies only to law enforcement, not nurses
D) The duty to warn applies only after a homicide has occurred
Answer: B
Rationale: Nurses have a duty to warn family members of the possibility of homicide when severe abuse is present, just as they warn them of the hazards of smoking.

Q: Which statement about firearm fatalities is accurate?
A) Females account for 85% of firearm fatality victims
B) Males account for 85% of firearm fatality victims and 88% of nonfatal firearm injuries
C) Household gun ownership is associated with increased stranger homicide rates
D) More people die from firearms than suffer nonfatal firearm injuries
Answer: B
Rationale: Males account for 85% of firearm fatality victims and 88% of nonfatal firearm injuries. Higher household gun ownership is associated with increased NONstranger firearm homicides, not stranger homicides, and more people suffer nonfatal injuries than die.

Subtopic: assault and suicide

Q: What is named as the greatest risk factor for an individual's victimization through violence?
A) Income level
B) Age, with youths at significantly higher risk
C) Geographic region
D) Marital status
Answer: B
Rationale: The greatest risk factor for an individual's victimization through violence is age, and youths are at significantly higher risk.

Q: Which statement correctly describes sex differences in assault and homicide victimization?
A) More females than males are victims of homicide and assault
B) More males than females are victims of homicide and assault, but women are more likely to be victimized by a relative or male partner
C) Men and women are equally likely to be victimized by a relative
D) Homicide and assault show no sex-based patterns
Answer: B
Rationale: More males than females are victims of homicide and assault overall, but women are more likely to be victimized specifically by a relative, especially a male partner.

Q: Which statement about suicide rates is accurate, and which population is the notable exception to the general socioeconomic pattern?
A) Suicide rates are highest among the poorest and least educated, with no exceptions
B) Affluent and educated people often have higher suicide rates than disadvantaged people, except Native Alaskan and American Indian populations
C) Suicide rates show no relationship to socioeconomic status
D) Suicide rates are highest among the unemployed, with no notable exceptions
Answer: B
Rationale: Affluent and educated people often have higher rates of suicide than economically and educationally disadvantaged people; the exception is Native Alaskan and American Indian populations, who are often poor yet have alarmingly high suicide rates.
Trap: This inverts the usual "poverty equals worse outcomes" pattern common throughout this chapter, which is exactly why it is testable.

Q: What proportion of individuals who complete suicide had visited a health care provider in the prior month, and what is the significance of this finding?
A) A minority; this argues against universal screening
B) The majority; this is the rationale for universal screening
C) None; most avoid health care entirely before suicide
D) Exactly half; the data is inconclusive
Answer: B
Rationale: The majority of individuals who complete suicide have visited a health care provider in the prior month — this is the rationale for universal screening for suicide risk.

Q: Which statement best describes common reactions among suicide survivors (family/friends)?
A) They typically feel no guilt or self-blame
B) They often feel angry toward the dead person, yet may turn the anger inward, and may question their own liability
C) They generally increase social activities to cope
D) Survivors are unaffected if the death was expected
Answer: B
Rationale: Survivors often feel angry toward the dead person yet may turn the anger inward, question their own liability for the death, and may limit social activities because others are unable to talk about the suicide.

Topic: sexual violence and rape
Subtopic: statistics and misconceptions

Q: Which statement best reflects the chapter's framing of rape?
A) Rape is fundamentally a crime of passion driven by sexual desire
B) Rape is a crime of violence, not passion; the underlying issues are hostility, power, and control
C) Rape is defined primarily by the use of a weapon
D) Rape requires evidence of physical injury to be classified as such
Answer: B
Rationale: Rape is a crime of violence, not a crime of passion. The underlying issues are hostility, power, and control rather than sexual desire, and the defining issue is lack of consent of the victim.

Q: Which statement correctly reflects the chapter's teaching on evidence of sexual violence?
A) Absence of physical evidence means sexual violence did not occur
B) Absence of physical evidence does not mean sexual violence did not occur
C) Physical evidence is required for a valid disclosure
D) Only visible injuries indicate lack of consent
Answer: B
Rationale: Per WHO 2015, cited in the chapter, the absence of physical evidence does not mean that sexual violence such as rape did not occur.

Q: Which statements about campus sexual assault are accurate, per the chapter? Select all that apply.
A) One in four college women will be a victim of sexual assault
B) About 60% of these assaults are perpetrated by an acquaintance
C) Fewer than 5% of incidents are brought to the attention of administrators or authorities
D) Graduate students are more likely to be victimized than undergraduates
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: One in four college women will be assaulted, 60% by an acquaintance, and fewer than 5% are reported to authorities. Female undergraduates are more likely to be victimized than graduate students — the reverse of option D.

Q: How does heavy episodic drinking affect the chance of being raped, per the chapter?
A) It has no measurable effect
B) It increases the chance eight-fold
C) It decreases the chance by increasing vigilance
D) It only affects male victims
Answer: B
Rationale: Heavy episodic drinking increases the chance of being raped eight-fold.

Q: Regarding circumstances of reported rapes, which statement is accurate?
A) Rural areas are higher-risk than cities, and most rapes occur in the early morning
B) Cities are higher-risk than rural areas, and the most critical times are between 8 pm and 2 am, weekends, and summer
C) The majority of rapists are strangers to the victim
D) Time of day and season have no bearing on risk
Answer: B
Rationale: Cities are higher-risk areas than rural areas, with the most critical times being 8 pm to 2 am, weekends, and summer. The majority of rapists are known to the victim, not strangers.

Q: Which of the following are misconceptions about rape that the chapter explicitly corrects? Select all that apply.
A) That women say "no" when they really mean "yes"
B) That victims are culpable because of how they dress or act
C) That people have the right to change their mind even after seeming initially agreeable
D) That pressure from intoxication-inducement is a violation of the law
Answer: A, B
Rationale: The chapter identifies the "no means yes" myth and victim-blaming based on dress/behavior as myths to be corrected. Options C and D are stated as TRUE facts by the chapter, not misconceptions to correct.

Subtopic: dating violence and post-assault nursing care

Q: Which four forms of behavior are included under both dating violence and intimate partner violence?
A) Sexual violence, physical violence, psychological aggression, and stalking
B) Financial abuse, neglect, isolation, and intimidation
C) Physical violence, verbal abuse, financial control, and abandonment
D) Stalking, financial abuse, neglect, and coercion
Answer: A
Rationale: Dating violence includes sexual violence, physical violence, psychological aggression, and stalking — occurring in person or electronically — and these same four forms also apply to intimate partner violence.

Q: When interviewing a patient after a suspected rape, why should the nurse ask "who hit you?" rather than "what happened to you?" in the context of visible facial injury?
A) "What happened to you" is more direct and should be preferred
B) "What happened to you" implies the nurse is neither knowledgeable nor comfortable with violence, potentially prompting a fabricated cause
C) Both phrasings produce identical disclosure rates
D) Neither phrasing affects disclosure
Answer: B
Rationale: Asking "what happened to you?" implies the nurse is neither knowledgeable nor comfortable with violence, which may prompt the woman to fabricate a more acceptable cause of her injury. The direct "who hit you?" signals comfort and openness to disclosure.
Trap: This is the single most quotable and testable line in the intimate partner violence screening section — the specific wording matters, not just the general idea of "ask about violence."

Q: When cutting clothing off a gunshot victim to provide care, what evidence-preservation principle should the nurse follow?
A) Cut directly through the bullet hole for the fastest access
B) Avoid cutting through the bullet hole; cut to the side of it to preserve the point of origin for investigation
C) Evidence preservation is not a nursing responsibility
D) Discard all clothing immediately regardless of cut location
Answer: B
Rationale: When cutting the shirt off a person shot in the chest, avoid cutting through the bullet hole — cut to the side of the hole to protect the point of origin of the bullet for later criminal investigation.

Q: Why should a single-session debriefing be completed during the initial examination after a sexual assault?
A) Because most victims request repeat sessions
B) Because many victims deny the event once the initial crisis has passed
C) Because insurance requires a single visit
D) Because follow-up sessions are more effective than initial debriefing
Answer: B
Rationale: Because many victims deny the event once the initial crisis has passed, a single-session debriefing should be completed during the initial examination.

Q: What are the CDC's five "STOP SV" strategies to prevent sexual violence? Select all that apply.
A) Promoting social norms that protect against violence
B) Providing opportunities to empower girls and women
C) Creating protective environments
D) Increasing criminal penalties as the primary strategy
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: The five STOP SV strategies are promoting protective social norms, teaching skills to prevent sexual violence, providing opportunities to empower girls and women, creating protective environments, and supporting victims/survivors — increasing criminal penalties is not among the five.

Topic: human trafficking
Subtopic: identifying and responding to trafficking

Q: Which statement best describes why trafficking victims often go unidentified in health care settings?
A) They rarely have any contact with health care providers
B) Most will contact a provider, often through the ED, but are seldom appropriately identified as trafficking victims
C) Health care providers are required to ignore trafficking indicators
D) Trafficking victims are always identified correctly on first contact
Answer: B
Rationale: Most trafficked individuals will come into contact with a health care provider, often through the emergency department, at some point during captivity — yet they are seldom appropriately identified as victims of trafficking.

Q: Which are named red flags suggesting a patient may be a trafficking victim? Select all that apply.
A) A companion who answers all questions for the patient
B) Inability to provide a home address
C) Unawareness of the city or state where services are being received
D) Arriving to the appointment alone and answering all questions independently
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: Red flags include a companion who won't let the patient answer, inability to provide an address, no identification documents, and being unaware of the city/state — arriving alone and answering independently would be the opposite of a red flag.

Q: What is the correct first step when a nurse suspects a patient may be a trafficking victim?
A) Immediately contact law enforcement regardless of the patient's wishes
B) Interview the patient alone; explaining this is routine practice can decrease suspicion in the captor
C) Confront the accompanying person directly about the suspicion
D) Wait for the patient to volunteer information unprompted
Answer: B
Rationale: The nurse should interview the patient alone, which facilitates disclosure. Explaining that interviewing patients alone is routine practice in the setting can decrease suspicion in the captor.

Q: If a suspected trafficking victim is an adult and NOT in immediate danger, what is the correct sequence for involving law enforcement?
A) Contact law enforcement immediately without informing the patient
B) Notify the patient before engaging law enforcement, to improve trust and facilitate disclosure
C) Never involve law enforcement for adult victims
D) Wait for the patient to request law enforcement involvement before any other action
Answer: B
Rationale: If not in immediate danger, notify the patient before engaging law enforcement on their behalf — involving the patient may improve trust and facilitate disclosure. If immediate danger is suspected, contact law enforcement directly.
Trap: The sequencing decision depends on immediacy of danger: immediate danger bypasses the notify-first step; no immediate danger requires notifying the patient first. For minors, a call to child protective services is necessary regardless.

Q: What is the phone number and key feature of the National Human Trafficking Resource Center hotline?
A) 911; available only during business hours
B) 1-888-373-7888; toll-free, available 24 hours/day, in 180 languages
C) A local number that varies by state
D) Available only in English and Spanish
Answer: B
Rationale: The National Human Trafficking Resource Center hotline is 1-888-373-7888, toll-free, available 24 hours a day, in 180 languages.

Q: In 2018, which type of trafficking was reported most frequently to the National Human Trafficking Hotline?
A) Labor trafficking
B) Sex trafficking
C) Sex and labor combined
D) Unspecified trafficking
Answer: B
Rationale: In 2018 the hotline reported 41,088 contacts; sex trafficking was the top type reported (7,859), followed by labor (1,249), then combined sex and labor (637), with 1,202 unspecified.

Topic: development of abusive patterns
Subtopic: risk factors and intergenerational transmission

Q: What are the three characterizing factors in the development of abusive patterns, and which is most predictably present?
A) Poverty, education level, and marital status; poverty is most predictable
B) Upbringing, living conditions, and increased stress; previous exposure to some form of violence is most predictably present
C) Age, gender, and occupation; occupation is most predictable
D) Substance use, mental illness, and unemployment; mental illness is most predictable
Answer: B
Rationale: The three characterizing factors are upbringing, living conditions, and increased stress. Of these, the one most predictably present is previous exposure to some form of violence.

Q: Which statement correctly describes the intergenerational mechanism of abuse?
A) Only men who witnessed childhood abuse are more likely to abuse their own children
B) Both men and women who witnessed abuse as children were more likely to abuse their children
C) Witnessing childhood abuse has no bearing on adult behavior
D) Financial solvency has no relationship to child abuse incidence
Answer: B
Rationale: Both men and women who witnessed abuse as children were more likely to abuse their own children, and childhood physical punishment teaches children to use violent conflict resolution as adults. Financial solvency and support tended to DECREASE the incidence of child abuse.

Q: Which statement best reflects why frequent household moves warrant careful nursing assessment?
A) Frequent moving is only a risk factor for abuse, never a sign of concealment
B) Frequent moving may be both a risk factor for abuse and a sign of a family avoiding detection
C) Frequent moving has no established relationship to family violence
D) Frequent moving is protective because it reduces community scrutiny
Answer: B
Rationale: Because frequent moving may be both a risk factor for abuse and a sign of an abusive family trying to avoid detection, nurses should assess such families carefully for abuse.
Trap: A stem describing a family that has moved several times in a short period is cuing careful assessment, not reassurance that nothing is wrong.

Q: Which statements about cross-contamination between types of family violence are accurate? Select all that apply.
A) Physical abuse of women may be accompanied by sexual abuse
B) When one child is abused, others in the family may also be abused
C) Severe wife abusers may commit other acts of violence, especially child abuse
D) Abuse of one family member guarantees the safety of other members
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: The chapter states no member of the family is guaranteed immunity from abuse and neglect — directly contradicting option D. Nurses who detect one form of abuse should suspect other forms may be present.

Q: What does the chapter identify as the personality profile most associated with abusers? Select all that apply.
A) Low tolerance for frustration and emotional instability
B) Verbally aggressive, hostile personality style
C) Emotional insecurity leading to dependence on a child or spouse to meet needs for feeling valued
D) High tolerance for frustration and emotionally stable temperament
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: Abusers tend to show hostile, verbally aggressive personality styles, low tolerance for frustration, emotional instability, and emotional insecurity that creates dependence on a child or spouse — the opposite of high frustration tolerance and emotional stability.

Topic: child abuse and neglect
Subtopic: types, statistics, and indicators

Q: The chapter's stated objective names four general types of child abuse. The body text adds a fifth. Which type is added in the body text but excluded from the four-type objective?
A) Physical abuse
B) Emotional abuse
C) Trafficking
D) Neglect
Answer: C
Rationale: The chapter objective states four types: neglect, physical, emotional, and sexual. The body text (p. 492) lists five, adding trafficking.
Trap: If a question asks for "the four types," trafficking is the distractor. If a SATA item offers five options including trafficking, trafficking is likely intended as correct. Know both framings.

Q: Which statements about child abuse statistics (2018) are accurate? Select all that apply.
A) At least 1 in 7 children experienced abuse or neglect the previous year
B) Children in their first year of life have the highest rate of being victims
C) Children living in poverty are five times more likely to be abused and neglected
D) The majority of perpetrators are strangers to the victim
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: At least 1 in 7 children experienced abuse/neglect; infants have the highest victimization rate; poverty increases risk five-fold. The majority of perpetrators (77.5%) are a parent of the victim, not a stranger.

Q: In families where only one child is targeted for abuse, which factors are named as increasing the likelihood a particular child is singled out? Select all that apply.
A) The child resembles a relative
B) The child is particularly bright, capable, or strong willed
C) The child tries to resist the violence
D) The child is the youngest and most passive
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: Named qualities that provoke a parent include resembling a relative, being handicapped, resisting the violence, or being bright, capable, or strong willed — not simply being the youngest and most passive.

Q: Which are among the eleven indicators of child abuse listed in the chapter? Select all that apply.
A) Subdural hematomas
B) Recent or healed fractures
C) Unusual fear of the nurse or others
D) Consistently improved hygiene compared to siblings
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: Indicators include unexplained injury, burns and scarring, recent or healed fractures, subdural hematomas, trauma to the genitalia, whiplash from shaking, dehydration/malnourishment, inappropriate food/drugs, poor general care (not improved hygiene), unusual fear, and being called a "bad" child.

Q: What does the chapter say about bedwetting in the context of ongoing child abuse?
A) Bedwetting has no relationship to abuse patterns
B) Bedwetting is often a trigger for further abuse, creating a particularly vicious cycle
C) Bedwetting always resolves once abuse is reported
D) Bedwetting is a reliable indicator that abuse is not occurring
Answer: B
Rationale: Bedwetting is often a trigger for further abuse, which creates a particularly vicious cycle — one of the physical/behavioral stress symptoms named alongside hyperactivity, withdrawal, and other stress-related conditions like asthma, stuttering, enuresis, and encopresis.

Subtopic: neglect, sexual abuse, and incest

Q: How does the chapter reconcile "physical neglect is most often associated with extreme poverty" with "neglect is not directly related to poverty"?
A) The two statements are contradictory errors
B) Physical neglect specifically correlates with extreme poverty, but neglect as a broader category occurs across the socioeconomic spectrum
C) Only emotional neglect is unrelated to poverty; physical neglect never occurs outside poverty
D) Neglect is entirely unrelated to any socioeconomic factor
Answer: B
Rationale: Physical neglect (failure to provide food, clothing, shelter, medical care) is most often associated with extreme poverty, but the chapter states neglect as a whole is NOT directly related to poverty and occurs across the socioeconomic spectrum of families — meaning emotional neglect and neglect broadly are not confined to poor families.
Trap: A stem implying that neglect only happens in poor families is wrong; the chapter explicitly separates physical neglect's poverty association from neglect's broader distribution.

Q: Which statement best describes the typical pattern of father-daughter incest, per the chapter?
A) The father typically relies on overt physical force
B) The daughter is usually about 9 years old at onset, often the oldest or only daughter, and the father relies on threats, bribes, or exploiting the child's need for affection rather than physical force
C) Incest is confined to specific socioeconomic or racial groups
D) Mothers never engage in child sexual abuse
Answer: B
Rationale: The daughter is usually about 9 years old at onset and often the oldest or only daughter; the father or stepfather seldom uses physical force, relying instead on threats, bribes, intimidation, or exploiting the daughter's need for affection. Incest occurs across all races, religions, and socioeconomic classes, and mothers do engage in child sexual abuse as well.

Q: How does the chapter reframe an adolescent running away from a violent home?
A) It is always a sign of delinquency requiring disciplinary action
B) It may be a healthy response to a violent family situation, not simply delinquency
C) Running away indicates the abuse allegations are likely false
D) It has no connection to family violence
Answer: B
Rationale: Running away is usually considered a sign of delinquency; however, an adolescent who runs away may be using a healthy response to a violent family situation.
Trap: This reframe is explicitly flagged as a likely correct-answer stem — the "obvious" answer (delinquency) is the trap.

Q: How does the chapter frame a "replacement pregnancy" after a child has been removed by the courts?
A) As definitive evidence of continued poor judgment
B) As a normal grief response and an opportunity for intensive intervention
C) As grounds for automatic termination of parental rights
D) As unrelated to the child's removal
Answer: B
Rationale: Abusive parents trying to replace a removed child is described as a normal response to the grief of losing a child, and the pregnancy can be perceived by the nurse as an opportunity for intensive intervention to prevent abuse of the expected child — rather than a sign of continued poor judgment or pathology.

Topic: intimate partner violence
Subtopic: prediction, screening, and process of abuse

Q: According to the chapter, which factor is most predictive of intimate partner abuse?
A) A violent childhood history in the woman
B) The male partner's violent background combined with possessiveness, controlling behavior, and extreme jealousy
C) The couple's income level
D) The length of the relationship
Answer: B
Rationale: Using evidence of a violent childhood to identify women at risk is less useful, because abuse cannot be predicted from characteristics of the individual woman. The violent background of an abusive male, combined with tendencies to be possessive, controlling, and extremely jealous, is most predictive of abuse.
Trap: Predicting IPV works on the male's characteristics, not the female's. Distractors will offer victim characteristics as the predictive factor — that is backwards from what the chapter states.

Q: What does the chapter say about substance abuse and battering?
A) Substance abuse directly causes intimate partner violence
B) Substance abuse is associated with battering, but cannot be said to cause the violence
C) There is no established association between substance abuse and battering
D) Substance abuse is only relevant to elder abuse, not IPV
Answer: B
Rationale: Substance abuse is also associated with battering, although it cannot be said to cause the violence — association is not the same as causation.

Q: Where on the body are abuse-related injuries often carefully inflicted so they can be disguised by clothing? Select all that apply.
A) Breasts and abdomen
B) Upper thighs and back
C) Face and hands
D) Feet
Answer: A, B
Rationale: Attacks are often carefully inflicted on parts of the body that can easily be disguised by clothing — breasts, abdomen, upper thighs, and back. Bruises and lacerations of the face, head, and trunk are also common but are not the "disguisable" pattern described.

Q: A woman describes minimizing an incident of violence early in her relationship, with both partners blaming external stressors, followed by her partner's apology. According to the process of abuse described by Ford-Gilboe et al., what tends to happen over time?
A) Abuse tends to de-escalate in frequency and severity as the relationship matures
B) Abuse tends to escalate in frequency and severity over time, and the man's remorse tends to lessen
C) The pattern remains static indefinitely once established
D) External blame decreases while personal accountability increases equally for both partners
Answer: B
Rationale: Abuse tends to escalate in frequency and severity over time, and the man's remorse tends to lessen. Women often come to feel responsible for the relationship's success and may blame themselves.

Q: Why is "leave immediately" not automatically the safest advice for a woman in an abusive relationship?
A) Leaving has no bearing on safety outcomes
B) Women who try to leave an abusive relationship are at significant risk for homicide
C) Leaving always resolves the danger immediately
D) The chapter recommends staying in all cases for safety
Answer: B
Rationale: Women who try to leave an abusive relationship are at significant risk for homicide — as a woman tries to leave, the risk for homicide increases. This does not mean she should stay; it means leaving requires careful safety planning, an order of protection, and shelter arrangements rather than being treated as automatically safe.
Trap: Any answer option treating immediate departure as automatically the safest choice, without a safety plan, is the trap.

Q: At which stage of an abusive relationship does the chapter say marital counseling may be useful, and what is true of all other stages?
A) Marital counseling is useful at all stages equally
B) Marital counseling may be useful at the early stage but is generally contraindicated at all other stages due to safety risk
C) Marital counseling is never appropriate at any stage
D) Marital counseling is most useful only after the relationship has ended
Answer: B
Rationale: Although marital counseling may be useful at the early stage, it is generally contraindicated at all other stages because of the risk to the woman's safety.

Q: What is the nurse's top priority when encountering a family with severe abuse present?
A) Preserving the family unit at all costs
B) The safety of the woman and her children as the priority
C) Immediately pursuing criminal charges against the abuser
D) Marital counseling to resolve underlying conflict
Answer: B
Rationale: A nurse encountering a family in which there is severe abuse needs to consider the safety of the woman and her children as the priority.

Q: How does the chapter describe a formerly battered woman who feels depressed and lonely after the relationship has ended?
A) This indicates unresolved pathology requiring intensive intervention
B) This is a normal response for which support is needed
C) This suggests the woman wishes to return to the relationship
D) This is an unusual and concerning reaction
Answer: B
Rationale: A formerly battered woman who is feeling depressed and lonely after the relationship has ended is exhibiting a normal response, including grief for the ended relationship, for which support is needed.

Subtopic: violence during pregnancy and strangulation

Q: What is the recommended frequency for assessing pregnant women for abuse?
A) Once, at the first prenatal visit only
B) At each prenatal care visit, plus postpartum home visits assessing for both child and partner abuse
C) Only if visible injury is present
D) Only during the third trimester
Answer: B
Rationale: All pregnant women should be assessed for abuse at each prenatal care visit, and postpartum home visits should include assessment for both child abuse and partner abuse.

Q: Which mechanism links a man's control of contraception to subsequent abuse risk?
A) Contraceptive control has no relationship to abuse
B) A man's control of contraception, a form of abusive controlling behavior, may lead to unintended pregnancy and subsequent abuse
C) Contraceptive control only affects unmarried couples
D) This mechanism applies only to postpartum abuse, not prenatal
Answer: B
Rationale: A man's control of contraception, a form of abusive controlling behavior, may lead to unintended pregnancy and subsequent abuse; his refusal to use a condom also places the woman at increased risk for STDs including HIV.

Q: Why might a strangulation victim be hesitant to report the assault, and why is this dangerous?
A) Because strangulation always leaves immediate, visible bruising that is embarrassing to disclose
B) Because visible evidence may not appear until hours or days later, so victims fear they won't be believed, despite strangulation being a violent and deadly act
C) Because strangulation is not associated with any risk of death
D) Because strangulation is unrelated to intimate partner violence
Answer: B
Rationale: Strangulation may not leave visible evidence until hours or days after the incident, so victims are often hesitant to report because they fear they will not be believed. It is described as "a violent and deadly act" and is a form of IPV that is often overlooked.

Q: Which physical signs should raise suspicion for nonfatal strangulation? Select all that apply.
A) Subconjunctival hemorrhage
B) Bruising on the upper neck, chin, or face
C) Conjunctival petechiae
D) Isolated ankle swelling
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: Physical signs of nonfatal strangulation include linear abrasions, bruising on the upper neck/chin/face, subconjunctival hemorrhage, conjunctival petechiae, and neck swelling or tenderness — not ankle swelling, which is unrelated.

Topic: elder abuse
Subtopic: definitions, types, and prevalence

Q: According to the CDC, at what age does a person qualify as an "older adult" for the purposes of defining elder abuse?
A) 55 or older
B) 60 or older
C) 65 or older
D) 70 or older
Answer: B
Rationale: The CDC defines an older adult as a person age 60 or older for purposes of the elder abuse definition — an intentional act or failure to act that causes a risk of harm to an older adult.
Trap: This is easy to confuse with the more familiar age-65 threshold used elsewhere in health policy. For elder abuse specifically, the chapter uses 60.

Q: Which prevalence statistics for elder abuse are accurate, per the chapter? Select all that apply.
A) CDC: abuse affects about 1 in 10 elders who live at home
B) WHO (2020): about 1 in 6 people 60 or older experienced abuse in community settings in the past year
C) Rates are also high in nursing homes and long-term care facilities
D) Elder abuse is exclusively a community-setting phenomenon, absent in institutional care
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: CDC estimates 1 in 10 elders living at home are affected; WHO's 2020 estimate is 1 in 6 people 60+ in community settings in the past year; rates are also high in nursing homes and long-term care facilities — contradicting option D.

Q: Which of the following are among the CDC's six named types of elder abuse? Select all that apply.
A) Abandonment — the caregiver leaves and no longer provides care
B) Financial — misuse of the elder's money, property, or assets
C) Emotional — behaviors that demean or affect self-esteem, including not letting the person see family
D) Medical — inappropriate prescribing by a physician
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: The six named types are physical, sexual, emotional, neglect, abandonment, and financial. "Medical abuse" as a standalone category is not among the six named types.

Q: What does the chapter identify as the most common form of psychological abuse of older adults?
A) Physical confinement
B) Rejection or simply ignoring older adults, signaling they are worthless and useless
C) Financial exploitation
D) Excessive caregiving attention
Answer: B
Rationale: The most common form of psychological abuse is rejection or simply ignoring older adults, indicating that they are worthless and useless to others.

Q: Which factors are named as precipitating elder abuse? Select all that apply.
A) The elder may be a physical, emotional, or financial burden on the caregiver
B) The elder may have previously been the abuser
C) Caregiver depression related to caring for confused and frail elders
D) Abundant caregiver respite resources reducing family stress
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: Precipitating factors include the elder being a burden leading to caregiver frustration and resentment, the elder having previously been an abuser, and caregiver depression from around-the-clock caregiving demands — abundant respite resources would reduce risk, not precipitate abuse, making option D the opposite of a risk factor.

Q: Why does the chapter caution that determining whether an elder's injuries result from abuse can be difficult?
A) Elder injuries are always clearly attributable to a single cause
B) It is often difficult to determine whether injuries result from abuse, falls, or other natural causes, given the fragility of elders' skin and vascular systems
C) Elder abuse never presents with physical injury
D) Only law enforcement can make this determination, never nursing assessment
Answer: B
Rationale: It is often difficult to determine whether the injuries of elders result from abuse, falls, or other natural causes, because of the fragility of elders' skin and vascular systems; careful assessment through observation and discussion can help determine the cause.

Q: Which of the CDC's six recommended actions to prevent elder abuse are listed? Select all that apply.
A) Listen to older adults and caregivers to understand challenges
B) Report abuse or suspected abuse to Adult Protective Services
C) Check in often on adults with few friends and family
D) Discourage caregivers from seeking outside support to maintain family privacy
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: The six recommended actions include listen, report, educate, learn the difference between abuse signs and normal aging, check in often, and provide overburdened caregivers with support — the opposite of discouraging outside support.

Topic: nursing interventions and prevention
Subtopic: levels of prevention and legal responsibilities

Q: Per the textbook's Levels of Prevention box for violence, which intervention represents primary prevention?
A) Early screening for abuse
B) Strengthening the individual and family by teaching parenting skills
C) Referring to community organizations once abuse signs are evident
D) Case management coordination after abuse is confirmed
Answer: B
Rationale: Primary prevention is strengthening the individual and family by teaching parenting skills. Secondary is early screening plus teaching stress management and recreation; tertiary is referral to community organizations once signs of abuse are evident.

Q: Per Box 27.3, which of the following prevention activities are tagged as secondary prevention? Select all that apply.
A) Assessing for marital discord
B) Counseling for at-risk parents
C) Assisting with controlling anger
D) Teaching parenting techniques
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: Box 27.3 tags assessing during routine exams, assessing for marital discord, counseling at-risk parents, and assisting with anger control as secondary prevention. Teaching parenting techniques is tagged as primary prevention.

Q: According to the chapter, in what role do nurses primarily engage when providing tertiary prevention for families experiencing violence?
A) Law enforcement liaison
B) Case manager, coordinating other agencies and activities
C) Marital counselor
D) Primary prevention educator
Answer: B
Rationale: Nurses engage in tertiary prevention by acting as a case manager and coordinating the other agencies and activities involved.

Q: What are the five principles of giving care to families experiencing violence? Select all that apply.
A) Intolerance for violence
B) Respect and caring for all family members
C) Safety as the first priority
D) Punitive judgment toward the abuser to reinforce accountability
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: The five principles are intolerance for violence, respect and caring for all family members, safety as the first priority, absolute honesty, and empowerment. Punitive judgment is explicitly rejected — the chapter states the behavior, not the person, should be condemned.
Trap: "Intolerance for violence" and "condemning the behavior, not the person" coexist without contradiction — a nurse can categorically oppose violence while still treating the abuser with respect as a person. An answer framing the nursing stance as punitive toward the abuser is wrong even though intolerance for violence is one of the five principles.

Q: What does the chapter say about nurses' mandatory reporting obligations for child abuse?
A) Reporting is required only when abuse is confirmed, not merely suspected
B) Nurses are mandatory reporters of child abuse, even when only suspected, in all states
C) Mandatory reporting applies only in states with specific nurse-reporting statutes
D) Nurses may choose whether to report based on family circumstances
Answer: B
Rationale: Nurses are mandatory reporters of child abuse, even when only suspected, in all states. They are also mandatory reporters of elder abuse and abuse of physically/cognitively dependent adults, and of felony assaults of anyone in most states.

Q: What documented bias affects provider willingness to report suspected abuse?
A) Providers report poor and affluent families at equal rates
B) Providers may be more willing to report abuse in a poor family than in a middle-class one
C) Providers are more willing to report abuse in affluent families
D) Reporting rates show no relationship to family socioeconomic status
Answer: B
Rationale: Physicians and nurses are sometimes reluctant to report abuse, and they may be more willing to report abuse in a poor family than in a middle-class one, or think an older adult or child is better off at home.
Trap: A stem where a nurse hesitates to report because a family appears affluent or professional is testing recognition of this exact bias — report consistently regardless of social class.

Q: How does the chapter frame referral to protective service agencies?
A) As an automatic step toward removal of the victim
B) As a way to get help, rather than an automatic step toward removal or criminal justice action
C) As a purely punitive legal mechanism
D) As applicable only to confirmed cases of abuse
Answer: B
Rationale: Referral to protective service agencies is a way to get help, rather than an automatic step toward removal of the victim or toward criminal justice action.

Q: What does the chapter recommend regarding family involvement when a nurse reports suspected abuse?
A) Reporting should be done covertly, without informing the family
B) Families should be included in reporting, with absolute honesty about what will be reported and what the nurse is feeling
C) Family involvement should be avoided to protect the reporting nurse
D) Only the abuser should be informed, not other family members
Answer: B
Rationale: Families should be included in any reporting so they can have input. Absolute honesty about what will be reported to officials, what the family can expect, what is being entered into records, and what the nurse is feeling is essential.

Q: The Nurse-Family Partnership (NFP) program delivers which service, and what outcome has it demonstrated?
A) Group therapy for batterers; reduced recidivism
B) Intensive prenatal and postnatal home visits by RNs to low-income first-time mothers, ideally to the child's second birthday; reduced child maltreatment rates
C) Emergency shelter placement; reduced homelessness
D) School-based curricula for adolescents; reduced bullying
Answer: B
Rationale: NFP provides intensive prenatal and postnatal home visits, ideally to the child's second birthday, delivered by registered nurses to low-income first-time mothers, and has been shown to reduce the rate of child maltreatment.

Q: What does the chapter teach about firearm safety as a primary prevention measure? Select all that apply.
A) Handguns are far more likely to kill family members than intruders
B) If a gun is kept, it should be stored unloaded and locked, with ammunition stored separately
C) Firearm accidents are a leading cause of death for young children
D) Nursing assessments should avoid asking about guns in the home to respect privacy
Answer: A, B, C
Rationale: The chapter teaches that handguns more often kill family members than intruders, firearm accidents are a leading cause of death for young children, and safe storage means unloaded, locked, with ammunition stored separately. Nursing assessments SHOULD include a question about guns in the home — the opposite of avoiding the topic.
