## §1 Foundational Concepts (p. 360–363)

### What is a family?

> **"Rather than settling for a universal definition, it seems more appropriate to DEFINE FAMILIES ACCORDING TO THE PARTICULAR ISSUE INVOLVED"** (Purdue University, 2015).

**Changes since the 1960s (Pew Research Center, 2018):** more unmarried couples raising children; more gay and lesbian couples raising children; more single women having children without a male partner; more people living together without getting married; more mothers of young children working outside the home; more people of different races marrying each other; and **more women not ever having children.**

### Family policy

> **"A nation's family health care policy is a PRIMARY DETERMINANT of family health. Family policy means ANYTHING DONE BY THE GOVERNMENT that DIRECTLY OR INDIRECTLY AFFECTS FAMILIES."**

- **The responsibility for family health programs is SHARED by the federal government with state and local governments.** Each state and each region within states has programs and laws related to family services.
- **The desired outcome:** "families derive a **sense of empowerment** and are able to **take responsibility for their own health.**"
- **Why disparities persist:** they resulted "at least in part, from previous attempts to develop and implement 'family policies' that either directly or indirectly affect specific issues related to family health **but which have FAILED TO TAKE A COMPREHENSIVE SYSTEM-WIDE APPROACH.**"
- **An effective family health policy "might begin with developing an INFRASTRUCTURE OF PROGRAMS designed to provide access to PRIMARY AND PREVENTIVE health care."** **Nurses would be key builders of this process.**

### The family as the unit where health behavior forms — the chapter's central claim

> **"It is WITHIN THE FAMILY that health VALUES, health HABITS, and health RISK PERCEPTIONS are DEVELOPED, ORGANIZED, AND CARRIED OUT."**

**What families maintain major responsibility for (p. 369):**
- **Determining what food is purchased and prepared**
- **Setting sleep patterns**
- **Planning family activities**
- **Setting and monitoring norms and expected behaviors about health and health risks**
- **Determining when a family member is ill**
- **Deciding when health care should be obtained**
- **Carrying out treatment regimens**

**The societal-norms example:** a television commercial for an over-the-counter stimulant features a man who coaches basketball, works at a rehabilitation center, works as a borough inspector, and pursues a college degree at night, with the drug credited for his energy. **"The message is clear: You can, and must, do it all, and taking drugs to succeed is a viable option."** The chapter uses this to show that **health risks are affected by societal norms.**

### Early approaches — three historical study areas

Historically, studies of the family in health and illness focused on:
1. **The effect of illness on families**
2. **The role of the family in the CAUSE of disease**
3. **The role of the family in its USE OF SERVICES**

**Named theorists:**

| Theorist | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Litman (1974)** | The family is **a primary unit of health care**; the relationship among health, health behavior, and family **"is a highly dynamic one in which each may have a dramatic effect on the other."** |
| **Mauksch (1974)** | Proposed **distinguishing between FAMILY health and INDIVIDUAL health** |
| **Pratt (1976)** | Proposed the **ENERGIZED FAMILY** as the ideal family type most effective in meeting health needs |
| **Doherty and McCubbin (1985)** | Proposed a **family health and illness cycle** comprising **SIX phases**, beginning with **family health promotion and risk reduction** and continuing through **vulnerability to illness, illness response, interaction with the health care system, and adapting to illness** |

**The ENERGIZED FAMILY (Pratt) — four characteristics:**
1. **Promotes freedom and change**
2. **Is actively engaged with a variety of other groups and organizations**
3. **Has FLEXIBLE ROLE RELATIONSHIPS and an EQUAL POWER STRUCTURE**
4. **Exhibits a HIGH DEGREE OF AUTONOMY in family members**

### The Alameda County seven lifestyle habits (Belloc et al., 1972)

**"These habits that were identified in 1972 remain beneficial today for health promotion."**

1. **Sleeping 7 to 8 hours a day**
2. **Eating breakfast almost every day**
3. **Never or rarely eating between meals**
4. **Being at or near the recommended height-adjusted weight**
5. **Never smoking cigarettes**
6. **Never or rarely drinking alcohol**
7. **Regularly participating in physical activity**

### Buettner's Blue Zones — nine lessons

1. **Move naturally** — be active without thinking about it
2. **Hara Hachi Bu** — painlessly cut calories by **20%**
3. **Plant slant** — avoid meat and processed foods
4. **Grapes of life** — drink red wine in moderation
5. **Purpose now** — take time to see the big picture
6. **Downshift** — take time to relieve stress
7. **Belong** — participate in a spiritual community
8. **Loved ones first** — make family a priority
9. **Right tribe** — be surrounded by those who share Blue Zone values

**Epidemiologic shift named:** **"the leading causes of morbidity and mortality have SHIFTED from INFECTIOUS DISEASES to CHRONIC DISEASES, ACCIDENTS, and VIOLENCE, all of which have strong LIFESTYLE and ENVIRONMENTAL components."**

### The Neuman Systems Model applied to family health

**Family health is defined in terms of SYSTEM STABILITY, characterized by FIVE interacting sets of factors:**
1. **Physiological**
2. **Psychological**
3. **Sociocultural**
4. **Developmental**
5. **Spiritual**

**"The Neuman Systems Model is a WELLNESS-ORIENTED model in which the nurse uses the STRENGTHS AND RESOURCES of the family to maintain system stability."** **It focuses on family wellness IN THE FACE OF CHANGE.**

**The three lines:**

| Line | Function |
|---|---|
| **Flexible external line of defense** | **A protective mechanism** — the outer buffer |
| **Normal line of defense** | The usual level of wellness |
| **Internal line of resistance** | The innermost protection |

> **"When a life event is big enough to CONTRACT the FLEXIBLE line of defense and BREAKS THROUGH the NORMAL line of defense, the family feels STRESS."**

**How wellness is determined:** **"The degree of wellness is determined by the AMOUNT OF ENERGY it takes for the system to become and remain stable. When MORE ENERGY IS AVAILABLE THAN IS BEING USED, the system REMAINS STABLE."**

**Energy-building characteristics named:** **social support, resources, and prevention (or avoidance) of stressors.**

**The Harris family example** (Ms. Harris/Gloria, 12-year-old Kevin newly diagnosed with type 2 diabetes, 8-year-old Leisha, and 75-year-old grandmother Betty) — the model's five assessment questions:
1. **Physiological:** Is the family physically able to deal with Kevin's illness? Is everyone else currently healthy? Are there current health stressors?
2. **Psychological:** How well will the family deal with the illness psychologically? Are relationships stable and healthy? Are there memories of other family members with diabetes?
3. **Sociocultural:** Does the family have social support? Are treatment and diagnosis culturally sensitive? Can family members support each other?
4. **Developmental:** How will Kevin's development as a preadolescent be affected? How will the family's development change? How will the diagnosis affect Leisha?
5. **Spiritual:** How will the family's spiritual beliefs be affected? What effect will they have on Kevin's treatment and willingness to adhere to therapy?

### Health risk — the three categories

**Health risks can be classified into THREE general categories (USDHHS, 2010):**

| Category | Contents |
|---|---|
| **1. Inherited BIOLOGICAL risks** | **Including age-related risks** |
| **2. ENVIRONMENTAL risks** | **Composed of SOCIAL factors, ECONOMIC, and PHYSICAL environments** |
| **3. BEHAVIORAL risks** | Personal health habits / lifestyle |

> ⚠️ **The synergy principle (a likely item):** **"Although SINGLE risk factors can influence outcomes, THE COMBINED EFFECT OF ACCUMULATED RISKS IS OFTEN GREATER THAN THE SUM OF THE INDIVIDUAL EFFECTS."**

**The worked example:** a **family history of cardiovascular disease** (single biological risk) is **exacerbated by smoking** (a behavioral risk **more likely to occur if other family members also smoke**), and is **affected either positively or negatively by diet and exercise**, which are **influenced by both family and society's norms.** Regional example: **residents of the Northwest and West have historically been more likely to eat heart-healthy diets and exercise** than people in the Midwest and South, and **those communities are more supportive of exercise programs, bicycle paths, and lower-fat diets.**

**"A nurse working with POPULATIONS of families and intervening WITHIN A COMMUNITY is MORE LIKELY to reduce the effects of the health risks overall and produce a healthier community as well as a healthier family unit."**

### Health risk appraisal

**Definition:** **the process of ASSESSING FOR THE PRESENCE of specific factors in each of the categories associated with an increased likelihood of an illness (such as cancer) or an unhealthy event (such as an automobile accident).**

**Methods:** computer software programs and paper-and-pencil instruments.

**Named instrument: the Youth Risk Behavior Surveillance System (YRBSS)** of the CDC. **Monitors priority health-risk behaviors and the prevalence of OBESITY and ASTHMA among youth and young adults.**

**The general approach:** **"determine whether a risk factor is PRESENT and TO WHAT DEGREE. On the basis of scientific evidence, each factor is WEIGHTED and a TOTAL SCORE is derived."** ⚠️ **"This appraisal method provides an INDIVIDUAL score that can be examined AS A WHOLE WITHIN THE FAMILY, thus appraising the health risks LIKELY TO BE EXPERIENCED BY OTHER MEMBERS of the family."**

### Health risk reduction

**Assumption:** **decreasing the NUMBER or the MAGNITUDE of risks will decrease the PROBABILITY of an undesired event occurring.**

**The adolescent substance abuse example:** family behaviors such as **parents not drinking, alcohol not available in the home, and family contracts related to alcohol and drug use.** Also **family discussions about the pros and cons of drinking.**

**Murdaugh's three risk-tolerance principles — counterintuitive and testable:**

| Principle | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Voluntary vs. imposed** | **"VOLUNTARILY assumed risks, such as overeating, are TOLERATED BETTER than those IMPOSED BY OTHERS."** |
| **Scientific uncertainty** | **"Risks about which scientists DEBATE AND ARE UNCERTAIN are MORE FEARED than risks about which scientists AGREE"** (such as the causes of colon cancer) |
| **Natural vs. human-made** | **"Risks of NATURAL origin, such as hurricanes, are often considered LESS THREATENING than those CREATED BY HUMANS."** |

> **The public health principle:** **"A public health approach to risk reduction would say that IT IS ALWAYS MORE EFFECTIVE TO PREVENT DISEASE OR HEALTH DISRUPTION THAN TO TREAT, CURE, OR REHABILITATE."**

### Family crisis, resources, and coping

**Definition:** **"A family crisis occurs when the family is NOT ABLE TO COPE with an event and becomes DISORGANIZED OR DYSFUNCTIONAL."** And: **"A family crisis exists when the DEMANDS of the situation EXCEED the RESOURCES of the family."**

**Named crisis-producing life events:** **a child leaving home for college or to live independently; divorce or death in the family; job loss or job change; relocation.**

**Resources vs. coping strategies (Price et al., 2017) — a clean confusable pair:**

| | Definition | Example (unexpected illness of the primary wage earner) |
|---|---|---|
| **Family RESOURCES** | **What the family HAS** — money and extended family members | **Financial assistance from relatives; emotional support** |
| **Family COPING STRATEGIES** | **Known PROCESSES AND BEHAVIORS used to manage or adapt** | **Being ABLE TO ASK a relative to loan emergency funds; being ABLE TO TALK with relatives about worries** |

> ⚠️ **"The amount of support available to families in times of crisis from government and nongovernment agencies VARIES in different locales. In addition, the RULES AND CONDITIONS of support often differ and MAY INHIBIT FAMILIES FROM SEEKING SUPPORT, PARTICULARLY IF THE CONDITIONS ARE DEMEANING."**

### Conger's family stress theory — three levels

Families develop **"rules of transformation"** — processes to manage or transform inputs to the system (energy, time) into outputs (cohesion, growth, love). **"When families DO NOT HAVE AN ADEQUATE VARIETY OF RULES to allow them to respond to an event, the event becomes STRESSFUL."**

| Level | Change in |
|---|---|
| **Level I** | **The more SPECIFIC patterns of behavior and transforming processes** — e.g., **change in WHO DOES WHICH household chores** |
| **Level II** | **Processes at a HIGHER LEVEL OF ABSTRACTION** — e.g., **changes in WHAT ARE CONSIDERED family chores** |
| **Level III** | **HIGHLY ABSTRACT processes** — e.g., **FAMILY VALUES** |

**Healthy People 2030 objectives (housing and homes):**
- **TU-18:** Increase the proportion of **smoke-free homes**
- **DH-04:** Increase the proportion of homes that have **an entrance without steps**
- **MICH-19:** Increase the proportion of children and adolescents who **receive care in a medical home**
- **MHMD-R01:** Increase the proportion of **homeless adults with mental health problems who get mental health services**
- **EH-04:** **Reduce blood lead levels in children aged 1–5 years**
- **SDOH-04:** Reduce the proportion of families that **spend more than 30% of income on housing**

## §2 Biological and Age-Related Risk (p. 364–367)

**"Several illnesses are associated with either GENETICS or LIFESTYLE PATTERNS."** Named: **cardiovascular disease** (patterns "often can be traced through several generations"), **hypertension**, **diabetes mellitus**, and **family patterns of obesity**, which increase risks for **heart disease, hypertension, diabetes, some types of cancer, and gallbladder disease.**

**Hypertension management named:** **consuming a low-sodium diet, maintaining a normal weight, exercising regularly at the age-appropriate type and amount, and practicing effective stress management techniques such as meditation.**

> **"It is often DIFFICULT TO SEPARATE BIOLOGICAL RISKS FROM INDIVIDUAL LIFESTYLE FACTORS."**

### Normative vs. non-normative events — the key distinction

| | Definition | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **NORMATIVE (anticipated)** | Expected, stage-related events | **"Families may be able to IDENTIFY NEEDED RESOURCES, MAKE PLANS to cope with the change, LEARN NEW SKILLS, and PREPARE for the event and its consequences."** ⚠️ **"This kind of ANTICIPATORY PREPARATION can INCREASE the family's coping ability and DECREASE stress and negative outcomes."** |
| **NON-NORMATIVE (unexpected)** | Unanticipated events | **"Families have LITTLE OR NO TIME TO PREPARE and the outcome can be INCREASED STRESS, CRISIS, OR EVEN DYSFUNCTION."** |

**Normative events named:** usually related to **the ADDITION OR LOSS of a family member** — the **birth or adoption of a child, death of a grandparent, a child moving out to go to school or take a job, or the marriage of a child.**

**Health responsibilities of a normative event (birth or adoption):** families must learn about **human growth and development, parenting, immunizations, management of childhood illnesses, normal childhood nutrition, and safety issues.**

**Non-normative events can be POSITIVE OR NEGATIVE:**
- **Positive but disruptive:** **a job promotion** (may include more responsibility, stress, or travel affecting all members) or **inheriting a substantial sum of money** (can change family dynamics, "including who decides how the money will be used")
- **Negative:** **a major illness, divorce, a new marriage or partner living arrangement, the death of a child, or a substantial decrease in family income**

### Table 21.1 — Family Life Cycle Stages and Tasks (Wright & Leahey)

| Stage | Tasks |
|---|---|
| **Launching: single young adult leaves home** | **Coming to terms with the family of origin**; development of intimate relationships with peers; **establishment of self: career and finances** |
| **Marriage: joining of families** | **Formation of identity as a couple**; inclusion of spouse in realignment of relationships with extended families; **parenthood: making decisions** |
| **Families with young children** | **Integration of children into family unit**; adjustment of tasks: child rearing, financial, and household; **accommodation of new parenting AND GRANDPARENTING roles** |
| **Families with adolescents** | **Development of increasing autonomy for adolescents**; **midlife reexamination of marital and career issues**; **initial shift toward concern for the OLDER generation** |
| **Families as launching centers** | **Establishment of independent identities for parents and grown children**; **renegotiation of marital relationship**; readjustment of relationships to include in-laws and grandchildren; **dealing with disabilities and death of older generation** |
| **Aging families** | **Maintenance of couple and individual functioning while adapting to the aging process**; **support role of MIDDLE generation**; **support and AUTONOMY of older generation**; **preparation for own death and dealing with the loss of spouse and/or siblings and other peers** |

### The genogram — biological risk assessment tool

**Definition:** **"a DRAWING that shows the family unit of immediate interest and includes SEVERAL GENERATIONS using a series of circles, squares, and connecting lines."**

**Symbol conventions (memorize these):**

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Square** | **Male** |
| **Circle** | **Female** |
| **X through either a square or a circle** | **A DEATH** |
| **Solid HORIZONTAL line** | **Marriage** |
| **Solid VERTICAL line** | **Offspring and children** |
| **BROKEN horizontal line** | **Divorce or separation** |

**Also indicated:** **dates of birth, marriage, death, and other important events**; **major illnesses or conditions listed for each individual family member.**

**Why it works:** **"Patterns can be QUICKLY ASSESSED and provide a guide for the health interviewer about health areas that need further exploration."** The Graham family genogram revealed repetition of **hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, cancer, and hypercholesterolemia.**

> **"Completing a genogram requires INTERVIEWS WITH AS MANY FAMILY MEMBERS AS POSSIBLE. It is important to develop a FAMILY CHRONOLOGY, a TIMELINE of family events over THREE GENERATIONS, to extend the genogram."**

## §3 Environmental Risk (p. 367–368)

### Social risk

**A family's health risk increases if they live in:** **high-crime neighborhoods; communities without adequate recreation or health resources; communities with major noise pollution or chemical pollution; or other high-stress environments.**

> **"One social risk is DISCRIMINATION, whether racial, cultural, or other... The PSYCHOLOGICAL BURDEN RESULTING FROM DISCRIMINATION IS ITSELF A STRESSOR, AND IT ADDS TO THE EFFECTS OF OTHER STRESSORS."**

**The chapter's worked example:** a mother with children aged 6, 8, and 12 whose children **cannot play outside their one-bedroom apartment** because the area **lacks parks or green areas**, the apartment is **on a busy street**, and the area has **two aggressive youth gangs known to bully younger children.**

**Also named:** **families from ethnic populations or families with same-sex parents may experience discrimination in finding housing.** **"Even if they find housing, they may not be welcome and may be harassed, resulting in increased stress."**

### Economic risk

**Definition:** **"determined by the relationship between the FINANCIAL RESOURCES of a family and the DEMANDS on those resources."**

**What adequate financial resources buy:** **adequate housing, clothing, food, education, and health or illness care.**

> ⚠️ **The above-poverty trap:** **"A family may have an income WELL ABOVE THE POVERTY LEVEL, but because of a DEVASTATING ILLNESS of a family member, they may NOT be able to meet current financial demands."**

**The insurance gradient described:**
- **Families at the poverty level:** programs such as **Medicaid** are available
- **Families in upper income brackets:** usually have employer insurance, or can afford to purchase it or pay out of pocket
- ⚠️ **"An INCREASING NUMBER OF MIDDLE-INCOME FAMILIES have major wage earners in jobs that DO NOT HAVE HEALTH BENEFITS. These people often DO NOT HAVE ENOUGH INCOME TO PURCHASE HEALTH CARE BUT EARN TOO MUCH MONEY TO QUALIFY FOR PUBLIC ASSISTANCE PROGRAMS."**

**Named programs families may qualify for:** **Medicaid; Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and Children (WIC); Temporary Assistance to Needy Families (TANF).**

**The WIC finding (Thomas et al., 2014):** **"A family's children who participated in WIC were MORE LINKED TO THE HEALTH CARE SYSTEM than children who were not. Children in WIC were MORE LIKELY TO RECEIVE BOTH PREVENTIVE AND CURATIVE CARE more often than children not participating in WIC."**

**"Nurses play an important role in TEACHING FAMILIES ABOUT THE RESOURCES AVAILABLE to them and GIVING THEM CLEAR DIRECTIONS ABOUT HOW AND WHERE TO APPLY for needed services."**

### The ecomap — environmental risk assessment tool

**Definition:** **"An ecomap represents THE FAMILY'S INTERACTIONS WITH OTHER GROUPS AND ORGANIZATIONS, accomplished by using a series of CIRCLES AND LINES."**

**Conventions (memorize alongside the genogram's):**

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Circle in the MIDDLE of the page** | **The family** |
| **Other circles** | **Other groups and organizations** |
| **Lines between circles** | **The FLOW OF ENERGY** |
| **ARROWHEAD at the end of each line** | **The DIRECTION of the flow of energy (INTO or OUT OF the family)** |
| **DARKNESS of the line** | **The INTENSITY of the energy** |

**Line types shown in Fig. 21.2:** **strong; tenuous; stressful.**

**What an ecomap assesses:** **relationships the family has with others (relatives, neighbors); their connections with other social units (church, school, work, clubs, organizations); and the flow of energy, positive or negative.**

**The Graham family ecomap finding:** **much of the family energy goes INTO work (also a source of stress for the parents).** **Major SOURCES of energy are their immediate and extended families and friends.**

### Assessment cautions for environmental risk

- **Information about the ORIGINS of the family** is useful to understand other social resources and stressors
- **Information about HOW LONG the family has lived in their current location and the IMMIGRATION PATTERNS of the family and their ancestors** helps the nurse understand pressures they may experience
- ⚠️ **On finances:** **"Families often consider financial information PRIVATE, and BOTH the nurse and the family may be UNCOMFORTABLE when discussing finances. The nurse would ONLY NEED TO KNOW THE ACTUAL FAMILY INCOME to help the family determine whether they are ELIGIBLE for programs or benefits. It is helpful to know IF THE FAMILY'S RESOURCES ARE ADEQUATE TO MEET THEIR NEEDS."**
- ⚠️ **"It is important to remember that the family may have a STANDARD OF LIVING DIFFERENT FROM THAT OF THE NURSE, and they may be COMFORTABLE OR AT LEAST ACCEPTING of their standard of living. BE CAREFUL TO AVOID IMPOSING YOUR FINANCIAL VALUES ONTO THE FAMILY."**

## §4 Behavioral (Lifestyle) Risk (p. 369–370)

**"The pattern of personal health habits and behavioral risk defines individual and family lifestyle risk."**

**"In 2016 MORE THAN HALF of all deaths in the United States were attributed to HEART DISEASE OR CANCER, BOTH of which identify DIET as a causative factor."**

**USDHHS/USDA dietary guidelines named:** **eating a variety of foods; maintaining healthy weight; choosing a diet low in fat and cholesterol, including plenty of vegetables, fruits, and grain products; limiting use of sugars, salt, and sodium; and consuming alcohol only in moderation.**

**Benefits of regular physical activity named:** **increased muscle strength, endurance, and flexibility; management of weight; PREVENTION of colon cancer, stroke, and back injury; and PREVENTION AND MANAGEMENT of heart disease, hypertension, diabetes, osteoporosis, and depression.**

**"It is helpful when the COMMUNITY in which they live promotes exercise by having ACCESSIBLE PARKS AND WALKING OR BIKING PATHS."**

### Substance use

**Secondhand smoke has been associated with:** **several types of cancer, heart disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, LOW BIRTH WEIGHT, PREMATURE BIRTHS, and SUDDEN INFANT DEATH SYNDROME.**

> **When caring for a family in which one or more members smokes, "consider not only talking with them about smoking CESSATION, but also PROVIDE EDUCATION ABOUT THE EFFECTS OF SECONDHAND SMOKE."**

**Drug use (including alcohol) is associated with:** **transmission of HIV, fetal alcohol syndrome, liver disease, unwanted pregnancy, delinquency, school failure, violence, and crime.**

**Three family factors that DECREASE the risk for substance use in children (consistently identified in the literature):**
1. **Family closeness**
2. **Families doing activities together**
3. **Behavior modeled in the family**

### Violence in families

- **"The amount of INTRAFAMILIAL violence is thought to be UNDERESTIMATED. It is difficult to collect data and obtain accurate statistics on family violence because THE ISSUE IS SO SENSITIVE for families."**
- **"Evidence supports the INTERGENERATIONAL nature of violence and abuse — that is, ABUSERS WERE OFTEN ABUSED AS CHILDREN."** (This aligns with Ch. 27, p. 491.)
- ⚠️ **"This is NOT a topic that patients and families READILY BRING UP in a visit. OFTEN IT IS WHAT IS NOT SAID AS MUCH AS WHAT IS SAID that will provide a clue to violent behavior in the family. OBSERVE CLOSELY FOR NONVERBAL BEHAVIOR and LISTEN CAREFULLY to what families say when they describe their interactions with one another."**

### Seven critical dimensions for assessing lifestyle

> **"Various tools exist for assessing INDIVIDUALS' lifestyle risks, but FEW ARE AVAILABLE for assessing FAMILY lifestyle patterns."** **"It is important to look at risks of THE FAMILY AS A UNIT."**

1. **Value placed on the behavior**
2. **Knowledge of the behavior and its consequences**
3. **Effect of the behavior on the family**
4. **Effect of the behavior on the individual**
5. **Barriers to performing the behavior**
6. **Benefits of the behavior**

**Also assess:** **the FREQUENCY, INTENSITY, and REGULARITY of specific behaviors**, and **the RESOURCES available to the family for implementing the behaviors.**

**The physical activity worked example — assess:** **the VALUE a family places on physical activity; the HOURS a family spends in exercise; the KINDS of exercise the family does; the RESOURCES available for exercise; and the family's DESCRIPTION AND SELF-REPORT of the activity.**

## §5 Home Visits (p. 370–373) — the blueprint's named topic

### Purpose and rationale

**What home visits give that clinic visits do not:**
- **"A MORE ACCURATE ASSESSMENT of the family structure, the natural or home environment, and BEHAVIOR IN THE HOME ENVIRONMENT"**
- **"Opportunities to identify both BARRIERS AND SUPPORTS for reaching family health promotion goals"**
- **"The nurse can work with the patient DIRECTLY TO MODIFY INTERVENTIONS TO MATCH RESOURCES"**
- **"Visiting the family in the home may also contribute to the family's SENSE OF CONTROL AND ACTIVE PARTICIPATION in meeting its health needs"**

> **"It is important to remember that a home visit is MORE THAN JUST TAKING CARE OF PEOPLE IN A DIFFERENT SETTING. Instead, it is a USEFUL INTERVENTION FORMAT."**

**"Long-term effects of home visits are POSITIVE and can be COST EFFECTIVE for society in contrast to caring for individuals in hospitals or other inpatient sites. As a result, SEVERAL STATES HAVE REINSTITUTED HOME VISITS FOR HIGH-RISK FAMILIES."**

### Advantages and disadvantages

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| **Convenience for patients, especially those with mobility issues or unable/unwilling to travel** | **COST is the major disadvantage** |
| **Patient CONTROL and comfort of the setting** | High cost due to **preparation for the previsit, TRAVEL TIME to and from the home, the amount of time spent with one patient, and POSTVISIT FOLLOW-UP** |
| **The ability to INDIVIDUALIZE services** | |
| **A natural, relaxed environment for the discussion of concerns and needs** | |

> ⚠️ **The cost-effectiveness caveat:** **"When looking at cost versus effectiveness, IT IS NOT ALWAYS THE LEAST COSTLY SERVICE THAT IS THE MOST EFFECTIVE; rather THE SOCIAL VALUE PRODUCED MAY BE MORE COMPELLING... Although MORE COSTLY IN THE SHORT TERM, some services are MORE EFFECTIVE in family health outcomes using home visitation rather than visits to the health department or clinic."**

### Five fundamental skills for effective home visits

**OBSERVING, LISTENING, QUESTIONING, PROBING, and PROMPTING.**

**"BUILDING A TRUSTING RELATIONSHIP with the family patient is THE CORNERSTONE of successful home visits."**

### Table 21.2 — The five phases and their activities

| Phase | Activities |
|---|---|
| **I. INITIATION** | **Clarify the SOURCE of referral for the visit**; **clarify the PURPOSE for the home visit**; **share information on the reason and purpose with the family** |
| **II. PREVISIT** | **Initiate contact with the family**; **establish a SHARED PERCEPTION OF PURPOSE with the family**; **determine the family's WILLINGNESS for a home visit**; **schedule the home visit**; **review the referral and/or family record** |
| **III. IN-HOME** | **Introduce self and professional identity**; **INTERACT SOCIALLY to establish rapport**; **establish the nurse–patient relationship**; **implement the nursing process** |
| **IV. TERMINATION** | **Review the visit with the family**; **plan for future visits** |
| **V. POSTVISIT** | **RECORD the visit**; **plan for the next visit** |

### HOW TO Prepare for the Home Visit (Initiation phase) — the specifics

- **Contact the family BY TELEPHONE before the home visit** to introduce yourself, identify the reason for contact, and schedule the visit. ⚠️ **"A FIRST TELEPHONE CONTACT SHOULD BE A MAXIMUM OF 15 MIN."**
- **Give your name and professional identity** — e.g., "This is Karen Smith. I'm a nurse from the Fayette County Health Department."
- **Inform the family HOW THEY CAME TO THE ATTENTION OF THE NURSE.** ⚠️ **"If a referral was received, it is important and useful to LEARN IF THE FAMILY IS AWARE OF THE REFERRAL."**
- **Give a brief summary of the nurse's knowledge about the family's situation** to allow the family to clarify their needs
- **Schedule a visit AS SOON AS POSSIBLE.** Tell the family the agency hours available, the approximate length, and the purpose. ⚠️ **"Although the length of the visit may vary, APPROXIMATELY 30–60 MIN IS USUAL."**
- **Arrange the visit when AS MANY FAMILY MEMBERS AS POSSIBLE will be available FOR THE ENTIRE VISIT**
- ⚠️ **"It is important to TELL PATIENTS ABOUT ANY FEE for the visit and subsequent visits and POSSIBLE METHODS FOR PAYMENT"**
- **Terminate the call with a REVIEW of the TIME, PLACE, and PURPOSE**, and **a means for the family to CONTACT the nurse**
- **If the family does not have a telephone:** **a note can be dropped off at the family home or sent by mail** informing them of when and why the visit will occur and providing a way to contact the nurse

> **"The nurse must be CLEAR ABOUT THE PURPOSE of the home visit, and this purpose or understanding MUST BE SHARED WITH THE FAMILY."** Otherwise **"the visit may be compromised and come aimlessly or abruptly to a premature halt."**

### When a family refuses a visit

> ⚠️ **"Be aware that the family MAY REFUSE a home visit. DO NOT IMMEDIATELY INTERPRET THIS AS A PERSONAL REJECTION."**

- **Families may have a variety of reasons** when deciding when and which outsiders are allowed entry
- **The nurse needs to EXPLORE THE REASONS for the refusal** — there may be **a misunderstanding about the reason for a visit** or **a lack of information about services, including payment**
- **The contact may be terminated as requested if (1) the situation has been resolved or services obtained elsewhere, AND (2) the family understands that services are available and how to contact the agency**
- ⚠️ **"However, the nurse should LEAVE OPEN THE POSSIBILITY OF FUTURE CONTACT."**
- ⚠️ **"In some instances, the nurse will be MANDATED TO PERSIST in requesting a home visit BECAUSE OF LEGAL OBLIGATIONS, such as follow-up of certain COMMUNICABLE AND INFECTIOUS DISEASES."**

**What families may fear:** **"that the nurse is CHECKING UP ON THEM, that the nurse views them as being INADEQUATE OR DYSFUNCTIONAL, or that the nurse is IMPINGING ON THEIR PRIVACY."** Also, health department services **"have been identified by the public as being 'PUBLIC SERVICES' for NEEDY families or those with INADEQUATE FUNDS to pay for care."**

### Voluntary vs. required visits — expect an item

| | Voluntary (requested by the patient) | Required (often legally mandated) |
|---|---|---|
| **Entry** | **EASIER entry for the nurse** | **MORE DIFFICULT** entry |
| **Control of interaction** | **PATIENT-controlled** | **NURSE-controlled** |
| **Tone** | **MORE INFORMAL** | **MORE FORMAL, INVESTIGATORY**, with **DISTORTED nurse–patient communication** |
| **Future visits** | **MUTUAL discussion of frequency** | **May NOT be any mutual discussion of frequency** |
| **Example** | **A new mother requests the nurse come and assist with effective breastfeeding** | **A family member diagnosed with TUBERCULOSIS and the nurse needs to verify the patient is taking medication regularly**; or **the family using quarantine measures for COVID-19** |

### Personal safety

> **"Personal safety is an issue that may arise EITHER IN APPROACHING the family home OR ONCE THE FAMILY HAS OPENED THE DOOR to the nurse. Nurses need to EXAMINE PERSONAL FEARS AND OBJECTIVE THREATS to determine if safety is INDEED an issue."**

**Precautions in known high-risk situations:**
- **Agencies may provide ESCORTS for nurses or have them VISIT IN PAIRS**
- **Readily identifiable UNIFORMS may be required**
- **A SIGN-OUT PROCESS indicating the timing and location of home visits may be used routinely**

> ⚠️ **"Home visits are GENERALLY SAFE; however, as with all worksites, the POSSIBILITY OF VIOLENCE EXISTS. Therefore, the nurse needs to use CAUTION and exercise GOOD JUDGMENT. IF A REASONABLE QUESTION EXISTS ABOUT THE SAFETY OF MAKING A VISIT, THE NURSE SHOULD NOT MAKE THE VISIT."**

### Scheduling challenges

**"The number of working single-parent or dual-income, two-parent families is increasing, which means families have more demands on their time."** **The IDEAL is to visit when the ENTIRE family is present**, but this often conflicts with agency hours and school or work schedules. **"It may be possible to schedule a visit at the BEGINNING OR END OF A DAY"**; some agencies are **reconsidering traditional hours and Monday-through-Friday visits.**

**Culture:** **"Practitioners need to take the responsibility to LEARN ABOUT THEIR PATIENT'S CULTURE AS THEY PREPARE for visits"** — addressed **during the PREVISIT phase.**

### In-home phase sequence

1. **Provide personal and professional identification and tell the patient WHERE THE AGENCY IS LOCATED**
2. ⚠️ **"Next, a BRIEF SOCIAL PERIOD allows the PATIENT TO ASSESS THE NURSE and establish rapport"**
3. **The nurse describes his or her ROLE, RESPONSIBILITIES, AND LIMITATIONS**
4. **Determine the PATIENT'S EXPECTATIONS**
5. **The major portion:** **establishing the relationship and implementing the nursing process.** **Assessment, intervention, and evaluation are ONGOING**

**What the in-home phase lets the nurse assess:** **the family's HOME, LAWN, NEIGHBORHOOD, and COMMUNITY RESOURCES, as well as FAMILY INTERACTIONS.**

**Managing interruptions:** **"Telephones ring, pets join in the visit, people come and go, and televisions are left on. THE NURSE CAN ASK THAT FOR A LIMITED TIME, TELEVISIONS BE TURNED OFF or that other disruptive activities be limited. FAMILIES MAY BE SO USED TO THE BACKGROUND NOISES AND ROUTINE ACTIVITIES THAT THEY DO NOT RECOGNIZE THEM AS BEING POTENTIALLY DISRUPTIVE."**

**Being realistic:** **"In some situations, ONE VISIT MAY BE ALL THAT IS POSSIBLE OR APPROPRIATE."** If further services are indicated and the nurse's agency is not appropriate, **the nurse can help the family identify other services and help initiate referrals.**

### Box 21.2 — Reasons for the Home Visit (17 Minnesota interventions)

**Advocacy; case management; coalition building; collaboration; community organizing; consultation; counseling; delegated medical treatment and observations; disease and other health investigation; health teaching; outreach; policy development and enforcement; case finding; referral and follow-up; screening; social marketing; surveillance.**

**The Intervention Wheel (Keller et al.) is recommended to guide nursing practice during home visits.**

### Termination phase

- **Review with the family WHAT HAS OCCURRED AND WHAT HAS BEEN ACCOMPLISHED** — this is **the major focus** and **"provides a basis for planning further home visits"**
- ⚠️ **"IDEALLY, TERMINATION OF THE VISIT AND, ULTIMATELY, TERMINATION OF SERVICE BEGIN AT THE FIRST CONTACT with the establishment of a goal or purpose."**
- **Planning for future visits is part of setting goals and planning service**

### Postvisit phase — documentation

> **"Even though the nurse has concluded the home visit and left the patient's home, RESPONSIBILITY FOR THE VISIT IS NOT COMPLETE UNTIL THE INTERACTION HAS BEEN RECORDED."**

**The records problem:**
- **Agencies MAY organize records by families** — a "family" folder with all members included, **useful for family history and background**
- ⚠️ **"However, this OFTEN DOES NOT OCCUR... MORE OFTEN, EACH FAMILY MEMBER HAS A SEPARATE RECORD, and other family members' records are CROSS-REFERENCED. This is because THE FOCUS OFTEN SHIFTS FROM THE FAMILY TO THE INDIVIDUAL. Consequently, nursing diagnoses, goals, and interventions are directed toward INDIVIDUAL family members RATHER THAN THE FAMILY UNIT. THIS APPROACH HAS ITS SHORTCOMINGS."**

**All record systems should have:** **a DATABASE; a NURSING DIAGNOSIS and PROBLEM LIST; a PLAN including specific goals, actual actions, and interventions; and an EVALUATION.** **"These are the basic elements needed for LEGAL AND CLINICAL purposes."**

**Formats named:** **narratives; flow sheets; a problem-oriented medical record (POMR); a subjective, objective, assessment plan (SOAP); or a combination.** ⚠️ **"It is important that recording be CURRENT, DATED, AND SIGNED."**

**Individual-focused vs. family-focused nursing diagnosis — the chapter's own contrast:**

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **INDIVIDUAL-focused** | **"Ineffective mothering skill related to lack of knowledge of normal growth and development"** |
| **FAMILY-focused** | **"Inability of a family to accomplish the stage-appropriate task of providing a safe environment for a preschooler related to lack of knowledge and resources"** |

> **"At times, it may be necessary to present information for a specific family member. However, THE EMPHASIS SHOULD BE ON THE INDIVIDUAL AS A MEMBER OF, AND WITHIN THE STRUCTURE OF, THE FAMILY."**

## §6 Contracting with Families (p. 373–375)

**Definition:** **"making an agreement between two or more parties"** that **"involves a SHIFT IN RESPONSIBILITY AND CONTROL TOWARD A SHARED EFFORT by the patient and professional as opposed to an effort by the professional alone."**

> **"THE PREMISE OF CONTRACTING IS FAMILY CONTROL. It is assumed that WHEN THE FAMILY HAS LEGITIMATE CONTROL, ITS ABILITY TO MAKE HEALTHFUL CHOICES IS INCREASED."**

⚠️ **Cultural caution:** **"This approach... MAY NOT BE CONSISTENT WITH OTHER CULTURES that look to health care providers for MORE DIRECT GUIDANCE; therefore, it is important to DETERMINE THE FAMILY'S VALUE SYSTEM BEFORE ASSUMING THAT CONTRACTING WILL WORK."**

**The nursing contract:** **"a working agreement that is CONTINUOUSLY RENEGOTIABLE and MAY OR MAY NOT BE WRITTEN."**

### Contingency vs. noncontingency — a clean pair

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| **CONTINGENCY contract** | **States a SPECIFIC REWARD for the patient AFTER COMPLETION of the patient's portion of the contract** |
| **NONCONTINGENCY contract** | **Does NOT specify rewards.** **"The IMPLIED rewards are THE POSITIVE CONSEQUENCES OF REACHING THE GOALS specified in the contract"** |

**Who must be involved:** **"For family health risk reduction, it is ESSENTIAL that the contract be made with ALL RESPONSIBLE AND APPROPRIATE MEMBERS of the family. INVOLVING ONLY ONE INDIVIDUAL IS NOT SUFFICIENT if the goal is family health risk reduction, which requires a TOTAL FAMILY SYSTEM EFFORT AND CHANGE."**

⚠️ **The workaround:** **"If meeting with the entire family is NOT possible, EACH FAMILY MEMBER CAN REVIEW A CONTRACT, GIVE INPUT, AND SIGN IT. This allows active participation by all family members without the necessity of finding a time when everyone involved can be present."**

### Table 21.3 — Phases and Activities in Contracting

| Phase | Activities |
|---|---|
| **I. BEGINNING** | **MUTUAL data collection and exploration of needs and problems**; **MUTUAL establishment of goals**; **MUTUAL development of a plan** |
| **II. WORKING** | **MUTUAL division of responsibilities**; **MUTUAL setting of time limits**; **MUTUAL implementation of a plan**; **MUTUAL evaluation and renegotiation** |
| **III. TERMINATION** | **MUTUAL termination of a contract** |

> **Note that EVERY activity begins with "mutual." That is the point of the table.**

**Process details:**
- **First, collect and analyze the data** — involving **both** the family and the nurse. **"An important aspect of this step is OBTAINING THE FAMILY'S VIEW of the situation and its needs and problems."** The nurse **presents observations, validates them with the family, and then obtains the family's view**
- ⚠️ **"It is important that goals be MUTUALLY SET AND REALISTIC. At times, NURSES AND PATIENTS WHO ARE NEW TO CONTRACTING MAY SET OVERLY AMBITIOUS GOALS."**
- **"The nurse should recognize that there MAY BE DISCREPANCIES BETWEEN PROFESSIONAL PRIORITIES AND THOSE OF THE PATIENT and decide if negotiating is required."**
- ⚠️ **"THE GOALS OF CONTRACTING ARE NOT STATIC because the process INCLUDES RENEGOTIATING when appropriate."**
- **Setting time limits involves deciding on a DEADLINE for accomplishing (or evaluating progress toward) a goal AND THE FREQUENCY OF CONTACTS**
- **The contract can be MODIFIED, RENEGOTIATED, OR TERMINATED on the basis of the evaluation**

### Advantages and disadvantages

**Disadvantages:** **"Contracting TAKES TIME AND EFFORT and may require the family and nurse to REORIENT THEIR ROLES."** **"INCREASED CONTROL on the part of the family ALSO MEANS INCREASED RESPONSIBILITY."** ⚠️ **"SOME NURSES MAY HAVE DIFFICULTY RELINQUISHING THE ROLE OF THE CONTROLLING EXPERT PROFESSIONAL."**

**Four patient groups who may not choose to contract:**
1. **Persons with MINIMAL COGNITIVE SKILLS**
2. **Those who are involved in an EMERGENCY SITUATION**
3. **Persons who are UNWILLING to be more active in their own care**
4. **Those who DO NOT SEE CONTROL OR AUTHORITY FOR HEALTH CONCERNS AS BEING WITHIN THEIR DOMAIN**

**"Some of these patients MAY LEARN to contract; OTHERS NEVER WILL BE ABLE TO DO SO."**

**The critique of the nursing process that contracting addresses:** **"The nursing process does NOT necessarily provide an ACTIVE ROLE for the family as a patient; the assumption that a need exists is based on PROFESSIONAL JUDGMENT ONLY, and it is also assumed that changes can and should be made within the family unit."**

## §7 Empowering Families (p. 375–376)

> **"Approaches for helping individuals and families assume an active role in their health care should focus on EMPOWERMENT RATHER THAN ENABLING OR HELP-GIVING."**

**Why help-giving can backfire:**
- **"HELP-GIVING INTERVENTIONS DO NOT ALWAYS HAVE POSITIVE OUTCOMES for patients."**
- ⚠️ **"IF FAMILIES DO NOT PERCEIVE A SITUATION AS A PROBLEM OR NEED, OFFERS OF HELP MAY CAUSE RESENTMENT."**
- **"Help-giving also may have NEGATIVE CONSEQUENCES IF THERE IS NOT A MATCH BETWEEN WHAT IS EXPECTED AND WHAT IS OFFERED."**
- ⚠️ **"A nurse's FAILURE TO RECOGNIZE A FAMILY'S COMPETENCIES and to define an active role for them CAN LEAD TO THE FAMILY'S DEPENDENCY AND LACK OF GROWTH. This can be frustrating for BOTH the nurse AND the family."**

### Three characteristics of the empowered family seeking help

1. **Access and control over needed resources**
2. **Decision-making and problem-solving abilities**
3. **The ability to communicate and to obtain needed resources**

**The third characteristic in detail:** **"families may need to learn HOW TO IDENTIFY SOURCES OF HELP, HOW TO CONTACT AGENCIES, HOW TO ASK CRITICAL QUESTIONS, and HOW TO NEGOTIATE WITH AGENCIES to meet family needs."**

### Four outcomes of empowerment

1. **Positive self-esteem**
2. **The ability to set and reach goals**
3. **A sense of control over life and change processes**
4. **A sense of hope for the future**

### The empowerment stance

> **"Empowerment requires a viewpoint that OFTEN CONFLICTS WITH THE VIEWS OF MANY HELPING PROFESSIONS, INCLUDING NURSING. Empowerment's underlying assumption is one of a PARTNERSHIP between the professional and the patient AS OPPOSED TO ONE IN WHICH THE PROFESSIONAL IS DOMINANT. Families are assumed to be EITHER COMPETENT OR CAPABLE OF BECOMING COMPETENT. This implies that THE PROFESSIONAL IS NOT AN UNCHALLENGED AUTHORITY WHO IS IN CONTROL."**

**What an empowering nursing intervention does:**
- **Is directed toward BUILDING NURSE–FAMILY PARTNERSHIPS**
- **Emphasizes HEALTH RISK REDUCTION AND HEALTH PROMOTION**
- ⚠️ **"The nurse's approach to the family should be POSITIVE and FOCUSED ON COMPETENCIES RATHER THAN ON PROBLEMS OR DEFICITS"**
- **Interventions "need to be CONSISTENT WITH FAMILY CULTURAL NORMS and the family's PERCEPTION OF THE PROBLEM"**
- ⚠️ **"RATHER THAN MAKING DECISIONS FOR THE FAMILY, the nurse SUPPORTS the family in their decision making and BOLSTERS THEIR SELF-ESTEEM by RECOGNIZING AND USING FAMILY STRENGTHS AND SUPPORT NETWORKS"**
- **Interventions that promote desired family behaviors "INCREASE FAMILY COMPETENCY, DECREASE THE NEED FOR OUTSIDE HELP, and result in FAMILIES SEEING THEMSELVES AS BEING ACTIVELY RESPONSIBLE for bringing about desired changes"**

> **"The GOAL of an empowering approach is to CREATE A PARTNERSHIP between the nurse and the family characterized by COOPERATION AND SHARED RESPONSIBILITY."**

### Levels of Prevention box — Strategies for Prevention Related to Families

| Level | Intervention as printed |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | **Complete a family GENOGRAM and assess health risks with the family to CONTRACT for family health activities to PREVENT diseases from developing.** |
| **Secondary** | **Use a BEHAVIORAL HEALTH RISK SURVEY to IDENTIFY the factors leading to health problems, such as obesity in the family.** |
| **Tertiary** | **Develop a CONTRACT with the family to CHANGE NUTRITIONAL PATTERNS to REDUCE FURTHER COMPLICATIONS from the specified health problem.** |

## §8 Vulnerable Family Populations, Resources, and Policy (p. 375–378)

### LGBTQIA+ families

**The acronym as given:** **lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer/questioning, intersexual, ally/asexual, and a plus sign to designate those who are not included.** **"It is EVER EVOLVING."**

**Legal landmarks named:** **same-sex marriage established in a civil rights case in 2015 by the US Supreme Court**; adoptions by same-sex couples; opening of the military; **a plethora of antidiscrimination laws.** ⚠️ **"However, antidiscrimination laws OFTEN ARE NOT CLEARLY WRITTEN OR ADHERED TO,"** as in the 2018 Supreme Court decision ruling in favor of a baker who refused to bake a wedding cake for a gay couple.

**The Obama directive (April 15, 2010):** instructed **hospitals that accept Medicaid and Medicare to allow adult patients the right to DESIGNATE SPECIFIC INDIVIDUALS who can VISIT them in the hospital or MAKE MEDICAL DECISIONS on their behalf.**

**Nursing obligations named:**
- **"Nurses have an ETHICAL OBLIGATION to provide culturally competent care to LGBTQIA+ families"**
- ⚠️ **"To fulfill this obligation, nurses should FIRST SEEK TO PROVIDE A SAFE ENVIRONMENT FOR PATIENTS TO DISCUSS THEIR SEXUAL ORIENTATION"**
- **"Although some nurses may feel a degree of discomfort... it is important to DEVELOP LEARNING STRATEGIES TO HELP OVERCOME THIS BARRIER"**
- **"JUST AS THERE IS GREAT VARIATION AMONG HETEROSEXUAL FAMILIES, ALL LGBTQIA+ FAMILIES ARE NOT THE SAME"**

**Health risk:** **"Sexual and gender minorities face a HIGHER RISK for DEPRESSION, ANXIETY, SUBSTANCE ABUSE, THOUGHTS OF SUICIDE, AND SUICIDE."**

**The "traditional" family with a nonheterosexual member:** **"After a family member declares his or her sexual preferences, families MAY NEED INITIAL SUPPORT TO PROCESS THE INFORMATION."** Named referral resource: **Parents and Friends of Lesbians and Gays (PFLAG), www.pflag.org.**

### Teenage parent families

**Challenges named:** **affordable and accessible health care; the recruitment and development of mentors to help teen parents acquire health care.**

**"Teenage parents may feel HUMILIATION AND UNCERTAINTY and have LITTLE CONFIDENCE IN HANDLING ADULT RESPONSIBILITIES OTHER THAN ACTUAL PARENTING."**

**What is needed:** **teenage-instructional literature on family health policy information and health care service accessibility WRITTEN FROM THE PERSPECTIVE OF TEENAGERS.**
- **Single teenage parents need to understand WELFARE and HOW TO APPLY for it, as well as HOW TO FIND SUPPORT COMMUNITIES**
- **Teenage parents who decide NOT to be involved in a child's life need to understand CHILD SUPPORT, ADOPTION, and LEGAL VISITATION AND INVOLVEMENT ISSUES**
- **"EDUCATION, VOCATIONAL OPPORTUNITY, AND SOCIAL ACCEPTANCE ARE 'LUXURIES' OFTEN MISSED BY ADOLESCENT PARENTS"**

> ⚠️ **The cycle:** **"Children of teenage parents often make the same mistakes as their parents because of factors of POOR LIVING CONDITIONS, LOW SOCIOECONOMIC STATUS, AND A DIFFICULT CHILDHOOD. ADEQUATE FAMILY HEALTH CARE CAN HELP BREAK THE CYCLE of teen parents."** The chapter argues that **"it is also time now for POSTPREGNANCY PROGRAMS"** in addition to sex education and prevention.

### Community resources

**Government resources named:** **Medicare, Medicaid, TANF, WIC, Supplementary Security Income, food stamps, and State Children's Health Insurance Program (SCHIP).** These **"primarily provide support for BASIC NEEDS (illness or health care, nutrition, funds for housing, clothing) and funds are based on MEETING ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA."**

**Voluntary (nongovernment) organizations named:** **American Cancer Society, American Heart Association, American Lung Association, Muscular Dystrophy Association.** These provide **primary prevention and health promotion services, screening programs, and assistance after diagnosis.**

**Other voluntary direct services named:** **shelters for homeless or battered individuals, substance abuse counseling and treatment, Meals on Wheels, transportation, clothing, food, furniture.** Local social service agencies (e.g., Catholic Social Services) provide **counseling.**

**Where to find resources:** **the telephone book and the Internet**; **community service organizations such as the local chamber of commerce and health department** publish community resource listings; **brochures are often available in clinics and providers' offices.**

**What nurses do as patient advocates:** **"sharing information with families, REHEARSING WITH FAMILIES WHAT QUESTIONS TO ASK, PREPARING REQUIRED MATERIALS, MAKING THE INITIAL CONTACT, and ARRANGING TRANSPORTATION. The appropriateness and effectiveness of resources should be EVALUATED WITH FAMILIES AFTERWARD."**

> ⚠️ **"It is important to remember that NAVIGATING THE MAZE OF RESOURCES IS OFTEN DIFFICULT FOR THE NURSE. If a family is in crisis or does not have a phone or a home base from which to call or receive return calls, this process is EVEN MORE DIFFICULT AND THEIR SENSE OF HELPLESSNESS MAY BE INCREASED."**

### Telehomecare

**Also called telehealth or telemedicine. "The goal is for patients to COMMUNICATE WITH AND TRANSFER INFORMATION TO PROVIDERS FROM THEIR HOME."**

- **"Telehomecare monitoring requires LESS TIME PER PATIENT INTERACTION, so it allows nurses to feasibly care for MORE PATIENTS PER DAY."**
- **"Patients (including elderly individuals), as well as their caregivers, report FEW TECHNOLOGICAL PROBLEMS."**
- **Particularly useful when ONGOING AND FREQUENT MONITORING is necessary**, and in areas where **the distance between the patient and the source of health care is large or would take a long time because of traffic**, or **when the patient does not have transportation**
- ⚠️ **The limitation (a likely correct answer):** **"It should be recognized that it is NOT A SUBSTITUTE FOR THE IN-HOME TRUST AND RELATIONSHIP BUILDING AND ASSESSMENT OF BOTH FAMILY AND COMMUNITY RESOURCES THAT CAN BE ACCOMPLISHED ONLY BY AN ATTENTIVE AND ENGAGED NURSE SPENDING TIME WITH THE FAMILY IN THEIR HOME ENVIRONMENT."**

### The Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA)

> ⚠️ **FMLA is listed in the blueprint under CHAPTER 22, but the FMLA content is in CHAPTER 21, p. 377.** Drill it from here.

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Signed** | **February 5, 1993, by President Clinton** (PL 103-3) |
| **Duration** | **Up to 12 WEEKS of leave EACH YEAR** |
| **Paid?** | ⚠️ **UNPAID leave of absence** |
| **Qualifying reasons** | **Their OWN serious illness; the illness of their CHILD, PARENT, or SPOUSE; and the BIRTH OR ADOPTION of a child** |
| **Benefits while on leave** | ⚠️ **"Employees STILL RECEIVE THEIR MEDICAL BENEFITS"** |

## Source conflicts and traps

- ⚠️ **FMLA is in the blueprint's Ch. 22 row but lives in Ch. 21 (p. 377).** The Ch. 22 row lists "FMLA" as a topic, and Chapter 22 does not cover it. All FMLA content is in this chapter. **Drill FMLA from this bank, not the Ch. 22 bank.** The four most invertible FMLA facts: **12 weeks, UNPAID, benefits continue, same or similar position guaranteed.**

- ⚠️ **"Common health risks across the lifespan" is the blueprint's Ch. 21 topic but is really Ch. 22's subject.** Chapter 21 gives the **family life cycle stages table (Table 21.1)** and the **three risk categories**, but the actual lifespan-specific health problems and leading causes of death are Chapter 22. Cross-reference.

- ⚠️ **Genogram vs. ecomap.** **GENOGRAM = the family itself, across generations, biological/health patterns** (squares, circles, marriage lines, X for death). **ECOMAP = the family's relationships with OUTSIDE groups and organizations, and the flow of energy** (circles, lines with arrowheads, darkness for intensity). Both use circles, which is exactly why they get swapped in items. **The genogram looks INWARD across time; the ecomap looks OUTWARD across the community.**

- ⚠️ **Normative events are protective because they allow ANTICIPATORY PREPARATION.** The trap is treating any major life change as automatically harmful. The chapter's point is that **anticipation increases coping and decreases stress**, so a normative event like a planned birth is far less risky than an unanticipated one — and **non-normative events can be POSITIVE (a promotion, an inheritance) and still disruptive.**

- ⚠️ **Voluntarily assumed risks are tolerated BETTER; scientifically uncertain risks are feared MORE; natural risks are feared LESS.** All three of Murdaugh's principles run against intuition and are stated as bullet points, which is the classic setup for a SATA item.

- ⚠️ **Contracting is not universally appropriate.** Four named groups may decline: **minimal cognitive skills, emergency situations, unwilling to be active, and those who do not see health authority as their domain.** Also **cultural fit must be assessed first** — some cultures expect direct provider guidance. An item assuming contracting is always the best approach is wrong.

- ⚠️ **Refusal of a home visit is not personal rejection**, and the nurse should **explore reasons and leave the door open** — except where **legally mandated follow-up (communicable disease) requires persistence.** Both halves are testable.

- ⚠️ **If a reasonable question exists about safety, do NOT make the visit.** Stated flatly. An option where the nurse proceeds anyway with precautions is wrong when the stem describes a genuine safety concern.

- ⚠️ **The brief social period at the start of the in-home phase is for the PATIENT to assess the NURSE**, not the reverse. Stated explicitly and easy to invert.

- ⚠️ **Empowerment vs. help-giving.** Offering help to a family that does not perceive a problem **may cause resentment**; failing to recognize competencies **creates dependency.** The correct nursing stance is **focused on competencies rather than problems or deficits**, supporting decision-making rather than making decisions.

- **Telehomecare is not a substitute for the home visit.** Stated as an explicit limitation immediately after the advantages, which is a strong item signal.

- **Contingency contracts specify a reward; noncontingency ones do not.** Two words apart, opposite meanings.

- **The first phone contact is a maximum of 15 minutes; the visit itself is usually 30–60 minutes.** Two numbers in the same box, easy to swap.

- **OCR bleed:** **Fig. 21.1 (the Graham family genogram) and Fig. 21.2 (the ecomap) are images and produce ~2,000 characters of unrecoverable garbage** in the raw pull. Table 21.1's two columns interleave with body text on p. 364–365, so the family life cycle stages appear scrambled; the table above was reconstructed by matching stage names to task lists. The "Vulnerable Populations at Risk" section on p. 375–376 contains a **duplicated paragraph** (the LGBTQIA+ mental health paragraph appears twice in the source scan).
