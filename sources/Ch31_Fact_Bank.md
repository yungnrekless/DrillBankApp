## §1 What school nursing is (p. 553)

- **Definition (ANA & NASN, 2017):** a specialty practice focused on providing health care and illness prevention to school-age children **with the goal of facilitating their participation in educational opportunities.**
- Services are provided to **children, staff, and teachers** within the school setting.
- School nursing is **population focused**: the population is all children, families, staff, and teachers in the school.

---

## §2 Federal legislation (p. 554) — the highest-yield cluster

Know the sequence and what each law did:

| Law | Year | What it did |
|---|---|---|
| **Education for All Handicapped Children Act** | 1975 | Established the right of children with disabilities to public education |
| **Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act** | — | Prohibits disability discrimination; requires accommodations |
| **ADA** | 1992 | Broadened disability protections |
| **IDEA** (PL 105-17) | 1997 | Required schools to allow more children to attend and to accommodate special needs via **IEPs and IHPs** |
| **No Child Left Behind Act** (PL 107-110) | 2001 | Requires a **healthy environment** in schools |
| **IDEIA** (renamed IDEA) | 2004 | Extended coverage until the child turns **22** |
| **Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA)** (PL 114-95) | 2015 | Revised and replaced No Child Left Behind |

- **The effect on school nursing:** IDEA/ADA meant more children with **HIV/AIDS, chronic illness, or mental health problems** are in classrooms and need more attention from the school nurse.
- **IEP vs. IHP (the distinction to memorize):**
  - **IEP = Individualized Education Plan** → the child's **educational** accommodations.
  - **IHP = Individualized Health Plan** → the child's **health care** needs met in balance with school.
- **FERPA** protects the privacy of student **education records**; **HIPAA** protects health information. In schools, student health records fall under FERPA's protection.

## §3 School nurse roles (p. 556–557)

- **Direct caregiver:** hands-on nursing care to students and staff.
- **Case manager:** coordinates health care for children with complex needs; attends parent-teacher meetings to know families.
- **Consultant:** the person best able to provide health information to administrators, teachers, and parent-teacher groups.
- **Researcher:** contributes to the growing evidence base on school nursing practice and impact.
- **Community outreach:** links the school to community resources.
- **Health educator and counselor:** teaches health content and addresses student concerns.
- **Population-level role:** the school nurse is responsible for evidence-based nursing care for the whole population of children, families, staff, and teachers.

---

## §4 Levels of prevention in schools (p. 559) — secondary is the biggest

- **Primary:** educating children and adolescents about **substance abuse, injury prevention, and disease prevention**, and providing **vaccinations.** All states have laws requiring immunizations before enrollment; this protects the whole community and lowers costs (herd-immunity logic).
- **Secondary:** early intervention for children who need health care. **This is the largest responsibility for the school nurse.** It includes caring for ill or injured students and employees, **screening and assessing** children, and **referral** to appropriate health agencies.
- **Tertiary:** nursing care (using the nursing process) for children with existing conditions to limit disability and support function; also addresses bullying and mental health sequelae.

**Individualized emergency plan** (for a student with a health condition) should include the student's medical history, list of medications, and emergency response steps; school personnel involved should have current **CPR and AED** certification.

---

## §5 Confidentiality and frameworks (p. 570)

- **Confidentiality:** the school nurse **must maintain confidentiality.** Protected information **cannot be released to anyone, including teachers or other health personnel**, without authorization. This is the FERPA principle in practice.
- **Whole School, Whole Community, Whole Child (WSCC) model:** the CDC framework school health programs are encouraged to follow.

## Source conflicts and traps

- **IEP vs. IHP is the classic school-nursing trap.** IEP = education plan; IHP = health plan. Swapping the two, or attributing health-care coordination to the IEP, is the distractor.
- **Secondary prevention is the school nurse's largest responsibility.** A stem asking which level occupies most of the school nurse's work is testing this. Primary is education/vaccination; secondary is screening, early care, and referral; tertiary is managing existing conditions.
- **FERPA governs student education records** (which include health records in schools); HIPAA governs health information generally. A question about who can see a student's health record turns on FERPA and the confidentiality rule that information cannot be shared even with teachers without authorization.
- **IDEIA extended coverage to age 22**, not 18 or 21. A different age is the distractor.
- **All states require immunizations before school enrollment.** An option saying immunization is optional or varies such that some states require none contradicts the chapter.
- **School nursing is population focused**, serving staff and teachers as well as students, not only individual children. An option limiting the role to direct student care misses the population-level framing.
- **Vaccination is primary prevention; screening is secondary prevention.** Mislabeling a required school screening as primary prevention is a trap (mirrors the Ch. 10/12 screening-is-secondary rule).
