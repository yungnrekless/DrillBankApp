## §1 Windshield survey (named blueprint term)

- A **method of simple observation** that gives a **quick overview** of a community.
- Described as the **"community equivalent of a head-to-toe assessment."**
- **Best conducted with more than one person** — one drives/watches the road, another observes and takes notes; a shared experience adds insight.
- May require a **second tour** to fill in blanks.
- Captures community characteristics (housing, businesses, open space, transportation, gathering places, signs of decay or vitality) that provide **context for health status**, strengths, risks, and vulnerabilities.
- It is a **primary data** method (the nurse collects it firsthand).

> **Test cue:** if the stem describes a nurse **driving or walking through a neighborhood to get a general first impression** of housing, businesses, and conditions, that is a **windshield survey.** It is observational and preliminary — not a substitute for the fuller assessment if interventions are planned.

## §2 Key informant (named blueprint term)

- Key informants provide a critical viewpoint on **local issues, strengths, and concerns.**
- **They are not always people with a formal title or position** — they often hold an **informal role** in the community.
- Common examples given: **county health department nurses and church leaders.**
- They **know many community members** and can identify **other** key informants (snowball referral).
- Key informant interviews are a **primary data** method.

> **Test cue:** a "key informant" is chosen for their **knowledge of and connection to the community**, not their official rank. The classic distractor is to assume it must be an elected official or agency head — it doesn't have to be.

## §3 The confusable pair: primary vs. secondary data

| | **Primary data** | **Secondary data** |
|---|---|---|
| Who collects | The **nurse / assessment team**, firsthand | **Someone else** (an agency), already existing |
| Examples | Windshield survey, key informant interviews, focus groups, participant observation, photovoice, town hall meetings, surveys | Census, vital statistics, morbidity/mortality reports, County Health Rankings, BRFSS, CDC WONDER |
| Nature | Direct interaction with the community | Numeric reports from recognized sources |

> A **comprehensive community assessment uses both.** The trap: windshield survey and key informant interviews are **primary** (collected firsthand), whereas census and vital statistics are **secondary.**

---

## §4 The framework these terms sit in

- Community assessment uses the **nursing process** (assessment → diagnosis → planning → implementation → evaluation) applied to the **community as client.**
- **Community as client** vs. **community as partner:** "client" frames the community as the recipient of care; "partner" emphasizes the community's active collaboration in the process.
- **Community diagnosis** is written using **NANDA** or the **Omaha system.**
- Assessment should weigh **both strengths and problems** — the PHN is told to place **community strengths high** on the list, not just deficits.
- Useful secondary sources named: **County Health Rankings** (updated annually, county-level, comparable across states), **BRFSS**, **CDC WONDER**, **US Census**, **Healthy People 2030.**
