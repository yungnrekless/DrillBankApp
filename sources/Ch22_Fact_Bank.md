## §1 Status of Children (p. 381)

**2018 demographics:** **73.4 million children** in the US ages 1 to 17. Of these:
- **65% lived in a home with two married parents**
- **17% lived in poverty**
- **17% experienced food insecurity**

**2016:** of US children under 18, **41% were considered LOW INCOME and 19% were POOR.**

> **"Being a child in a low-income or poor family DOES NOT HAPPEN BY CHANCE"** (National Center for Children in Poverty). **Factors associated with children's economic insecurity: EDUCATION OF PARENTS, EMPLOYMENT, and RACE/ETHNICITY.**

- **Hispanics comprise the largest share** of low-income and poor children under 18
- **Black, American Indian, and children of immigrants are more likely to be in low-income families**
- ⚠️ **"Interestingly, in 2016, the LARGEST PERCENTAGE of low-income children lived in the SOUTH"**
- **Children of immigrants face more health barriers than native-born children**, including **lack of health insurance, poverty, language barriers, and substandard housing**

**Federal poverty level (2020):** **$26,200 for a family of four; $44,120 for a family of eight.** **Higher for residents of HAWAII** due to higher cost of living.

**Publicly funded child coverage:** **Medicaid and SCHIP** (State Children's Health Insurance Plan) are **federal AND state plans.**

> **Why access matters at these ages:** **"Because PHYSICAL, COGNITIVE, AND EMOTIONAL CHANGES OCCUR MORE RAPIDLY DURING CHILDHOOD AND ADOLESCENCE THAN AT ANY OTHER TIME IN THE LIFE SPAN, access to regular health visits at key ages is important in monitoring these changes."**

**Nursing assessments include:** **evaluation of growth, development, health status, QUALITY OF THE PARENT-CHILD RELATIONSHIP, and FAMILY SUPPORT SYSTEMS.**

## §2 Childhood Obesity (p. 381–383)

### Table 22.1 — BMI classification in children aged 2 and above

| Plotted percentile for age and gender | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **< 5th percentile** | **Underweight** |
| **5th–85th percentile** | **Normal** |
| **85th–95th percentile** | **Overweight** |
| **≥ 95th percentile** | **Obese** |

**Prevalence (2015–2016):**
- **13.9%** in children aged **2 to 5**
- **18.4%** in children aged **6 to 11**
- **20.6%** in adolescents aged **12 to 19**

**Higher in non-Hispanic African American and Hispanic children and teens** compared to non-Hispanic White and non-Hispanic Asian children and teens.

> ⚠️ **"At least 70% of OVERWEIGHT CHILDREN WILL BECOME OVERWEIGHT ADULTS."**

**CDC's contributing factors (2018):** **genetics; metabolism; eating and physical activity behavior; community and neighborhood design and safety; SHORT SLEEP DURATION; and NEGATIVE CHILDHOOD EVENTS.**

**Also named:** **time spent watching television, using digital media** (computer/tablet, phone, other electronic devices); **built-up environments without space for parks, walking paths, or recreation sites.** **"Food insecurity INCREASED during the COVID-19 pandemic when many people lost their jobs."**

### Physiological consequences by system

| System | Consequences |
|---|---|
| **Cardiovascular** | **High blood pressure and high cholesterol** |
| **Metabolic** | **Increased or impaired glucose tolerance, insulin resistance, and type 2 diabetes** |
| **Musculoskeletal** | — |
| **Respiratory** | **Asthma and sleep apnea** |
| **Renal** | **Fatty liver disease, gallstones, and gastroesophageal reflux** |

**Psychosocial:** **anxiety, depression, low self-esteem, and social problems such as BULLYING AND STIGMA.**

**Also:** **"Excessive body fat at a young age is likely to PERSIST INTO ADULTHOOD and is associated with physical and psychosocial comorbidities, as well as LOWER COGNITIVE ABILITY, RETARDATION IN SCHOOL, and LATER LIFE ACHIEVEMENT."**

### Diabetes in children

**Approximately 210,000 US children and adolescents under age 20 had diabetes in 2018.**

⚠️ **"WHITES and MALES between the ages of 10 to 14 are MORE LIKELY than other groups to be diagnosed with TYPE 1 diabetes"**, although the rate since 2011 has significantly increased among **Asians and Pacific Islanders.** ⚠️ **"There are NO KNOWN WAYS TO PREVENT TYPE 1 DIABETES."**

**Screening for TYPE 2 diabetes is recommended for children with a BMI from the 85th to the 95th percentile with TWO OR MORE of the following risk factors:**
1. **Family history of type 2 diabetes in a FIRST- OR SECOND-DEGREE relative**
2. **Native American, African American, Latino, Asian American, or Pacific Islander descent**
3. **Signs of insulin resistance or conditions associated with insulin resistance**
4. **Maternal history of diabetes or GESTATIONAL DIABETES during the child's gestation**

### Box 22.1 — Family Recommendations for Obesity Prevention

- ⚠️ **"BREASTFEEDING is associated with a LOWER RISK for developing childhood obesity"**
- **Parents' responsibilities include providing healthy meals and snacks**
- ⚠️ **"LIMIT 100% FRUIT JUICES and AVOID ALL OTHER SUGARY BEVERAGES. These are EMPTY CALORIES and fill children up so they are not hungry at meals. APPROPRIATE BEVERAGES ARE MILK AND WATER"**
- ⚠️ **"For toddlers and preschoolers, it sometimes takes 10–15 TASTES of a new food before learning to like that food. BE PERSISTENT!"**
- **Parents should ROLE MODEL good eating behaviors**
- **Family meals are important for teaching manners, LISTENING TO HUNGER CUES, and quality family time**
- **Encourage children to HELP WITH FOOD SELECTION AND PREPARATION** as appropriate to developmental skills; **allow them to select new foods to try in the produce section**
- ⚠️ **"AVOID USING FOOD AS A PUNISHMENT OR REWARD. Do NOT expect your child to 'CLEAN THE PLATE.' These feeding techniques have been ASSOCIATED WITH INCREASED RISK FOR OBESITY"**
- ⚠️ **"TURN OFF THE TELEVISION during meals... CHILDREN DO NOT LISTEN TO THEIR CUES OF SATIETY WHEN DISTRACTED"**
- **Cook meals at home. BROIL, BAKE, STIR-FRY, OR POACH rather than frying**
- **Encourage family members to STOP EATING WHEN THEY ARE SATISFIED**
- **Schedule regular times for meals and snacks. INCLUDE BREAKFAST AND DO NOT SKIP MEALS**
- **Have low-calorie, nutritious snacks ready and available**
- **Decrease salt, sugar, and fat; increase complex carbohydrates — whole grains**
- **Select family activities and vacations that INCLUDE OR FOCUS ON PHYSICAL ACTIVITY**

### The management goal

> **"The GOAL of managing weight in children and adolescents is to NORMALIZE weight. This may involve SLOWING THE RATE OF WEIGHT GAIN and ALLOWING CHILDREN TO 'GROW' INTO THEIR WEIGHT, improving dietary habits, increasing physical activity, IMPROVING SELF-ESTEEM, and IMPROVING PARENT RELATIONSHIPS."**

**"Let's Move!" campaign (Michelle Obama) — four primary components:** **healthy schools; access to affordable and healthy food; raising children's physical activity levels; and helping parents make healthy choices.**

**AAP physical activity recommendation: 60 MINUTES of MODERATE AEROBIC physical activity PER DAY for every child and adolescent.**

**Evidence-Based Practice finding (McKee et al., 2016):** ⚠️ **"MORE THAN TWO OUT OF FIVE PARENTS MISPERCEIVED THE WEIGHT STATUS OF THEIR CHILDREN. Parents who misperceived their child's weight were NEARLY 12 TIMES MORE LIKELY TO HAVE AN OBESE CHILD."** **"PARENTAL MISPERCEPTION OF THEIR CHILD'S WEIGHT WAS THE STRONGEST PREDICTOR OF CHILDHOOD OBESITY."**

## §3 Injuries and Accidents (p. 383–386)

> **"Injuries and accidents are THE MOST COMMON CAUSES OF PREVENTABLE DISEASE, DISABILITY, AND DEATH AMONG CHILDREN."** And: **"MOST INJURIES ARE PREDICTABLE AND PREVENTABLE."**

**More than 22,200 children are seen in emergency departments DAILY for treatment from a nonfatal unintentional injury.**

**Why children are especially at risk:** **their SIZE, GROWTH AND DEVELOPMENT, INEXPERIENCE, and NATURAL CURIOSITY.** **"The KEY to changing behaviors is TEACHING AGE-APPROPRIATE SAFETY."**

**Leading causes of unintentional injuries in children overall:** **motor vehicle accidents, suffocation, drowning, poisoning, fire, and falls.**

**Motor vehicle (2018):** **1,196 children ages 15 and younger died as occupants**; **more than 198,000 were injured.** ⚠️ **Age- and size-appropriate car seats, booster seats, and seat belts "REDUCES SERIOUS AND FATAL INJURIES BY MORE THAN HALF."**

### Table 22.3 — Leading causes of unintentional injury death by age group (2016) — memorize the top row

| 0–1 years | 1–4 years | 5–9 years | 10–14 years | 15–19 years |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SUFFOCATION** | **DROWNING** | **MVT-related** | **MVT-related** | **MVT-related** |
| Homicide | MVT-related | Drowning | **SUICIDE** | Poisoning |
| MVT-related | Suffocation | Burns/fire | Drowning | Homicide |
| Drowning | Homicide | Homicide | Homicide | Suicide |
| Adverse effects from injury | Fire/burns | Suffocation | Burns/fire | Drowning |

> ⚠️ **The three to memorize: INFANTS = SUFFOCATION. AGES 1–4 = DROWNING. AGES 5 AND UP = MOTOR VEHICLE.**

### Developmental considerations

| Group | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Infants** | ⚠️ **"Infants have the SECOND HIGHEST INJURY RATE of all children groups."** **Small airway may be easily occluded; the small body fits through places where THE HEAD MAY BE ENTRAPPED.** In crashes, small size **increases the risk for crushing or being propelled into surfaces.** Second half of infancy brings **rolling, sitting, pulling up, and walking**; **developing motor skills remain IMMATURE, which LIMITS THEIR ABILITY TO ESCAPE from injury** and places them at risk for **drowning, suffocating, and burns** |
| **Toddlers and preschoolers** | **Large number of NONFATAL FALLS and BEING STRUCK BY OR AGAINST AN OBJECT.** **"They are active and LACK AN UNDERSTANDING OF CAUSE AND EFFECT, and their increasing motor skills MAKE SUPERVISION DIFFICULT."** **Inquisitive with relatively immature logic abilities** |
| **School-age children** | ⚠️ **"The school-age group has the LOWEST INJURY DEATH RATE."** **"At this age, it is DIFFICULT TO JUDGE SPEED AND DISTANCE,"** placing them at risk for **pedestrian and bicycle accidents.** **BOYS are TWICE as likely as girls to sustain a nonfatal bicycle injury; the HIGHEST injury rate is at 10 to 14 years.** ⚠️ **"UNIVERSAL USE OF BICYCLE HELMETS WOULD PREVENT MOST DEATHS."** **PEER PRESSURE and LACK OF PARENTAL ROLE MODELING often inhibit use of protective devices** |
| **Adolescents** | **Motor vehicle-related injuries AND VIOLENCE are the leading causes of morbidity and mortality.** **"Risk-taking becomes MORE CONSCIOUS at this time, especially among BOYS."** ⚠️ **"The injury death rates for BOYS are TWICE AS HIGH as those for girls."** **Adolescents are at the HIGHEST RISK OF ANY AGE GROUP for motor vehicle deaths AND FATAL POISONINGS.** **HOMICIDES are the THIRD leading cause of death of US adolescents 10–19; SUICIDES are the SECOND.** Risk factors for suicide: **poor social adjustment, psychiatric problems, and family disorganization** |

**Adolescent survey findings (Kann et al., 2018):** **23.6% reported being in a physical fight at least once in the previous 12 months**; **6.7% reported missing school at least 1 day in the previous month because they FELT UNSAFE at school or on their way to school.**

**Sports safety guidelines (four):** **children should be grouped according to WEIGHT, SIZE, MATURATION, AND SKILL LEVEL; QUALIFIED AND COMPETENT PERSONS should supervise games and practices; ADEQUATE AND APPROPRIATE-SIZE EQUIPMENT should be used; goals should be DEVELOPMENTALLY AND PHYSICALLY APPROPRIATE.** Children in sports should have **annual sports physicals.**

### Box 22.2 — Injury Prevention Topics

**Car restraints, seat belts, air-bag safety; preventing fires and burns; preventing poisoning; preventing falls; preventing drowning and water safety; bicycle safety; safe driving practices; sports safety; pedestrian safety; gun safety; decreasing gang activities; preventing substance abuse.**

**Three intervention categories:** **ANTICIPATORY GUIDANCE, ENVIRONMENTAL MODIFICATION, and SAFETY EDUCATION.**

### Gun violence

**Characteristics associated with gun violence:** **history of aggressive behaviors, poverty, school problems, substance abuse, and CULTURAL ACCEPTANCE OF VIOLENT BEHAVIOR.**

> ⚠️ **"A SIGNIFICANT NUMBER OF ACCIDENTAL FIREARM INJURIES AND DEATHS IN CHILDREN OCCUR IN THE HOMES OF FRIENDS AND FAMILY MEMBERS."**

**AAP-recommended gun storage teaching (if the family will not remove guns):**
1. **Store all firearms UNLOADED AND UNCOCKED in a securely LOCKED container, with ONLY THE PARENTS knowing where the container is located**
2. **Store the guns and ammunition in SEPARATE LOCKED LOCATIONS**
3. **NEVER LEAVE A GUN UNATTENDED when handling or cleaning it, EVEN FOR A MOMENT; it should be in the parent's view at all times**

**Other nursing actions:** urge legislators to support gun control legislation; collaborate with schools on violence-prevention programs; **encourage families to REMOVE guns from their homes**; initiate community programs on gun storage and safety at school; ⚠️ **educate parents on COMMUNICATING WITH THE HOMEOWNERS OF THE HOMES THEIR CHILDREN VISIT about gun access and safety**; children learning to hunt in rural areas should take gun safety courses; identify populations at risk and target aggression or anger management; **DISCOURAGE MIXING ALCOHOL OR DRUGS WITH GUNS**; encourage families to avoid gun violence in media at home.

### Child maltreatment (2018 data)

- **An estimated 678,000 victims of abuse and neglect nationally**, a rate of **9.2 victims per 1000 children**
- ⚠️ **"Children in their FIRST YEAR OF LIFE have the HIGHEST rate of victimization at 26.7 per 1000"**
- **Girls (9.6) higher than boys (8.7)**
- ⚠️ **"AMERICAN INDIAN OR ALASKA NATIVE children have the HIGHEST rate of victimization at 15.2 per 1000"**, followed by **African Americans at 14.0**
- **Children with SPECIAL NEEDS are also at higher risk**

**CAPTA categories:**

| Acts of COMMISSION (abuse) | Acts of OMISSION (neglect) |
|---|---|
| **Physical abuse, sexual abuse, psychological abuse** | **Failure to provide** (physical neglect, emotional neglect, medical or dental neglect, educational neglect) and **failure to supervise** (inadequate supervision, **exposure to violent environments**) |

**Common parental characteristics:** **poor understanding of child development and children's needs; history of abuse in the family of origin; substance abuse in the household; and NONBIOLOGICAL TRANSIENT CAREGIVERS IN THE HOME (e.g., mother's boyfriend).**

**Families at HIGHEST risk:** those experiencing **social isolation, family violence, parenting stress, and poor parent–child relationships.**

## §4 Mental Health, Acute Illness, and Chronic Conditions in Children (p. 386–387)

**Behavioral problems named:** **eating disorders; attention problems including ADD/ADHD; substance abuse; elimination problems; conduct disorders and delinquency; sleep disorders; anxiety disorders; autism spectrum disorder; depression; bipolar disorder; school maladaptation.**

**The DSM-5 is named as "the most comprehensive and up-to-date source of information for practitioners."**

> **"EARLY RECOGNITION and COORDINATED MANAGEMENT of pediatric mental health issues are CRITICAL to the child's functioning in school, at home, and in the community."**

⚠️ **"Many families DO NOT UNDERSTAND the behaviors or symptoms they observe in their child. EMBARRASSMENT MAY PREVENT PARENTS FROM SEEKING HELP."**

**The MEDICAL HOME is named twice** — for coordinating mental health management (oversight of subspecialties, medications, therapies) and for children with chronic conditions (**"one provider or clinic has ALL of the child's records"**).

**Coping mechanisms are influenced by:** **individual developmental level, temperament, previous stress experiences, role models, and support of parents and peers.**

### Handwashing and germ spread

**How germs spread (CDC):** touching eyes, nose, and mouth with unwashed hands; preparing or eating food and drinks with unwashed hands; blowing your nose, coughing, or sneezing into hands and then touching other people's hands or common objects.

**Box 22.3 — how to wash hands:** wet with clean warm or cold running water, **turn off the tap**, apply soap; **lather the BACK of your hands, BETWEEN your fingers, and UNDER YOUR NAILS**; ⚠️ **"Rub your hands for AT LEAST 20 SECONDS (sing the 'Happy Birthday' song from beginning to end TWICE)"**; rinse well; dry with a clean towel or air dryer.

**Key times to wash:** before/during/after preparing foods; before eating; before and after caring for someone sick with vomiting or diarrhea; before or after treating a cut or wound; after using the toilet; after changing diapers or cleaning up a child who used the toilet; after blowing nose/coughing/sneezing; **after touching an animal, animal feed, or animal waste**; after handling pet food or pet treats; after touching garbage.

**Other teaching:** children should **cough or sneeze into their ELBOW rather than directly into the air**; **children 2 years and older should learn how to wear a face mask** when needed. ⚠️ **If a child is diagnosed with influenza, parents can be instructed to KEEP THE CHILD AT HOME UNTIL SYMPTOMS HAVE IMPROVED AND FEVER ABSENT FOR 24 HOURS.**

### SIDS

- **Peak age: between ONE AND FOUR MONTHS of age**, although SIDS may occur **up to 1 year**
- ⚠️ **"The rate of SIDS in non-Hispanic AFRICAN AMERICAN and AMERICAN INDIAN infants is MORE THAN TWICE that of White infants, and that in ALASKA NATIVE infants is TWO TO THREE TIMES the national average"**
- **Incidence has decreased dramatically since the "BACK TO SLEEP" campaign promoted in 1994**
- ⚠️ **"There is NO TEST to identify infants who may die, making this a FRUSTRATING CLINICAL PROBLEM"**
- **Nursing role:** **empathetic support; assist the family through the grief process; provide guidance for SIBLINGS and other family members; refer to support groups**

### Chronic health conditions in children

**Named examples:** **Down syndrome, spina bifida, cerebral palsy, asthma, ADHD, diabetes, congenital heart disease, cancer, hemophilia, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, and AIDS.**

**Five variables to assess for each child and family:**
1. **What is the actual health status? Is the condition STABLE OR LIFE THREATENING?**
2. **What is the degree of impairment to the child's ABILITY TO DEVELOP?**
3. **What types of treatments and therapy are required and WITH WHAT FREQUENCY?**
4. **How often are health care visits and hospitalizations required?**
5. **To what degree are FAMILY ROUTINES DISRUPTED?**

**Common issues to evaluate:** all children with chronic problems **still need ROUTINE health care** (health promotion and acute care); ongoing condition-specific medical care; **COORDINATION of scheduling across multiple specialists**; skilled care procedures (suctioning, positioning, medications, feeding techniques, breathing treatments, physical therapy, appliances); complex equipment needs (monitors, oxygen, ventilators, positioning/ambulation devices, infusion pumps, suction machines); **complex educational needs requiring communication among family, providers, school administrators, and teachers**; **safe transportation**; **financial resources**; and **behavioral issues affecting the child and other family members.**

> **"The ULTIMATE GOAL is for children with chronic health conditions to achieve OPTIMAL HEALTH AND FUNCTIONING."**

**The playground point:** **"All children love to play, but MOST PLAYGROUNDS ARE DESIGNED WITH EQUIPMENT THAT IS NOT SAFE FOR CHILDREN WITH PHYSICAL DISABILITIES."**

### Asthma

- **2017: an estimated 5.5 million children under 18 affected**
- **Characterized by EXCESSIVE LUNG SENSITIVITY to various stimuli** — viral infection, allergies, irritating gases, particles in the air
- ⚠️ **"SECONDHAND SMOKE CAN WORSEN ASTHMA, and asthma is the THIRD LEADING CAUSE OF HOSPITALIZATION in children under the age of 15 years"**
- **A MAJOR CAUSE OF SCHOOL ABSENTEEISM**
- **2017: 3,564 deaths and 1.6 million emergency department visits**
- **PRESCHOOL children are increasingly among the newly diagnosed cases**
- ⚠️ **"LOW-INCOME AND MINORITY GROUPS are MORE LIKELY TO BE HOSPITALIZED FOR OR TO DIE OF ASTHMA"**

**Population-focused asthma strategies:** education programs for families; **development of HOME AND ENVIRONMENTAL ASSESSMENT GUIDES to identify TRIGGERS**; education and outreach in high-risk populations for case finding (low income, high unemployment, substandard housing, secondhand smoke exposure); **development of COMMUNITY CLEAN AIR POLICIES** (no burning of leaves, smoke-free zones); **improved access to care** (clinic services with consistent providers to decrease ED use); **assessment of schools and daycare centers for asthma triggers.**

## §5 Target Areas for Prevention with Children (p. 387–389)

### Smoking

**Environmental tobacco smoke (ETS)** = **exhaled smoke, smoke from burning tobacco, or from the mouthpiece or filter end of a cigarette, cigar, or pipe.** ⚠️ **"These effects are particularly harmful to children UNDER 5 YEARS and those LIVING IN POVERTY."**

**Effects in children exposed to ETS:** **increased episodes of MIDDLE EAR INFECTIONS, ASTHMA, UPPER RESPIRATORY TRACT INFECTION, and MORE MISSED SCHOOL DAYS.**

**More than 23 million children in the US — about 35% — have been exposed to secondhand smoke.**

**ENDS:** Goniewicz et al. (2013) **"found CANCER-CAUSING SUBSTANCES IN ALL of the e-cigarette samples that were tested."**

⚠️ **"Children of smokers are MORE LIKELY TO SMOKE, and it is ESPECIALLY HARD FOR ADULTS TO QUIT SMOKING IF THEY BEGAN AS TEENS."**

**Four things to offer parents:** (1) **educational programs on the negative effects of smoking on children**; (2) **interventions to STOP smoking**; (3) **ways to create a SMOKE-FREE ENVIRONMENT**; (4) **behavior modification techniques.**

> ⚠️ **The teaching principle (a very likely item):** **"Antismoking programs directed toward children and teenagers are MORE SUCCESSFUL IF THE FOCUS IS ON SHORT-TERM EFFECTS RATHER THAN ON LONG-TERM EFFECTS. Developmentally, children and teenagers CANNOT VISUALIZE THE FUTURE CONSEQUENCES of smoking. THE IMMEDIATE HEALTH RISKS AND THE COSMETIC EFFECTS SHOULD BE EMPHASIZED."** Teaching should also include **how ADVERTISING puts pressure on people to smoke.**

### Nutrition

> ⚠️ **"THE FIRST 6 YEARS ARE THE MOST IMPORTANT FOR DEVELOPING SOUND LIFETIME EATING HABITS."**

**"ATHEROSCLEROSIS BEGINS DURING CHILDHOOD."** Other diseases with early beginnings: **obesity, diabetes, osteoporosis, and cancer.**

**"Low-income and minority families are at increased risk for poor nutrition, BUT ALL GROUPS SHOW POOR DIETARY HABITS."**

**Child characteristics affecting nutrition:** **being slow eaters, having picky food choices, allergies, acute or chronic health problems, and changes in growth patterns.**

**Nursing actions:** providing information in individual or group sessions; **conducting diet assessment**; **delivering educational activities focusing on the effects of FAD FOODS AND DIETS**; giving information about daily food needs and **suggesting healthy snacks**; and **ASSESSING FOR RISKS FOR EATING DISORDERS.**

| Food group | Range across ages |
|---|---|
| **Milk** (select low-fat) | **2 cups → 3 cups** |
| **Meat and beans** (lean meats, beans, eggs, seafood) | **2 oz → 5–6½ oz** |
| **Vegetables** (fresh best) | **1 cup → 2½–3 cups** |
| **Fruits** (LIMIT FRUIT JUICES) | **1 cup → 2 cups** |

### Immunizations

> **"The GOAL of immunization is to PROTECT THE INDIVIDUAL by using immunizing agents to STIMULATE ANTIBODY FORMATION."**

**Why some families decline (four reasons named):**
1. ⚠️ **"NOT ALL PARENTS APPRECIATE THE SERIOUSNESS OF VACCINE-PREVENTABLE DISEASES BECAUSE THE PREVALENCE IS LOW"**
2. **"They are CONFUSED BY MEDIA MISINFORMATION about the consequences of vaccines, including AUTISM"**
3. **"They have concerns about THE DATA SHOWING THE SAFETY of vaccines"**
4. **"They DOUBT THE AGENCIES making recommendations and THE COMPANIES that manufacture vaccines"**

> **"It is important to UNDERSTAND THEIR CONCERNS and to EDUCATE THEM ABOUT VACCINE SAFETY."**

**Access barriers:** **"For some people, COST AND CONVENIENCE are critical issues."** **"In many communities, successful programs combine LOW-COST OR FREE IMMUNIZATIONS PROVIDED AT CONVENIENT TIMES AND LOCATIONS."**

**COVID-19 effect:** **"Many children were NOT immunized due to CLOSED MEDICAL PRACTICES and PARENTAL FEAR of taking their children to a medical practice. There may be LONG-TERM EFFECTS of these missed immunizations."**

**Source named:** the **CDC** is "the most comprehensive place to look," including the **"Recommended child and adolescent schedule for ages 18 years or younger."** Approved by the **Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices.** Both the **AAP and the American Academy of Family Physicians refer readers to this site.**

### Environmental health hazards

> ⚠️ **"As discussed in Chapter 8, LEAD POISONING IS THE MOST COMMON ENVIRONMENTAL HEALTH HAZARD FOR CHILDREN."** Also named: **pesticides and poor air quality.**

**Why children are more vulnerable — six mechanisms:**
1. **"GROWING TISSUES ABSORB TOXINS READILY"**
2. **"DEVELOPING ORGAN SYSTEMS ARE MORE SUSCEPTIBLE TO DAMAGE"**
3. **"A SMALLER SIZE MEANS AN INCREASED CONCENTRATION OF TOXINS PER POUND OF BODY WEIGHT"**
4. ⚠️ **"The fact that children are small exposes them to LOWER AIR SPACES, WHERE HEAVY CHEMICALS TEND TO CONCENTRATE"**
5. **"OUTDOOR PLAY, especially during SUMMER months, increases the opportunity for exposure to air pollutants. When they are playing, children often RUN AND BREATHE HARD, WHICH INCREASES THE VOLUME OF POLLUTANTS INHALED"**
6. **"CHEWING AND MOUTHING BEHAVIORS offer contact to toxins such as LEAD. PLAYING ON THE FLOOR increases exposure to chemicals in RUGS AND FLOORING, and ROLLING IN GRASS can expose children to PESTICIDES"**

**Also:** **playground materials may be treated with chemicals**; **indoor air pollutants increased as houses were built "TIGHTLY" TO CONSERVE ENERGY** and as more chemicals were used in production.

**Children at GREATEST risk:** **those with RESPIRATORY DISEASES and those from LOW-INCOME families.** **"Low-income populations are more likely to have SUBSTANDARD HOUSING. POOR NUTRITIONAL STATUS INCREASES THE RISK for complications. SCREENING AND TREATMENT MAY BE DELAYED if access to health care is limited. Low-income neighborhoods are likely to be located CLOSER TO WASTE AREAS, and they often have HIGHER LEVELS OF CONTAMINANTS IN THE WATER SOURCE."**

**Adolescent exposure:** **"Exposure risks for adolescents are SIMILAR TO THOSE FOR ADULTS and are primarily through WORK, SCHOOL, AND HOBBIES including participating in sports."**

**The built environment:** **"In highly concentrated living areas, there are often FEW GREEN SPACES where children and adolescents can play active games."** **"Many of the advertisements on television and the Internet are TARGETED TO CHILDREN and may promote UNHEALTHY FOOD CHOICES."**

## §6 Health Policy and Legislation for Adults (p. 389–391)

**Historical framing:** **"Historically, MEN HAVE DOMINATED the medical and research professions... EARLY RESEARCH WAS TYPICALLY CONDUCTED ON MEN, with MENTAL HEALTH, REPRODUCTION, and THE ROLE OF WOMEN AS MOTHERS being the exceptions."** In the **1980s**, the **US Public Health Services Task Force on Women's Health Issues** recommended **increasing gender equity in biomedical research** and **establishing guidelines for including women in federally sponsored studies.**

### Five key pieces of federal legislation

| Act | Year | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Older Americans Act (OAA)** | **1965** | Established the **Administration on Aging (AOA)** and state agencies for the **social service needs** of older people. **Mission of AOA: "help older adults MAINTAIN DIGNITY AND LIVE INDEPENDENTLY in their communities."** Funds **"meals, job training, senior centers, health promotion, benefits enrollment, caregiver support, transportation, and more."** **Reauthorized 2020, extended through 2024** |
| **Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)** | **1990** | **Protection against discrimination.** Requires government and businesses to provide **equal opportunities for jobs, education, access to transportation and public buildings, and other accommodations for BOTH physical AND mental limitations.** ⚠️ **One effect: "greater emphasis on COMMUNITY CARE for the disabled, RATHER THAN INSTITUTIONALIZATION"** |
| **Patient Self-Determination Act** | **1990** | ⚠️ **Does NOT create new rights — REAFFIRMS the common law right of SELF-DETERMINATION per the FOURTEENTH AMENDMENT.** Providers receiving **Medicare and Medicaid** funds **must ASK whether patients have advance directives** and **provide information about rights under state law** |
| **Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA)** | **1993** | **Job protection and continuous health benefits for eligible employees needing extended leave for their own illness or to care for a family member.** ⚠️ **Amended in 2008 to increase MILITARY FAMILY entitlements** |
| **Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Reconciliation Act** | **1996** | **"Welfare reform."** **Targeted women receiving public assistance**; **changed AFDC → TANF**, a work program mandating **women heads of households find employment to retain benefits.** Administered by the **Administration for Children and Families within USDHHS** |

**Also named:** the **Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act of 2010.**

**Policy vs. legislation:** **"HEALTH POLICY is action taken by public and private agencies to promote health. It is A REFLECTION OF THE VALUES HELD IN SOCIETY."** **"LEGISLATION consists of LAWS THAT REGULATE health care and promote health."** **Legislation "must be CONGRUENT WITH THE CONSTITUTION being the overarching umbrella."** (Fig. 22.2, the cascading policy framework: National Constitution → National Health Policy → State Health Policy → Organizational Plans → Organizational Policies.)

### Advance directives — the two parts

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **LIVING WILL** | **"Allows the patient to EXPRESS WISHES REGARDING THE USE OF MEDICAL TREATMENTS in the event of a TERMINAL ILLNESS"** |
| **DURABLE POWER OF ATTORNEY** | **"The legal way for the patient to DESIGNATE SOMEONE ELSE to make health care decisions WHEN HE OR SHE IS UNABLE to do so"** |

**A DNR order** is **"a SPECIFIC ORDER FROM A PHYSICIAN not to use cardiopulmonary resuscitation."**

**The values history instrument:** **"asks questions about SPECIFIC WISHES REGARDING DIFFERENT MEDICAL SITUATIONS."** **"This clarifying process THEN LEADS TO COMPLETION OF ADVANCE DIRECTIVES to document these preferences IN WRITING."**

> ⚠️ **"STATE LAWS VARY WIDELY regarding the implementing of these tools, so it is important to consult a knowledgeable source of information. It is ALSO IMPORTANT TO INVOLVE THE FAMILY, ESPECIALLY THE DESIGNATED DECISION MAKER OR AGENT, in these discussions so that EVERYONE UNDERSTANDS THE PATIENT'S CHOICES."**

### Legislated rights of the elderly (the full list)

**Individualized care; freedom from discrimination; privacy; freedom from neglect and abuse; control of one's own funds; ability to sue; freedom from PHYSICAL AND CHEMICAL RESTRAINT; involvement in decision making; the right to VOTE; access to community services; the right to raise grievances, obtain a will, and enter into contracts; the right to practice the religion of one's choice; and the right to dispose of one's own personal property.**

### Elder abuse (repeated from Ch. 27 with slight variation)

**Abuse consists of:**
- **The WILLFUL INFLICTION of physical pain or injury**
- **Debilitating MENTAL ANGUISH AND FEAR**
- **THEFT OR MISMANAGEMENT of money or resources**
- **UNREASONABLE CONFINEMENT or the DEPRIVATION OF SERVICES**

**"It is estimated that annually ONE OUT OF SIX older adults WORLDWIDE experience abuse."**

**Neglect** = **"a lack of services or obligations that are necessary for the physical and mental health of an individual BY THE INDIVIDUAL OR A CAREGIVER."** Can include **fiduciary responsibilities.**

**Exploitation** = **"the ILLEGAL OR IMPROPER USE of a person or their resources for another's profit or advantage."**

> ⚠️ **The self-determination caveat:** **"OLDER PERSONS CAN MAKE INDEPENDENT CHOICES WITH WHICH OTHERS MAY DISAGREE. THEIR RIGHT TO SELF-DETERMINATION CAN BE TAKEN FROM THEM ONLY IF THEY ARE DECLARED INCOMPETENT."**

**Three things nurses must watch for during assessment:**
1. **CONFLICTS BETWEEN INJURIES AND EXPLANATION OF CAUSE**
2. **DEPENDENCY ISSUES between patient and caregiver**
3. **SUBSTANCE ABUSE BY THE CAREGIVER**

**"NEARLY ALL 50 STATES have enacted MANDATORY REPORTING LAWS and have instituted PROTECTIVE SERVICE PROGRAMS."** **The LOCAL SOCIAL SERVICES AGENCY or AREA AGENCY ON AGING can help with information on reporting requirements.**

## §7 Adult Chronic Disease (p. 391–394)

### The chronic care shift

> **"In chronic illness, CURE IS NOT EXPECTED, so nursing activities need to be MORE HOLISTIC, addressing FUNCTION, WELLNESS, and PSYCHOSOCIAL ISSUES."**
>
> ⚠️ **"With chronic illness, the focus is on HEALING (a unique process resulting in a SHIFT IN THE BODY/MIND/SPIRIT SYSTEM) RATHER THAN CURING (elimination of the signs and symptoms of disease)."**

**Eliopoulos's SEVEN goals for chronic care:**
1. **Maintain or improve SELF-CARE CAPACITY**
2. **Manage the disease effectively**
3. **BOOST THE BODY'S HEALING ABILITIES**
4. **Prevent complications**
5. **Delay deterioration and decline**
6. **Achieve the highest possible QUALITY OF LIFE**
7. **DIE WITH COMFORT, PEACE, AND DIGNITY**

**"The focus is on the development of SELF-MANAGEMENT SKILLS."** **"Individuals are often MOTIVATED to make lifestyle changes when they FEAR DEATH; DISABILITY; PAIN; and NEGATIVE EFFECTS ON WORK, FAMILY, OR ACTIVITY."**

### The most common chronic diseases and their shared risk factors

**Most common chronic diseases and conditions (NCCDPHP, 2019):** **HEART DISEASE AND STROKE, CANCER, and DIABETES.** ⚠️ **"They are not only the most common; they are also the MOST COSTLY AND PREVENTABLE of all health problems."**

**Four major shared risk factors:**
1. **Excessive alcohol use**
2. **Poor nutrition**
3. **Lack of physical exercise**
4. **Tobacco use and exposure to secondhand smoke**

**2019: 6 IN 10 US adults had one of these chronic diseases and 4 IN 10 had two.** ⚠️ **"People with chronic physical and mental health conditions consume approximately 86% OF THE NATION'S HEALTH CARE EXPENDITURES."**

**Leading causes of death for ALL AGES (2017), in order:** **heart disease, cancer, unintentional injuries (accidents), chronic lower respiratory diseases, cerebrovascular disease (stroke), Alzheimer's disease, and diabetes.**

### Health status indicators and life expectancy

**Definition:** **"the QUANTITATIVE OR QUALITATIVE MEASURES used to describe the level of WELL-BEING OR ILLNESS present in a defined population."** Presented as **RATES** (mortality, morbidity) or **PROPORTIONS** (percentages immunized).

> ⚠️ **"Although the United States SPENDS MORE MONEY PER CAPITA ON HEALTH THAN ANY OTHER COUNTRY, OTHER DEVELOPED COUNTRIES HAVE A LONGER LIFE EXPECTANCY FOR BOTH GENDERS."**

**2017 US life expectancy at birth:** **all persons 78.6 years; MALES 76.1; FEMALES 81.1.**

### Cardiovascular disease

> **"The LEADING CAUSE OF DEATH for BOTH MEN AND WOMEN including African Americans, American Indians or Alaska Natives, Hispanics and Whites in the United States is CARDIOVASCULAR DISEASE."**

- **CVD accounted for approximately 17.8 million deaths in 2017**, expected to grow to **more than 22.2 million by 2030**
- **Approximately every 40 SECONDS an American will have a heart attack**
- **On average in 2016, someone died of stroke every 3 minutes 35 seconds**

### Hypertension — the 2017 AHA reclassification (memorize the four categories)

| Category | Systolic | Diastolic |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | **< 120** | **and < 80** |
| **Elevated** | **120–129** | **and < 80** |
| **Hypertension STAGE 1** | **130–139** | **OR 80–89** |
| **Hypertension STAGE 2** | **≥ 140** | **OR ≥ 90** |

> ⚠️ **The change: hypertension is now 130/80, NOT 140/90.**

- **HBP occurs in about HALF of US adults**
- ⚠️ **"About 1 IN 4 adults with hypertension have their condition UNDER CONTROL"**
- **A greater percent of MEN (47%) have HBP than WOMEN (43%)**
- **More common in non-Hispanic BLACK adults (54%)** than non-Hispanic White (46%), non-Hispanic Asian (39%), or Hispanic (36%)
- ⚠️ **"BECAUSE HYPERTENSION DOES NOT HAVE SYMPTOMS, ONE-THIRD OF THESE PEOPLE DO NOT KNOW THEY HAVE THE DISEASE"**
- **Uncontrolled hypertension leads to HEART ATTACK, STROKE, KIDNEY DAMAGE, and many other complications**

### Physical activity guidelines (2018, second edition)

**"Physical activity is ANY FORM OF EXERCISE OR MOVEMENT OF THE BODY THAT USES ENERGY"** — including **household chores, yard work, and walking the dog.** Guidance is for **people 3 years and older.**

> ⚠️ **ADULTS: "at least 150 to 300 MINUTES of MODERATE intensity OR 75 to 150 MINUTES of VIGOROUS intensity, aerobic physical activity PER WEEK."**

**Regular physical activity prevents:** **early death and chronic diseases including CORONARY HEART DISEASE, STROKE, TYPE 2 DIABETES MELLITUS, DEPRESSION, and SOME TYPES OF CANCER.**

### Stroke

- **Approximately every 40 SECONDS someone has a stroke; every 4 MINUTES someone dies of stroke**
- ⚠️ **"The country's HIGHEST STROKE RATES are in the SOUTHEASTERN United States"**
- **A leading cause of serious LONG-TERM DISABILITY; reduces mobility in MORE THAN HALF of stroke survivors age 65 and over**
- **Major risk factors: HBP, high cholesterol, smoking, obesity, and diabetes**
- **About 87% of strokes are ISCHEMIC strokes**, which occur when blood flow to the brain is blocked
- **Cost: about $4 billion per year**
- ⚠️ **"The incidence of ISCHEMIC STROKE IS TWICE AS HIGH IN SMOKERS than in adults who do not smoke"**

### Diabetes in adults

- **2018: 34.2 million people, or 10.5% of the US population**, had diagnosed and undiagnosed diabetes
- ⚠️ **"The percentage of people with diabetes INCREASED WITH AGE, reaching 26% of people aged 65 or older"**
- **Highest rates in AMERICAN INDIANS/ALASKA NATIVES**, followed by Hispanics, non-Hispanic Blacks, non-Hispanic Asians, and non-Hispanic Whites
- **Complications: blindness, kidney failure, heart disease, stroke, and loss of toes, feet, or legs**
- ⚠️ **"MEDICAL COSTS ARE TWICE AS HIGH for people with diabetes as those without"**
- **Risk factors for complications: smoking, overweight and obesity, physical inactivity, HbA1c levels, HBP, and high cholesterol**
- **ADA estimate of national cost: $327 billion**

**Levels of prevention for diabetes as stated:**
- **PRIMARY:** **educating adults about nutrition and the risks of obesity, smoking, and physical inactivity**; community interventions addressing healthy eating, exercise, and weight reduction
- **SECONDARY:** **screening with FINGER-STICK BLOOD GLUCOSE TESTS or GLUCOSE TOLERANCE TESTS**; also **thorough history and physical examination**
- **TERTIARY:** **activities aimed at REDUCING THE COMPLICATIONS of the disease**

### Mental illness

**Two broad NIMH categories:**

| Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Any mental illness (AMI)** | **A mental, behavioral, or emotional disorder** varying in impact, ranging from **no impairment to mild, moderate, or severe** |
| **Serious mental illness (SMI)** | **A mental, behavioral, or emotional disorder resulting in SERIOUS FUNCTIONAL IMPAIRMENT** |

- **2018: 19.1% of US adults experienced mental illness (47.6 million people)**
- **2016: 16.5% of US youth aged 6–17 experienced a mental health disorder**
- **"Mental illness has a RIPPLE EFFECT on the person, the family, and the community"**

> ⚠️ **"Mental illness is generally viewed by society in a NEGATIVE manner and STIGMAS surrounding mental health are among the BARRIERS THAT DISCOURAGE PEOPLE FROM SEEKING TREATMENT. Because NEGATIVE STIGMAS ARE TIED TO THE DECREASED UTILIZATION OF MENTAL HEALTH SERVICES, public health nurses can take steps to DECREASE NEGATIVE STIGMAS."**

**Two named strategies:** **community education programs** to **"dispel their stereotypes and fears"**; and **local and mass media outlets broadcasting POSITIVE aspects of those living with mental disabilities and FUNCTIONING AS A PRODUCTIVE PART OF SOCIETY.**

### Cancer

> **"Cancers (MALIGNANT NEOPLASMS) are the SECOND LEADING CAUSE OF DEATH in the United States, EXCEEDED ONLY BY HEART DISEASE."**

- ⚠️ **ACS estimate: "AT LEAST 42% of newly diagnosed cancers in the US — about 750,000 in 2020 — are POSSIBLY AVOIDABLE"**, including those caused by **smoking, a combination of excess body weight, alcohol consumption, poor nutrition, and physical inactivity, as well as the many SKIN CANCERS caused by excessive sun exposure**
- **More than 1.8 million new cases expected in 2020; about 606,520 deaths expected**
- **Screening can help prevent: BREAST, COLON, RECTUM, CERVIX, LUNG, SKIN, and PROBABLY PROSTATE**
- **"Finding cancer lesions in a PRECANCEROUS STATE, such as those found in SKIN, CERVICAL, COLORECTAL, AND BREAST cancer, ALLOWS FOR TREATMENT WHILE IN A HIGHLY TREATABLE STAGE"**

**Colorectal cancer risk factors named:** **obesity, physical inactivity, smoking, heavy alcohol consumption, A DIET HIGH IN RED OR PROCESSED MEATS, and INSUFFICIENT INTAKE OF FRUITS AND VEGETABLES.**

### Adult weight control

- **BMI: 25 to 29.9 = OVERWEIGHT; 30 and above = OBESE** (NIH began using BMI in **1998**)
- **2017–2018 age-adjusted prevalence of obesity: 42.4%**, with **no significant differences between men and women** among all adults or by age groups
- ⚠️ **"The prevalence of SEVERE obesity was HIGHEST among adults between the ages of 40 and 59"**
- **Severe obesity increases the risk of HEART DISEASE and END-STAGE RENAL DISEASE**
- **Lowest obesity prevalence: non-Hispanic ASIAN adults (17.4%)**; among women, **non-Hispanic Asian (17.2%) lowest and non-Hispanic BLACK (56.9%) HIGHEST**, with non-Hispanic White (39.8%) and Hispanic (43.7%) in between

- **HDS-D03:** Increase the proportion of adult heart attack survivors **referred to a rehabilitation program**
- **C-10:** Reduce the proportion of students in grades 9–12 **who report sunburn**
- **IVP-02:** Reduce **emergency department visits for nonfatal injuries**

## §8 Women's Health Concerns (p. 394–396)

### Eating disorders

| | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Anorexia nervosa** | **A FEAR OF GAINING WEIGHT coupled with DISTURBANCES IN PERCEPTIONS OF THE BODY.** ⚠️ **"Excessive weight loss is the most noticeable clue. Individuals with anorexia RARELY COMPLAIN OF WEIGHT LOSS because THEY VIEW THEMSELVES AS NORMAL OR OVERWEIGHT."** Many also struggle with **depression, obsessive symptoms, and social phobias** |
| **Bulimia** | **Persistent concern with body SHAPE and WEIGHT; recurrent BINGE EATING; LOSS OF CONTROL during binges; and EXTREME METHODS to prevent weight gain** — purging, strict dieting, fasting, laxatives or diuretics, or vigorous exercise |

**"MEN MAY EXHIBIT EATING DISORDERS, although these are MORE COMMON IN WOMEN."**

**Nursing role:** **comprehensive physical and psychosocial assessments, as well as HISTORIES OF DIETARY PRACTICE**, to identify women with eating disorders and **provide appropriate referrals.** At the population level, nurses **"ADVOCATE AGAINST ADVERTISING THAT PROMOTES EXCEPTIONALLY THIN BODIES FOR WOMEN."**

### Reproductive health

**Contraceptive counseling** requires **"accurate knowledge of current contraceptive choices and A NONJUDGMENTAL APPROACH."** **"The GOAL of contraceptive counseling is to ensure that women have appropriate instruction TO MAKE INFORMED CHOICES about reproduction."**

**Choice depends on:** **the woman's health, frequency of sexual activity, number of partners, and plans to have future children.** ⚠️ **"EXCEPT FOR ABSTINENCE, NO METHOD PROVIDES A 100% GUARANTEE against unintended pregnancy or disease."**

**Preconceptual counseling** addresses risks **BEFORE conception** and includes **education, assessment, diagnosis, and intervention.** **Purpose: reduce and/or eliminate health risks for women and infants.**

**Folic acid:**
- **Neural tube defects = birth defects of the BRAIN AND SPINAL CORD** — **anencephaly and spina bifida**
- **More than 300,000 babies worldwide annually are born with neural tube defects**
- ⚠️ **"Women of childbearing age are advised to consume 400 MCG of FOLIC ACID to prevent neural tube defects"**
- **Good food sources: BREAKFAST CEREALS and SOME GRAIN PRODUCTS**

**Alcohol:** **"A MAJOR PREVENTABLE CAUSE of birth defects, mental retardation, and neurodevelopmental disorders is FETAL EXPOSURE TO ALCOHOL DURING PREGNANCY."** ⚠️ **"The CDC and the AAP recommend NO ALCOHOL DURING PREGNANCY."**

### Gestational diabetes (GDM)

**Definition:** **"a condition characterized by CARBOHYDRATE INTOLERANCE that is FIRST IDENTIFIED OR DEVELOPS DURING PREGNANCY."**

**Complications:** **infant MACROSOMIA (extra-large baby), NEONATAL HYPOGLYCEMIA, PREECLAMPSIA, and CESAREAN DELIVERY.**

**Epidemiology:** incidence is **increasing**, following the rise in obesity and type 2 diabetes. ⚠️ **"The prevalence of GDM INCREASES with MATERNAL AGE, NUMBER OF CHILDREN, and WIC USE, and DECREASES with HIGHER EDUCATION."**

**CDC's five tips for women with GDM:** **eat healthy foods; exercise regularly; monitor blood sugar often; take insulin if needed; and GET TESTED FOR DIABETES AFTER PREGNANCY.**

### Menopause and HRT

**The hormone decline can affect:** **the vaginal and urinary tract, cardiovascular system, bone density, libido, sleep patterns, memory, and emotions.**

**"Women's attitudes toward menopause VARY GREATLY and are influenced by CULTURE, AGE, SUPPORT, and THE RECOUNTED EXPERIENCES OF OTHER WOMEN."**

> ⚠️ **The framing shift:** **"For decades, the PREVAILING MEDICAL VIEW of menopause was A STATE OF DEFICIENCY that required hormone replacement... A MORE POSITIVE OUTLOOK of menopause encourages women to view it as A TRANSITIONAL AND NATURAL STAGE in the life of a woman."**

**The HRT trial history — high-yield:**
- HRT **"remained untested by rigorous scientific study"** until the **Women's Health Initiative** clinical trials **initiated in 1991**
- ⚠️ **Findings: "HRT DID NOT PREVENT HEART DISEASE"**
- **Long-term studies showed ADVERSE EFFECTS: increased risk of STROKE, CORONARY HEART DISEASE, and THROMBOEMBOLISM**
- ⚠️ **"HRT IS STILL CONSIDERED USEFUL FOR MANAGEMENT OF VASOMOTOR SYMPTOMS OR 'HOT FLASHES' BUT ONLY FOR A LIMITED AMOUNT OF TIME to avoid the adverse side effects"**
- **To prevent heart disease, women should instead: AVOID SMOKING, REDUCE FAT AND CHOLESTEROL INTAKE, LIMIT SALT AND ALCOHOL INTAKE, MAINTAIN A HEALTHY WEIGHT, AND BE PHYSICALLY ACTIVE**
- **"Evidence has been INCONCLUSIVE about the effectiveness of COMPLEMENTARY AND ALTERNATIVE THERAPIES in treating menopause symptoms"**

### Breast cancer

- **"The MOST FREQUENTLY DIAGNOSED CANCER IN WOMEN"**
- **2019 ACS estimate: 268,600 women and 2,670 MEN would be diagnosed**; **41,760 women and 500 men expected to die**
- ⚠️ **The disparity: "Although the INCIDENCE of breast cancer is HIGHER IN WHITE WOMEN, the DEATH RATE IS HIGHER FOR AFRICAN AMERICAN WOMEN"** — thought to be due to **low screening activity, social determinants such as low income, and poor access**
- **Secondary prevention includes MAMMOGRAPHY EVERY 2 YEARS AFTER AGE 50 and CLINICAL BREAST EXAMINATION**
- **"EARLY DETECTION CAN PROMOTE A CURE, whereas LATE DETECTION TYPICALLY ENSURES A POOR PROGNOSIS"**

### Osteoporosis

**Definition:** **"'POROUS BONE,' a disease characterized by LOW BONE MASS and STRUCTURAL DETERIORATION OF BONE TISSUE, leading to BONE FRAGILITY and an increased risk for fractures of the HIP, SPINE, AND WRIST."**

**Risk factors:** **WOMEN are more likely than men**; **AGE increases likelihood** because bones become thinner and weaker; **SMALL, THIN-BONED women are at greater risk**; and ⚠️ **"WHITE AND ASIAN WOMEN ARE AT HIGHEST RISK."** **"HIP FRACTURES are a common health problem due to osteoporosis."**

**Prevention:** **diets rich in CALCIUM AND VITAMIN D**; **avoiding medications that cause bone loss** (⚠️ **"Always check with the pharmacist and read medication labels"**); **exercise, especially WEIGHT-BEARING activities such as WALKING, RUNNING, STAIR CLIMBING, AND WEIGHT LIFTING**; **limiting alcohol consumption and avoiding smoking**; and **several approved medications.** Also: **HOME ASSESSMENT AND CORRECTION OF RISK FACTORS FOR FALLS, BONE DENSITY TESTING, and ANNUAL HEIGHT MEASUREMENT.**

## §9 Men's Health Concerns (p. 396–397)

> **"Although health policies, campaigns, and community health organizations offer services for men, WOMEN'S HEALTH IS MORE OFTEN EMPHASIZED."**

**Why men do not participate at the same level:** **"apparently because of the TRADITIONAL MASCULINE GENDER ROLE LEARNED THROUGH SOCIALIZATION."**

**Four factors contributing to poor health outcomes for men (Giorgianni et al., 2013):** **"SOCIOECONOMIC STATUS, ACCESS TO HEALTH CARE, MALE ACCULTURATION TO HEALTH ISSUES, HARMFUL PERCEPTIONS ABOUT MASCULINITY, and LACK OF UNDERSTANDING OF MALE HEALTH BEHAVIORS."**

**Behavioral differences named:** men **"tend to SMOKE MORE, are MORE LIKELY TO BE OVERWEIGHT, and LESS LIKELY TO RECEIVE ROUTINE CARE OR SEEK OUT CARE EARLY in the disease process than are women."**

> ⚠️ **The community argument for men's health:** when a man's health is compromised, **his ability to work is reduced and THE FAMILY CAN SUFFER FROM LACK OF INCOME. If the man DIES, THE WIDOW generally experiences the LOSS OF COMPANIONSHIP AND ASSUMES THE RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE LOST SPOUSE. RESOURCES TO PROMOTE AND SUSTAIN HEALTH OUTCOMES OF BOTH GENDERS MUST BE BALANCED FOR THE OVERALL HEALTH OF THE COMMUNITY."**

**Four nursing roles named:** **EDUCATOR** (provides knowledge and skill for replacing unhealthy behaviors); **PATIENT ADVOCATE** (supports and interacts with agencies to obtain needed resources); **CHANGE AGENT** (assesses needs and system influences, identifies and sets priorities, plans and implements programs, evaluates results).

### Testicular cancer

- **"This form of cancer can often be TREATED SUCCESSFULLY; therefore, THE DEATH RATE REMAINS LOW"**
- ⚠️ **"WHITE MEN ARE FOUR TO FIVE TIMES MORE LIKELY than are African American and Asian American men to develop this cancer"**

HOWEVER, in 2011 the USPSTF updated previously published guidelines that SIGNIFICANTLY ALTERED THAT TRADITION for asymptomatic adolescent and adult males. The new guidelines RECOMMEND AGAINST SCREENING by self-examination or clinical examination in ASYMPTOMATIC adult or adolescent males due to INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE, LOW INCIDENCE RATE, AND HIGH CURE RATE even with advanced testicular cancer."**

- **"Many men are EMBARRASSED AND RELUCTANT TO DISCUSS the subject"** despite increased publicity from ED medication marketing
- **"Men who respond positively to treatment for ED report SIGNIFICANTLY BETTER QUALITY OF LIFE"**

## §10 Health Disparities Among Special Groups of Adults (p. 397–399)

**US definition of health disparities:** **"differences that exist among specific population groups in THE ATTAINMENT OF FULL HEALTH POTENTIAL that can be measured by differences in INCIDENCE, PREVALENCE, MORTALITY, BURDEN OF DISEASE, and other adverse health conditions."**

> **"HEALTH INEQUALITIES ARE OFTEN DUE TO THE UNEQUAL DISTRIBUTION OF POWER AND RESOURCES."**
>
> ⚠️ **"POVERTY IS A STRONG AND UNDERLYING CURRENT THROUGHOUT ALL OF THE SPECIAL GROUPS."**

### Adults of color

- **In 2000, about 44% of the US population identified as members of racial or ethnic minority groups. By 2050, projected to comprise ALMOST HALF of the US population**
- **2018 NHQDR priority areas measured:** **person-centered care, patient safety, healthy living, effective treatment, care coordination, and care affordability**
- ⚠️ **"Some disparities were DECREASING but DISPARITIES PERSIST especially for POOR AND UNINSURED populations in ALL priority areas"**
- ⚠️ **"BLACKS, AMERICAN INDIANS AND ALASKA NATIVES AND NATIVE HAWAIIANS/PACIFIC ISLANDERS received WORSE CARE than Whites for about 40% of the quality measures. HISPANICS received worse care than Whites for about 35% of the quality measures"**

**The stated goal:** **"to CLOSE THE GAP with regard to health disparities in adults of color WHILE AT THE SAME TIME PRESERVING AND RESPECTING THE RICHNESS AND UNIQUE INFLUENCES OF VARIOUS CULTURES."**

### Incarcerated adults

- **At year-end 2016, about 1 IN 38 persons in the United States were under CORRECTIONAL SUPERVISION** (prisons, jails, probation, or parole)
- **The combined state and federal imprisonment rate was 431 per 100,000 population — the LOWEST RATE SINCE 1996**
- **At the end of 2018, the total prison population was 1,465,200** (excluding probation and parole)
- **States with the HIGHEST rates, in order: LOUISIANA, OKLAHOMA, MISSISSIPPI, ARKANSAS, and ARIZONA**
- **States with the LOWEST: MINNESOTA, MAINE, MASSACHUSETTS, RHODE ISLAND, and VERMONT**

### LGBTQI adults

> **"LGBTQI adults represent a SOMETIMES-HIDDEN special population, in part because of the SOCIAL STIGMA associated with homosexuality COUPLED WITH THE FEAR OF DISCRIMINATION."**

**Documented disparities:** **HIGHER RISKS FOR MENTAL HEALTH ISSUES, SUICIDE, AND SUBSTANCE ABUSE.**

**"To improve the health of LGBTQI persons, SERVICES NEED TO ADDRESS THEIR UNIQUE NEEDS, and PLACES NEED TO BE SAFE for them to receive health promotion, disease prevention, and treatment."**

### Adults with physical and mental disabilities

**"Disability status is based on a person's ability to complete MAJOR LIFE ACTIVITIES INDEPENDENTLY."**

**Major life activities named:** **self-care, receptive and expressive language, learning, mobility, self-direction, capacity for independent living, and financial sufficiency.**

**Two definitions:**

| Source | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Social Security Administration** | **The inability "to engage in any SUBSTANTIAL GAINFUL ACTIVITY by reason of any MEDICALLY DETERMINABLE physical or mental impairment that is expected to result in DEATH, or that HAS LASTED OR IS EXPECTED TO LAST FOR A CONTINUOUS PERIOD OF AT LEAST 12 MONTHS"** |
| **ADA** | **(1) A physical or mental impairment that SUBSTANTIALLY LIMITS one or more major life activities, (2) A RECORD OF such an impairment, or (3) BEING REGARDED AS HAVING such an impairment** |

⚠️ **"Care should be taken to recognize the PHYSICAL BARRIERS that prevent disabled adults from accessing health care, such as STRUCTURES THAT ARE NOT ACCESSIBLE DESPITE THE ADA RECOMMENDATIONS, TRANSPORTATION, or THE ASSISTANCE OF FAMILY, FRIENDS, OR CAREGIVERS to assist them in getting to a care facility."**

### Frail elderly

**Demographics:**
- **From 2007 to 2017, the 65-and-over population increased by 34%, from 37.8 to 50.9 million.** Projected to reach **94.7 million in 2060**
- **The 85-and-over population is projected to MORE THAN DOUBLE from 6.5 million in 2017 to 14.4 million in 2024 (123% increase)**
- **OLDER WOMEN OUTNUMBER OLDER MEN (28.3 to 22.6 million)**
- **2018: 32% of older women were WIDOWS; about 28% of older men and women LIVED ALONE**
- **2017: 23% of persons 65+ were members of racial or ethnic minority populations** — African American 9%, Hispanic 8%, Asian 4%, American Indian and Alaska Native 0.5%, Native Hawaiian/Pacific Islander 0.1%

**Need for personal care by age (Jan–June 2018) — note the gradient:**

| Age | Percentage needing personal care |
|---|---|

**Major sources of income:** **Social Security, income from assets, private or government employee pensions, and earnings.**

**FRAILTY defined:** **"a GERIATRIC SYNDROME that places older adults at risk for ADVERSE HEALTH OUTCOMES, including FALLS, WORSENING DISABILITY, INSTITUTIONALIZATION, AND DEATH."** ⚠️ **"Frailty is a COMPLEX STATE OF IMPAIRMENT that signifies loss in areas of PHYSICAL FUNCTIONING, PHYSIOLOGICAL RESILIENCY, METABOLISM, AND IMMUNE RESPONSE."**

> ⚠️ **The public health argument:** **"The prevalence of frailty in the older population poses a MAJOR PUBLIC HEALTH DILEMMA because THE MAJORITY OF THIS GROUP WILL RESIDE IN A COMMUNITY SETTING, placing new demands on HEALTH CARE SYSTEMS, FAMILY CAREGIVERS, AND COMMUNITY RESOURCES. To improve the health of frail elderly, community-based nursing programs need to ADDRESS RACIAL/ETHNIC AND SOCIOECONOMIC DISPARITIES."**

### The Mrs. Eldridge case (p. 402) — the blueprint's "family with numerous health problems"

**The scenario:** Neighbors and the administrator of a **senior high-rise residence** reported **Mrs. Eldridge, a 79-year-old widow living alone**, to the visiting nurse. **No one had been observed coming or going from her apartment recently.** When seen, she **appeared SELF-NEGLECTED and DID NOT APPEAR TO RECOGNIZE HER NEIGHBORS.**

**What the nurse found on the visit:**
- She was **pleasant**, but there was **an odor of STALE URINE**
- **Unkempt appearance of BOTH Mrs. Eldridge AND the apartment**
- She was **HESITANT AND UNSURE in her answers**; the history revealed **medical problems**
- **Both she and her clothes were dirty**; she **moved WITHOUT AIDS and appeared UNSTEADY ON HER FEET**
- The kitchen was **littered with unwashed dishes and empty frozen-food boxes, which she COULD NOT RECALL BEING BOUGHT OR HAVING BEEN DELIVERED**
- **Several pill bottles** on the counter with the names of a local physician and pharmacist
- ⚠️ **A billfold with several bills LYING OPEN on the kitchen counter, as well as an UNCASHED SOCIAL SECURITY CHECK**

**The family layer — this is the "numerous health problems" part:**
- A **son and daughter-in-law** live in the next county and **phone at least once a week**; their number is **taped to the table by the phone**
- ⚠️ **The son is an ALCOHOLIC**
- ⚠️ **The daughter-in-law has BEGINNING SYMPTOMS OF CARDIOVASCULAR DISEASE**
- ⚠️ **Mrs. Eldridge's GREAT-GRANDCHILD HAS ASTHMA and is cared for by the son and daughter-in-law**

> **This case is constructed so that the nurse must weigh: self-neglect vs. cognitive decline; fall risk; medication management; financial exploitation risk (open billfold, uncashed check); and a caregiving family that is itself carrying alcoholism, cardiac disease, and a chronically ill child. Expect a scenario item built on prioritizing among these.**

## Source conflicts and traps

- ⚠️ **FMLA is in this chapter's blueprint row but the substantive content is in Ch. 21, p. 377.** Ch. 22 gives only the one-sentence description (job protection, continuous health benefits, own illness or family member) plus **the 2008 military family amendment**, which Ch. 21 does NOT mention. **Use both banks: Ch. 21 for the 12 weeks / unpaid / benefits / position facts, Ch. 22 for the 2008 military amendment.**

- ⚠️ **Leading causes of unintentional injury DEATH shift by age group and the first three are the ones tested: infants = SUFFOCATION, ages 1–4 = DROWNING, ages 5+ = MOTOR VEHICLE.** Also note **suicide enters the top five at ages 10–14** (second) and **homicide is present in every age band from infancy on.**

- ⚠️ **Infants have the SECOND highest injury rate; school-age children have the LOWEST injury death rate; adolescents have the HIGHEST risk of any age group for motor vehicle deaths and fatal poisonings.** Three superlatives in one section, easy to shuffle.

- ⚠️ **Hypertension is now 130/80, not 140/90.** The 2017 AHA reclassification created four categories. **Elevated BP is 120–129 with diastolic under 80** — note that elevated has no diastolic range of its own.

- ⚠️ **Testicular self-examination is NO LONGER recommended for asymptomatic males** (USPSTF, 2011). Box 22.4 lists testicular cancer screening for **SYMPTOMATIC males** only. This directly contradicts what most students were taught, which makes it a near-certain item. The reasons given: **insufficient evidence, low incidence rate, and high cure rate even with advanced disease.**

- ⚠️ **HRT does NOT prevent heart disease** and carries increased risk of **stroke, CHD, and thromboembolism.** It remains useful only for **vasomotor symptoms, for a limited time.** The trap answer is HRT for cardiovascular or bone protection.

- ⚠️ **Breast cancer incidence is higher in White women, but the DEATH RATE is higher in African American women.** Same inversion pattern appears with **prostate cancer (African American mortality nearly twice as high)** and **testicular cancer (White men 4–5x more likely to develop it)**. Read carefully for incidence vs. mortality.

- ⚠️ **Antismoking teaching for children and teens should emphasize SHORT-TERM and COSMETIC effects, not long-term consequences**, because they cannot developmentally visualize the future. The intuitive answer (lung cancer in 30 years) is wrong.

- ⚠️ **There are no known ways to prevent TYPE 1 diabetes**, and Type 1 is more common in **Whites and males aged 10–14** — the reverse of the Type 2 racial pattern. Type 2 screening uses the **85th–95th percentile BMI plus two or more risk factors.**

- ⚠️ **The mature minors doctrine is 15 years and older, recognized in ALL 50 STATES.** Four situations allow treatment without parental consent: **emergency care, substance abuse, pregnancy, and birth control.**

- ⚠️ **Parental misperception of a child's weight is the STRONGEST PREDICTOR of childhood obesity**, and **more than 2 in 5 parents misperceive it.** An assessment item may hinge on the nurse addressing perception before diet.

- **BMI cutoffs differ for children (percentiles) and adults (absolute numbers).** Children: 85th–95th = overweight, ≥95th = obese. Adults: 25–29.9 = overweight, ≥30 = obese. Do not apply adult numbers to a pediatric stem.

- **Lead poisoning is the MOST COMMON environmental health hazard for children** — stated in this chapter, detailed in Ch. 8.

- **Asthma is the THIRD leading cause of hospitalization in children under 15**, and low-income and minority groups are **more likely to be hospitalized for or die of** it, despite similar prevalence.

- **The Patient Self-Determination Act creates NO new rights.** It reaffirms an existing common-law right under the Fourteenth Amendment and imposes a duty on providers to ask and inform.

- **Adult day health is a MEDICAL model; senior centers are a SOCIAL/recreational model.** Adult day health participants **return home to caregivers at night.**

- **Handwashing is 20 seconds — "Happy Birthday" TWICE.** Influenza exclusion is **until symptoms improve AND fever absent for 24 hours.**
