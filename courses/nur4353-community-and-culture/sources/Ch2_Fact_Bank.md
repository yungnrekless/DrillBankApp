## The people you have to know (highest yield)

| Person | Why they matter — the "first" or signature contribution |
|---|---|
| **Florence Nightingale** | Model of trained nursing and nursing education. Trained briefly at **Kaiserswerth, Germany** (Lutheran deaconesses, district nursing). In the **Crimean War (1854–1856)** at **Scutari**, used a **population-based approach** and **simple epidemiology** to cut soldier mortality from **415 per 1000 to 11.5 per 1000**. Founded the Nightingale Training School at St. Thomas' Hospital, London (1860). |
| **William Rathbone** | Established **district nursing** in **Liverpool, England** (1859). |
| **Lillian Wald** | **First public health nurse in the United States.** Coined the term "public health nurse." With **Mary Brewster**, founded the **Henry Street Settlement** (1893) in New York's Lower East Side, which became the Visiting Nurse Service of NYC. Arranged the **first insurance reimbursement for nursing** (Metropolitan Life, 1909). First president of the **NOPHN** (1912). Helped establish the **Children's Bureau** and school nursing. |
| **Mary Brewster** | Co-founded the Henry Street Settlement with Wald. |
| **Lina Rogers** | A Henry Street resident who became the **first school nurse** (New York City, 1902). |
| **Jessie Sleet (Scales)** | **First African American public health nurse** (hired by the NY Charity Organization Society, 1900). Studied TB among African Americans in Manhattan. |
| **Mary Breckinridge** | Founded the **Frontier Nursing Service (FNS)** (1925), using **nurse-midwives** to serve rural Appalachian Kentucky. |
| **Frances (Francis) Root** | First trained nurse hired (1877, Women's Board of the NY Mission) to **visit the sick poor at home** — the beginning of organized visiting nursing in the US. |
| **Mary S. Gardner** | Wrote the textbook *Public Health Nursing* (1919). |
| **Pearl McIver** | **First nurse employed by the US Public Health Service** (1934). |
| **Lemuel Shattuck** | Author of the **Shattuck Report** (1850), a landmark public health document. |

> **The single most common stem:** "Who was the first public health nurse in the United States?" → **Lillian Wald.** Don't confuse with Nightingale (British, founder of trained nursing generally) or Jessie Sleet (first *African American* PHN).

## Key terms (with printed page)

| Term | p. | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| district nursing | 23 | Care organized by geographic district; originated by Rathbone in Liverpool. Nightingale saw it at Kaiserswerth. |
| visiting nurses / VNAs | 23 | Nurses who cared for **several families per day** in their homes (more economical than private-duty nursing, which served one family). VNAs founded in Buffalo (1885), Philadelphia and Boston (1886). |
| settlement houses | 24 | Community centers in poor urban neighborhoods (e.g., Henry Street Settlement) providing nursing, education, and social services. |
| instructive district nursing | 24 | District nursing that emphasized **teaching and prevention**, not just bedside care. |
| official health agencies | 29 | Government (public) health agencies, as distinct from voluntary/private ones. |
| Shattuck Report | 22 | 1850 report; foundational public health document in the US. |
| Frontier Nursing Service (FNS) | 27 | Breckinridge's nurse-midwife service for rural Kentucky (1925). |
| Sheppard-Towner Act | (1921) | Maternity and Infancy Act; early federal funding for maternal-child health. |
| Social Security Act of 1935 | 29 | Landmark law that funded public health and expanded the federal role in health and welfare. |

## Key organizations (abbreviations that show up as distractors)

- **APHA** — American Public Health Association (1872).
- **NOPHN** — National Organization for Public Health Nursing (1912; Wald first president). Later folded into the NLN.
- **NLN** — National League for Nursing (1952 consolidation).
- **ANA** — American Nurses Association (roots in the 1896 Associated Alumnae; renamed 1911).
- **AACN** — American Association of Colleges of Nursing.
- **American Red Cross** — ran rural/town-and-country nursing services (Wald helped establish these).
- **USPHS** — US Public Health Service; established a division of public health nursing in 1918.

## The one conceptual point worth carrying forward

Nightingale and Wald both worked at the **population level**, not the individual level: Nightingale used **epidemiology and environmental change** to lower aggregate soldier mortality, and Wald identified needs affecting a whole community and then mobilized resources to meet them. This is the historical root of the Ch 1 principle that **the unit of care in public health nursing is the population.** If a history question asks *why* a figure is significant to public health nursing specifically, the answer usually points back to a **population-focused, prevention-oriented** approach rather than individual bedside care.
