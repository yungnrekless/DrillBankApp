## 1. Health care for children and health promotion (pp. 1-3)

"The major goal for pediatric nursing is to improve the quality of health care for children and their families."

Population and status figures: "In 2016 almost 74 million children 0 to 17 years old lived in the United States, making up 24% of the population." Areas of improvement: "increased immunization rates for all children, decreased adolescent birth rate, and improved child health outcomes." From the 2017 *America's Children in Brief*: "preterm births increased slightly in 2015, after a continuous decline since 2007"; average math scores for fourth- and eighth-graders decreased; youth violent crime victimization decreased over the last 20 years; the child poverty rate "remains high at 20%"; and children with at least one parent employed full time "remained steady at 75% in 2015."

**Box 1.1, health and health care priorities for American children:** poverty; hunger; lack of health insurance; child abuse and neglect; overweight and obesity; firearm deaths and injuries; mental health; racial and ethnic disparities; immigration.

**Box 1.2, Healthy People 2020.** Two goals: "Increase quality and length of healthy life" and "Eliminate health disparities." Ten Leading Health Indicators: physical activity; overweight and obesity; tobacco use; substance abuse; responsible sexual behavior; mental health; injury and violence; environmental quality; immunization; access to health care.

**Research Focus, the National Children's Study** (p. 1): "the largest prospective, long-term study of children's health and development conducted in the United States," designed "to follow 100,000 children and their families from birth to 21 years old to understand the link between children's environments and their physical and emotional health and development." Its hoped-for outcomes: information "to eradicate unhealthy diets, dental caries, and childhood obesity and to bring a significant reduction in violence, injury, substance abuse, and mental health disorders among the nation's children." It "supports the Healthy People 2020 primary goals."

**Bright Futures** (p. 2): "a national health promotion initiative with a goal to improve the health of our nation's children." Its major themes: "promoting family support, child development, mental health, and healthy nutrition that leads to healthy weight, physical activity, oral health, healthy sexual development and sexuality, safety and injury prevention, and the importance of community relationships and resources."

### The three named health promotion themes "essential for all age-groups" (p. 2)

**Development.** "The most dramatic time of physical, motor, cognitive, emotional, and social development occurs during infancy," and "Interactions between the parent and infant are central to promoting optimal developmental outcomes." Early childhood: "early identification of developmental delays is critical for establishing early interventions." Middle childhood: surveillance "provides opportunities to strengthen cognitive and emotional attributes, communication skills, self-esteem, and independence." Adolescence: "Recognition that adolescents differ greatly in their physical, social, and emotional maturity is important."

*Screen time (AAP, 2016):* the guidelines "shift the importance from what is on the screen to who is viewing the information with the young child." **"For infants less than 18 months of age, no screen time is recommended except for video calling with a grandparent or loved one. Parents should be advised to use technology sparingly before 5 years of age and to always participate during screen-time viewing."**

**Nutrition.** "Human milk is the preferred form of nutrition for all infants. Breastfeeding provides the infant with micronutrients, immunologic properties, and several enzymes that enhance digestion and absorption of these nutrients." **"Children establish lifelong eating habits during the first 3 years of life."** "Most eating preferences and attitudes related to food are established by family influences and culture. During adolescence, parental influence diminishes and the adolescent makes food choices related to peer acceptability and sociability."

**Oral health.** "Preventing dental caries and developing healthy oral hygiene habits must occur early in childhood." Disparities: "In children ages 2 to 8 years, Hispanic and non-Hispanic black children are twice as likely to experience any dental caries in primary teeth compared with non-Hispanic white children," and "Preschoolers of low-income families are twice as likely to develop tooth decay and only half as likely to visit the dentist as other children." The nurse's teaching starts "beginning with the first tooth eruption; drinking fluoridated water, including bottled water; and instituting early dental preventive care."

## 2. Childhood health problems (pp. 3-7)

Groups named as highest risk: "children born preterm or with very low birth weight (VLBW) or low birth weight (LBW), children attending child care centers, children who live in poverty or are homeless, children of immigrant families, and children with chronic medical and psychiatric illness and disabilities."

### Obesity and type 2 diabetes (p. 3)

"Childhood obesity, the most common nutritional problem among American children, is increasing in epidemic proportions." "Over 30% of America's children are overweight, and 17% are obese." The Healthy People target: reduce "the incidence of overweight children from the current 20% in all ethnic groups to less than 6%." "Lifestyle interventions show promise in preventing obesity and decreasing occurrence if targeted at children 6 to 12 years old." Maternal obesity is "a major influence on offspring health during childhood and in adult life," and "An optimal nutritional and microbial environment during pregnancy may reduce the risk of infants being obese or overweight during early life."

The three rankings the table hands you outright, and which the narrative repeats:
- **Infants (<1 year): mechanical suffocation ranks 1** in both sexes. The narrative confirms it: "During infancy, more boys die of aspiration or suffocation than do girls."
- **Ages 1 to 4: drowning ranks 1 in males, motor vehicle ranks 1 in females.**
- **Ages 5 to 14 and 15 to 24: motor vehicle ranks 1 in both sexes.** "the overwhelming causes of death in children are MVAs, including occupant, pedestrian, bicycle, and motorcycle deaths; these account for more than half of all injury deaths."

Other narrative facts: "The majority of deaths from injuries occur in boys." "Although the rate of injuries is high in children younger than 9 years old, most fatal injuries occur in later childhood and adolescence." "accidents continue to account for more than three times as many teen deaths as any other cause." "Children ages 5 to 9 years are at greatest risk of bicycling fatalities. The majority of bicycling deaths are from traumatic head injuries." Pedestrian accidents "occur at midblock, at intersections, in driveways, and in parking lots. Driveway injuries typically involve small children and large vehicles backing up."

Poisoning: "more than 500,000 children ages 5 years and younger experience a potential poisoning related to medications" each year, and "Approximately 95% of medication-related ED visits in children younger than 5 years are due to ingesting medication while unsupervised." "Intentional poisoning, associated with drug and alcohol abuse and suicide attempt, is the second leading cause of death in adolescent females and the third leading cause in adolescent males."

**Box 1.3, childhood injury risk factors.** These are exactly the SATA options.
- *Sex:* "Preponderance of males; difference mainly the result of behavioral characteristics, especially aggression."
- *Temperament:* "Children with difficult temperament profile, especially persistence, high activity level, and negative reactions to new situations."
- *Stress:* "Predisposes children to increased risk taking and self-destructive behavior; general lack of self-protection."
- *Alcohol and drug use:* "Associated with higher incidence of motor vehicle injuries, drownings, homicides, and suicides."
- *History of previous injury:* "Associated with increased likelihood of another injury, especially if initial injury required hospitalization."
- *Developmental characteristics:* mismatch between developmental level and the skill an activity requires; natural curiosity; desire to assert self and challenge rules; in the older child, "desire for peer approval and acceptance."
- *Anatomic characteristics:* "Large head - Predisposes to cranial injury"; "Large spleen and liver with wide costal arch - Predisposes to direct trauma to these organs"; "Small and light body - May be thrown easily, especially inside a moving vehicle."
- *Other:* "Poverty, family stress (e.g., maternal illness, recent environmental change), substandard alternative child care, young maternal age, low maternal education, multiple siblings."

### Violence (pp. 5-6)

"Strikingly higher homicide rates are found among minority populations, especially African American children." "The causes of violence against children and self-inflicted violence are not fully understood."

**The firearm risk numbers to memorize:** "The presence of a gun in a household increases the risk of suicide by about fivefold and the risk of homicide by about threefold."

## 3. The art of pediatric nursing (pp. 7-10)

### Family-centered care (p. 7)

The philosophy "recognizes the family as the constant in a child's life." "Nurses support families in their natural caregiving and decision-making roles by building on their unique strengths and acknowledging their expertise in caring for their child both within and outside the hospital setting."

**Box 1.4, key elements of family-centered care:**
- "Incorporating into policy and practice the recognition that the family is the constant in a child's life, whereas the service systems and support personnel within those systems fluctuate."
- "Facilitating family-professional collaboration at all levels of hospital, home, and community care": care of an individual child, program development and implementation and evaluation, and policy formation.
- "Exchanging complete and unbiased information between family members and professionals in a supportive manner at all times."
- Recognizing and honoring cultural diversity, strengths, and individuality across "ethnic, racial, spiritual, social, economic, educational, and geographic diversity."
- "Recognizing and respecting different methods of coping" with "developmental, educational, emotional, environmental, and financial support."
- "Encouraging and facilitating family-to-family support and networking."
- Ensuring service and support systems are "flexible, accessible, and comprehensive."
- "Appreciating families as families and children as children, recognizing that they possess a wide range of strengths, concerns, emotions, and aspirations beyond their need for specialized health and developmental services and support."

**The two basic concepts: enabling and empowerment** (see key terms). The distinction is who holds the control. Enabling is what professionals *create*: opportunities and means for families to display and acquire competencies. Empowerment is the *interaction* through which families "maintain or acquire a sense of control over their family lives."

### Atraumatic care (pp. 7-8)

The four components the definition names are **setting, personnel, interventions, and therapeutic care**, applied to preventing psychologic and physical distress. The book summarizes it as "concerned with the where, who, why, and how of any procedure performed on a child."

**The overriding goal: "First, do no harm."**

**Three principles that provide the framework:**
1. "prevent or minimize the child's separation from the family"
2. "promote a sense of control"
3. "prevent or minimize bodily injury and pain"

Examples given: "fostering the parent-child relationship during hospitalization, preparing the child before any unfamiliar treatment or procedure, controlling pain, allowing the child privacy, providing play activities for expression of fear and aggression, providing choices to children, and respecting cultural differences."

### The therapeutic relationship (p. 8)

"Pediatric nurses need to have meaningful relationships with children and their families and yet remain separate enough to distinguish their own feelings and needs."

*Nursing Care Guidelines, exploring your relationships.* The negative actions divide into two failure modes, and both are testable.

| Overinvolvement | Underinvolvement |
|---|---|
| Working overtime to care for the family; spending off-duty time with families; calling frequently to check on them; showing favoritism; buying clothes, toys, or food for the family; competing with other staff for a family's affection; other staff commenting on your closeness; "attempt to influence families' decisions rather than facilitate their informed decision making" | "restrict parent or visitor access to children, using excuses such as the unit is too busy"; focusing on technical aspects and losing "sight of the person who is the patient" |

A third pattern is named separately: being "overinvolved with children and underinvolved with their parents," shown by becoming "critical when parents do not visit their children" or competing with parents for their children's affection.

Positive actions include striving to empower families, developing "teaching skills to instruct families rather than doing everything for them," working "to find ways to decrease their dependence on health care providers," separating families' needs from your own, being able to "'step back' and withdraw emotionally, if not physically, when emotional overload occurs, yet remain committed," and recognizing when "a therapeutic relationship can change to a social relationship or an intimate friendship" and arranging for a more objective colleague to take your place.

### The nurse's roles (pp. 8-10)

- **Family advocacy and caring.** "their primary responsibility is to the consumer of nursing services: the child and family." Parents describe "personable" care as "acknowledging the parent's presence, listening, making the parent feel comfortable in the hospital environment, involving the parent and child in the nursing care, showing interest in and concern for their welfare, showing affection and sensitivity to the parent and child, communicating with them, and individualizing the nursing care."
- **Disease prevention and health promotion.** The care plan must include "a thorough assessment of all aspects of child growth and development, including nutrition, immunizations, safety, dental care, socialization, discipline, and education." **"The best approach to prevention is education and anticipatory guidance."**
- **Health teaching.** "inseparable from family advocacy and prevention"; it "involves transmitting information at the child's and family's level of understanding and desire for information."
- **Injury prevention.** "The nurse plays an important role in preventing injuries by using a developmental approach to safety counseling for parents of children of all ages."
- **Support and counseling.** "The nurse can offer support by listening, touching, and being physically present. Touching and physical presence are most helpful with children because they facilitate nonverbal communication." Counseling "involves a mutual exchange of ideas and opinions that provides the basis for mutual problem solving."
- **Coordination and collaboration.** "A nurse working in isolation rarely serves the child's best interests." "Failure to recognize limitations can be nontherapeutic at best and destructive at worst." The example given: a nurse who feels competent in counseling but is inadequate at it "may not only prevent the child from dealing with a crisis but also impede future success with a qualified professional."
- **Ethical decision making.** "Ethical dilemmas arise when competing moral considerations underlie various alternatives. Parents, nurses, physicians, and other health care team members may reach different but morally defensible decisions by assigning different weights to competing moral values." The four values are autonomy, nonmaleficence, beneficence, and justice. The nurse decides "within the framework of societal mores, professional practice standards, the law, institutional rules, the family's value system and religious traditions, and the nurse's personal values."

### Research and evidence-based practice (p. 10)

"Nurses should contribute to research because they are the individuals observing human responses to health and illness."

| Nursing process phase | EBP activity as given |
|---|---|
| Assessment and diagnosis | "the nurse establishes important clinical questions and completes a critical review of existing knowledge"; the nurse "asks clinical questions in a concise, organized way that allows for clear answers," then searches for the best information |
| Planning | "the care plan is established on completion of a critical appraisal of what is known and not known about the defined problem" |
| Implementation | "By integrating evidence with clinical expertise, the nurse focuses care on the patient's unique needs" |

## 4. Clinical reasoning and the nursing process (pp. 11-12)

**Clinical reasoning** "is based on the scientific method of inquiry, which is also the basis for the nursing process. Clinical reasoning and the nursing process are considered crucial to professional nursing in that they constitute a holistic approach to problem solving." It is "a complex developmental process based on rational and deliberate thought" that "transforms the way in which individuals view themselves, understand the world, and make decisions."

**The nursing process model as this book states it: "assessment, diagnosis outcomes identification, planning, implementation, and evaluation."**

| Step | What the source says happens |
|---|---|
| Assessment | "a continuous process that operates at all phases of problem solving and is the foundation for decision making." Consider "the patient's biophysical, psychologic, sociocultural, and spiritual background" |
| Diagnosis | "the nurse must interpret and make decisions about the data gathered." Distinguish actual from potential problems: "To differentiate actual from potential health problems, the word **risk** is included in the nursing diagnosis statement (e.g., Risk for Infection)" |
| Outcomes identification | "to establish priorities and select expected patient outcomes or goals," clustering data into categories and making one of four decisions (below) |
| Planning | "the nurse develops a care plan specific to the identified outcomes. The care plan must be established before specific nursing interventions are developed and implemented" |
| Implementation | "begins when the nurse puts the selected intervention into action and accumulates feedback data regarding its effects." Throughout, "the main concerns are the patient's physical safety and psychologic comfort in terms of atraumatic care" |
| Evaluation | "either completes the nursing process (outcome is met) or serves as the basis for selecting alternative interventions" |

**The four decisions at outcomes identification** (p. 11):
- "No dysfunctional health problems are evident; health promotion is emphasized."
- "Risk for dysfunctional health problems exists; interventions are needed for health promotion and illness prevention."
- "Actual dysfunctional health problems are evident; interventions are needed for illness management, illness prevention, and health promotion."
- "Specific outcomes are formulated to address the realistic patient- and family-focused goals."

"It is imperative that continual assessment of the patient's status occurs throughout all phases of the nursing process, thus making the process a **dynamic rather than static** problem-solving method."

**Documentation** (p. 12): "Although documentation is not one of the steps of the nursing process, it is essential for evaluation. The nurse can assess, diagnose, and identify problems; plan; and implement without documentation. However, evaluation is best performed with written evidence of progress toward outcomes."

*Nursing Care Guidelines, documentation of nursing care:* "Initial assessments and reassessments; Nursing problem and/or patient care needs; Interventions identified to meet the patient's nursing care needs; Nursing care provided; Patient's response to, and the outcomes of, the care provided; Abilities of patient and/or, as appropriate, significant other(s) to manage continuing care needs after discharge."

## 6. Trap notes

Written cue-first against the distractor tactics in the rulebook.

### Confusable pairs

- **Family-centered care vs. atraumatic care.** *Family is the constant, partnership, enabling and empowerment, collaboration at all levels* to family-centered care. *Eliminate or minimize psychologic and physical distress, first do no harm, setting-personnel-interventions* to atraumatic care. Distractors swap the goal statements between them.
- **Enabling vs. empowerment.** *Professionals create opportunities and means for families to display and acquire competencies* is enabling. *The interaction through which families maintain or acquire a sense of control* is empowerment. The axis is who is acting: enabling is what the professional does, empowerment is what the family holds.
- **Overweight vs. obesity.** 85th percentile up to but not including the 95th is overweight; at or above the 95th is obesity. A distractor placing obesity at the 85th percentile is the classic error.
- **Neonatal vs. postneonatal mortality.** Under 28 days is neonatal; 28 days to 11 months is postneonatal. Both are subdivisions of the infant mortality rate, which is per 1000 live births, not per 100,000.
- **Infant mortality rate denominators.** The infant mortality *rate* is per 1000 live births (5.82 in 2014); Table 1.2's cause-specific rates are per 100,000 live births. Table 1.1 and Table 1.3 are per 100,000 population. An option that mixes the denominators is wrong even with the right number.
- **Overinvolvement vs. underinvolvement.** *Visiting on days off, buying gifts, calling from home, favoritism, steering the family's decisions* is overinvolvement. *Restricting parent access, focusing only on technical care* is underinvolvement. Both are nontherapeutic; a distractor that treats only overinvolvement as a problem misses half the concept.
- **Nonmaleficence vs. beneficence.** *Minimize or prevent harm* is nonmaleficence; *promote the patient's well-being* is beneficence. Autonomy is self-governance, justice is fairness.
- **Clinical reasoning vs. the nursing process.** Clinical reasoning is the cognitive process; the nursing process is the method that describes what the nurse does. Both rest on the scientific method of inquiry, so a distractor calling one a substitute for the other misstates the relationship.
- **Actual vs. potential (risk) health problem.** The word "risk" in the diagnosis statement is the marker of a potential problem. Nursing interventions for a risk state "are directed toward reducing risk factors," not toward treating an existing problem.
- **Signs and symptoms vs. risk factors.** Signs and symptoms are "a cluster of cues and defining characteristics... [that] indicate actual health problems." Risk factors "predispose" and belong to potential problems.
- **PNP vs. CNS.** PNPs "are able to diagnose illnesses and prescribe medication." CNSs are "master's-prepared nurses who function in a variety of settings in both the direct and indirect role."

### Level, stage, and ranking swaps

- **Leading cause of death is age-banded and the bands matter.** Under 1 year, mechanical suffocation ranks first among unintentional injuries. Ages 1 to 4, drowning ranks first for males and motor vehicle for females. Ages 5 through 24, motor vehicle ranks first for both sexes. Injuries overall rank first as a cause of death in every group from 1 to 19 years.
- **"Lowest death rate" belongs to ages 5 to 14 years,** not to infancy. Infancy has the highest rate.
- **Suicide enters the picture at 10 years.** It is rank 2 at 10 to 14 and 15 to 19 in Table 1.3.
- **Injury cause maps to cognitive stage, not to chronological age alone.** Aspiration goes with sensorimotor exploration by mouth in the crawling infant; falls, burns, and collisions with the mobile toddler; dares and risk-taking with the adolescent's "feeling of invulnerability." A distractor pairing "preschooler riding a two-wheeled bike recklessly" with a developmental stage is the book's own wrong answer in its review question 1.
- **No screen time under 18 months** except video calling; use technology sparingly before 5 years; always participate during viewing.
- **Lifelong eating habits are established during the first 3 years**, not by 18 months. The book's own review question 5 uses "by 18 months" as a distractor.
- **The five-step nursing process is stated by this book as assessment, diagnosis outcomes identification, planning, implementation, evaluation.** Documentation is deliberately *not* one of the steps.

### Plausible-but-adjacent facts

- **Atraumatic care is not only about pain.** Its three principles include preventing separation from the family and promoting a sense of control, and physical distress in the definition includes "sleeplessness and immobilization to disturbances from sensory stimuli, such as pain, temperature extremes, loud noises, bright lights, or darkness."
- **The overriding goal of atraumatic care is "First, do no harm."** The three principles are the framework for reaching that goal, not the goal itself. This is exactly the discrimination in the book's own review question 4.
- **Repeated exposure to violence does not desensitize children protectively.** "multiple exposures to episodes of violence do not inoculate children against the negative effects."
- **A gun in the home raises suicide risk about fivefold and homicide risk about threefold.** Do not swap the two multipliers.
- **The chief illness of childhood is the common cold**, and respiratory illness is about 50% of all acute conditions.
- **Children who have had a particular problem are more likely to have it again.** Morbidity "is not distributed randomly in children."
- **A positive family-centered care action is teaching families rather than doing for them.** An option describing the nurse taking over care to spare the family is a distractor even though it sounds kind.
- **Documentation supports evaluation.** An option that says the nurse cannot assess or implement without documentation overstates it; the book says evaluation is what suffers.

### Priority and first-action items

- **Prevention priority is education and anticipatory guidance.** "The best approach to prevention is education and anticipatory guidance."
- **When a nurse's competence is exceeded, collaborate rather than proceed.** Failure to recognize limitations is "nontherapeutic at best and destructive at worst."
- **When a therapeutic relationship becomes social or intimate, the action is to acknowledge it and ensure a more objective colleague can take your place**, not to end contact abruptly or continue as before.
