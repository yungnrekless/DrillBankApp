# Chapter 7 drills — Theory and Research Frameworks

Authored from `sources/Ch7_Fact_Bank.md` (Grove & Gray, 7th ed.).
Topics follow the chapter's own structure: the parts of a theory, the
abstraction ladder from construct to variable, the proposition-to-hypothesis
hierarchy, the three levels of theory, the four grand nursing theorists, and
the explicit-versus-implicit framework question the appraisal assignment turns
on. Every inversion the fact bank flags as a standard swap is drilled from both
directions.

---

Topic: theory components
Subtopic: concepts and statements

Q: A theorist writes that adherence to medications improves clinical outcomes and reduces the development of complications, patient suffering, and healthcare costs. In this sentence, what is patient suffering?
A) A statement
B) A philosophy
C) An assumption
D) A concept
Answer: D
Rationale: Concepts abstractly describe and name an object, idea, experience, or phenomenon, giving it a separate identity or meaning, and they are the basic element of a theory. Adherence to medications, clinical outcomes, complications, patient suffering, and healthcare costs are all concepts here.
Trap: The sentence as a whole is the statement, because it expresses the relationships among the concepts. A single named idea inside it is a concept, not a statement.

Q: Which characteristics describe concepts as the chapter defines them? Select all that apply.
A) They abstractly describe and name an object, idea, experience, or phenomenon
B) They are narrower and more measurable than variables
C) They provide what they name with a separate identity or meaning
D) They are the basic element of a theory
E) Each one must be defined by the theorist
Answer: A, C, D, E
Rationale: Concepts are the basic element of a theory, they name and describe abstractly, they give what they name a separate identity or meaning, and each must be defined by the theorist. Theories with clearly identified and defined concepts provide a stronger basis for a research framework.
Trap: (B) inverts the abstraction ladder. A variable is more specific than a concept, not the reverse.

Subtopic: philosophies and assumptions

Q: A theorist opens a paper with the claim that people want to assume control of their own health problems and offers no supporting evidence for it. How should a reader classify that claim?
A) An assumption
B) A hypothesis to be tested in the study
C) A finding carried over from prior research
D) A specific proposition
Answer: A
Rationale: Assumptions are statements taken for granted or considered true even though they have not been scientifically tested, and this is the chapter's own example. You may doubt the truth of it, but theorists begin with assumptions, explicit or implicit.
Trap: Assumptions are never what the study tests. Calling one a hypothesis, or treating it as a finding, is the standard error on this term.

Q: Which term names rational intellectual explorations of truths or principles of being, knowledge, or conduct?
A) Assumption
B) Theory
C) Philosophy
D) Research framework
Answer: C
Rationale: Philosophies are rational intellectual explorations of truths or principles of being, knowledge, or conduct, and they describe viewpoints on what reality is, how knowledge is developed, and which ethical values and principles should guide practice.

Subtopic: metaparadigm concepts

Q: Which of the following are among nursing's metaparadigm concepts? Select all that apply.
A) Environment
B) Caring
C) Health
D) Adaptation
E) Person
Answer: A, C, E
Rationale: The metaparadigm concepts are person, environment, health, and nursing, described as the essence of nursing. Three of them appear here; nursing is the fourth.
Trap: Caring is Swanson's middle range theory and adaptation is the core concept of Roy's grand theory. Neither is a metaparadigm concept. There are four, and only these four.

Topic: levels of abstraction
Subtopic: construct, concept, and variable

Q: A research team diagrams its framework with resources at the top, social support and household income beneath it, and emotional support beneath those. What is resources in this structure?
A) A concept
B) A construct
C) A variable
D) An operational definition
Answer: B
Rationale: A construct is a broader category or idea that may encompass several concepts. In the chapter's example the construct resources encompasses the concepts social support and household income.
Trap: Reversing construct and concept is the standard swap in this chapter. The construct is the broader of the two, and the concept sits inside it.

Q: In that same framework, emotional support is scored by summing a three-item subscale of the Functional Social Support Questionnaire. What is emotional support?
A) The construct
B) The concept
C) The phenomenon
D) The variable
Answer: D
Rationale: A variable is defined so that it is measurable and its numerical values can vary from one instance to another, and it is more specific than a concept. Emotional support is the variable; the FSSQ subscale is the measurement method.
Trap: This is the mirror image of the construct item. Sort the three by breadth, not by which term appears in the sentence: construct is broadest, concept sits under it, variable is the narrow measurable one.

Q: A nurse discusses the experience of chronic pain in general terms, apart from any specific patient. This way of thinking is best described as which of the following?
A) Concrete
B) Denotative
C) Abstract
D) Operational
Answer: C
Rationale: Abstract means the expression of an idea apart from any specific instance and focuses on a general view. Concrete refers to realities or actual instances and focuses on the particular.
Trap: Concrete is not a criticism and abstract is not a compliment. They describe level of generality, not quality of thinking.

Subtopic: conceptual and operational definitions

Q: A researcher defines home as including feelings of security, love, and comfort. A dictionary defines home as a dwelling in which a group of people live, who may or may not be related. Which statement is accurate?
A) The researcher's definition is denotative and therefore more precise
B) The researcher's definition is connotative and more comprehensive
C) The dictionary definition is the conceptual definition
D) The two definitions are interchangeable in a research report
Answer: B
Rationale: Conceptual definitions are connotative and are more comprehensive than a denotative dictionary definition, because they include associated meanings.
Trap: A conceptual definition is more comprehensive, not more precise. The narrower and more specific one is the dictionary definition.

Q: What is the correct relationship between the conceptual and operational definitions of a study variable?
A) They are developed independently of one another
B) The operational definition comes from the theory and provides the basis for the conceptual definition
C) The conceptual definition comes from the theory and provides the basis for the operational definition
D) The conceptual definition is derived from the scoring rules of the measurement instrument
Answer: C
Rationale: The conceptual definition comes from a theory and provides the basis for the operational definition. The operational definition then states how the concept is manipulated or measured.
Trap: The dependency runs one way only. (B) is the reflex answer for students who remember that the operational definition is what appears in the methods section and assume the methods drive the theory.

Q: A study's intervention is described as a 6-week mindfulness program delivered in weekly 60-minute groups. This description functions as which of the following?
A) The conceptual definition of mindfulness
B) The operational definition of the independent variable
C) A proposition of the framework
D) An assumption of the study
Answer: B
Rationale: An operational definition states how a concept is manipulated, for an intervention or independent variable, or measured, for a dependent or outcome variable.
Trap: The conceptual definition of mindfulness would come from the theory and describe what mindfulness means, not how many minutes the sessions ran.

Q: Which statements about conceptual definitions are accurate? Select all that apply.
A) They are described as connotative
B) They state the specific method for assigning numerical values to the variable
C) They are more comprehensive than a denotative dictionary definition
D) They are drawn from a theory
E) They may be explicit or implicit in a published study
Answer: A, C, D, E
Rationale: Conceptual definitions are connotative, more comprehensive than a dictionary definition, drawn from theory, and may be explicit or implicit. The chapter warns that identifying the researcher's conceptual definitions is part of critically appraising a study.
Trap: (B) describes measurement, the specific method for assigning numerical values, which belongs to the operational definition rather than the conceptual one.

Q: A student is appraising a quantitative study and wants to trace each study variable back through the framework. Which elements should ideally be identifiable for each variable? Select all that apply.
A) The concept the variable represents
B) A dictionary definition of the term
C) The conceptual definition of that concept
D) The operational definition
E) An untested assumption that supports the variable
Answer: A, C, D
Rationale: Each variable is ideally associated with a concept, a conceptual definition, and an operational definition, with the conceptual definition drawn from the theory.
Trap: (B) is denotative and is exactly what a conceptual definition is not. (E) confuses assumptions, which are global and untested, with the definitional chain attached to a variable.

Topic: propositions and hypotheses
Subtopic: levels of propositions

Q: A grand theory states that health-related quality of life is related to depression. A framework built on it states that among persons on antiretroviral therapy for HIV infection, domains of health-related quality of life are related to depressive symptoms. The second statement is best labeled as which of the following?
A) A general proposition
B) A hypothesis
C) An assumption
D) A specific proposition
Answer: D
Rationale: General propositions are the relational statements found in more abstract theories, while a specific proposition states the relationship in a more narrow way, making it more concrete and testable. Specific propositions live in less abstract frameworks such as middle range theories.
Trap: It reads like a hypothesis because it names a population, but the chapter's hypothesis narrows further still, to African American persons and to named domains and symptom parameters.

Q: Which statement is written at the lowest level of abstraction and is the one actually tested in the study?
A) Health-related quality of life is related to depression
B) Among persons on antiretroviral therapy for HIV infection, domains of health-related quality of life are related to depressive symptoms
C) Among African American persons on antiretroviral therapy for HIV infection, poor physical functioning and increased role limitations are related to increased frequency and severity of depressive symptoms
D) People want to assume control of their own health problems
Answer: C
Rationale: Hypotheses are written at the lowest level of abstraction to be tested in a study, and they are developed from propositions in a grand or middle range theory.
Trap: (D) is the chapter's assumption example. Assumptions are taken as true without testing and are never the study's tested claim.

Q: Which statements about propositions and hypotheses are accurate? Select all that apply.
A) A proposition is a label given to statements describing relationships among concepts
B) Hypotheses are more abstract than general propositions
C) Specific propositions in middle range theories may lead to hypotheses
D) Hypotheses are developed based on propositions from a grand or middle range theory
E) A specific proposition is more concrete and testable than a general proposition
Answer: A, C, D, E
Rationale: The order runs general proposition, then specific proposition, then hypothesis, in decreasing abstraction, and hypotheses are derived from propositions.
Trap: (B) inverts the hierarchy. Any option that places the hypothesis above the proposition in abstraction is wrong.

Subtopic: testing a theory

Q: A single study finds support for one relational statement in a middle range theory. What conclusion is warranted?
A) One statement has been supported, and many studies are required to validate all the statements in a theory
B) The theory has been proven
C) The theory has been validated for that population
D) The theory should be reclassified as a grand theory
Answer: A
Rationale: Testing a theory involves determining the truth of each statement in it, and many studies are required to validate all the statements in a theory.
Trap: One supporting study never proves or validates a theory, no matter how well the study was conducted. The claim size has to match the number of statements tested.

Q: When research tests a theory, what is it about the theory that is being tested?
A) Its concepts
B) Its assumptions
C) Its philosophy
D) Its statements of relationships
Answer: D
Rationale: The statements of relationships are what are tested through research.
Trap: Concepts are defined rather than tested, and assumptions are taken for granted precisely because they have not been scientifically tested.

Topic: levels of theory
Subtopic: grand, middle range, and practice theories

Q: A theory addresses nursing actions and patient responses across multiple settings and sits at the most abstract level of theoretical thinking. Which level is this?
A) Grand nursing theory
B) Middle range theory
C) Practice theory
D) Scientific theory
Answer: A
Rationale: Grand nursing theories are the most abstract and encompass nursing actions and patient responses in multiple settings.
Trap: Early scholars called these conceptual models or conceptual frameworks. Recognizing that older label does not make them a different or less abstract level.

Q: A researcher wants a framework with clearly identified concepts, definitions of those concepts, and relational statements, and notes that this level is the one most commonly applied as a framework in nursing studies. Which level fits?
A) Middle range theory
B) Grand nursing theory
C) Practice theory
D) Philosophy
Answer: A
Rationale: Middle range theories are less abstract and narrower in scope, are more closely linked to clinical practice and research, and are more commonly applied as frameworks in nursing studies because they have clearly identified concepts, definitions of concepts, and relational statements.
Trap: Grand theories are the better known names, so they are the reflex answer. Fame is not the criterion; usable concepts, definitions, and relational statements are.

Q: Why are middle range theories also called substantive theories?
A) They have been substantiated by more research than grand theories
B) They substitute for a grand theory when none exists for the topic
C) They contain a larger number of concepts than grand theories do
D) They are closer to the substance of clinical practice
Answer: D
Rationale: Middle range theories are called substantive theories because they are closer to the substance of clinical practice.
Trap: Substantive sounds like substantiated, which is where (A) comes from, but the word points at clinical substance, not at accumulated evidence.

Q: Which description of practice theories is accurate?
A) They are a type of middle range theory designed to propose specific approaches to particular nursing practice situations
B) They are a fourth, independent level that is more abstract than middle range theories
C) They are another name for grand nursing theories
D) They are theories derived from physiology, genetics, and physics
Answer: A
Rationale: Practice theories, also called situation-specific theories, are a type of middle range theory that are more specific and are designed to propose specific approaches to particular nursing practice situations.
Trap: Practice theories are a subtype of middle range, not a separate fourth level, and they are the most specific rather than the most abstract. (D) describes scientific theory.

Subtopic: origins of middle range theories

Q: From which sources may a middle range theory emerge? Select all that apply.
A) A grounded theory study
B) The four metaparadigm concepts
C) Deduction from a grand nursing theory
D) A synthesis of the literature on a particular topic
E) A dictionary definition of the central concept
Answer: A, C, D
Rationale: Middle range theories may emerge from a grounded theory study, be deduced from a grand nursing theory, or be created through a synthesis of the literature on a particular topic.
Trap: An option requiring that middle range theories always derive from a grand theory is too narrow. Deduction is one of three routes, not the only one.

Q: Which of these are named in the chapter as middle range theories with ongoing use? Select all that apply.
A) Uncertainty in illness (Mishel)
B) Self-Care Deficit Theory of Nursing (Orem)
C) Comfort (Kolcaba)
D) Transitions (Meleis)
E) Unpleasant symptoms (Lenz et al.)
Answer: A, C, D, E
Rationale: The chapter's list of middle range theories in ongoing use includes caring, comfort, inner strength, nursing intellectual capital, self-transcendence, transitions, uncertainty in illness, unpleasant symptoms, and women's anger.
Trap: Orem's Self-Care Deficit Theory of Nursing is one of the four grand nursing theories, not a middle range theory. It is the theorist name most likely to be pulled into the middle range list.

Topic: grand nursing theories
Subtopic: matching theorist to core concept

Q: A nurse assesses a patient's responses to focal, contextual, and residual stimuli and plans care aimed at returning the patient to homeostasis and promoting growth. Which grand theory is guiding this nurse?
A) Orem's Self-Care Deficit Theory of Nursing
B) Rogers' Science of Unitary Human Beings
C) Roy's Adaptation Model
D) King's Theory of Goal Attainment
Answer: C
Rationale: In Roy and Andrews' Adaptation Model, people adapt to focal, contextual, and residual stimuli using processes and systems that are partly automatic and partly learned, with the goal of returning to homeostasis and promoting growth.
Trap: The three kinds of stimuli are the giveaway and belong to Roy alone. No other grand theory in the chapter uses focal, contextual, and residual.

Q: A nurse evaluates how a patient's developmental stage, current disease, and available resources have reduced her ability to care for herself, and then provides care in proportion to her remaining capacity. Which theory is guiding this nurse?
A) Orem's Self-Care Deficit Theory of Nursing
B) Roy's Adaptation Model
C) King's Theory of Goal Attainment
D) Rogers' Science of Unitary Human Beings
Answer: A
Rationale: In Orem's theory the ability to self-care is affected by developmental stage, presence of disease, and available resources and may result in a self-care deficit, and nursing provides care in proportion to the person's self-care capacity.
Trap: Developmental stage and resources also sound like contextual factors, which pulls toward Roy. The deciding feature is that the nursing dose is scaled to the patient's self-care capacity.

Q: A nurse describes the patient and the environment as energy fields interacting as open systems and looks for the patterns those fields produce. Which theory is this?
A) Roy's Adaptation Model
B) Orem's Self-Care Deficit Theory of Nursing
C) King's Theory of Goal Attainment
D) Rogers' Science of Unitary Human Beings
Answer: D
Rationale: Rogers' Science of Unitary Human Beings holds that persons and environment are energy fields interacting as open systems, and that the fields produce patterns usable for identification.
Trap: Open systems appear in King's work as well, which is where the confusion starts. Energy fields and patterns are what mark the answer as Rogers.

Q: A nurse and a patient sit down together, agree on goals for discharge, and the nurse then educates, supports, and guides the patient toward them. Which theory is this?
A) Roy's Adaptation Model
B) King's Theory of Goal Attainment
C) Orem's Self-Care Deficit Theory of Nursing
D) Rogers' Science of Unitary Human Beings
Answer: B
Rationale: In King's Theory of Goal Attainment, persons within systems are goal-oriented, the nurse and patient set mutually agreed-upon goals, and through interaction the nurse educates, supports, and guides.
Trap: Orem is the near-neighbor any time the nurse's role is described as supportive. The deciding feature here is that the goals are set mutually by nurse and patient, not scaled to a self-care deficit.

Subtopic: programs of research around a grand theory

Q: Which statements about building nursing knowledge around a grand theory are accurate? Select all that apply.
A) It requires an organized program of research and a group of scholars
B) The Roy Adaptation Association analyzes, critiques, and synthesizes the English-language studies based on the model
C) The International Orem Society publishes the journal Nursing Science Quarterly
D) The Roy Adaptation Model has been in use for over 25 years
E) The Society of Rogerian Scholars publishes the journal Visions
Answer: A, B, D, E
Rationale: Building knowledge around a grand theory requires an organized program of research and a group of scholars, and the chapter names the Roy Adaptation Association, the Society of Rogerian Scholars with its journal Visions, and the International Orem Society.
Trap: The International Orem Society's journal is Self-Care, Dependent-Care, and Nursing. Nursing Science Quarterly is not the journal the chapter attaches to any of these societies.

Topic: research frameworks
Subtopic: what a framework does

Q: Which statements accurately describe a research framework? Select all that apply.
A) It is an abstract and logical structure of meaning
B) It may be a portion of a theory
C) It is developed after data analysis to explain unexpected results
D) It guides the development of the study
E) It enables the researcher to link the findings to nursing's body of knowledge
Answer: A, B, D, E
Rationale: A research framework is an abstract and logical structure of meaning, such as a portion of a theory, which guides the development of the study and enables the researcher to link the findings to nursing's body of knowledge.
Trap: (C) reverses the timing. A framework is in place to guide the study, not assembled afterward to account for results.

Q: How does the chapter characterize a clearly expressed framework in a quantitative study?
A) One indication of a well-developed quantitative study
B) A requirement for institutional review board approval
C) Evidence that the underlying theory has been validated
D) A feature unique to physiological studies
Answer: A
Rationale: A clearly expressed framework is one indication of a well-developed quantitative study.
Trap: A clear framework says something about the quality of the study's development. It says nothing about whether the theory itself has been validated, which would take many studies.

Subtopic: explicit versus implicit frameworks

Q: A student appraising a quantitative article cannot find any section naming a theory or framework. Which conclusion is correct?
A) The study has no framework
B) The study must actually be qualitative
C) The framework must be a scientific theory drawn from physiology
D) The framework is implicit, because every quantitative study has an implicit or explicit framework
Answer: D
Rationale: Every quantitative study has an implicit or explicit framework, whether the focus is physiological, psychological, social, or cultural. The appraisal question is not whether a framework exists but whether it is explicit and how well developed it is.
Trap: (A) is the reflex answer and it is wrong by the chapter's own rule. There is no such thing as a quantitative study with no framework.

Q: A study's introduction notes that earlier work linked the variables but never explains why they should be related, and no framework section follows. What does the chapter recommend the reader do?
A) Reject the study as unappraisable
B) Extract the implicit framework from the text and, if it helps, draw a model of how the variables are linked
C) Substitute a grand nursing theory of the reader's own choosing
D) Treat the literature review as the study's operational definitions
Answer: B
Rationale: In most cases a careful reader can extract an implicit framework from the text, and when researchers do not clearly describe the framework you may want to draw a model, which helps you visualize the framework and how the variables are linked.
Trap: This is the classic implicit-framework signature: the linkage appears in the introduction or literature review and then the researcher stops without developing it into a framework.

Q: What is the consequence of a researcher leaving the framework implicit?
A) It invalidates the study's statistical results
B) It converts the study into a qualitative design
C) It provides limited guidance for the development and conduct of the study and limits the contribution of the findings to nursing knowledge
D) It means the study's hypotheses cannot be tested
Answer: C
Rationale: Implicit frameworks provide limited guidance for the development and conduct of a study and limit the contribution of the study findings to nursing knowledge.
Trap: An implicit framework weakens guidance and contribution. It does not make the statistics invalid or change the design, so options that escalate to invalidation overshoot.

Subtopic: models and maps

Q: In a diagram of a study's framework, an arrow drawn from one concept to another is a graphic illustration of what?
A) A statistical correlation found in the study's data
B) A relational statement, or proposition, of the theory
C) A causal pathway already proven by prior research
D) An operational definition of the concept
Answer: B
Rationale: A model is a diagram with the concepts and relationships graphically displayed, it includes all the major concepts in a research framework, and each linkage shown by an arrow is a graphic illustration of a relational statement, or proposition, of the theory.
Trap: An arrow is a claim from the theory, not a finding from the data. Reading arrows as correlations or as proven causation is the standard misread.

Subtopic: sources of frameworks

Q: Which of the following are identified as sources of research frameworks? Select all that apply.
A) Grand nursing theories
B) Middle range theories from nursing and from other professions
C) The study's data analysis plan
D) Syntheses of concepts and relationships from more than one theory
E) Syntheses of research findings
Answer: A, B, D, E
Rationale: The four sources are grand nursing theories, middle range theories from nursing and other professions, syntheses of concepts and relationships from more than one theory, and syntheses of research findings.
Trap: Middle range theories borrowed from other professions count. Restricting frameworks to nursing-only theories drops a legitimate source.

Q: A researcher combines concepts from two different theories with findings from several published studies to build a framework for one particular study. What is this framework called?
A) A grand theory
B) A scientific theory
C) A tentative theory
D) A practice theory
Answer: C
Rationale: A tentative theory is a newly proposed framework, often a synthesis of concepts from more than one theory or of research findings, and it is usually developed for a particular study.
Trap: Synthesizing across theories does not produce a grand theory. Tentative theories are built for one study rather than for a program of research across settings.

Q: A study of a cooling protocol's effect on cerebral perfusion draws its framework from pathophysiology and physics. This framework is best described as which of the following?
A) A grand nursing theory
B) A scientific theory
C) A tentative theory
D) A practice theory
Answer: B
Rationale: Frameworks for physiological studies come from physiology, genetics, pathophysiology, and physics, and are scientific theories, which have extensive research evidence to support their claims, concepts clearly linked to study variables, and valid and reliable methods for measuring each concept.
Trap: A framework that is not a nursing theory is still a framework. Physiological studies are exactly the case students use to argue, wrongly, that a study has no framework.
