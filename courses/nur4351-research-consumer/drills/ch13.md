# Chapter 13 drills — Building an Evidence-Based Practice

Authored from `sources/Ch13_Fact_Bank.md` (Grove & Gray, 7th ed.).
This is the chapter the exam's EBP line is named after, so the weight sits on
the two named models (Stetler and Iowa), the PICO structure, and the strength
of evidence hierarchy. The IM-injection guideline is drilled as clinical
content, not just as an example, because the aspiration recommendation has
reversed and the chapter uses that reversal to make its central point.

---

Topic: evidence-based practice foundations
Subtopic: definition and benefits

Q: A nurse manager states that the unit's new fall-prevention protocol is evidence-based because it was drawn entirely from the most recent meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Which response best reflects the chapter's definition of evidence-based practice?
A) Evidence-based practice requires that only findings from meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials be used
B) Evidence-based practice requires that expert clinical opinion take precedence over published research findings
C) Evidence-based practice requires integrating the best research evidence with clinical expertise, patient circumstances, and patient values
D) Evidence-based practice requires that each unit generate its own research before changing a protocol
Answer: C
Rationale: EBP is defined as a complex phenomenon that requires integration of the best research evidence with clinical expertise, patient circumstances, and patient values in the delivery of quality, safe, cost-effective care.
Trap: (A) sounds rigorous and does name the strongest evidence source, but research evidence is only one of the four elements. A protocol built from evidence alone, with no attention to clinical expertise, patient circumstances, or patient values, is not EBP.

Q: A chief nursing officer is building the case for investing in an evidence-based practice program. Which statements about the benefits and institutional drivers of EBP are accurate? Select all that apply.
A) The greatest benefits of EBP are improved outcomes for patients, providers, and healthcare agencies
B) Magnet status is approved through the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality
C) EBP improves nurses' satisfaction and facilitates achievement of accreditation requirements
D) The Joint Commission revised its accreditation criteria to emphasize patient care outcomes achieved through EBP
E) Agencies seeking or maintaining Magnet status must document research-related outcomes
Answer: A, C, D, E
Rationale: The greatest benefits of EBP are improved outcomes for patients, providers, and healthcare agencies; leaders recognize that EBP promotes quality outcomes, improves nurses' satisfaction, and facilitates achievement of accreditation requirements. Magnet status is approved through the American Nurses Credentialing Center.
Trap: AHRQ is real in this chapter and is easy to reach for, but its role here is funding the evidence-based practice centers, not conferring Magnet status. Magnet runs through the ANCC.

Subtopic: challenges to implementing EBP

Q: A staff nurse objects that evidence-based guidelines cannot account for the patient in front of her. Which statement identifies the challenge she is naming along with the chapter's recommended response?
A) Nursing has few systematic reviews compared with medicine and psychology, so the response is to require randomized controlled trials before any guideline is adopted
B) Research evidence is generated from population data and applied to individual patients, so poor outcomes under a guideline should be reported and, if possible, published as a case study
C) Guidelines should be applied uniformly to all patients regardless of circumstances or values, so individual variation is not a concern
D) Research evidence is generated from individual case studies and applied to populations, so poor outcomes should be managed by discontinuing the guideline on the unit
Answer: B
Rationale: Evidence is generated based on population data and then applied in practice to individual patients, who respond in unique ways or have unique circumstances and values. Patients with poor outcomes under a guideline should be reported and, if possible, published as a case study, and the NIH supports translational research to improve use of evidence across patient populations and settings.
Trap: (C) is the reflex answer for anyone who reads evidence-based as protocol-driven, but uniform application regardless of patient circumstances contradicts both this challenge and the definition of EBP. (A) names a genuine challenge from the chapter and attaches the wrong response to it.

Topic: strength of research evidence
Subtopic: ranking sources of evidence

Q: A committee is grading the evidence it has gathered on a wound-care intervention. Which source provides the strongest evidence?
A) A single randomized controlled trial with a large sample
B) A meta-synthesis of qualitative studies of patients' wound-care experiences
C) A mixed-methods systematic review of intervention and descriptive studies
D) A systematic review that includes a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
Answer: D
Rationale: The strongest evidence comes from systematic reviews that include meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials.
Trap: (A) is tempting because the RCT is the strongest individual design, so students pick the design rather than the synthesis. A synthesis of RCTs outranks any single trial.

Q: Which statements about sources of evidence for changing practice are consistent with the chapter? Select all that apply.
A) The strongest evidence comes from systematic reviews that include meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials
B) Meta-syntheses provide important evidence for changing practice
C) Only meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials may be used to develop research-based protocols
D) Mixed-methods systematic reviews provide important evidence for changing practice
E) Individual studies also provide important evidence for changing practice
Answer: A, B, D, E
Rationale: The chapter names systematic reviews containing meta-analyses of RCTs as the strongest evidence but explicitly states that meta-syntheses, mixed-methods systematic reviews, and individual studies also provide important evidence for changing practice.
Trap: (C) overstates the text. Ranking meta-analysis highest is not the same as excluding everything else, and the chapter goes further: research evidence can be combined with other sources of knowledge such as theories, scientific principles, expert opinion, and case reports to build research-based protocols. Expert opinion is weakest on its own but legitimate as a supplement.

Topic: PICO clinical questions
Subtopic: PICO elements

Q: In the chapter's PICO question on IM injection technique, the statement "IM injection given with 5 to 10 seconds of aspiration in all sites, regardless of age and volume" occupies which position?
A) P, the population
B) I, the intervention
C) C, the comparison intervention
D) O, the outcome
Answer: C
Rationale: The comparison intervention is the practice being measured against, which here is aspirating 5 to 10 seconds in all sites regardless of age and volume. The intervention is IM injection given without aspiration in the right site based on volume of medication and age.
Trap: Aspirating feels like the thing being done, so students slot it into I. In this question the comparison is the older outgoing practice and the intervention is the newer no-aspiration technique. The outcome is IM injection of vaccine without complications.

Subtopic: IM injection aspiration guideline

Q: A nurse is administering a vaccine into an adult patient's deltoid. Based on the current evidence in the chapter, what should the nurse do about aspiration?
A) Aspirate for 5 to 10 seconds before injecting
B) Do not aspirate, because aspiration is recommended only for the dorsogluteal site
C) Aspirate for 5 to 10 seconds only if the volume exceeds 2 mL
D) Do not aspirate, because aspiration is no longer recommended at any injection site
Answer: B
Rationale: Current evidence supports no aspiration in the deltoid, ventrogluteal, and vastus lateralis sites, and aspiration only in the dorsogluteal site because of the close proximity of the gluteal artery.
Trap: (D) overcorrects. The dorsogluteal exception survives, though researchers recommend that the dorsogluteal site not be used if possible. (A) is the older guideline that this chapter uses as its example of practice lagging behind evidence.

Q: Which statements about IM injection site selection are consistent with the chapter's clinical practice guideline? Select all that apply.
A) The vastus lateralis is the preferred site for infants
B) The ventrogluteal or deltoid site is appropriate for adults
C) The dorsogluteal site is preferred for adults because it tolerates large volumes
D) Aspiration for 5 to 10 seconds is required in the vastus lateralis in infants
E) Large volumes of 2 to 5 mL should be given in the ventrogluteal site in adults
Answer: A, B, E
Rationale: Infants receive injections in the vastus lateralis; toddlers and children in the vastus lateralis or deltoid; adults in the ventrogluteal or deltoid. Large volumes of 2 to 5 mL go in the ventrogluteal site for adults, and small volumes of 2 mL or less go in the deltoid for toddlers, children, and adults.
Trap: (C) is the specific practice error the chapter documents nurses still making. Nurses over-used the dorsogluteal site despite evidence favoring the ventrogluteal. (D) applies the outdated aspiration rule to a site where current evidence says not to aspirate.

Q: A study by Thomas and colleagues reported what finding about aspiration practice, and what point does the chapter use it to make?
A) 90% of nurses were still aspirating after IM injections 74% of the time, showing that the guideline itself was flawed
B) 74% of nurses had adopted the no-aspiration guideline within one year, showing that dissemination in nursing is efficient
C) 74% of nurses reported complications from IM injections, showing the need for a new comparison intervention
D) 74% of nurses were still aspirating after IM injections 90% of the time, showing that evidence existing is not the same as evidence being used
Answer: D
Rationale: 74% of nurses were still aspirating after IM injections 90% of the time, and researchers therefore recommended additional education in nursing programs and continuing education. This is the chapter's central point about the gap between available evidence and used evidence.
Trap: (A) inverts the two percentages. 74% is the proportion of nurses; 90% is how often those nurses aspirated.

Topic: Stetler model of research utilization
Subtopic: the five phases

Q: A nurse using the Stetler Model critically appraises the retrieved reports and finds only three small, methodologically weak studies. According to the model, what happens next?
A) The nurse proceeds to comparative evaluation and weighs feasibility despite the weak evidence
B) The nurse combines the weak studies into a meta-analysis to strengthen them
C) The findings are considered inadequate for use in practice and the process stops
D) The nurse proceeds to translation and applies the findings on a trial basis
Answer: C
Rationale: In Phase II, Validation, the reports are critically appraised for scientific soundness. If the studies are limited in number, weak, or both, the findings are considered inadequate for use in practice and the process stops.
Trap: (A) reflects the reflex that a model is a checklist you always run to the end. Stetler builds in a stop. (B) borrows a true statement from Phase II, that a systematic review, meta-analysis, or meta-synthesis greatly strengthens the evidence, and misapplies it as a way around a failed appraisal.

Q: After reading a synthesis on delirium, a nurse changes no order and no procedure, but now understands why her older postoperative patients become confused and analyzes her unit's practice dynamics differently. Under the Stetler Model, this is which kind of application?
A) Cognitive
B) Instrumental
C) Symbolic
D) Comparative
Answer: A
Rationale: Phase IV, Translation/Application, describes cognitive, instrumental, and symbolic application. Cognitive application means the research base is a means of modifying a way of thinking or one's appreciation of an issue, such as improving understanding of a situation or allowing analysis of practice dynamics.
Trap: (D) is not a kind of application at all. Comparative evaluation is the name of Phase III, and reusing that word as an application type is the easiest way to be pulled off the three real options.

Subtopic: comparative evaluation and decision making

Q: Which items are the four parts of Phase III, Comparative Evaluation/Decision Making, in the Stetler Model? Select all that apply.
A) Substantiation of the evidence
B) Pilot testing of the research-based protocol
C) Fit of the evidence with the healthcare setting
D) Feasibility of using the findings
E) Concerns with current practice
Answer: A, C, D, E
Rationale: Phase III has four parts: substantiation of the evidence, fit of the evidence with the healthcare setting, feasibility of using the findings, and concerns with current practice.
Trap: (B) belongs to the Iowa Model, where research-based protocols are pilot-tested before adoption. Importing an Iowa step into a Stetler phase is the most common error on this content.

Q: A nurse assessing feasibility under the Stetler Model is applying the model's three Rs. What are they?
A) Relevance, reliability, and rigor
B) Research, review, and revision
C) Results, reporting, and replication
D) Risks, resources, and readiness
Answer: D
Rationale: Feasibility is assessed as potential risks, resources needed, and readiness of those involved.
Trap: The three Rs are the feasibility test only, which is one of the four parts of Phase III. They are not a summary of the whole model.

Q: An evidence-based practice team finds sound evidence supporting a change, but the change is complex and would involve pharmacy, respiratory therapy, and nursing, and coordinating it will take considerable time. Which Phase III decision fits this situation?
A) To consider using the research evidence
B) To use the research evidence
C) Not to use the research evidence
D) To return to Phase II for revalidation
Answer: A
Rationale: Phase III allows three decisions: to use the evidence, to consider using it, or not to use it. To consider using it applies when a change is complex and involves multiple disciplines, so more time is needed to coordinate.
Trap: Students who remember only a use or do-not-use binary force this item into (B) or (C). Not to use is reserved for evidence that is not strong, or for risks or costs of change that are too high in comparison with the benefits.

Topic: Iowa model of evidence-based practice
Subtopic: triggering issues and opportunities

Q: A hospital's risk-management reports show a cluster of medication errors on night shift, and the unit council decides to search the literature. In the Iowa Model, this beginning is best described as which of the following?
A) A knowledge-focused opportunity
B) A problem-focused trigger
C) Stetler Phase I preparation
D) An expanded philosophy of care
Answer: B
Rationale: Problem-focused triggers are clinical problems and data from risk management, process improvement, benchmarking, and financial reports.
Trap: (C) is the near-neighbor across models. An individual nurse deciding whether to apply findings is Stetler; a trigger arising from agency risk-management data is Iowa.

Q: Which of the following would the Iowa Model classify as knowledge-focused opportunities? Select all that apply.
A) New research findings published on an intervention the unit uses
B) A change in national agency standards and guidelines
C) Benchmarking data showing the unit's catheter-associated infection rate is above its peers
D) An updated professional model of care, which is a Magnet recognition requirement
E) A question raised by the institutional standards' committee
Answer: A, B, D, E
Rationale: Knowledge-focused opportunities include new research findings, changes in national agency or organizational standards and guidelines, an expanded philosophy of care, an updated professional model of care (a Magnet recognition requirement), and questions from the institutional standards' committee.
Trap: (C) is benchmarking data, which the chapter lists as a problem-focused trigger alongside risk management, process improvement, and financial reports. Sorting a given example into the right kind of trigger is the whole point of this distinction.

Subtopic: from pilot testing to adoption

Q: An Iowa Model team has confirmed that its topic is an agency priority and has developed a research-based protocol for oral care in ventilated patients. What comes next, before the protocol is adopted?
A) Implement the protocol agency-wide and monitor outcomes through quality improvement
B) Submit the protocol to the institutional standards' committee for symbolic approval
C) Conduct additional studies, because a protocol cannot be built from existing evidence
D) Pilot-test the protocol and evaluate its impact on patient care, personnel, and the agency
Answer: D
Rationale: Research-based protocols, algorithms, guidelines, and policies are pilot-tested and then evaluated for impact on patient care, personnel, and the agency. If outcomes are favorable, the change is adopted, integrated, and sustained, with key outcomes monitored through quality improvement.
Trap: (A) skips the pilot. Agency-wide rollout and QI monitoring are what follow a favorable pilot, not what replace it. (C) applies only when the evidence found is inadequate, which is not the case here.

Topic: choosing between the EBP models
Subtopic: Stetler versus Iowa

Q: Which statement correctly distinguishes the Stetler Model from the Iowa Model?
A) Stetler is a user-initiated, conscious critical-thinking process organized in five phases, while Iowa is an agency-level model that begins with triggering issues or opportunities
B) Stetler is an agency-level model organized around triggers, while Iowa is an individual nurse's critical-thinking process
C) Stetler applies only at the institutional level, while Iowa applies only to individual practitioners
D) Both models require pilot testing before any decision about using the evidence is made
Answer: A
Rationale: The intent of the Stetler Model is to make using research evidence in practice a conscious, critical thinking process that is initiated by the user, organized in five phases. The Iowa Model promotes the implementation of EBP by nurses in clinical agencies and begins with triggering issues or opportunities.
Trap: (C) half-remembers a real detail and inverts it. Stetler evidence can be used at the institutional level, producing policies, algorithms, procedures, protocols, and formal programs, or at the individual level by RNs, educators, and policy makers. It is not restricted to institutions.
