## §1 The philosophy behind mixed methods

**Mixed methods is neither** "the objective **postpositivistic** view of quantitative researchers **nor** the subjective **constructivist** view of qualitative researchers."

> **Researchers "have exchanged the dichotomy of positivism and constructivism for the middle ground of PRAGMATISM."**

**Pragmatism** = solving problems "by whatever methods fit the problem or question." **"Pragmatists want to obtain the information that they believe they need to resolve an issue."**

**The chapter's worked scenario:** a clinic manager concerned about missed follow-up appointments partners with a researcher to run **focus groups (qualitative)** plus an **exit survey (quantitative)**. The design was chosen "because the goal was to obtain the information they needed to **improve the attendance rate** — a goal consistent with a pragmatic approach."

**Why combine at all:** "the researcher can allow the **strengths of one method to compensate for the possible limitations of the other**" — or, stated positively, "mixed methods research allows the **strengths of each method to interact in a complementary way.**"

## §2 The three mixed methods designs — highest-yield structure

**Three questions identify any mixed methods design (Fig. 14.1 decision tree):**
1. Were the quantitative and qualitative components implemented **concurrently or sequentially**?
2. If sequential, **which came first**?
3. **How did the researchers combine or interpret** the findings of one component in light of the other?

| Design | Order | Purpose | Chapter's exemplar |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Convergent concurrent** (parallel) | **Both at the same time** | **Confirm, cross-validate, or corroborate** | Hagstrom (2017): family stress in parents of children in the **PICU** > 1 week; 9 parents from 8 families |
| **Exploratory sequential** | **QUAL → QUAN** | Explore an **underresearched topic**; qualitative results **inform/design the quantitative phase** | Al-Yateem et al. (2016): interviews with **25 adolescents/young adults with cystic fibrosis** → questionnaire → **113** respondents |
| **Explanatory sequential** | **QUAN → QUAL** | Qualitative data **explain the quantitative findings** | Lenz & Lancaster (2017): intensive outpatient **PTSD** treatment; 30 women, 18 men, mean age 43.48 |

### Convergent concurrent
- Uses **a single sample, or two samples from the same population.**
- **Integration happens after data are analyzed, during the interpretation phase.**
- **Same findings from both methods = convergence → strengthens the evidence.**
- **Different findings** are not a failure: "researchers may be able to use the different findings to provide a **broader description of the problem.**"
- In Hagstrom's study, the **qualitative component was emphasized**, with quantitative findings used "to **enrich** the description" and to compare quantified stress with verbal accounts. **Note the design logic:** interviews came first chronologically, "**but the transcripts were NOT analyzed before the quantitative phase, and both types of data were collected during one interaction**" — which is what makes it concurrent rather than sequential.

### Exploratory sequential
- "Often selected for **underresearched topics**, with the first phase allowing for **exploration of the topic to inform the quantitative phase.**"
- In the CF study, **four determinants of quality** identified by content analysis were **transformed into questionnaire items** — "each proposed healthcare service determinant formed an individual item." A **panel of 10 experts** rated clarity and relevance, giving **content validity of 0.91**; the resulting **24-item** questionnaire went to 113 participants.

### Explanatory sequential
- "A qualitative examination of the phenomenon **facilitates a fuller understanding** and is **well suited to explaining and interpreting relationships.**"
- In the PTSD study the researchers **lacked resources for a comparison/control group**, so they "supplemented quantitative estimates of treatment effects with qualitative data" to identify which aspects of treatment patients found helpful. The qualitative arm used **grounded theory coding** because the goal was to "generate a tentative theory."

## §3 Challenges of mixed methods

**Combining the data.** Congruence "can be especially challenging because quantitative and qualitative methods were **founded within discrepant philosophies.**" **Integration "is the CRITICAL phase and should be given significant thought during the development of the study"** — ideally planned in the proposal.

**Three integration strategies named:**
1. **Building the second phase on the findings of the first**
2. **Expanding the view** of a phenomenon
3. **Strengthening support** by interpreting the findings together

**Data conversion:** some researchers convert one data type to the other — **qualitative codes may be counted and converted to quantitative data**, or **quantitative data converted to conceptual ideas or themes.** Others "systematically compare and contrast the findings from each arm," using **graphs, tables, and figures.**

> "The **capacity of mixed methods designs to answer the research questions depends on effective methods for INTEGRATING the data** from each component."

**Resources.** Mixed methods studies "require a **time commitment that may exceed** that required for single-method studies." The chapter's example: one team **collected data for 4 years** (Goldman & Little's study of women's empowerment among Maasai groups in Tanzania and Uganda). **In sequential designs, phase one must be completed and analyzed before phase two can be developed or revised.** "Additional time often translates into a need for **greater financial support.**"

**Funding and teams.** "Because of the **complexity of concurrent designs, additional personnel are needed** to conduct all aspects of quantitative and qualitative research **at the same time.**" Ideally the team is **multidisciplinary**, bringing "different perspectives, skills, and expertise," with members assigned to match their expertise.

## §4 Outcomes research — what it is

**"Focused on the END RESULTS of patient care."** In nursing, it "focuses on **how a patient's health status changes as a result of the nursing care received or the nursing services delivered.**"

**AHRQ's framing:** end results include "effects that people experience and care about, such as **a change in their ability to function.**" For **individuals with chronic conditions, for whom cure is not always possible**, end results include **quality of life, functioning, symptom management, and mortality.**

> **"By linking the care people receive to the outcomes that they experience, outcomes research has become the KEY to developing better ways to monitor and improve quality of care."**

**Where the momentum comes from:** "**policy makers, insurers, and the public**," who are "basing payment for care on the **economic efficiency** of healthcare systems." This creates demand for data that **document interventions, justify costs, and demonstrate improved patient outcomes.**

**How outcomes research differs from everything else in this book:** studies are "**more complex in design, population, settings, and data analyses**," and researchers "are often from **a mix of disciplines**, such as **economics and public health**, as well as nursing." They "use a **unique theoretical framework.**"

**The level of analysis is different too:** outcomes studies "**do NOT examine patient care at the individual nurse or individual patient level**... rather, they might examine **all the nursing care provided to patients in a particular ICU.**"

**The questions outcomes researchers ask:**
- What are the **end results of patients' care** (all care by all providers)?
- What effect does **nursing care** (all care by all nurses) have on those end results?
- **"Are there some nursing acts that have no effects at all on outcomes or that actually cause harm?"**
- **When** do we measure — immediately after care, at discharge, or much later?
- **How do we distinguish care provided by nurses from care provided by other professionals?**

## §5 Donabedian's theory — the framework for outcomes research

**Avedis Donabedian (1978, 1980, 1987)** proposed a theory of quality health care. **"This theory is still the DOMINATING framework for outcomes research."**

> **A detail worth noting:** "Although **quality is the overriding construct** of Donabedian's theory, **he never actually defined this concept himself.**" The **WHO** definition is used instead.

**The three foci of evaluation:**

| Focus | What it covers | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | Organization and administration; provider and patient characteristics | **Nursing units, hospitals, home health agencies** |
| **Process** | **How care is provided** | **Practice style, standard of care** |
| **Outcome** | **End results of care** | Symptoms, function, quality of life, mortality |

> **The gap between theory and practice:** "A complete quality outcomes study requires the **simultaneous inclusion of ALL THREE** and an examination of the relationships among them. **However, researchers have had little success in accomplishing this theoretical goal.**" Doing so would require large samples of various structures, each with different processes, plus large samples of patients experiencing the outcomes — "the funding required and cooperation necessary **have not yet been realized.**"

**Donabedian's cube (Fig. 14.5):** three dimensions — **health, the subjects of care, and the providers of care** — incorporating three aspects of health: **physical function, psychological function, and social function.**

**Structural variables:** "the organization of nursing care and nursing leadership have an effect on nursing practice and, in turn, on patient outcomes." **Autonomy in clinical nursing practice is a structural variable** "recognized as **critically important** to achieving positive patient outcomes." **Magnet hospital designation** is an identified **autonomy-enabling structure** (verify a hospital's status via the **ANCC**).

**Nursing services variables studied:** skill mix and configuration of personnel; **staffing levels**; assignment patterns (primary nursing, functional assignments, team nursing); **shift patterns**; levels of nursing **education, experience, and expertise**; ratios of full-time to part-time nurses; nursing leadership; **cohesion and communication** among nursing staff and between nurses and physicians; clinical care maps.

**Nursing interventions** "reflect the care delivered by nurses" — e.g. a **falls risk assessment** and a **pressure ulcer risk assessment.**

## §6 Nursing-sensitive outcomes, proximal and distal

> **The definition to read carefully:** NSPOs "are considered sensitive because they are **influenced by nursing care decisions and actions.** It **may not be CAUSED by nursing but is ASSOCIATED with nursing.**"

"Nursing" here may mean **the individual nurse, nurses as a working group, the approach to nursing practice, or the nursing unit/institution** that determines numbers of nurses, salaries, and educational level.

| | **Proximal outcome** | **Distal outcome** |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | **Close to the delivery of care** | **Removed from proximity** to the care received |
| **Influenced by external factors?** | Less | **More** — "more influenced by external (nontreatment or nonintervention) factors" |
| **Example** | **Signs and symptoms of disease** | **Quality of life** |

**The Nursing Outcomes Classification (NOC)** (Moorhead et al., 2013) "identifies **proximal and distal** nurse-sensitive outcomes for research and practice."

**Sensitivity to change** is an important measurement property in outcomes research, because researchers evaluate **how outcomes change in response to interventions.**

## §7 National initiatives

| Organization | Founded | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **AHRQ** (part of **DHHS**) | — | Supports research to "improve the outcomes and quality of health care, reduce healthcare costs, address patient safety and medical errors, and broaden access" |
| **PCORI** | Funded via the **Recovery Act, 2009** | **$473 million** designated for **patient-centered outcomes research (PCOR)**. "Helps people make informed healthcare decisions... by producing and promoting high-integrity, evidence-based information that comes from **research guided by patients, caregivers, and the broader healthcare community**" |
| **National Quality Forum (NQF)** | **1999** | **National standard-setting organization for healthcare performance measures.** Portfolio includes **performance measures, serious reportable events, and preferred (safe) practices** |
| **NDNQI** | ANA plan launched **1994**; database funded **1998** | National database housing data on **nursing-sensitive quality indicators** |

**AHRQ's number one funding priority is "improving healthcare quality by supporting the implementation of PCOR."** Its current initiative is **comparative effectiveness research.**

**NQF details:** approximately **one-third of its measures are patient outcomes** (mortality, readmissions, health functioning, depression, experience of care).

**The nursing-sensitive measures submitted by the ANA under NDNQI — "the FIRST nationally standardized performance measures of nursing-sensitive outcomes in acute care hospitals":**
- **Nursing staff skill mix**
- **Nursing care hours per patient day**
- **Catheter-associated urinary tract infection rate**
- **Central line–associated bloodstream infection rate**
- **Fall and injury rates**
- **Hospital- and unit-acquired pressure ulcer rates**
- **Nurse turnover rate**
- **RN practice environment scale**
- **Ventilator-associated pneumonia rate**

They were designed to assess **healthcare quality, patient safety, and a professional and safe work environment.**

> **The NQF's stated philosophy:** "although most of the measures in use focus on the **failure to meet expected standards**, the NQF believes that **quality is as much about influencing POSITIVE outcomes as about avoiding negative outcomes**." It is developing standards based on **how patients feel**, noting that process measures like re-hospitalization or infection rates don't "always take into account **what is most important to the patient and families**, such as the management of long-term symptoms or ability to conduct daily activities."

**NDNQI history:** the ANA worked with the **American Academy of Nursing Expert Panel on Quality Health Care**; **Donabedian's theory was used as the framework** for the project. The indicators were first called the **ANA Nursing Care Report Card**, which could facilitate **benchmarking** — "setting a desired standard that would allow comparisons of hospitals in terms of their nursing care quality."

## §8 Evaluating structure, process, and outcomes

**Evaluating structure of care** begins by **identifying and describing the elements of the structure**, using administration and management theories. Elements may include **leadership, organizational hierarchy, decision-making processes, distribution of power, and financial management.**

**Process concepts:** a **standard of care** is "a **norm on which quality of care is judged**"; a **practice pattern** is "a concept closely related to **practice style.**"

**Adverse outcomes** are caused by **medical management or complication, not the underlying disease**, and generally include **prolonged health care, disability, or death.**

**The National Guideline Clearinghouse** is "a public resource for summaries of **evidence-based clinical practice guidelines.**"

## Source conflicts and traps

- **The two sequential designs are defined by WHICH COMES FIRST, and their names are counterintuitive.** **EXPLORATORY = QUAL first** (you explore, then measure). **EXPLANATORY = QUAN first** (you get numbers, then explain them). Students reverse these constantly — anchor on *explain the numbers you already have.*
- **Convergent concurrent = both at once**, and its purpose is **cross-validation.** Note the subtlety in the chapter's exemplar: interviews happened before the questionnaires **chronologically**, but the design is still **concurrent** because the qualitative data were **not analyzed before** the quantitative phase and both were collected in one interaction. **Sequence of analysis, not sequence of contact, determines the design.**
- **Divergent findings in a convergent design are not a failure** — they can "provide a broader description of the problem." An option calling non-matching results invalid contradicts the chapter.
- **Pragmatism is the philosophy of mixed methods** — explicitly *not* postpositivism (quantitative) or constructivism (qualitative). A stem asking for the philosophical foundation wants **pragmatism.**
- **Integration is the critical phase of a mixed methods study** and should be planned up front. An option treating integration as an optional final step understates it.
- **Donabedian = structure, process, outcome.** Sorting examples is the likely item: **nursing units/hospitals = structure; practice style/standard of care = process; end results = outcome.** Note **Magnet designation and nurse autonomy are STRUCTURE**, not process.
- **A complete Donabedian study includes all three simultaneously — and almost none do.** The chapter says researchers "have had little success" and the necessary funding "has not yet been realized." An option asserting that outcomes studies routinely capture all three is wrong.
- **Donabedian never defined "quality"** despite it being his overriding construct. The WHO definition fills the gap.
- **Nursing-sensitive outcomes are ASSOCIATED with nursing, not necessarily CAUSED by it.** This hedge is in the definition and is a fair exam target — an option claiming NSPOs prove nursing causation overstates it.
- **Proximal = close to care (signs and symptoms); distal = removed (quality of life).** And **distal outcomes are MORE influenced by external factors** — which is why they are harder to attribute to nursing.
- **Outcomes research operates at the UNIT/system level, not the individual nurse or patient level.** A stem describing evaluation of one nurse's patients is not outcomes research as defined here.
- **An adverse outcome is caused by medical management or complication — NOT by the underlying disease.** Disease progression alone is not an adverse outcome.
- **NDNQI came from the ANA and used Donabedian's theory as its framework**; its indicators were the **first nationally standardized** nursing-sensitive performance measures in acute care. The **NQF (1999)** is the standard-setting body; **AHRQ** is the federal research agency; **PCORI** funds patient-centered work. Matching organization to role is an easy item to write.
- **Mixed methods costs more time AND money**, and **concurrent designs need more personnel** (both arms run simultaneously) while **sequential designs need more elapsed time** (phase one must finish first). The reason differs by design.
