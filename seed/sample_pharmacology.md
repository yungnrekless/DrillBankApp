# Sample drill file — replace with your own fact banks

Topic: pharmacology
Subtopic: anticoagulants

Q: A patient on warfarin has an INR of 6.2 with no active bleeding. Which intervention is the most appropriate initial step?
A) Administer protamine sulfate
B) Hold warfarin and recheck the INR
C) Give fresh frozen plasma immediately
D) Increase the warfarin dose to stabilize the level
Answer: B
Rationale: With a supratherapeutic INR and no bleeding, holding the drug and rechecking is the standard first move; the INR falls as the existing dose clears.
Trap: (A) reverses heparin, not warfarin. Reversal agent pairings are the highest-yield thing to have automatic here.

Q: Which laboratory value is used to monitor unfractionated heparin therapy?
A) INR
B) aPTT
C) Platelet count alone
D) Fibrinogen
Answer: B
Rationale: Unfractionated heparin is monitored with the aPTT (or anti-Xa), while warfarin is monitored with the INR.
Trap: (A) is the reflex answer if you pattern-match "anticoagulant" to "INR" without separating the two drug classes.

Subtopic: adverse effects

Q: Which findings would raise concern for heparin-induced thrombocytopenia? Select all that apply.
A) Platelet count falling more than 50% from baseline
B) Onset typically 5 to 10 days after heparin exposure
C) New thrombosis despite anticoagulation
D) Immediate improvement after increasing the heparin dose
Answer: A, B, C
Type: SATA
Rationale: HIT is an immune-mediated reaction marked by a substantial platelet drop, a characteristic 5-to-10-day onset window, and paradoxical thrombosis.
Trap: (D) inverts the pathophysiology — continuing or escalating heparin makes HIT worse, which is why the management step is to stop it.
