palliative care vs hospicecore distinction
1. Which statement correctly distinguishes palliative care from hospice care?
  • ✓Palliative care is the broad umbrella that can accompany curative treatment; hospice is a formalized, funded subset requiring a prognosis of 6 months or fewer
  • BHospice is the broad umbrella that can accompany curative treatment; palliative care requires a prognosis of 6 months or fewer
  • CThe two terms are interchangeable ways of describing end-of-life care
  • DPalliative care is only available after hospice eligibility has been established
Rationale

Palliative care is the broad approach occurring over a longer period, offering a wider range of services not limited to the days or weeks before death, and it can run alongside curative treatment. Hospice is a formalized, funded program for patients with a physician-certified terminal prognosis of 6 months or fewer who choose comfort care over life-extending treatment.

Watch for

The classic trap reverses the two terms or claims palliative care requires a terminal prognosis; palliative care is not time-limited.

palliative care vs hospicecore distinction
2. For a patient to be eligible for hospice care, the physician must certify which prognosis?
  • A3 months or fewer
  • ✓6 months or fewer
  • C12 months or fewer
  • DNo specific timeframe is required
Rationale

Hospice eligibility requires the physician to indicate the patient has 6 months or fewer to live, and the patient must acknowledge the terminal prognosis and select comfort-focused rather than life-extending care.

Watch for

Any option naming a different timeframe, or claiming hospice has no timeframe requirement, is the distractor.

3. A patient with advanced heart failure is still pursuing aggressive, curative-intent treatment but also wants help managing pain and shortness of breath, six months before any expectation of the disease being terminal. Which type of care fits this scenario?
  • AHospice care, because comfort needs have emerged
  • ✓Palliative care, because it is not limited to end of life and can run alongside curative treatment
  • CNeither, because comfort measures cannot be offered until curative treatment stops
  • DHospice care, because the patient has expressed a preference for comfort
Rationale

Palliative care occurs over a longer period, offers a wider range of services, is not limited to the days or weeks before death, and can be provided alongside curative treatment. This patient has not chosen to forgo life-extending treatment, so hospice does not fit.

Watch for

The presence of comfort needs alone does not make a scenario hospice; hospice additionally requires a terminal prognosis of 6 months or fewer and a choice to forgo life-extending care.

palliative care vs hospicecore distinction
5. Which statement about the relationship between palliative care and hospice is accurate?
  • ✓Hospice is a subset of palliative care with a shorter, defined timeframe
  • BPalliative care is a subset of hospice reserved for patients who decline the hospice program
  • CThe two are unrelated services offered by different professions
  • DPalliative care replaced hospice as the preferred model of end-of-life care
Rationale

Hospice is a subset of palliative care with a short timeframe; palliative is the broad umbrella and hospice is the narrower circle inside it, near the end of life.

palliative care vs hospicebarriers to hospice referral
6. Why does hospice care often begin late in the disease process, according to the chapter?
  • AMedicare regulations delay certification until the final weeks of life
  • ✓Barriers and stigma persist because patients, families, and physicians may equate hospice with hopelessness and giving up
  • CHospice agencies require a second physician's confirmation before enrollment
  • DPalliative care must be completed in full before hospice can begin
Rationale

Hospice often begins late because of barriers and stigmas — some patients, families, and physicians equate hospice with hopelessness and giving up. Many who resist hospice will accept palliative care.

Watch for

The tested point is that late referral is a known barrier caused by stigma, not that hospice is intentionally meant to be brief.

8. Which home health terminology system has three components — the Problem Classification Scheme, the Intervention Scheme, and the Problem Rating Scale for Outcomes?
  • AOASIS
  • ✓The Omaha System
  • COBQI
  • DThe CMS Conditions of Participation
Rationale

The Omaha System has three components designed to be used together: the Problem Classification Scheme, the Intervention Scheme, and the Problem Rating Scale for Outcomes.

Watch for

Attributing the three components to OASIS is the classic trap. OASIS is the outcome-and-assessment data set, not the three-part terminology.

  • ✓The only ANA-recognized terminology developed inductively by and for nurses who practice in the community
  • CA physician-developed hospital discharge protocol

The Omaha System is the only ANA-recognized terminology developed inductively by and for community nurses; it operationalizes the nursing process and is primarily used by home health agencies.

An option calling it physician-developed or hospital-based misattributes both its origin and its setting.

the initial home visitguest role and visit sequence
15. What is the governing principle for a nurse entering a patient's home for the first time?
  • AThe nurse directs the visit as the clinical authority in the home
  • ✓The nurse is a guest whose services can be accepted or rejected, and must earn the family's trust to establish a partnership
  • CThe nurse may proceed with assessment and treatment regardless of the family's response, since a physician referral authorizes the visit
  • DThe nurse's primary task is to complete as many billable interventions as possible in the first visit
Rationale

When a nurse enters a patient's home, he or she is a guest and must recognize that the services offered can be accepted or rejected. The nurse needs to earn the trust of the family and establish a partnership with the patient and family.

Watch for

A stem where the nurse asserts control over the home, or proceeds without agreement, is the distractor.

23. A home health nurse teaches a patient newly diagnosed with diabetes how to modify the diet and administer insulin injections. This is an example of which level of prevention?
  • APrimary
  • BSecondary
  • ✓Tertiary
  • DPrimary, because the teaching prevents future complications
Rationale

Tertiary prevention in home health includes instruction about dietary modifications and insulin injections for patients with a new diabetes diagnosis, which the patient and family then implement.
