population and sampling terminologythe population funnel
1. A researcher is studying adult males 18 years and older who have type 2 diabetes and have been hospitalized with a lower-extremity infection. Every adult male anywhere who fits that description belongs to which group?
  • AThe accessible population
  • ✓The target population
  • CThe sample
  • DThe sampling frame
Rationale

The target population is the entire set of individuals or elements who meet the sampling criteria, regardless of whether the researcher can reach them.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex answer because the description sounds like the group being studied. The accessible population is only the portion the researcher can reasonably reach.

population and sampling terminologythe population funnel
2. That same researcher can realistically recruit only from patients with type 2 diabetes admitted to one acute care hospital in Dallas, Texas. Those Dallas patients constitute which group?
  • AThe target population
  • BThe element
  • ✓The accessible population
  • DThe stratum
Rationale

The accessible population is the portion of the target population to which the researcher has reasonable access.

Watch for

This is the mirror image of the previous item. Sort it by the question the stem is asking: everyone who meets criteria is the target; whoever the researcher can actually get to is the accessible population.

population and sampling terminologythe population funnel
3. A study of central line care treats each individual intravenous catheter as one unit of study. What is each catheter called?
  • ✓An element
  • BA cluster
  • CA stratum
  • DA sampling frame
Rationale

An element is a person, event, object, or any other single unit of study. When elements are persons they are called participants or subjects, and participant is the term commonly used in nursing research.

population and sampling terminologythe population funnel
4. From which group is a study's sample actually obtained, and in what direction does generalization travel?
  • AFrom the target population; findings generalize from the sample to the sampling frame
  • BFrom the accessible population; findings generalize from the accessible population back to the sample
  • CFrom the sampling frame; findings generalize from the target population to the accessible population
  • ✓From the accessible population; findings generalize from the sample to the accessible population and then, more abstractly, to the target population
Rationale

The sample is obtained from the accessible population using a sampling method, and generalization flows back up the funnel: sample to accessible population, then more abstractly to the target population.

Watch for

(B) reverses the direction. Generalization always moves from the smaller, studied group outward, never from a population down onto the sample.

population and sampling terminologygeneralization
5. A study of males with type 2 diabetes in a Dallas hospital found a benefit from a nursing intervention. What determines how far the researcher may extend those findings?
  • AThe size of the accessible population
  • BWhether participants were randomly assigned to groups
  • ✓The quality of the study and the consistency of its findings with previous research
  • DWhether the refusal rate was below 5%
Rationale

How far findings can be generalized depends on the quality of the study and the consistency of the study's findings with previous research. High quality plus agreement with prior work permits generalization to, for example, males with type 2 diabetes in Texas urban hospitals or urban hospitals in the southern United States.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex because random assignment feels like the thing that makes results trustworthy. Random assignment is a design strategy about group equivalence, not about the reach of the findings.

6. Which statements about a sampling frame are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓It is a list of every member of the population
  • ✓The sampling criteria are used to define who is eligible to appear on the list
  • CIt is the portion of the target population to which the researcher has reasonable access
  • ✓Probability sampling methods require one
  • ✓HIPAA requirements have increased the difficulty of obtaining a complete list
Rationale

A sampling frame is a list of every member of the population, with the sampling criteria defining eligibility for the list. Probability sampling requires a sampling frame, and HIPAA's requirements to protect health information have made complete frames harder to obtain.

(C) is the definition of the accessible population, not the sampling frame. A frame is a list of names; an accessible population is a group of people.

7. In the chapter's study of preoperative teaching about early ambulation in older adults undergoing knee replacement, which were inclusion sampling criteria? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • AHistory of previous joint replacement surgery
  • ✓Adults 60 years of age or older
  • CDiagnosis of dementia
  • ✓Able to speak and read English
  • ✓Undergoing surgical replacement of one knee joint
Rationale

The inclusion criteria were adults 60 years or older, able to speak and read English, undergoing surgical replacement of one knee joint.

(A) and (C) are the exclusion criteria from the same study, along with debilitating chronic muscle disease. Both lists come from one worked example, which is exactly why they get blended together.

sampling criteriainclusion versus exclusion criteria
8. A 68-year-old who speaks and reads English is scheduled for replacement of one knee joint but carries a diagnosis of dementia. How is this person best described for that study?
  • AA member of the accessible but not the target population
  • ✓Someone who meets the inclusion criteria but is removed by an exclusion criterion
  • CSomeone who fails to meet the inclusion criteria
  • DAn eligible participant, because dementia is only a demographic variable
Rationale

Exclusion criteria are the characteristics that cause a person who meets the inclusion criteria to be excluded or removed from the target population. This person satisfies all three inclusion criteria and is then removed by one exclusion criterion.

Watch for

(C) is the common misread. Failing an inclusion criterion and being caught by an exclusion criterion are two different events, and this person fails no inclusion criterion.

sampling criteriainclusion versus exclusion criteria
9. Which statement correctly defines exclusion sampling criteria?
  • AAny characteristic not named among the inclusion criteria
  • BThe characteristics that define the accessible population
  • CThe characteristics an element must possess to belong to the target population
  • ✓Characteristics that can cause a person who meets the inclusion criteria to be excluded or removed from the target population
Rationale

Exclusion criteria act only on people who already meet the inclusion criteria, removing them from the target population.

Watch for

(A) is the classic inversion — simply not meeting an inclusion criterion is not an exclusion criterion. (C) is the definition of inclusion criteria, which is the other half of the pair students flip.

sampling criteriainclusion versus exclusion criteria
10. A researcher writes very narrow and restrictive sampling criteria. What is the stated consequence?
  • ✓Difficulty obtaining an adequately sized sample, which can limit the generalization of the findings
  • BA more heterogeneous sample with a broader range of values
  • CElimination of systematic variation in the sample
  • DA guaranteed increase in the acceptance rate
Rationale

Criteria should produce a sample that is heterogeneous with a broad range of values; if the criteria are too narrow and restrictive, researchers may have difficulty obtaining an adequately sized sample, which can limit generalization.

Watch for

(B) inverts the trade-off. Narrowing criteria makes a sample more homogeneous, not more heterogeneous.

sampling criteriainclusion versus exclusion criteria
11. A study enrolled only English speakers because the teaching materials and the instruments were written in English, yet the discussion recommends the intervention for all postoperative patients. What is the appraisal problem?
  • AThe exclusion criteria were too broad
  • BThe accessible population exceeded the target population
  • ✓The researchers generalized beyond their sampling criteria
  • DThe sampling frame was incomplete
Rationale

The chapter cautions that researchers sometimes generalize beyond their sampling criteria; non-English-speaking persons come from another culture and may not respond to the teaching in the same way. Examine the inclusion and exclusion criteria to judge whether a generalization is appropriate.

Watch for

(A) is tempting because an English-language requirement does restrict the sample, but the criterion itself was justified by the study materials. The error is in the discussion, not the criteria.

sample representativenessrandom versus systematic variation
12. Every participant in a knowledge study has an IQ above 120, while the population they came from has a mean IQ near 100. Their test scores run consistently higher than the population mean. What is this?
  • ARandom variation
  • ✓Systematic variation
  • CSampling error created by proportional allocation
  • DAttrition bias
Rationale

Systematic variation, or systematic bias, is a consequence of selecting participants whose measurement values differ from those of the population in some specific way, with variation from the mean mostly in the same direction.

sample representativenessrandom versus systematic variation
13. A reviewer suggests that a study with a biased recruitment process fix the problem by enrolling twice as many participants. What is the best response?
  • ✓Increasing sample size decreases random variation but has no effect on systematic variation
  • BIncreasing sample size corrects both random and systematic variation
  • CIncreasing sample size converts a convenience sample into a probability sample
  • DRandom assignment to groups would remove the systematic variation instead
Rationale

Increasing the sample size decreases random variation and improves representativeness, but increasing the sample size has no effect on systematic variation.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex answer because a bigger sample feels like it fixes everything. This is the chapter's sharpest point: a biased selection process is not diluted by adding more people selected the same biased way.

sample representativenessrandom versus systematic variation
14. In another study, participants' scores fall above and below the mean in both directions by the amount expected among different people from the same sample. What is this, and what does a larger sample do to it?
  • ASystematic bias; a larger sample will not change it
  • BSampling error; a larger sample has no effect
  • CSystematic variation; a larger sample increases it
  • ✓Random variation; a larger sample decreases it, placing more values near the mean and improving representativeness
Rationale

Random variation is the expected difference in values that occurs when different participants from the same sample are examined, scattering both above and below the mean. Increasing sample size decreases it and improves representativeness.

Watch for

This is the mirror of the previous item. Read the direction word in the stem: scattered both ways means random; mostly one direction means systematic.

sample representativenessrandom versus systematic variation
15. Why is systematic bias especially dangerous in quasi-experimental and experimental studies?
  • AIt lowers the acceptance rate below the acceptable range
  • BIt makes the attrition rate impossible to calculate
  • ✓It can lead the researcher to conclude that the treatment made a difference when the values would have been different even without the treatment
  • DIt drives power above 0.8 and inflates the Type I error rate
Rationale

When systematic bias occurs in quasi-experimental or experimental studies, it can lead the researcher to conclude that the treatment has made a difference when in actuality the values would have been different even without the treatment.

sample representativenessevaluating representativeness
16. Which statements about evaluating the representativeness of a sample are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Representativeness is evaluated in the setting, the characteristics of the participants, and the distribution of values on the measured variables
  • BStudies drawing on large databases produce less representative samples than single-site studies
  • ✓Private hospitals are less likely to recruit participants with low incomes
  • ✓People who do not have access to care are usually excluded from studies
  • ✓Researchers who gather data across a variety of settings have a more representative sample than those using a single setting
Rationale

Representativeness must be evaluated in three areas — setting, participant characteristics, and distribution of values. Private hospitals recruit fewer low-income participants, people without access to care are usually excluded, and multiple settings yield a more representative sample. Sample characteristics come from the demographic or attribute variables and must reasonably represent the population; when a study has groups, those groups must have comparable demographics.

Watch for

(B) is the inversion — large databases produce more representative samples, not less.

sampling rates and attritioncalculating the four rates
17. Investigators approached 80 eligible patients about a preoperative teaching study and 4 declined to participate. What is the refusal rate?
  • A4%
  • ✓5%
  • C8%
  • D20%
Rationale

Refusal rate equals the number refusing divided by the number approached, times 100 — 4 divided by 80 equals 5%.

sampling rates and attritioncalculating the four rates
18. In that same study, 76 of the 80 patients approached consented. What is the acceptance rate, and how should it be appraised?
  • A76%, a study weakness
  • B5%, a study strength
  • C95%, but it cannot be appraised without the retention rate
  • ✓95%, a study strength
Rationale

Acceptance rate equals the number accepting divided by the number approached — 76 divided by 80 equals 95%, which the chapter identifies as a study strength. Acceptance rate also equals 100% minus the refusal rate.

Watch for

(A) uses the raw count of acceptors as if it were a percentage. The denominator is always the number approached, not 100.

sampling rates and attritioncalculating the four rates
19. That study began with a sample of 76 participants, and 31 of them withdrew before completion. What is the attrition rate?
  • A31%
  • B39%
  • ✓41%
  • D59%
Rationale

Attrition rate equals the number withdrawing divided by the sample size, times 100 — 31 divided by 76 equals 41%.

Watch for

(A) treats the raw number withdrawing as the percentage, and (D) is the retention rate. Attrition and retention are complements, so having one does not mean you may report the other figure as the answer.

sampling rates and attritioncalculating the four rates
20. Of the 76 participants who entered that study, 45 completed it. What is the retention rate?
  • ✓59%
  • B41%
  • C45%
  • D76%
Rationale

Retention rate equals the number completing divided by the sample size — 45 divided by 76 equals 59%, which is also 100% minus the 41% attrition rate.

sampling rates and attritioninterpreting attrition
21. A study reports 41% attrition overall, with 12 of 38 (32%) withdrawing from the treatment group and 19 of 38 (50%) withdrawing from the comparison group. Which conclusions are supported? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓The study has strong potential for systematic variation
  • ✓Systematic variation is greatest when a large number withdraws, or when a large number withdraws from one group but not the others
  • CAttrition is never higher in a treatment group than in a comparison group
  • ✓Comparison group participants may be more likely to withdraw because they receive no treatment
  • ✓A higher retention rate would make the results more likely to be an accurate reflection of reality
Rationale

This study has strong potential for systematic variation because attrition was 41% and the comparison group's 50% exceeded the treatment group's 32%. The higher the retention rate, the more representative the sample and the more likely the results reflect reality.

Watch for

(C) overstates the rule. The chapter says attrition can be higher in the treatment group when the intervention is complex or time-consuming, so the direction of differential attrition is not fixed.

sampling rates and attritioninterpreting attrition
22. Which statements about calculating and reporting the sampling rates are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • AA high refusal rate with a low acceptance rate indicates less chance for systematic variation
  • ✓Refusal and acceptance rates use the number of people approached as the denominator
  • ✓Attrition and retention rates use the sample size as the denominator
  • ✓Researchers usually report either the refusal rate or the acceptance rate, but not both
  • ✓Best practice is to report the number withdrawing, the attrition rate, and the rationales for withdrawal
Rationale

The two recruitment rates are figured on the number approached and the two completion rates on the sample size; researchers report one of each pair, since each is the complement of the other.

Watch for

(A) inverts the interpretation rule. It is a high acceptance rate with a low refusal rate that means less chance for systematic variation and a sample more likely to be representative.

probability sampling methodssystematic sampling
23. A researcher has an ordered list of 1200 clinic patients and wants a sample of 100. She uses a random procedure to choose the starting name and then takes every 12th name thereafter. Which sampling method is this, and where did the 12 come from?
  • ASimple random sampling; 12 was chosen arbitrarily
  • ✓Systematic sampling; k equals the population size divided by the desired sample size
  • CCluster sampling; 12 is the number of clusters
  • DStratified random sampling; 12 is the number of strata
Rationale

Systematic sampling selects every kth individual on a list from a randomly selected starting point, with k equal to population size divided by desired sample size — 1200 divided by 100 equals 12.

probability sampling methodssystematic sampling
24. Which statement about systematic sampling is accurate?
  • AIt gives every element an equal and random chance of inclusion, making it equivalent to simple random sampling
  • BIt can be carried out without a sampling frame
  • CIt is a nonprobability method under all conditions
  • ✓If the initial starting point is not random, the sample is a nonprobability sample
Rationale

Systematic sampling qualifies as probability sampling only when the starting point is randomly selected, and it provides a random but not equal chance for inclusion.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex because the word random appears in the method's description. A random but not equal chance is not the same as simple random sampling's equal chance.

probability sampling methodsstratified random sampling
25. A researcher knows that ethnicity is a variable critical to achieving representativeness for her outcome and wants every level of it adequately represented in the sample. Which method fits?
  • ACluster sampling
  • BQuota sampling
  • ✓Stratified random sampling
  • DConvenience sampling
Rationale

Stratified random sampling is used when the researcher knows some of the variables in the population that are critical for achieving representativeness; it ensures all levels of the variable are adequately represented and achieves that representativeness with a smaller sample than simple random sampling would need.

Watch for

(B) is the nonprobability lookalike. Quota sampling also fills subgroups, but its resemblance to stratified random sampling is in the stratification, not in the randomness.

probability sampling methodsstratified random sampling
26. A researcher stratifies by four age bands and enrolls exactly 25 participants in each, although the bands are very different sizes in the population. What is the consequence?
  • ✓The smaller groups are overrepresented, which can create sampling error
  • BThe larger groups are overrepresented
  • CRepresentativeness is guaranteed because all levels are present
  • DThe sample becomes proportional to the population
Rationale

With equal numbers of study participants in each group, the smaller groups are overrepresented, which can create sampling error. Proportional allocation instead matches each stratum's share of the population.

Watch for

(C) is the reflex because equal groups feel fair. Equal allocation is one of two options, and it is the one that distorts the population's proportions.

probability sampling methodscluster sampling
27. A researcher needs a nationally dispersed sample of hospital nurses, travel to individual sites would be prohibitively expensive, and no list of all eligible nurses can be obtained. Which method fits, and why?
  • ASimple random sampling, because it is the most basic probability plan
  • ✓Cluster sampling, because a geographically dispersed sample is needed at prohibitive travel cost and individual elements cannot be listed
  • CCluster sampling, because it produces a more representative sample than any other probability method
  • DSystematic sampling, because it does not require a sampling frame
Rationale

Cluster sampling is used for two stated reasons: a geographically dispersed sample is needed but travel cost is prohibitive, or individual elements cannot be listed so no sampling frame exists. It builds a frame of states, cities, institutions, or clinicians and samples those randomly, with several stages called multistage sampling.

Watch for

(C) is the reflex answer because cluster sampling is a probability method and probability methods improve representativeness. Choosing it purely for representativeness misses both of its actual rationales.

probability sampling methodscomparing probability methods
28. Which statements about simple random sampling are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓It is the most basic of the probability sampling plans
  • ✓Elements may be selected using a computer program, which is the most common technique
  • ✓A table of random numbers is useful only when the population number is less than 100
  • DThe researcher must identify strata before selecting elements
  • ✓Drawing names from a container is one acceptable technique
Rationale

Simple random sampling randomly selects elements from the sampling frame using names in a container, a computer program, or a table of random numbers, with the computer program most common and the random numbers table useful only for populations under 100.

(D) imports stratified random sampling's requirement. Simple random sampling does not define strata.

probability sampling methodscomparing probability methods
29. Which statements about probability sampling are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Probability sampling methods are designed to increase representativeness and decrease systematic bias
  • BA sampling frame is unnecessary when probability sampling is used
  • ✓Each person or element in the population has an opportunity to be selected
  • ✓Without random selection, researchers might consciously or unconsciously select individuals whose conditions or behaviors are consistent with the study hypotheses
  • ✓Random sampling increases the validity of the study findings
Rationale

Random selection leaves the choice of participants to chance rather than to a researcher who might exclude people for being too sick, not sick enough, coping too well, uncooperative, or noncompliant, thereby increasing the validity of the findings.

(B) is the inversion — a sampling frame is required for probability sampling, and HIPAA has made complete frames harder to obtain.

nonprobability sampling methodsconvenience sampling
30. A nurse researcher enrolls every patient who happens to be admitted to her unit during her scheduled shifts, continuing until she reaches her desired sample size of 60. Which sampling method is this?
  • ASystematic sampling
  • ✓Convenience (accidental) sampling
  • CQuota sampling
  • DPurposeful sampling
Rationale

In convenience sampling, also called accidental sampling, participants are included merely because they happen to be in the right place at the right time, and enrollment continues until the desired sample size is reached.

Watch for

(A) is tempting because enrolling in admission order sounds orderly and systematic. Systematic sampling requires an existing list, a computed k, and a random starting point.

nonprobability sampling methodsconvenience sampling
31. Which statements about convenience sampling are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • AAny study using a convenience sample is invalid and should not inform practice
  • ✓It provides little opportunity to control for biases and is the weakest sampling approach
  • ✓Multiple biases may exist, some of which may be subtle and unrecognized
  • ✓Serious biases are not always present in convenience samples
  • ✓A convenience sample is acceptable when it is used with reasonable knowledge and care
Rationale

The chapter gives a balanced view — convenience sampling is the weakest approach and carries subtle unrecognized biases, but serious biases are not always present and it is acceptable when used with reasonable knowledge and care. It is inexpensive, accessible, less time-consuming, and lets researchers study nursing interventions when probability sampling is not possible.

Watch for

(A) overstates the text. Many quasi-experimental studies and randomized controlled trials in nursing and medicine use convenience sampling; calling all of them invalid is a stronger claim than the chapter makes.

nonprobability sampling methodsconvenience sampling
32. A convenience sample is randomly assigned to a treatment group and a comparison group. What does the random assignment accomplish?
  • AIt makes the convenience sample representative of the target population
  • BIt converts the convenience sample into a probability sample
  • ✓It strengthens the equivalence of the study groups but does not alter the risk of biases resulting from convenience sampling
  • DIt removes the need for a power analysis
Rationale

Random assignment to groups is not a sampling method but a design strategy; it does not alter the risk of biases resulting from convenience sampling but does strengthen the equivalence of the study groups.

Watch for

(A) and (B) are the same reflex in two costumes — hearing the word random and crediting it to the sampling plan. Assignment happens after sampling and cannot repair how the sample was obtained.

nonprobability sampling methodsconvenience sampling
33. A team wants to strengthen the representativeness of a convenience sample. What does the chapter direct them to do?
  • ✓Increase the sample size
  • BRandomly assign participants to groups
  • CAdd more exclusion criteria
  • DReport the effect size along with the findings
Rationale

To strengthen representativeness in a convenience-sampled study, researchers increase the sample size.

Watch for

(C) moves in the wrong direction entirely — more restrictive criteria shrink the pool and can limit generalization.

nonprobability sampling methodsquota sampling
34. A researcher continues to enroll available patients but adds a strategy that fills a preset number of slots for adults over 65, chosen to replicate their proportion in the target population. Which sampling method is this, and how is it classified?
  • AStratified random sampling, a probability method
  • BCluster sampling, a probability method
  • CNetwork sampling, a nonprobability method
  • ✓Quota sampling, a nonprobability method
Rationale

Quota sampling uses a convenience sampling technique with an added feature — a strategy to ensure the inclusion of participant types likely to be underrepresented, such as females, minority groups, older adults, and the poor, rich, and undereducated — with the goal of replicating subgroup proportions in the target population. It offers an improvement in representativeness over convenience sampling.

Watch for

(A) is the trap the chapter sets up by saying quota sampling is similar to stratified random sampling. The similarity is in identifying subgroups and proportions; the underlying selection is still convenience, so it never becomes a probability method.

35. Which statements about power in quantitative studies are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Power is the ability of a study to detect differences or relationships that actually exist in the population
  • ✓Power is the ability to reject a null hypothesis correctly
  • CAlpha, the level of significance, is frequently set at 0.8 in nursing studies
  • ✓The minimum acceptable level of power is 0.8
  • ✓Power at that minimum level leaves a 20% chance of a Type II error
Rationale

Power is the ability to detect differences or relationships that actually exist, which is the ability to reject a null hypothesis correctly. The minimum acceptable power is 0.8, which results in a 20% chance of a Type II error.

Watch for

(C) swaps the chapter's two numbers. Alpha is frequently set at 0.05; 0.8 is the power standard. Attaching the 20% risk to Type I error instead of Type II is the other half of this swap.

sample size and powerpower analysis
36. A study reports no significant difference between its groups and includes no power analysis. What appraisal question does the chapter direct you to ask?
  • AWhether alpha should have been set at 0.01
  • ✓Whether there is really no difference, or whether an actual difference was not found because of inadequacies in the research methods such as small sample size
  • CWhether a Type I error occurred
  • DWhether the effect size was too large for the design
Rationale

Researchers must perform a power analysis to evaluate sample adequacy for all nonsignificant findings and report it in the discussion section, while the study's planned power analysis belongs in the sample section. Low-powered studies increase the risk of a Type II error.

Watch for

(C) names the wrong error. A nonsignificant result that may be wrong is a missed real effect, which is Type II, not Type I.

37. In a review of 103 randomized controlled trials, power analyses were reported for 81 of them (79%). What did the review find about those reporting studies?
  • AAll 81 enrolled the number of participants their power analysis required
  • B81% of them had power below 0.8
  • ✓24 of them (30%) did not include the required number of participants indicated by their own power calculations
  • D30 of them (24%) failed to state an alpha level
Rationale

Of the 81 studies reporting power analyses, 24 (30%) did not include the required number of participants indicated by their own power calculations.

(D) reverses the count and the percentage. The figure is 24 studies representing 30% of the 81, not 30 studies representing 24%.

sample size and powerfactors influencing sample size
38. A researcher expects a large difference between the treatment and comparison groups on the outcome. What is the effect on the sample size needed?
  • AA larger sample is needed
  • BSample size is unaffected by the expected difference
  • CPower must be reduced to compensate
  • ✓A smaller sample is needed
Rationale

Effect size and sample size are inversely related: the larger the effect size, the smaller the sample needed. Effect size is the extent to which the null hypothesis is false — the statistical test tells you whether a difference exists, and the effect size tells you how big it is.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex because bigger usually means more. Here it is the opposite: a large, obvious effect is easy to detect with few participants.

sample size and powerfactors influencing sample size
39. Which of these studies is likely to require the largest sample?
  • ✓A descriptive survey
  • BA quasi-experimental study of a nursing intervention
  • CAn experimental study using matched pairs
  • DA tightly controlled experimental study
Rationale

Descriptive studies, especially surveys, and correlational studies need very large samples, while quasi-experimental and experimental studies use smaller samples — as control increases, the sample size can be decreased. Matched pairs increase power and further decrease the sample size needed.

Watch for

This is counterintuitive, which is why it is tested. Students assume an experiment, being the more demanding design, must need more people; the control built into the design is what lets the sample shrink.

sample size and powerfactors influencing sample size
40. Which statements about the factors influencing needed sample size are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Studies analyzing more variables require larger samples
  • BDemographic variables used only to describe the sample create a power problem
  • ✓More precise and sensitive instruments allow a smaller sample
  • ✓Analyses at the interval and ratio levels are more powerful than those at the nominal and ordinal levels
  • ✓For t-tests and analysis of variance, equal group sizes increase power
Rationale

More variables, including multiple dependent variables, increase the needed sample size; less-developed tools produce more variance from the true score, and as variance in instrument scores increases, the sample size needed to obtain significance increases. Larger samples are needed when the power of the planned statistical analysis is weak.

Watch for

(B) misses the chapter's explicit exception — using demographic variables only to describe the sample does not cause a power problem. Only variables entered into the analyses to answer questions or test hypotheses drive the required sample size up.

qualitative samplingmatching method to purpose
41. A qualitative researcher wants in-depth understanding of a complex experience in a new area and deliberately selects the participants she judges most able to speak to it. Which sampling method is this?
  • ✓Purposeful (purposive) sampling
  • BQuota sampling
  • CTheoretical sampling
  • DConvenience sampling
Rationale

In purposeful sampling, also called purposive, judgmental, or selective sampling, the researcher consciously selects certain participants, elements, events, or incidents, with the ultimate goal of selecting information-rich cases. It is criticized because the accuracy or relevance of the researcher's judgment is difficult to evaluate, so researchers must report the characteristics they desired and their rationale for selecting them.

Watch for

(D) is the reflex because both methods lack randomness. Convenience takes whoever is available; purposeful takes whoever the researcher judges most informative.

qualitative samplingmatching method to purpose
42. A researcher studying adults with a history of criminal conviction asks each enrolled participant to help identify others who might take part. Which sampling method is this?
  • APurposeful sampling
  • BTheoretical sampling
  • CQuota sampling
  • ✓Network (snowball) sampling
Rationale

Network sampling takes advantage of social networks and the fact that friends tend to have characteristics in common, and is used for participants who are difficult or impossible to obtain in other ways.

Watch for

(A) is close, since researchers often begin with purposeful or convenience sampling and then expand using network sampling. The defining feature here is that existing participants locate the next ones.

43. A grounded theory researcher continues recruiting anyone who can provide relevant, varied, rich information and stops only when the codes are saturated. Which method is this, and what does saturation mean?
  • APurposeful sampling; saturation means the planned sample size has been reached
  • ✓Theoretical sampling; a code is saturated when it is complete and the researcher can see how it fits in the theory
  • CNetwork sampling; saturation means no further participants can be located
  • DMaximum variation sampling; saturation means the cases are maximally heterogeneous
Rationale

Theoretical sampling is used in qualitative research to develop a selected theory or model and is most frequent in grounded theory; diversity in the sample is encouraged so the theory covers a wide range of behaviors in varied situations. When a code or concept is unclear, the researcher continues to seek participants and gather data.

Watch for

(D) borrows a real term from the chapter — in maximum variation the researcher selects a small number of cases with maximum variation relevant to the research question — but attaches the wrong definition of saturation to it.

Select all that apply · scored all or nothing

Rationale

Four factors govern qualitative sample size — scope of the study, nature of the topic, quality of the data, and design of the study. A study of living with chronic illness in older adulthood would need a large sample, while the lived experience of adults over 60 with Parkinson disease could give credible findings with a smaller one. The sample is adequate when saturation is reached and themes emerge.

Watch for

(C) is the assumption most students carry into this section. Sensitive topics named in the chapter — medication administration errors, gender identity, loss of a child, history of child sexual abuse — are exactly the ones that require more participants, not fewer.
