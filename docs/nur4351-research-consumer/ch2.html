types of quantitative researchdescriptive research
1. A nurse researcher enrolls 480 patients across six medical units and records the frequency of falls, the time of day each fall occurred, and the characteristics of the patients who fell. No intervention is delivered and the units operate as they normally do. Which type of quantitative research is this?
  • ACorrelational
  • ✓Descriptive
  • CQuasi-experimental
  • DExperimental
Rationale

Descriptive research is the exploration and description of phenomena in real-life situations, giving an accurate account of the characteristics of particular individuals, situations, or groups using numbers. It is usually conducted with large numbers of participants in natural settings with no manipulation.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex answer because several variables are being measured at once. Correlational research requires that the study systematically investigate the relationship between those variables, not merely count and categorize them.

types of quantitative researchdescriptive research
2. Which of the following are outcomes of descriptive research as the chapter describes them? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Identification and description of concepts
  • BDetermination of the degree and strength of the relationship between two variables
  • ✓Identification of possible relationships
  • ✓Development of hypotheses that provide a basis for future quantitative research
  • EDetermination that one variable causes a change in another
Rationale

The chapter gives three outcomes of descriptive studies: identification and description of concepts, identification of possible relationships, and development of hypotheses that provide a basis for future quantitative research.

Watch for

(B) belongs to correlational research, which measures variables and then applies correlational statistics to determine the degree and type of relationship. Descriptive research identifies only possible relationships.

types of quantitative researchquasi-experimental and experimental research
3. A nurse implements a structured early-mobility protocol on one postoperative unit and compares outcomes with a unit receiving usual care. Participants could not be randomly selected because staffing patterns determined which unit each patient entered. Which type of quantitative research is this?
  • ADescriptive
  • BCorrelational
  • ✓Quasi-experimental
  • DExperimental
Rationale

Quasi-experimental research implements an intervention to examine causal relationships but has less control over the implementation of the intervention, the management of the setting, and/or the selection of participants. Inability to randomly select participants points to quasi-experimental.

Watch for

(D) is the reflex answer because an intervention is present. Both quasi-experimental and experimental studies involve interventions; the level of control, not the presence of an intervention, decides which one it is.

types of quantitative researchquasi-experimental and experimental research
4. Which statements about the four types of quantitative research are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Descriptive and correlational studies are typically designed with minimal researcher control
  • ✓Both quasi-experimental and experimental studies involve implementing an intervention
  • ✓Experimental research is the most highly controlled of the four types
  • DWhen little knowledge is available about a phenomenon, experimental studies are conducted first in order to establish control
  • ✓Descriptive and correlational studies are frequently conducted to provide a basis for quasi-experimental and experimental studies
Rationale

Descriptive and correlational research are conducted with minimal control, both quasi-experimental and experimental research implement interventions, and experimental research is the highly controlled investigation conducted to predict and control phenomena. When little knowledge is available, descriptive studies are conducted first and provide a basis for correlational and then for the more highly controlled designs.

Watch for

(D) reverses the progression. The four types build on one another from the least controlled to the most controlled, so descriptive work comes first when little is known.

types of quantitative researchquasi-experimental and experimental research
5. Why does the chapter state that nurse researchers conduct more quasi-experimental studies than experimental studies?
  • AQuasi-experimental designs provide stronger evidence of causality than experimental designs
  • BExperimental designs cannot examine the effect of one variable on another
  • CQuasi-experimental designs do not require an intervention, which makes them easier to run in practice
  • ✓In clinical settings researchers frequently cannot select participants randomly or control certain variables related to the intervention or the setting
Rationale

When studying human behavior, especially in clinical settings, researchers frequently are unable to select participants randomly or to control certain variables related to the intervention or setting, so nurse researchers conduct more quasi-experimental than experimental studies.

Watch for

(A) inverts the hierarchy. Quasi-experimental studies dominate nursing because of feasibility, not because they are the stronger design; the experimental design carries the highest control.

correlational reasoningdirection and strength of relationships
6. A study reports that as smoking pack-years increase, life span decreases. How is this relationship best described?
  • AA positive relationship, because both variables were measured with precision
  • BA weak relationship, because the direction is negative
  • ✓A negative relationship, because the variables vary in opposite directions
  • DNo relationship, because the two variables move in different directions
Rationale

A negative relationship means the variables vary in opposite directions, so that as one increases the other decreases. This is the chapter's own smoking and life-span example.

Watch for

(B) is the classic misread. Negative names the direction of the relationship, not its weakness; a value near negative 1 is a strong relationship.

correlational reasoningdirection and strength of relationships
7. A study finds that the more minutes people exercise each week, the greater their bone density. How is this relationship best described?
  • ✓A positive relationship, because the variables vary together
  • BA positive relationship, because the health outcome is a desirable one
  • CA negative relationship, because exercise reduces the rate of bone loss
  • DA causal relationship, because exercise preceded the change in bone density
Rationale

A positive relationship means the variables vary together, both increasing or both decreasing. This is the chapter's exercise and bone-density example.

Watch for

(B) is the mirror image of the smoking item's trap. Positive and negative name direction only; they do not mean the finding is good news or bad news.

correlational reasoningdirection and strength of relationships
8. Two correlational findings are reported from the same study: variable pair 1 has a value of negative 0.86 and variable pair 2 has a value of positive 0.14. Which interpretation is accurate?
  • APair 2 has the stronger relationship because its value is positive
  • BPair 1 shows no relationship because its value falls below zero
  • CBoth values fall outside the range possible for a correlational value
  • ✓Pair 1 has the stronger relationship, and pair 2 is close to showing no relationship
Rationale

Correlational values range from negative 1, a perfect negative relationship, to positive 1, a perfect positive relationship, with 0 indicating no relationship. Negative 0.86 is near a perfect negative relationship, while positive 0.14 sits close to zero.

Watch for

(B) treats a negative value as an absence of relationship. It is 0, not any negative number, that indicates no relationship.

correlational reasoningcorrelation and causation
9. Which statements about correlational research are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓It is the systematic investigation of relationships between or among variables
  • BIts primary intent is to determine whether one variable causes a change in another
  • ✓Its findings generate hypotheses that are then tested in quasi-experimental and experimental studies
  • ✓It uses correlational statistics to determine the degree and the type of relationship present
  • ✓It is usually conducted with minimal researcher control
Rationale

Correlational research systematically investigates relationships between or among variables, measures those variables in a sample and applies correlational statistics to determine strength and type of relationship, is designed with minimal control, and generates hypotheses for the causal designs.

Watch for

(B) is the single highest-yield distractor in this chapter. The primary intent of correlational studies is to explain the nature of relationships in the real world, not to determine cause and effect.

basic and applied researchknowledge for its own sake
10. Wacker et al. (2016) studied the muscle and bone pathology of rickets in mice in a laboratory. How is this study classified?
  • ✓Basic research, because it pursues knowledge for its own sake and works toward general scientific principles
  • BApplied research, because rickets is a clinical condition that nurses treat
  • CApplied research, because the investigators used an experimental design
  • DBasic research, because studies using animals cannot include an intervention
Rationale

Basic, or pure or bench, research is scientific investigation conducted for the pursuit of knowledge for its own sake, typically laboratory work with animals or humans on physiological functioning, genetics, or pathological processes. The mouse rickets study is the chapter's example of a basic experimental study.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex answer because the topic sounds clinical. Classification follows the purpose of the knowledge being generated, not whether the disease appears in practice.

basic and applied researchknowledge that changes practice
11. A nurse conducts a study comparing two catheter-care protocols to determine which one reduces catheter-associated infections on her unit. How is this study classified, and what is its aim?
  • ABasic research, aimed at establishing general scientific principles
  • BBasic research, aimed at the pleasure of learning and finding truth
  • CApplied research, aimed at describing phenomena in real-life situations without changing them
  • ✓Applied research, aimed at solving problems and making decisions about outcomes in real-life practice
Rationale

Applied, or practical, research generates knowledge that will directly influence or improve clinical practice, with the aim of solving problems, making decisions, and predicting or controlling outcomes in real-life practice settings.

Watch for

(C) attaches a descriptive-research purpose to the applied label. Applied names the use to which the knowledge is put; it does not name the study type or the level of control.

rigor and controlprecision in measurement
12. A researcher chooses to record heart rate with a cardiac monitor rather than palpating the radial pulse for 30 seconds and writing the value on a data sheet. Which element of a rigorous study is being strengthened, and what does strengthening it make possible?
  • AControl, which allows the researcher to manipulate the study setting
  • ✓Precision, which supports transparency and therefore replication
  • CRigor, which is achieved primarily by increasing the size of the sample
  • DIntervention fidelity, which supports consistent implementation of the intervention
Rationale

Precision encompasses accuracy, detail, and order and is most explicitly demonstrated in the measurement or quantification of the study variables. The chapter uses this exact monitor-versus-palpation contrast, and notes that precision enables transparency, which enables replication.

Watch for

(D) is plausible because fidelity also concerns consistency, but intervention fidelity is the detailed development and consistent implementation of the intervention, not the way an outcome variable is measured.

rigor and controlextraneous variables
13. A researcher studying the effect of relaxation therapy on incisional pain enrolls only patients who have abdominal incisions and who are receiving one type of intravenous pain medication. What is the researcher doing?
  • AEstablishing intervention fidelity
  • BOperationally defining the dependent variable
  • ✓Controlling extraneous variables that could interfere with understanding the relationships among the study variables
  • DObtaining a random sample of postoperative patients
Rationale

Extraneous variables can interfere with obtaining a clear understanding of the relationships among the study variables. In this worked example the type of surgical incision and the time, amount, and type of pain medication must be controlled, which the researcher accomplishes through the selection criteria.

Watch for

(D) confuses narrowing the sample for control with random selection. Restricting who is eligible controls extraneous variables; it does not make the resulting sample random.

rigor and controlextraneous variables
14. Which statement about extraneous variables and rigor is accurate?
  • AExtraneous variables occur only in experimental studies, where an intervention is delivered
  • ✓Extraneous variables exist in all studies, and descriptive and correlational studies are rigorously conducted even though they are designed with minimal control
  • CA tightly controlled design eliminates extraneous variables from a study entirely
  • DDescriptive studies cannot be considered rigorous, because the researcher exercises minimal control
Rationale

Extraneous variables exist in all studies and can interfere with understanding the relationships among the study variables. Descriptive and correlational studies are rigorously conducted but are often designed with minimal researcher control.

Watch for

(D) equates low control with low rigor. Rigor is the striving for excellence through discipline, adherence to detail, precision, and accuracy, and it is expected of every quantitative design regardless of how much control the design permits.

rigor and controldesign and control
15. Which statements about control and design in quantitative research are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Control is the imposing of rules by researchers to decrease the possibility of error
  • ✓Design is the set of rules used to achieve control in research
  • ✓A design is a blueprint for the conduct of a study that maximizes control over factors that could interfere with the study's desired outcome
  • DDesign refers strictly to the plan for collecting data from participants
  • ✓Quantitative studies range from uncontrolled to highly controlled
Rationale

Control is the imposing of rules to decrease the possibility of error, which increases the probability that the findings accurately reflect reality. The rules used to achieve control are the design, defined also as a blueprint that maximizes control, and studies range from uncontrolled to highly controlled.

Watch for

(D) is too narrow to be the chapter's definition. Data collection is one part of a design, but the chapter defines design by control, not by data collection.

research settingslevels of setting control
16. A nurse researcher measures and interviews participants in their own homes and deliberately changes nothing about the home environment. Which setting is this, and which types of study typically use it?
  • ✓Natural or field setting, typically used for descriptive and correlational studies
  • BPartially controlled setting, typically used for descriptive and correlational studies
  • CHighly controlled setting, typically used for bench and experimental studies
  • DNatural or field setting, typically used for experimental studies
Rationale

A natural or field setting is an uncontrolled real-life situation or environment that the researcher does not manipulate or change, and descriptive and correlational studies are commonly conducted in natural settings.

Watch for

(D) pairs the correct setting with the wrong study type. Experimental research requires a level of control that a natural setting by definition does not provide.

research settingslevels of setting control
17. A study is conducted in a research center built solely for the purpose of conducting research, where temperature, lighting, and participant activity are all regulated by the investigators. Which setting is this?
  • ANatural setting
  • BField setting
  • CPartially controlled setting
  • ✓Highly controlled setting
Rationale

A highly controlled setting is an artificially constructed environment developed for the sole purpose of conducting research, such as a laboratory, research center, or test unit, and it is where bench and experimental studies are conducted.

Watch for

(C) is the near neighbor and the mirror image of this item. A partially controlled setting is a real environment that the researcher has manipulated or modified in some way, not one constructed for research from the beginning.

sampling and assignmentrandom selection versus random assignment
18. Liao et al. (2017) recruited older adults through convenience sampling and then randomly placed each participant into either an exercise intervention group or a usual-activity group. Which statement about this study is accurate?
  • AThe sample must have been randomly selected, because random assignment was used
  • ✓The sample was obtained nonrandomly, and the design was strengthened by the random assignment
  • CThe study lacked control, because a nonrandom sample cannot be used in an intervention study
  • DThe random assignment converted the study environment into a highly controlled setting
Rationale

The sample of older adults was obtained by nonrandom convenience sampling, and the design was strengthened by the random assignment of those adults to the exercise intervention or usual activity.

Watch for

(A) is the inversion the chapter warns about directly. Random assignment to groups says nothing about how the sample was selected; nonrandom selection combined with random assignment is a real and common design in nursing research.

sampling and assignmentrandom selection versus random assignment
19. Which statements about sampling in quantitative nursing research are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Sampling is the process of selecting participants who are representative of the population being studied
  • ✓In random sampling, each member of the population is selected independently and has an equal chance of being included
  • CA population is a subset of the sample that is selected for a particular study
  • ✓Quantitative nursing studies often are conducted with nonrandom samples, because randomly selected samples are very difficult to obtain
  • ✓Randomly assigning participants to intervention and control groups increases control and rigor and decreases bias
Rationale

Sampling selects participants representative of the population; random sampling gives each member an independent and equal chance of inclusion; nursing studies often use nonrandom samples, and participants selected nonrandomly are often randomly assigned to groups to increase control and rigor and decrease bias.

Watch for

(C) reverses the two terms. The population is all the elements that meet the criteria for inclusion, and the sample is the subset of that population whose members are the participants.

conceptual and operational definitionstelling the two definitions apart
20. Moon et al. (2017) built a level-of-care variable as the sum of five questions about chores, shopping, personal care, getting around the home, and transportation, each scored from 1, meaning rarely, to 4, meaning every day. What kind of definition is this?
  • AA conceptual definition, because it was drawn from the study framework
  • BA conceptual definition, because it names the components that make up the concept
  • CAn operational definition, because it identifies the population of interest
  • ✓An operational definition, because it specifies how the variable can be measured
Rationale

An operational definition is developed so that the variable can be measured or an intervention implemented in a study, and scoring, coding, and summing items is measurement.

Watch for

(B) is tempting because the five questions do enumerate the components of caregiving, but attaching scores and summing them is measurement. If a definition names numbers, scales, coding, or instruments, it is operational.

conceptual and operational definitionstelling the two definitions apart
21. A researcher states that primary stressors are the care recipient's dementia status, the level of care the caregiver provides, and whether that care is given on a regular schedule, taking that meaning from a theorist's work. What kind of definition is this, and what does it do?
  • ✓A conceptual definition, which gives the variable theoretical meaning and links the study framework to the study variables
  • BAn operational definition, which makes the variable measurable within the study
  • CA conceptual definition, which specifies the instrument used to measure the variable
  • DAn operational definition, which is derived from a theorist's definition or a concept analysis
Rationale

A conceptual definition provides a variable or concept with theoretical meaning, is derived from a theorist's definition or a concept analysis, and links the study framework to the study variables.

Watch for

(C) is the mirror-image error to the previous item. It carries the conceptual label but describes operational work, and naming an instrument is always operational no matter which label the option attaches to it.

nursing process versus research processcomparing the two processes
22. Which statements accurately describe how the research process differs from the nursing process? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓The research process focuses on large groups, whereas the nursing process focuses on a specific patient and family
  • ✓The research process requires greater precision, rigor, and control
  • CThe research process has weaker theoretical underpinnings, because it is driven by collected data rather than by theory
  • ✓The research process is more complex and involves the rigorous application of a variety of research methods
  • ✓Research outcomes are shared widely through presentations and publications and can be synthesized across studies into evidence for practice
Rationale

The research process is more complex, focuses on large groups rather than an individual patient and family, demands greater precision, rigor, and control, and produces outcomes that are shared widely and synthesized across studies into evidence.

Watch for

(C) inverts the theory dimension. The research process has much stronger theoretical underpinnings than the nursing process, with all steps logically linked to each other and to the study's theoretical foundations.
