evidence-based practice foundationsdefinition and benefits
1. A nurse manager states that the unit's new fall-prevention protocol is evidence-based because it was drawn entirely from the most recent meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Which response best reflects the chapter's definition of evidence-based practice?
  • AEvidence-based practice requires that only findings from meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials be used
  • BEvidence-based practice requires that expert clinical opinion take precedence over published research findings
  • ✓Evidence-based practice requires integrating the best research evidence with clinical expertise, patient circumstances, and patient values
  • DEvidence-based practice requires that each unit generate its own research before changing a protocol
Rationale

EBP is defined as a complex phenomenon that requires integration of the best research evidence with clinical expertise, patient circumstances, and patient values in the delivery of quality, safe, cost-effective care.

Watch for

(A) sounds rigorous and does name the strongest evidence source, but research evidence is only one of the four elements. A protocol built from evidence alone, with no attention to clinical expertise, patient circumstances, or patient values, is not EBP.

evidence-based practice foundationschallenges to implementing EBP
3. A staff nurse objects that evidence-based guidelines cannot account for the patient in front of her. Which statement identifies the challenge she is naming along with the chapter's recommended response?
  • ANursing has few systematic reviews compared with medicine and psychology, so the response is to require randomized controlled trials before any guideline is adopted
  • ✓Research evidence is generated from population data and applied to individual patients, so poor outcomes under a guideline should be reported and, if possible, published as a case study
  • CGuidelines should be applied uniformly to all patients regardless of circumstances or values, so individual variation is not a concern
  • DResearch evidence is generated from individual case studies and applied to populations, so poor outcomes should be managed by discontinuing the guideline on the unit
Rationale

Evidence is generated based on population data and then applied in practice to individual patients, who respond in unique ways or have unique circumstances and values. Patients with poor outcomes under a guideline should be reported and, if possible, published as a case study, and the NIH supports translational research to improve use of evidence across patient populations and settings.

Watch for

(C) is the reflex answer for anyone who reads evidence-based as protocol-driven, but uniform application regardless of patient circumstances contradicts both this challenge and the definition of EBP. (A) names a genuine challenge from the chapter and attaches the wrong response to it.

strength of research evidenceranking sources of evidence
4. A committee is grading the evidence it has gathered on a wound-care intervention. Which source provides the strongest evidence?
  • AA single randomized controlled trial with a large sample
  • BA meta-synthesis of qualitative studies of patients' wound-care experiences
  • CA mixed-methods systematic review of intervention and descriptive studies
  • ✓A systematic review that includes a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
Rationale

The strongest evidence comes from systematic reviews that include meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials.

Watch for

(A) is tempting because the RCT is the strongest individual design, so students pick the design rather than the synthesis. A synthesis of RCTs outranks any single trial.

strength of research evidenceranking sources of evidence
5. Which statements about sources of evidence for changing practice are consistent with the chapter? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓The strongest evidence comes from systematic reviews that include meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials
  • ✓Meta-syntheses provide important evidence for changing practice
  • COnly meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials may be used to develop research-based protocols
  • ✓Mixed-methods systematic reviews provide important evidence for changing practice
  • ✓Individual studies also provide important evidence for changing practice
Rationale

The chapter names systematic reviews containing meta-analyses of RCTs as the strongest evidence but explicitly states that meta-syntheses, mixed-methods systematic reviews, and individual studies also provide important evidence for changing practice.

Watch for

(C) overstates the text. Ranking meta-analysis highest is not the same as excluding everything else, and the chapter goes further: research evidence can be combined with other sources of knowledge such as theories, scientific principles, expert opinion, and case reports to build research-based protocols. Expert opinion is weakest on its own but legitimate as a supplement.

PICO clinical questionsPICO elements
6. In the chapter's PICO question on IM injection technique, the statement "IM injection given with 5 to 10 seconds of aspiration in all sites, regardless of age and volume" occupies which position?
  • AP, the population
  • BI, the intervention
  • ✓C, the comparison intervention
  • DO, the outcome
Rationale

The comparison intervention is the practice being measured against, which here is aspirating 5 to 10 seconds in all sites regardless of age and volume. The intervention is IM injection given without aspiration in the right site based on volume of medication and age.

Watch for

Aspirating feels like the thing being done, so students slot it into I. In this question the comparison is the older outgoing practice and the intervention is the newer no-aspiration technique. The outcome is IM injection of vaccine without complications.

PICO clinical questionsIM injection aspiration guideline
7. A nurse is administering a vaccine into an adult patient's deltoid. Based on the current evidence in the chapter, what should the nurse do about aspiration?
  • AAspirate for 5 to 10 seconds before injecting
  • ✓Do not aspirate, because aspiration is recommended only for the dorsogluteal site
  • CAspirate for 5 to 10 seconds only if the volume exceeds 2 mL
  • DDo not aspirate, because aspiration is no longer recommended at any injection site
Rationale

Current evidence supports no aspiration in the deltoid, ventrogluteal, and vastus lateralis sites, and aspiration only in the dorsogluteal site because of the close proximity of the gluteal artery.

Watch for

(D) overcorrects. The dorsogluteal exception survives, though researchers recommend that the dorsogluteal site not be used if possible. (A) is the older guideline that this chapter uses as its example of practice lagging behind evidence.

PICO clinical questionsIM injection aspiration guideline
8. Which statements about IM injection site selection are consistent with the chapter's clinical practice guideline? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓The vastus lateralis is the preferred site for infants
  • ✓The ventrogluteal or deltoid site is appropriate for adults
  • CThe dorsogluteal site is preferred for adults because it tolerates large volumes
  • DAspiration for 5 to 10 seconds is required in the vastus lateralis in infants
  • ✓Large volumes of 2 to 5 mL should be given in the ventrogluteal site in adults
Rationale

Infants receive injections in the vastus lateralis; toddlers and children in the vastus lateralis or deltoid; adults in the ventrogluteal or deltoid. Large volumes of 2 to 5 mL go in the ventrogluteal site for adults, and small volumes of 2 mL or less go in the deltoid for toddlers, children, and adults.

Watch for

(C) is the specific practice error the chapter documents nurses still making. Nurses over-used the dorsogluteal site despite evidence favoring the ventrogluteal. (D) applies the outdated aspiration rule to a site where current evidence says not to aspirate.

PICO clinical questionsIM injection aspiration guideline
9. A study by Thomas and colleagues reported what finding about aspiration practice, and what point does the chapter use it to make?
  • A90% of nurses were still aspirating after IM injections 74% of the time, showing that the guideline itself was flawed
  • B74% of nurses had adopted the no-aspiration guideline within one year, showing that dissemination in nursing is efficient
  • C74% of nurses reported complications from IM injections, showing the need for a new comparison intervention
  • ✓74% of nurses were still aspirating after IM injections 90% of the time, showing that evidence existing is not the same as evidence being used
Rationale

74% of nurses were still aspirating after IM injections 90% of the time, and researchers therefore recommended additional education in nursing programs and continuing education. This is the chapter's central point about the gap between available evidence and used evidence.

Watch for

(A) inverts the two percentages. 74% is the proportion of nurses; 90% is how often those nurses aspirated.

Stetler model of research utilizationthe five phases
10. A nurse using the Stetler Model critically appraises the retrieved reports and finds only three small, methodologically weak studies. According to the model, what happens next?
  • AThe nurse proceeds to comparative evaluation and weighs feasibility despite the weak evidence
  • BThe nurse combines the weak studies into a meta-analysis to strengthen them
  • ✓The findings are considered inadequate for use in practice and the process stops
  • DThe nurse proceeds to translation and applies the findings on a trial basis
Rationale

In Phase II, Validation, the reports are critically appraised for scientific soundness. If the studies are limited in number, weak, or both, the findings are considered inadequate for use in practice and the process stops.

Watch for

(A) reflects the reflex that a model is a checklist you always run to the end. Stetler builds in a stop. (B) borrows a true statement from Phase II, that a systematic review, meta-analysis, or meta-synthesis greatly strengthens the evidence, and misapplies it as a way around a failed appraisal.

Stetler model of research utilizationthe five phases
11. After reading a synthesis on delirium, a nurse changes no order and no procedure, but now understands why her older postoperative patients become confused and analyzes her unit's practice dynamics differently. Under the Stetler Model, this is which kind of application?
  • ✓Cognitive
  • BInstrumental
  • CSymbolic
  • DComparative
Rationale

Phase IV, Translation/Application, describes cognitive, instrumental, and symbolic application. Cognitive application means the research base is a means of modifying a way of thinking or one's appreciation of an issue, such as improving understanding of a situation or allowing analysis of practice dynamics.

Watch for

(D) is not a kind of application at all. Comparative evaluation is the name of Phase III, and reusing that word as an application type is the easiest way to be pulled off the three real options.

Stetler model of research utilizationcomparative evaluation and decision making
12. Which items are the four parts of Phase III, Comparative Evaluation/Decision Making, in the Stetler Model? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Substantiation of the evidence
  • BPilot testing of the research-based protocol
  • ✓Fit of the evidence with the healthcare setting
  • ✓Feasibility of using the findings
  • ✓Concerns with current practice
Rationale

Phase III has four parts: substantiation of the evidence, fit of the evidence with the healthcare setting, feasibility of using the findings, and concerns with current practice.

(B) belongs to the Iowa Model, where research-based protocols are pilot-tested before adoption. Importing an Iowa step into a Stetler phase is the most common error on this content.

Stetler model of research utilizationcomparative evaluation and decision making
13. A nurse assessing feasibility under the Stetler Model is applying the model's three Rs. What are they?
  • ARelevance, reliability, and rigor
  • BResearch, review, and revision
  • CResults, reporting, and replication
  • ✓Risks, resources, and readiness
Rationale

Feasibility is assessed as potential risks, resources needed, and readiness of those involved.

Watch for

The three Rs are the feasibility test only, which is one of the four parts of Phase III. They are not a summary of the whole model.

Stetler model of research utilizationcomparative evaluation and decision making
14. An evidence-based practice team finds sound evidence supporting a change, but the change is complex and would involve pharmacy, respiratory therapy, and nursing, and coordinating it will take considerable time. Which Phase III decision fits this situation?
  • ✓To consider using the research evidence
  • BTo use the research evidence
  • CNot to use the research evidence
  • DTo return to Phase II for revalidation
Rationale

Phase III allows three decisions: to use the evidence, to consider using it, or not to use it. To consider using it applies when a change is complex and involves multiple disciplines, so more time is needed to coordinate.

Watch for

Students who remember only a use or do-not-use binary force this item into (B) or (C). Not to use is reserved for evidence that is not strong, or for risks or costs of change that are too high in comparison with the benefits.

Iowa model of evidence-based practicetriggering issues and opportunities
15. A hospital's risk-management reports show a cluster of medication errors on night shift, and the unit council decides to search the literature. In the Iowa Model, this beginning is best described as which of the following?
  • AA knowledge-focused opportunity
  • ✓A problem-focused trigger
  • CStetler Phase I preparation
  • DAn expanded philosophy of care
Rationale

Problem-focused triggers are clinical problems and data from risk management, process improvement, benchmarking, and financial reports.

Watch for

(C) is the near-neighbor across models. An individual nurse deciding whether to apply findings is Stetler; a trigger arising from agency risk-management data is Iowa.

Iowa model of evidence-based practicetriggering issues and opportunities
16. Which of the following would the Iowa Model classify as knowledge-focused opportunities? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓New research findings published on an intervention the unit uses
  • ✓A change in national agency standards and guidelines
  • CBenchmarking data showing the unit's catheter-associated infection rate is above its peers
  • ✓An updated professional model of care, which is a Magnet recognition requirement
  • ✓A question raised by the institutional standards' committee
Rationale

Knowledge-focused opportunities include new research findings, changes in national agency or organizational standards and guidelines, an expanded philosophy of care, an updated professional model of care (a Magnet recognition requirement), and questions from the institutional standards' committee.

Watch for

(C) is benchmarking data, which the chapter lists as a problem-focused trigger alongside risk management, process improvement, and financial reports. Sorting a given example into the right kind of trigger is the whole point of this distinction.

Iowa model of evidence-based practicefrom pilot testing to adoption
17. An Iowa Model team has confirmed that its topic is an agency priority and has developed a research-based protocol for oral care in ventilated patients. What comes next, before the protocol is adopted?
  • AImplement the protocol agency-wide and monitor outcomes through quality improvement
  • BSubmit the protocol to the institutional standards' committee for symbolic approval
  • CConduct additional studies, because a protocol cannot be built from existing evidence
  • ✓Pilot-test the protocol and evaluate its impact on patient care, personnel, and the agency
Rationale

Research-based protocols, algorithms, guidelines, and policies are pilot-tested and then evaluated for impact on patient care, personnel, and the agency. If outcomes are favorable, the change is adopted, integrated, and sustained, with key outcomes monitored through quality improvement.

Watch for

(A) skips the pilot. Agency-wide rollout and QI monitoring are what follow a favorable pilot, not what replace it. (C) applies only when the evidence found is inadequate, which is not the case here.

choosing between the EBP modelsStetler versus Iowa
18. Which statement correctly distinguishes the Stetler Model from the Iowa Model?
  • ✓Stetler is a user-initiated, conscious critical-thinking process organized in five phases, while Iowa is an agency-level model that begins with triggering issues or opportunities
  • BStetler is an agency-level model organized around triggers, while Iowa is an individual nurse's critical-thinking process
  • CStetler applies only at the institutional level, while Iowa applies only to individual practitioners
  • DBoth models require pilot testing before any decision about using the evidence is made
Rationale

The intent of the Stetler Model is to make using research evidence in practice a conscious, critical thinking process that is initiated by the user, organized in five phases. The Iowa Model promotes the implementation of EBP by nurses in clinical agencies and begins with triggering issues or opportunities.

Watch for

(C) half-remembers a real detail and inverts it. Stetler evidence can be used at the institutional level, producing policies, algorithms, procedures, protocols, and formal programs, or at the individual level by RNs, educators, and policy makers. It is not restricted to institutions.
