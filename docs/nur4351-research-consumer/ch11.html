probability and statistical significancedecision theory
1. A researcher comparing an intervention group and a control group begins from the assumption that both groups are components of the same population relative to the study variables. Which framework is being applied, and how is that assumption stated?
  • AProbability theory, stated as the effect size
  • ✓Decision theory, stated as the null hypothesis
  • CProbability theory, stated as the research hypothesis
  • DDecision theory, stated as the confidence interval
Rationale

Decision theory assumes that all of the groups in a study are components of the same population relative to the variables under study, an assumption expressed as the null hypothesis, which states there is no difference between or among the groups.

Watch for

Probability theory is the other named framework, but it is used to explain the extent of a relationship or the probability that an event will occur or be accurately predicted. It is not the same-population assumption, and it is up to the researcher to provide evidence for a genuine difference.

2. An analysis reports p = 0.23. What does that value mean?
  • AThe two groups differ by 23 percentage points
  • B23% of participants fell outside the confidence interval
  • ✓There is a 23% probability that the outcome occurs
  • DThe result is significant at the 0.23 level
Rationale

Probability ranges from 0 to 1 and can be expressed as a decimal or a percentage; p = 0.23 means a 23% probability that the outcome occurs.

probability and statistical significancealpha and p values
3. In a published study, which value reveals the risk of a Type I error in that particular study?
  • AThe effect size
  • BThe power level
  • CBeta
  • ✓The probability (p) value
Rationale

The chapter's summary is explicit: the probability (p) value reveals the risk of a Type I error in a particular study.

Watch for

Students routinely attach p to Type II risk. Type II risk is the province of power and beta; p speaks only to the chance of calling something significant when it is not.

probability and statistical significancealpha and p values
4. Which value, established before the study begins, reveals whether the probability value met the cutoff point?
  • ✓The alpha (level of statistical significance)
  • BThe probability (p) value
  • CThe coefficient of determination
  • DThe standard deviation
Rationale

The alpha value, set before the study, reveals whether the probability value met the cutoff point; alpha is the probability level at which results are judged to indicate a statistically significant difference between groups.

Watch for

This is the mirror of the previous item. Sort it by timing: alpha is chosen in advance and is a threshold; p comes out of the analysis and is a risk estimate.

5. Which statements about the alpha level in a nursing study are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓It is set before data collection begins
  • ✓The level of significance selected for most nursing studies is 0.05
  • CIt is calculated from the sample data once the analysis is complete
  • ✓If p is less than or equal to alpha, the groups are considered significantly different, that is, members of different populations
  • ✓It reveals whether the probability value met the cutoff point
Rationale

Alpha is set before data collection, is 0.05 for most nursing studies, and functions as the cutoff: p at or below it means the groups are considered significantly different and therefore members of different populations.

probability and statistical significancedegrees of significance
6. A researcher set alpha at 0.05 and obtained p = 0.051. The report states the findings "approached significance." How should a consumer of research evaluate this statement?
  • AAcceptable, because 0.051 rounds to 0.05
  • BAcceptable, provided the sample was large enough for adequate power
  • ✓Inappropriate; decision theory requires an absolute cutoff, so the groups are not significantly different
  • DInappropriate; the researcher should reset alpha to 0.10 and reanalyze
Rationale

Decision theory requires that the cutoff point selected for a study be absolute, and the chapter calls it inappropriate to state that findings approached significance at p = 0.051 when alpha was 0.05. Under decision theory that result means the groups are not significantly different.

Watch for

"Approached significance" sounds like careful hedging, which is exactly why it survives in published abstracts. The chapter names this phrase specifically as the error.

probability and statistical significancedegrees of significance
7. A study set alpha at 0.05. One comparison yields p = 0.001 and another yields p = 0.05. Which statements are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Both comparisons are statistically significant
  • BThe p = 0.001 comparison is more significant than the p = 0.05 comparison
  • ✓The level of significance is dichotomous, so there are no degrees of significance
  • ✓From a probability standpoint, the chance the groups are from the same population is 1 in 1000 at p = 0.001 and 5 in 100 at p = 0.05
  • EThe p = 0.05 comparison should be reported as approaching significance
Rationale

Once alpha is set at 0.05, a result of 0.001 is not considered more significant than 0.05 — the level of significance is dichotomous, with no degrees of significance. From a probability-theory perspective the two still differ in Type I error risk: 1 in 1000 versus 5 in 100.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex answer because the two p values genuinely do differ in Type I risk. The distinction the chapter draws is that they differ in risk, not in significance. And p = 0.05 with alpha at 0.05 is significant, not "approaching" anything.

type I and type II erroridentifying the error
8. A study concludes that a fall-prevention bundle significantly reduced falls. In fact the null hypothesis was true and there is no difference between the groups. What has occurred?
  • ✓A Type I error
  • BA Type II error
  • CA correct decision, because the p value met alpha
  • DA power failure, because power fell below 0.80
Rationale

A Type I error occurs when the null hypothesis is rejected when it is true — saying something is significant when it is not.

Watch for

The tell is which hypothesis was true and what the researcher did with it. Rejecting a true null is Type I; the significance of the p value is irrelevant to naming the error.

type I and type II erroridentifying the error
9. A study reports no significant difference in pressure injury rates between two turning schedules. A later, larger trial establishes that a real difference does exist. Which error did the first study make, and by which symbol is it represented?
  • AType I error, symbolized by alpha
  • BType I error, symbolized by beta
  • CType II error, symbolized by alpha
  • ✓Type II error, symbolized by beta
Rationale

A Type II error occurs when the null hypothesis is regarded as true but is in fact false — saying something is not significant when it is. It is symbolized by beta.

Watch for

This is the mirror of the previous item. Alpha belongs to Type I, beta to Type II; pairing the right error name with the wrong Greek letter is the standard half-credit trap.

type I and type II erroridentifying the error
10. In 5% of statistical tests, the value of one of the groups can be expected to fall outside the 95% range and yet still belong to the same population. What is this situation called?
  • AA nondirectional result
  • ✓A Type I error
  • CA Type II error
  • DAn effect size of zero
Rationale

If groups come from the same population their values fall within the 95% range; a value outside it leads to concluding they belong to different populations. In 5% of tests that conclusion is wrong while the groups still belong to the same population, which is a Type I error.

Watch for

The 5% here is the same 5% as alpha = 0.05. That is why the level of significance is what indicates Type I risk.

type I and type II erroralpha and the error tradeoff
11. A researcher changes the level of significance from 0.05 to 0.01 to be more conservative. What is the consequence?
  • ABoth Type I and Type II risk decrease
  • BBoth Type I and Type II risk increase
  • ✓Type I risk decreases and Type II risk increases
  • DType I risk increases and Type II risk decreases
Rationale

The risk of a Type I error is greater at 0.05 (5 in 100) than at 0.01 (1 in 100), while the risk of a Type II error is greater at 0.01 than at 0.05. Tightening alpha trades one error for the other.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex answer because "more conservative" sounds like it should be safer in every direction. Any option claiming a stricter alpha reduces both errors is wrong.

type I and type II erroralpha and the error tradeoff
12. Compared with an alpha of 0.01, an alpha of 0.05 carries which risk profile?
  • ALess Type I risk and more Type II risk
  • ✓More Type I risk, at 5 in 100 rather than 1 in 100, and less Type II risk
  • CMore Type I risk and more Type II risk
  • DIdentical risk, because significance is dichotomous
Rationale

At alpha = 0.05 the chance of a Type I error is 5 in 100, versus 1 in 100 at 0.01; Type II risk moves in the opposite direction and is greater at 0.01.

Watch for

This is the same tradeoff run backward from the previous item. (D) misapplies a true statement — significance is dichotomous, but that says nothing about error risk across different alpha levels.

type I and type II errortype II risk in nursing research
13. The chapter concludes that the risk of a Type II error is often high in nursing studies. Which reasons does it give? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Many nursing studies are conducted with small samples
  • ✓Instruments frequently do not accurately and precisely measure the variables
  • ✓Multiple variables interact, and examining only a few can let small differences between groups be overlooked
  • ✓Type II errors are often caused by flaws in the research methods
  • ENursing studies conventionally set alpha at 0.001 rather than 0.05
Rationale

The chapter attributes Type II errors to flaws in research methods, and names small samples, imprecise instruments, and multiple interacting variables of which only a few are examined as the reasons Type II risk is often high in nursing studies.

(E) is false on its own terms — 0.05 is the conventional nursing alpha — and it also reverses the tradeoff, since a stricter alpha would raise Type II risk rather than being an argument against it.

power and effect sizepower analysis
15. Which statement defines power?
  • AThe probability level at which results are judged to indicate a statistically significant difference
  • ✓The probability that a statistical test will detect a significant difference that exists
  • CThe degree to which the phenomenon is present in the population
  • DThe probability that the null hypothesis is rejected when it is true
Rationale

Power is the probability that a statistical test will detect a significant difference that exists.

Watch for

Every distractor is a real chapter definition attached to the wrong term — (A) is alpha, (C) is effect size, (D) is a Type I error.

power and effect sizeeffect size and sample size
16. A researcher planning a nursing intervention study can expect only a small effect size. What sample size does the chapter indicate is often needed to detect a significant difference?
  • A30 or more
  • B50 or more
  • C100 or more
  • ✓200 or more
Rationale

In many nursing studies only small effect sizes can be expected, and with a small effect a sample of 200 or more is often needed to detect a significant difference.

Watch for

Small effect sizes themselves arise from small samples, weak study designs, and instruments that measure only large changes — so the fix is not simply to accept the small effect and proceed.

power and effect sizeinterpreting nonsignificant findings
17. A study fails to reject the null hypothesis and reports a power level of 0.65. How should a consumer of research respond?
  • ✓Question the validity of the nonsignificant findings, because power is below 0.80
  • BAccept the nonsignificant findings, because power above 0.50 is adequate
  • CConclude that a Type I error occurred
  • DConclude that alpha was set too low
Rationale

The power level should be discussed in studies that fail to reject the null hypothesis, and if power is below 0.80 you need to question the validity of the nonsignificant findings. The minimum acceptable power is 0.80.

Watch for

(C) inverts the error. A nonsignificant finding that may be wrong is a candidate Type II error, not a Type I error.

the normal curve and tailednessstandard deviation landmarks
18. A student claims that the height data collected from one nursing class "fits the normal curve exactly." Why is that claim inaccurate?
  • ✓The normal curve is a theoretical frequency distribution of all possible values in a population, and no real distribution exactly fits it
  • BThe normal curve applies only to nominal-level data
  • CThe normal curve requires a sample of at least 200 before it can be applied
  • DThe normal curve describes samples rather than populations, so populations fit it less well
Rationale

The normal curve is a theoretical frequency distribution of all possible values in a population; however, no real distribution exactly fits the normal curve. It was developed by Johann Gauss in 1795 at age 18.

Watch for

Because so much of inferential statistics depends on the normal curve, students treat it as a description of real data. It is a model, not a measurement.

the normal curve and tailednessstandard deviation landmarks
19. Which statements about the normal curve and standard deviations are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Approximately 68% of values fall within 1 SD above and below the mean
  • BExactly 95% of values fall within 2 SDs above and below the mean
  • ✓95.5% of values fall between -2 and +2 SDs
  • ✓99% of values fall within 2.58 SDs
  • ✓Each tail beyond 1.96 SDs from the mean contains 2.5% of values
Rationale

The landmarks are 68% within 1 SD, 95% within 1.96 SDs, 95.5% within 2 SDs, and 99% within 2.58 SDs; the tails below -1.96 and above +1.96 SDs hold 2.5% each.

(B) is the fine-grained inversion. 95% goes with 1.96 SDs, not 2 SDs; a full 2 SDs captures 95.5%. Both numbers are in the chapter and the exam can use either.

the normal curve and tailednessone-tailed versus two-tailed tests
20. A researcher hypothesizes that anxiety scores will differ between two groups but does not predict which group will score higher. Which test is indicated, and where does the 5% lie?
  • AOne-tailed, with all 5% in the upper tail
  • BOne-tailed, with all 5% in the lower tail
  • ✓Two-tailed, with 2.5% in each tail
  • DTwo-tailed, with 5% in each tail
Rationale

A two-tailed test is the analysis of a nondirectional hypothesis, and the 5% is split across both tails at 2.5% each.

Watch for

(D) doubles the alpha without meaning to. Splitting 5% across two tails yields 2.5% per tail, not 5% per tail.

the normal curve and tailednessone-tailed versus two-tailed tests
21. A researcher predicts specifically that the intervention group will have lower anxiety scores than the control group. Which statement about the appropriate test is accurate?
  • AA two-tailed test is required, because comparing two groups always splits the 5% across both tails
  • BA one-tailed test is indicated, but it carries less power than a two-tailed test
  • CA one-tailed test is indicated, and it increases the possibility of a Type II error
  • ✓A one-tailed test is indicated, and it is more powerful, decreasing the possibility of a Type II error
Rationale

A one-tailed test is used when the hypothesis is directional, with all 5% in a single tail; one-tailed tests are uniformly more powerful than two-tailed tests, decreasing the possibility of a Type II error.

Watch for

(B) and (C) both assume that concentrating the alpha in one tail is a concession. It is the opposite — the cost of a one-tailed test is that the researcher must have sufficient knowledge of the variables to predict which tail the difference will fall in.

descriptive statisticsmeasures of central tendency
22. A researcher summarizes the blood types of a sample. Which measure of central tendency is appropriate?
  • ✓The mode
  • BThe median
  • CThe mean
  • DThe standard deviation
Rationale

The mode is the numerical value or score that occurs with greatest frequency and is the appropriate measure for nominal data.

Watch for

The mode does not necessarily indicate the center of the data set, which is why students reject it — but for nominal categories no other measure of central tendency applies.

descriptive statisticsmeasures of central tendency
23. A study's income data contain several extreme outliers. Which measure of central tendency is the research report most likely to present, and why?
  • AThe mean, because it uses every score in the data set
  • ✓The median, because it is not affected by extreme scores or outliers
  • CThe mode, because outliers cannot be the most frequent value
  • DThe mean, because outliers cancel out above and below
Rationale

If the study has outliers, the mean is most affected by these, and the median might be the measure of central tendency included in the research report.

(A) is true as a description of the mean and is exactly why it fails here — using every score is what lets a handful of extreme values drag it.

descriptive statisticsmeasures of central tendency
24. Which statements about measures of central tendency are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓In a perfect normal distribution the mean, median, and mode are equal
  • ✓The median is the 50th percentile of an ungrouped frequency distribution
  • CThe mean is the measure least affected by extreme scores
  • ✓With an even number of scores the median is the average of the two middle scores and may not be an actual score in the data set
  • EA distribution with more than two modes is described as bimodal
Rationale

All three measures coincide in a perfect normal distribution; the median is the exact center, or 50th percentile, and with an even number of scores it is the average of the two middle scores, so it may not be a member of the data set.

(C) reverses the outlier rule — the mean is the measure most affected by extreme scores. (E) misnames the count: two modes is bimodal, more than two is multimodal.

descriptive statisticsdispersion and standard deviation
25. A sample has a mean score of 5.28 with an SD of 2.22. Approximately 68% of the sample falls between which two values?
  • A0.84 and 9.72
  • B2.22 and 7.50
  • ✓3.06 and 7.50
  • D5.28 and 7.50
Rationale

One SD below the mean is 5.28 minus 2.22 = 3.06 and one SD above is 7.50, so about 68% of the sample falls in that interval. Two SDs would give 0.84 to 9.72.

Watch for

(A) is the 2 SD interval, which holds about 95.5%, not 68%. Match the percentage in the stem to the number of SDs before computing.

descriptive statisticsdispersion and standard deviation
26. A report gives a mean pain score of 5.28 with an SD of 2.22. What does the standard deviation tell the reader?
  • AThe distance between the highest and lowest scores in the sample
  • BThe proportion of participants who scored above the mean
  • CThe interval within which the population mean falls 95% of the time
  • ✓The average deviation of a value from the mean, indicating the degree of error that would result if the mean alone were used to interpret the data
Rationale

The standard deviation is the square root of the variance and is the average difference or deviation value; it indicates the degree of error that would result if the mean alone were used to interpret the data.

Watch for

(C) describes the 95% confidence interval, which is a different reported statistic even though both accompany a mean.

descriptive statisticsconfidence intervals and z-scores
27. Which statements about confidence intervals and z-scores are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓A 95% CI indicates that 95% of the time the population mean would fall within that interval
  • BA 95% CI indicates that 95% of the participants' scores fall within that interval
  • ✓Confidence intervals can be calculated around parameters such as correlation coefficients and t-test values
  • ✓The mean expressed as a z-score is 0
  • EA z-score of -2 indicates a score 2 SDs above the mean
Rationale

The 95% CI indicates that 95% of the time the population mean would fall within this interval; CIs are usually calculated for 95% and 99% and can be built around any population parameter, including correlation coefficients and t-test values. A z-score expresses deviation from the mean in SD units, so the mean itself is 0.

Watch for

(B) is the standard misreading of a CI — it is a statement about where the population mean sits, not about where participants sit. (E) drops the sign: values above the mean are positive z-scores, below are negative.

correlation and predictioninterpreting r
28. A study reports r = -0.72 between hours of sleep and perceived fatigue. How should this be interpreted?
  • AA weak relationship, because the coefficient is negative
  • BA moderate relationship in which both variables change in the same direction
  • CA strong relationship establishing that sleep loss causes fatigue
  • ✓A strong inverse relationship, in which high scores on one variable occur with low scores on the other
Rationale

Strength is judged by the absolute value: below 0.3 is weak, 0.3 to 0.5 moderate, and above 0.5 strong. A negative correlation is inverse, meaning a high score on one variable occurs with a low score on the other.

Watch for

(A) is the single most common correlation error — reading the minus sign as weakness. Strength decreases as r approaches 0 from either direction, so -0.72 is strong.

correlation and predictioninterpreting r
29. Which statements about the Pearson product-moment correlation are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓It examines the relationship between two variables measured on a single sample
  • ✓It indicates both the nature and the magnitude of the relationship
  • CIt establishes whether variable A leads to or causes variable B
  • ✓Its values range from -1 to +1, with 0 indicating no relationship
  • ✓It is symmetrical, giving no indication of the direction of the relationship
Rationale

Pearson correlation is an inferential technique for bivariate correlations, giving the nature (positive or negative) and the magnitude of a relationship between two variables measured on one sample, with values from -1 to +1.

(C) is the causal reading. Because correlational outcomes are symmetrical, it is not possible to establish whether A causes B or B causes A — the analysis gives no indication of direction at all.

correlation and predictionexplained variance and prediction
30. A study reports r = 0.6 for anxiety and depression. What percentage of the variance is explained by the relationship between the two variables?
  • A6%
  • ✓36%
  • C60%
  • D64%
Rationale

Explained variance is indicated by r squared and expressed as a percentage: r = 0.6 gives r squared = 0.36, or 36% explained variance.

Watch for

(C) reads r itself as a percentage. The correlation and the proportion of variance it explains are different numbers, and the second is always the smaller for any r between 0 and 1.

correlation and predictionexplained variance and prediction
31. A regression model is reported with R squared giving 26% and model significance tested by ANOVA, F(4,79) = 7.051, p < 0.001. What does the 26% represent?
  • ✓The percentage of variance in the dependent variable explained by the model
  • BThe percentage of participants whose outcomes the model predicted correctly
  • CThe correlation between the predictors and the outcome
  • DThe probability that the model result reflects a Type I error
Rationale

In regression, R squared multiplied by 100% gives the percentage of variance explained; the chapter's worked example reports 26%, with the model tested for significance by ANOVA at F(4,79) = 7.051, p < 0.001.

Watch for

(D) confuses the model's explanatory power with the p value. Type I risk here is carried by p < 0.001, not by R squared. Standardized beta coefficients, not R squared, identify which individual predictors are significant.

choosing a statistical testparametric versus nonparametric
33. Body weight in kilograms is measured for all participants, but the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test shows the values are not normally distributed. Which approach is appropriate?
  • AParametric, because weight is ratio-level data
  • BParametric, because the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test checks only for outliers
  • ✓Nonparametric, because parametric analysis requires interval or ratio data and a normal distribution
  • DNonparametric, because ratio-level data are always analyzed nonparametrically
Rationale

Parametric analyses are conducted when variables are at the interval or ratio level and the values are normally distributed; both conditions are required. Nonparametric analyses are conducted when variables are at the nominal and ordinal levels.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex answer because level of measurement is the criterion students memorize first. Interval or ratio data that are not normally distributed do not automatically qualify for parametric analysis.

choosing a statistical testindependent versus paired groups
34. Each participant's pretest score serves as the control for that same participant's posttest score. How are these observations classified?
  • AIndependent, because each participant contributes one score to each condition
  • BIndependent, because no randomization to separate arms occurred
  • CPaired, but only if the participants were also randomly assigned
  • ✓Paired (dependent), because the observations are related to one another
Rationale

Paired or dependent groups exist when participants or observations are related in some way to the selection of other participants or observations, which includes a participant serving as their own control with the pretest as the control.

Watch for

Misclassifying a pretest-posttest design as independent samples changes the correct test. Paired designs also include matched pairs and twins with one in each group, and groups sometimes matched on age and severity of illness.

choosing a statistical testindependent versus paired groups
35. Participants are randomly assigned to either a treatment group or a control group, and no participant appears in both. How are these samples classified?
  • ✓Independent, because the selection of one participant is unrelated to the selection of the others
  • BPaired, because both groups were drawn from the same recruitment pool
  • CIndependent, only if the groups are also matched on age and severity of illness
  • DPaired, because random assignment produces equivalent matched groups
Rationale

Independent groups exist when the selection of one study participant is unrelated to the selection of other participants, as when participants are randomly assigned to treatment and control groups.

Watch for

This is the mirror of the pretest-posttest item. (C) and (D) both treat matching as something random assignment accomplishes; matching is a deliberate pairing procedure and is what makes groups dependent.

36. Two independent groups are compared on mean serum sodium, a ratio-level variable that is normally distributed. Which test is appropriate?
  • AChi-square test of independence
  • ✓t-test
  • CANOVA
  • DPost hoc analysis
Rationale

The t-test is used to examine differences between two groups with interval or ratio level data.

(C) is the reflex answer for anyone who has learned ANOVA as the general difference test. ANOVA is for three or more groups; using the wrong one of this pair is the classic error in both directions.

choosing a statistical testmatching the test to the comparison
37. Three clinic sites are compared on whether patients did or did not return for follow-up, a yes or no outcome. Which test is appropriate?
  • At-test, because the outcome has two categories
  • BANOVA, because there are three groups
  • ✓Chi-square test of independence, because the outcome is nominal
  • DPearson product-moment correlation, because two variables are involved
Rationale

The chi-square test of independence is used with three or more groups when the data are nominal or ordinal; ANOVA and ANCOVA require interval or ratio data.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex answer because "three or more groups" points straight at ANOVA. Number of groups alone never settles the test — level of measurement has to be checked with it, and nominal data rule out the parametric options.

choosing a statistical testmatching the test to the comparison
38. A researcher compares four groups using ANOVA and reports a significant F value. Which statements are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓ANOVA tests for differences in variance, not in means alone
  • BThe significant F value identifies which specific pair of groups differs
  • ✓Post hoc analyses are conducted to determine the location of the differences among groups
  • ✓Bonferroni correction, Newman-Keuls, Tukey HSD, Scheffe, and Dunnett are post hoc options
  • ✓Between-group variance is the variation of the group means around the grand mean
Rationale

ANOVA tests for differences in variance, partitioned into within-group variance and between-group variance around the grand mean; with more than two groups it is not possible to determine from the ANOVA where the significant differences occur, so post hoc analyses are conducted.

Watch for

(B) is the single most tested misreading of ANOVA. A significant F tells you that the groups differ, not which ones — with only two groups the location would be obvious, which is precisely why post hoc tests exist for three or more.
