mixed methods philosophypragmatism as the middle ground
1. A research team states that it will use whatever combination of methods best answers the clinic's question rather than committing in advance to an objective or a subjective worldview. Which philosophical position does this describe?
  • APostpositivism
  • BConstructivism
  • CPositivism
  • ✓Pragmatism
Rationale

Pragmatism focuses on solving problems by whatever methods fit the problem or question; researchers have exchanged the dichotomy of positivism and constructivism for the middle ground of pragmatism.

Watch for

Postpositivism is the objective view of quantitative researchers and constructivism the subjective view of qualitative researchers. Mixed methods is explicitly neither.

mixed methods philosophypragmatism as the middle ground
2. A clinic manager concerned about missed follow-up appointments works with a researcher who runs focus groups and administers an exit survey. Which statement best explains the choice of design?
  • ✓The goal was to obtain the information they needed to improve the attendance rate, which is consistent with a pragmatic approach
  • BFocus groups are required before any survey instrument may be administered
  • CQualitative data are considered more trustworthy than survey data in clinic settings
  • DA mixed methods design was needed to satisfy the constructivist requirements of the study
Rationale

Pragmatists want to obtain the information they believe they need to resolve an issue, and the chapter names improving the attendance rate as the goal that made mixed methods the fitting choice.

Watch for

(D) misassigns the philosophy. Constructivism is the subjective view of qualitative researchers, and a mixed methods study is neither constructivist nor postpositivistic.

mixed methods designsexploratory versus explanatory sequential
4. A team knows little about how adolescents with cystic fibrosis judge the quality of their healthcare services. They interview 25 adolescents and young adults, content-analyze the transcripts, convert the determinants they identify into questionnaire items, and survey 113 respondents. Which design is this?
  • AConvergent concurrent
  • ✓Exploratory sequential
  • CExplanatory sequential
  • DParallel
Rationale

An exploratory sequential strategy begins with the collection and analysis of qualitative data, followed by the collection of quantitative data, and is often selected for underresearched topics so the first phase can inform the quantitative phase.

Watch for

Explanatory sequential is the reflex answer for anyone who treats explore and explain as interchangeable. Exploratory means the qualitative phase comes first.

mixed methods designsexploratory versus explanatory sequential
5. Researchers evaluating an intensive outpatient PTSD program measure symptom change in 30 women and 18 men with a mean age of 43.48 years, then interview participants about which parts of the treatment they found helpful. Which design is this?
  • ✓Explanatory sequential
  • BExploratory sequential
  • CConvergent concurrent
  • DParallel
Rationale

In an explanatory sequential strategy the researcher collects and analyzes quantitative data and then collects and analyzes qualitative data to explain the quantitative findings.

Watch for

This is the mirror of the cystic fibrosis item. Anchor on the word explain — the numbers already exist and the qualitative arm explains them, so quantitative came first.

mixed methods designsexploratory versus explanatory sequential
6. The qualitative arm of the explanatory sequential PTSD study used grounded theory coding. What was the stated reason?
  • AThe researchers lacked resources for a comparison or control group
  • ✓The goal of the qualitative arm was to generate a tentative theory
  • CGrounded theory is required whenever quantitative data are collected first
  • DGrounded theory produces countable codes suitable for conversion to quantitative data
Rationale

The qualitative arm used grounded theory coding because the goal was to generate a tentative theory.

Watch for

(A) is true of that study — the missing control group is why qualitative data were added to supplement the quantitative estimates of treatment effects — but it does not explain the choice of grounded theory coding in particular.

mixed methods designsconvergent concurrent design
7. In the PICU family-stress study, parents were interviewed and completed quantitative stress measures during a single interaction, and the transcripts were not analyzed before the quantitative phase. Why is the design classified as concurrent rather than sequential?
  • ABecause the qualitative component was emphasized over the quantitative
  • BBecause only nine parents from eight families were enrolled
  • ✓Because the sequence of analysis, not the sequence of contact, determines the design
  • DBecause both components addressed the same research question
Rationale

The interviews came first chronologically, but the transcripts were not analyzed before the quantitative phase and both types of data were collected during one interaction, which is what makes the design concurrent.

Watch for

(A) is a true statement about that study — the qualitative arm was emphasized and the quantitative findings were used to enrich the description — but emphasis is not what classifies the design.

mixed methods designsconvergent concurrent design
8. A researcher collects survey scores and interview narratives from a single sample during the same period and combines them only after each has been analyzed separately. Which statement about this study is accurate?
  • AIt is an explanatory sequential design, because the survey was analyzed first
  • BIt is an exploratory sequential design, because the interviews informed the survey
  • CIt is a convergent concurrent design, and integration must occur during data collection
  • ✓It is a convergent concurrent design, and integration occurs during the interpretation phase
Rationale

A convergent concurrent design uses a single sample, or two samples from the same population, and integration happens after the data are analyzed, during the interpretation phase.

Watch for

(C) is the near miss. Concurrent refers to when the data are collected, not to when the two arms are brought together.

mixed methods designsconvergent concurrent design
9. In a convergent concurrent study, the qualitative and quantitative arms yield the same findings. What is this called, and what does it mean?
  • ACross-validation, which means the two samples came from the same population
  • ✓Convergence, which strengthens the evidence
  • CCorroboration, which indicates the study should be repeated sequentially
  • DCongruence, which confirms that the sample was representative
Rationale

When the findings from the qualitative and quantitative methods yield the same findings, that is convergence, and it strengthens the evidence.

Watch for

Confirm, cross-validate, and corroborate are the stated purposes for selecting the design; convergence is the name for the result when the two arms agree.

mixed methods designsconvergent concurrent design
10. A convergent concurrent study's quantitative and qualitative findings do not agree. How does the chapter say researchers should treat this result?
  • AAs a failure of the design that requires the study to be repeated
  • BAs evidence that the qualitative arm introduced bias
  • ✓As an opportunity to provide a broader description of the problem
  • DAs grounds for reporting only the quantitative findings
Rationale

Different findings are not a failure; researchers may be able to use them to provide a broader description of the problem.

Watch for

Divergence is not invalidation. Any option calling non-matching results invalid contradicts the chapter.

11. Which statements about the three mixed methods designs are accurate? Select all that apply.

mixed methods challengesintegrating the data
13. A mixed methods team plans to decide how to combine its quantitative and qualitative findings after both data sets have been collected and analyzed. What does the chapter say about that plan?
  • AIt is appropriate, because integration cannot be planned before the data are seen
  • BIt is appropriate only in convergent concurrent designs
  • CIntegration should be handled by the qualitative arm, which interprets meaning
  • ✓Integration is the critical phase and should be given significant thought during development of the study, ideally in the proposal
Rationale

The capacity of mixed methods designs to answer the research questions depends on effective methods for integrating the data, so integration is the critical phase and should be planned during study development.

Watch for

In a convergent design integration does occur after both arms are analyzed, which makes (A) and (B) feel right. Performing integration late is not the same as planning it late.

mixed methods challengesintegrating the data
14. Which of the following are strategies the chapter names for integrating data in a mixed methods study? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Building the second phase on the findings of the first
  • BDiscarding the arm whose findings are weaker
  • ✓Expanding the view of a phenomenon
  • ✓Counting qualitative codes and converting them to quantitative data
  • ✓Strengthening support by interpreting the findings together
Rationale

The three named integration strategies are building the second phase on the first, expanding the view of a phenomenon, and strengthening support by interpreting the findings together; some researchers also convert data, counting qualitative codes or converting quantitative data into conceptual ideas or themes.

(B) contradicts the chapter's handling of divergent findings, which are used to broaden the description rather than to disqualify an arm.

mixed methods challengestime, funding, and team resources
15. Two teams report that their mixed methods studies cost more than a single-method study would have. One ran both arms at the same time; the other ran the qualitative phase first. Which explanation of their different burdens is accurate?
  • AThe concurrent team needed more elapsed time; the sequential team needed additional personnel
  • BBoth teams needed additional personnel, and neither needed more time than a single-method study
  • ✓The concurrent team needed additional personnel to conduct both arms at the same time; the sequential team needed more elapsed time because phase one had to be completed and analyzed first
  • DNeither design requires more resources than a single-method study once the proposal is funded
Rationale

Because of the complexity of concurrent designs, additional personnel are needed to conduct all aspects of both arms at the same time, whereas in sequential designs phase one must be completed and analyzed before phase two can be developed or revised.

Watch for

(A) is the straight inversion of the two burdens. Mixed methods studies also require a time commitment that may exceed single-method studies — one team collected data for 4 years — and additional time often translates into a need for greater financial support.

outcomes research foundationswhat outcomes research is
16. A hospital system asks a researcher to determine how patients' health status changed as a result of the nursing care they received. Which methodology is being described?
  • ✓Outcomes research, a rigorous scientific method focused on the end results of patient care
  • BAn explanatory sequential mixed methods study
  • CA quality improvement audit of individual nurses' documentation
  • DComparative effectiveness research comparing two nursing interventions
Rationale

Outcomes research is a rigorous scientific method focused on the end results of patient care; in nursing it focuses on how a patient's health status changes as a result of the nursing care received or the nursing services delivered.

Watch for

(C) is the reflex for anyone who equates outcomes with chart review. Outcomes research does not examine care at the individual nurse level. Comparative effectiveness research is a real term here, but it names AHRQ's current initiative rather than this study.

outcomes research foundationswhat outcomes research is
17. A researcher studies end results for patients with chronic conditions for whom cure is not always possible. According to AHRQ, which end results are relevant?
  • ACure rate, remission duration, and relapse rate
  • BStructure, process, and outcome
  • ✓Quality of life, functioning, symptom management, and mortality
  • DCost per case, length of stay, and readmission rate
Rationale

AHRQ frames end results as effects people experience and care about, such as a change in their ability to function; for individuals with chronic conditions these include quality of life, functioning, symptom management, and mortality.

Watch for

(D) names economic measures. Economic efficiency is what draws policy makers and insurers to outcomes research, but it is not AHRQ's list of end results.

outcomes research foundationslevel of analysis and complexity
18. Which study description matches the level of analysis used in outcomes research?
  • AEvaluating one nurse's effect on her six assigned patients
  • ✓Evaluating all of the nursing care provided to patients in a particular ICU
  • CEvaluating one patient's physiological response to a single intervention
  • DEvaluating the documentation accuracy of one unit's charge nurse
Rationale

Outcomes studies do not examine patient care at the individual nurse or individual patient level; they might instead examine all the nursing care provided to patients in a particular ICU.

Watch for

One of the chapter's own open questions is how to distinguish care provided by nurses from care provided by other professionals — a problem that only exists because the unit of analysis is the aggregate, not the individual.

22. A student claims that most published outcomes studies examine structure, process, and outcome together. Which response is accurate?
  • ACorrect, because Donabedian's theory is the dominating framework for outcomes research
  • BCorrect, because NDNQI participation requires all three
  • CIncorrect, because the theory calls for structure and outcome only
  • ✓Incorrect, because researchers have had little success including all three simultaneously
Rationale

A complete quality outcomes study requires simultaneous inclusion of all three and examination of the relationships among them, but researchers have had little success accomplishing this theoretical goal; the funding and cooperation required have not yet been realized.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex, and its premise is true — the theory does dominate outcomes research — which is exactly why students assume complete studies are the norm.

donabedian's quality frameworkstructural and nursing services variables
25. A hospital cites its Magnet designation and its nurses' autonomy in clinical practice as reasons its patients do well. How does the chapter classify these two features?
  • ✓Structural variables, because they are elements of organization that guide the processes of care
  • BProcess variables, because they describe how nurses deliver care
  • CNursing-sensitive patient outcomes, because they are influenced by nursing decisions
  • DNursing interventions, because they reflect the care delivered by nurses
Rationale

The organization of nursing care and nursing leadership affect nursing practice and in turn patient outcomes; autonomy in clinical nursing practice is a structural variable recognized as critically important, and Magnet designation is an identified autonomy-enabling structure, verifiable through the ANCC.

Watch for

(D) names a real category from this chapter — nursing interventions are things like a falls risk assessment or a pressure ulcer risk assessment — but autonomy sounds like something nurses do, which is why students file it under process.

26. A researcher studying nursing services variables would examine which of the following? Select all that apply.

nursing-sensitive outcomesattribution and definition
27. A nurse manager states that her unit's lower fall rate proves that nursing care caused the improvement. Which statement about nursing-sensitive patient outcomes corrects her?
  • ✓The outcome is influenced by nursing care decisions and actions, but it may be associated with nursing rather than caused by it
  • BThe outcome is caused by nursing care by definition, so her claim is accurate
  • CThe outcome is distal, so it cannot be linked to nursing at all
  • DThe outcome is a structural variable, so it reflects staffing rather than care
Rationale

Nursing-sensitive patient outcomes are considered sensitive because they are influenced by nursing care decisions and actions; an outcome may not be caused by nursing but is associated with nursing.

Watch for

The definition deliberately hedges — influenced by and associated with, never caused by. An option asserting nursing causation overstates what the term supports.

29. An outcomes study measures patients' signs and symptoms of disease at the completion of a nursing intervention. How is this outcome classified?

  • ✓Proximal, because it is close to the delivery of care

A proximal outcome is close to the delivery of care, and signs and symptoms of disease are the chapter's example.

nursing-sensitive outcomesproximal versus distal outcomes
30. An outcomes study measures patients' quality of life one year after discharge. Which statement about this outcome is accurate?
  • AIt is proximal, because quality of life is a nursing-sensitive measure
  • BIt is proximal, because it was measured on patients who received nursing care
  • CIt is distal, and it is easier to attribute to nursing than symptom measures are
  • ✓It is distal, and it is more influenced by external nontreatment factors
Rationale

A distal outcome is removed from proximity to the care or service received and is more influenced by external nontreatment or nonintervention factors; quality of life is the chapter's example.

Watch for

(C) inverts attribution. Being more influenced by external factors is exactly what makes distal outcomes harder, not easier, to attribute to nursing.

outcome measurement and evaluationadverse outcomes and standards of care
32. During an admission for pneumonia, a patient develops a central line infection that prolongs the hospital stay. Under the chapter's definition, how is this best classified?
  • AA distal outcome, because it developed after admission
  • BA process measure, because it reflects how care was provided
  • ✓An adverse outcome, because it results from medical management or a complication rather than the underlying disease
  • DA structural variable, because infection rates depend on staffing
Rationale

An adverse outcome is a consequence of injury caused by medical management or complication rather than by the underlying disease itself, and generally includes prolonged health care, resulting disability, or death.

Watch for

Deterioration caused by the pneumonia itself would not qualify. The injury has to come from the management or a complication, not from disease progression.

national quality initiativesmatching organization to role
36. A quality committee needs to consult the national standard-setting organization for healthcare performance measures. Which organization is it, and when was it founded?
  • AAHRQ, part of DHHS
  • ✓The National Quality Forum, founded in 1999
  • CPCORI, funded through the Recovery Act of 2009
  • DNDNQI, whose database was funded in 1998
Rationale

The National Quality Forum, founded in 1999, is the national standard-setting organization for healthcare performance measures, with a portfolio of performance measures, serious reportable events, and preferred practices.

Watch for

About one-third of the NQF's measures are patient outcomes such as mortality, readmissions, health functioning, depression, and experience of care. Note also that the NQF holds that quality is as much about influencing positive outcomes as about avoiding negative ones, even though most measures in use focus on failure to meet expected standards.
