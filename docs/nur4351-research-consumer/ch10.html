directness of measurementdirect versus indirect measures
1. A study's variables include waist circumference, resting heart rate, and days of hospitalization for one aim, and perceived coping and depression for a second aim. Which classification of these variables is correct?
  • ABoth sets are direct measures, because both are recorded with standardized procedures
  • BThe first set require indirect measures; the second set are direct measures
  • CBoth sets are indirect measures, because some error is present in every measurement strategy
  • ✓The first set are direct measures; the second set require indirect measures
Rationale

Direct measures determine the value of concrete factors such as waist circumference and heart rate, along with demographic variables such as days of hospitalization; abstract concepts such as coping and depression require indirect measures, which capture only some of the elements of the concept.

Watch for

(C) mistakes the universality of measurement error for a statement about directness. Even direct measures contain error, and that does not make them indirect.

directness of measurementdirect versus indirect measures
2. A researcher notes that the pain instrument chosen for a study might also be measuring aspects of anxiety and fear. Which characteristic of indirect measures does this illustrate?
  • AIndirect measures produce systematic rather than random error
  • BIndirect measures cannot be evaluated for reliability in the study sample
  • ✓Indirect measures may capture elements that are not part of the concept being measured
  • DIndirect measures are appropriate only when the concept can be measured concretely
Rationale

With indirect measures, the methods used may capture elements that are not part of the concept; the chapter's example is that pain measures might be measuring aspects of anxiety and fear in addition to pain.

Watch for

(D) inverts the definition. Concrete factors are exactly what direct measures handle; indirect measures exist because abstract concepts cannot be measured concretely.

levels of measurementnominal level and its rules
5. A researcher codes each participant's admitting diagnosis as kidney stone, gastric ulcer, or cholecystitis and enters the codes 1, 2, and 3 into the dataset. What level of measurement is this variable?
  • ✓Nominal
  • BOrdinal
  • CInterval
  • DRatio
Rationale

Medical diagnoses are nominal: the categories are exclusive and exhaustive but cannot be ordered, because a kidney stone cannot be rated higher than a gastric ulcer. The categories differ in quality but not in quantity.

Watch for

The numeric codes are what pull students upward to ordinal or interval. Codes assigned to categories are labels, not quantities, and nothing about 1, 2, 3 makes the diagnoses rankable.

6. A demographic form offers Single, Married, Divorced, and Widowed. A participant who was widowed and has since remarried cannot decide which box to mark. Which nominal rule is violated, and what fix does the chapter give?
  • AExhaustiveness; add an "Other" category
  • BExhaustiveness; redefine the variable as Current Marital Status
  • ✓Exclusiveness; redefine the variable as Current Marital Status
  • DOrderability; rank the categories by duration of the current status
Rationale

The exclusive rule requires that each datum fit into only one category, and the chapter's remedy for this exact example is to redefine the variable as Current Marital Status.

Watch for

(B) pairs the correct fix with the wrong rule. Two categories a person could legitimately claim is an exclusivity failure; a person for whom no category exists is an exhaustiveness failure.

Adding "Other" makes the categories exhaustive so that all data fit somewhere, but the chapter notes that these data are difficult to analyze and interpret.

levels of measurementordinal versus interval
9. Participants rate their shortness of breath on a 0-to-4 dyspnea scale. What level of measurement does this produce, and why?
  • ANominal, because the numbers function only as labels for categories
  • ✓Ordinal, because the ranks are ordered but the intervals between them are unequal
  • CInterval, because the numerals 0 through 4 are equally spaced on the form
  • DRatio, because 0 on the scale represents the absence of dyspnea
Rationale

The 0-to-4 dyspnea scale is ordinal: the ranks are ordered, but a smaller difference may exist between the ranks of 1 and 2 than between the ranks of 3 and 4. The same logic applies to pain ranked excruciating, severe, moderate, mild, none, where a greater difference may exist between mild and moderate than between excruciating and severe.

Watch for

(C) is the reflex because the printed numerals look evenly spaced. Equal spacing of the numerals is not equal spacing of the underlying attribute, and that is the entire ordinal-versus-interval distinction.

levels of measurementordinal versus interval
10. A study records each participant's body temperature in degrees Fahrenheit. What level of measurement is this, and why?
  • AOrdinal, because temperatures can be ranked from lowest to highest
  • BRatio, because a temperature of 0 degrees Fahrenheit indicates the absence of temperature
  • ✓Interval, because the intervals are equal but there is no absolute zero
  • DInterval, because temperature values can be multiplied and divided
Rationale

The difference between 70 and 80 degrees Fahrenheit is the same as the difference between 30 and 40 degrees, so the intervals are equal; but 0 degrees Fahrenheit does not indicate the absence of temperature, so there is no absolute zero.

Watch for

(D) attaches the wrong permission to the right level. The chapter's level table gives interval data addition and subtraction, and reserves multiplication and division for ratio data.

levels of measurementinterval versus ratio
11. A researcher reports that "Medication container A holds twice as much as medication container B." What property of the measurement level justifies that statement?
  • AEqual intervals between values, which is the defining addition of interval data
  • BExclusive and exhaustive categories, which nominal data require
  • CRanked ordering of values, which ordinal data provide
  • ✓An absolute zero point, which ratio data have
Rationale

Ratio-level data have an absolute zero, and that is what permits ratio statements such as one container holding twice as much as another; weight, length, and volume are the chapter's ratio examples.

Watch for

(A) is the near-miss. Equal intervals let you say participant A weighs 25 more pounds than participant B; only an absolute zero lets you say A is twice B.

  • BThere is an absolute zero point

13. A researcher redesigns a study so that a variable previously collected as ordinal ranks will now be collected at the interval level. According to the chapter, what does this change buy her?
  • ✓The data can be analyzed with statistical techniques of greater strength to determine significant relationships and differences
  • BThe instrument no longer requires reliability testing in this sample
  • CThe data will no longer contain measurement error
  • DThe results can be reported as percentages rather than as frequencies
Rationale

Interval- and ratio-level data can be added, subtracted, multiplied, and divided, so they can be analyzed with statistical techniques of greater strength to determine significant relationships and differences.

Watch for

(C) is the reflex that a higher level means a better measurement. Level of measurement governs which analyses are permitted; some error is always present regardless of the level.

measurement errorrandom versus systematic error
15. During data entry a research assistant occasionally transposes digits, and a few participants mark the wrong column on the answer sheet. The resulting values fall above and below the true values with no pattern. What type of error is this, and what does the chapter recommend?
  • ASystematic error; recalibrate the measurement instrument
  • ✓Random error; average multiple values, because a mean is a closer estimate of the true measurement
  • CSystematic error; average multiple values to cancel out the bias
  • DRandom error; discard the affected cases before analysis
Rationale

In random measurement error the difference between the measured value and the true value is without pattern or direction; an average value such as the mean is a closer estimate of the true measurement.

Watch for

(C) pairs the right remedy with the wrong error. Averaging works only because random errors fall in both directions and cancel one another.

measurement errorrandom versus systematic error
16. A digital scale used in a weight-loss study reads 0.5 lb high for every participant at every visit. How should this error be classified?
  • ARandom error, because the deviation is small relative to body weight
  • BRandom error, because weight is a direct measure and direct measures are precise
  • ✓Systematic error, because the variation from the calculated average is primarily in the same direction
  • DSystematic error, because the sample was too small to detect the difference
Rationale

Systematic measurement error is variation in measurement values from the calculated average that is primarily in the same direction, and a consistent flaw in the instrument or process produces it.

Watch for

Taking and averaging more readings will not help here. Every reading carries the same 0.5 lb bias, so the mean is biased by the same amount. This is the mirror image of the random-error item, where averaging is the fix.

measurement errorrandom versus systematic error
17. A reviewer argues that a study's physiological measures cannot contain measurement error because they are direct measures. How does the chapter answer this?
  • ADirect measures are error-free once the equipment has been calibrated before data collection
  • BDirect measures contain systematic error only, never random error
  • CDirect measures contain error only when more than one data collector is used
  • ✓Some error is always present in any measurement strategy, including direct measures
Rationale

Some error is always present in any measurement strategy; the chapter's own examples include a scale inaccurate by 0.5 lb, calibrated BP equipment that loses accuracy with use, and a tape measure held at different tensions.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex, but the chapter specifically names calibrated BP equipment losing accuracy with use as an error source, which is the counterexample to calibration as a guarantee.

18. Which statements about measurement error are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing

reliabilitystability and test-retest
19. A researcher administers the CES-D to the same participants on two occasions 4 weeks apart and correlates the two sets of scores. Which reliability is being examined?
  • ✓Stability, determined by test-retest
  • BEquivalence, determined by alternate forms
  • CEquivalence, determined by interrater comparison
  • DInternal consistency, determined by the Cronbach alpha
Rationale

Stability reliability is concerned with the reproducibility of scores with repeated measures of the same concept over time and is determined by test-retest; the CES-D shows r = 0.51 to 0.67 at 2- to 8-week intervals, which the chapter calls very solid.

Watch for

Nothing in the stem involves two versions or two raters. Repeat administration to the same people over time is the signature of stability, not equivalence.

reliabilitystability and test-retest
20. What assumption underlies test-retest reliability testing?
  • AThe two administrations use parallel versions of the instrument
  • ✓The attribute remains the same at the two testing times, and any change in the score is a consequence of random error
  • CThe instrument yields interval- or ratio-level data
  • DThe interval between administrations is long enough for the attribute to change
Rationale

Test-retest assumes that the attribute to be measured remains the same at the two testing times and that any change in the value or score is a consequence of random error.

Watch for

(C) is the constraint on the Cronbach alpha, not on test-retest. (D) inverts the assumption outright, since real change would confound the estimate.

reliabilityequivalence
21. Two trained observers independently rate the same infant feeding sessions and their ratings are compared. Which reliability is this, and what benchmark does the chapter give?
  • AAlternate forms reliability; values below 0.80 are a serious concern
  • BInternal consistency; 0.70 is acceptable for a relatively new tool
  • ✓Interrater reliability; values below 0.80 are a serious concern and reliability is best at 0.90 or higher
  • DInterrater reliability; 0.70 is acceptable for a relatively new observation tool
Rationale

Comparison of two observers or two judges is interrater reliability, one of the two forms of equivalence; the chapter states that a value below 0.80 is a serious concern and that reliability is best at 0.90, or 90 percent, or higher.

Watch for

(D) borrows the Cronbach alpha leniency for new scales. The 0.70 standard belongs to internal consistency of a relatively new scale, never to interrater agreement.

reliabilityequivalence
22. A researcher compares participants' scores on two paper-and-pencil versions of the same anxiety instrument. Which reliability is being tested?
  • AInterrater reliability
  • BStability reliability
  • CInternal consistency
  • ✓Alternate (parallel) forms reliability
Rationale

Equivalence is the comparison of two versions of the same paper and pencil instrument or of two observers measuring the same event; comparing two paper and pencil instruments or scales is alternate, or parallel, forms reliability.

Watch for

This is the mirror of the interrater item and both live under equivalence. Two observers gives interrater reliability; two instruments gives alternate forms.

reliabilityinternal consistency
23. A newly developed 15-item scale yields a Cronbach alpha of 0.74 in the study sample. How should this value be interpreted?
  • ✓Acceptable, because 0.70 is acceptable for a relatively new scale
  • BUnacceptable, because an alpha of at least 0.80 is required of every scale
  • CUnacceptable, because values below 0.80 are a serious concern for interrater reliability
  • DPerfect internal consistency, because 1.00 indicates none and 0.00 indicates perfect
Rationale

A Cronbach alpha of 1.00 indicates perfect reliability and 0.00 indicates none; 0.80 is strong for an established scale, and 0.70 is acceptable for a relatively new scale.

Watch for

(B) applies the established-scale standard to a new one. The benchmark shifts with the maturity of the scale, and (C) drags an interrater benchmark into an internal consistency question.

reliabilityinternal consistency
24. A researcher wants to estimate internal consistency for a screening tool whose items are all answered yes or no. Which statistic applies?
  • AThe Cronbach alpha, since it is the most commonly used measure of internal reliability for multi-item scales
  • ✓The Kuder-Richardson formula (K-R 20)
  • CA test-retest correlation over a 2-week interval
  • DAn interrater reliability coefficient between two scorers
Rationale

The Cronbach alpha can only be calculated for interval- and ratio-level data; for dichotomous yes/no data the Kuder-Richardson formula (K-R 20) is used.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex precisely because the alpha is the most commonly used measure for multi-item scales. Dichotomous items disqualify it no matter how common it is.

validityconstruct validity
28. A researcher administers a new depression scale and the existing Hamilton Rating Scale to the same participants at the same time and obtains correlations of 0.40 to 0.80. Which evidence is this?
  • APredictive validity
  • BDivergent validity
  • ✓Convergent validity
  • DContent validity from participant responses
Rationale

Convergence involves administering a new instrument and an existing one that measures the same construct at the same time, with a strong positive correlation expected; the CES-D correlated 0.40 to 0.80 with the Hamilton Rating Scale.

Watch for

Simultaneous administration also describes concurrent validity, so timing alone will not sort these. Convergence compares two measures of the same construct; concurrent validity estimates present performance on a different criterion.

validityconstruct validity
29. A hope scale correlates -0.6 with a hopelessness scale administered at the same time. How should this result be interpreted?
  • AThe hope scale lacks reliability, so the correlation cannot be interpreted
  • BValidity is not supported, because validity evidence requires positive correlations
  • CConvergent validity is supported, because the magnitude exceeds 0.4
  • ✓Divergent validity is supported, because instruments measuring opposite concepts are expected to correlate negatively
Rationale

Divergence involves administering instruments that measure opposite concepts, such as hope versus hopelessness or despair, and expecting a negative correlation, for example in the range of -0.4 to -0.8.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex that a minus sign means failure. This is the mirror of the convergence item: the same construct gives a positive correlation, opposite constructs give a negative one, and both support construct validity.

30. Investigators administer the CES-D to a group of people diagnosed with depression and a group without that diagnosis, predicting the diagnosed group will score higher. Which evidence is this?
  • ✓Construct validity from contrasting groups
  • BCriterion-related validity
  • CContent validity from expert review
  • DStability reliability
Rationale

Contrasting-groups evidence compares groups expected or known to differ; on the CES-D, the group diagnosed with depression is expected to score higher.

(B) is tempting because a clinical diagnosis sounds like a criterion. Criterion-related validity uses a participant's score to infer performance on another variable, not to separate two already-known groups.

validitycriterion-related validity
31. Scores on a health-promotion-behaviors scale collected at enrollment are used to forecast participants' blood pressure, BMI, and minutes of exercise one year later. Which validity is being examined?
  • AConcurrent validity
  • ✓Predictive validity
  • CConvergent validity
  • DContent validity
Rationale

Predictive validity is the extent to which an individual's score on an instrument can be used to predict future performance or behavior on a criterion; the chapter's example is a health-promotion-behaviors scale predicting future BP, BMI, and minutes of exercise.

Watch for

The distinguishing feature is timing alone. A criterion measured in the future makes it predictive no matter how strong or weak the resulting correlation is.

32. A researcher uses participants' current self-esteem scores to estimate their present ability to cope with illness. Which validity is this, and what distinguishes it from its counterpart?
  • APredictive validity; the two are distinguished by the strength of the correlation
  • BConcurrent validity; the two are distinguished by whether a criterion standard was used
  • ✓Concurrent validity; the two are distinguished by the timing of the measurement of the other criterion
  • DConvergent validity; the two are distinguished by whether both instruments measure the same construct
Rationale

Concurrent validity is the extent to which an individual's score can be used to estimate her or his present or concurrent performance on another variable; the difference between concurrent and predictive validity is the timing of the measurement of the other criterion.

(A) invokes strength. Strength never separates these two, and this item is the mirror of the predictive-validity item: present versus future is the whole distinction.

validitythe reliability dependency
33. An observation instrument used in a study achieves an interrater reliability of 0.55. What does the chapter conclude about that instrument's validity in this study?
  • AValidity is unaffected, because reliability and validity are independent properties
  • BValidity can still be established by adding more content experts to the review panel
  • COnly stability is compromised; criterion-related validity may still be acceptable
  • ✓If a measurement method does not have acceptable reliability, then it is not valid
Rationale

You cannot consider validity apart from reliability, and if a measurement method does not have acceptable reliability, then it is not valid.

The dependency runs one direction only. A reliable instrument is not automatically valid, but an unreliable one cannot be valid, and that one-way relationship is the testable point.

diagnostic test accuracysensitivity versus specificity
35. A screening tool detects 97.6 percent of adolescents who are truly at risk according to the criterion standard. Which property is being described, and what follows from a high value?
  • ✓Sensitivity; the test has a low percentage of false negatives
  • BSensitivity; the test has a low percentage of false positives
  • CSpecificity; the test has a low percentage of false negatives
  • DSpecificity; the test has a low percentage of false positives
Rationale

Sensitivity is the proportion of patients with the disease who have a positive test result, the true-positive rate; a highly sensitive test is very good at identifying the disease in a patient and has a low percentage of false negatives.

Watch for

(B) pairs the right term with the wrong error. Sensitivity is computed inside the diseased group, so the only mistake it can minimize is the false negative.

diagnostic test accuracysensitivity versus specificity
36. In the ASQ implementation study, only 43.3 percent of patients who were not at risk on the criterion standard screened negative. Which property is low, and what is the consequence?
  • ASensitivity is low, so a high percentage of true cases are missed
  • ✓Specificity is low, so the test produces a high percentage of false positives
  • CSpecificity is low, so the test produces a high percentage of false negatives
  • DNegative predictive value is low, so a negative screen cannot be trusted
Rationale

Specificity is the proportion of patients without the disease who have a negative test result, the true-negative rate; a very specific test has a low percentage of false positives, so a specificity of 43.3 percent means many false positives.

Watch for

(C) swaps the error type. Specificity is computed inside the disease-free group, where the only possible mistake is a false positive. This is the mirror of the sensitivity item.

diagnostic test accuracysensitivity versus specificity
37. Developers revise a screening instrument so that it captures a larger proportion of true cases. According to the chapter, what usually happens to its other property?
  • ASpecificity usually increases in parallel
  • BSpecificity is unaffected, because the two are computed on different groups
  • ✓The test usually becomes less specific
  • DThe negative predictive value usually falls below 0.1
Rationale

As a test becomes more sensitive, it usually becomes less specific.

Watch for

(D) misuses the likelihood ratio cutoffs. A likelihood ratio greater than 10 rules the disease in and one less than 0.1 almost rules it out; 0.1 has nothing to do with predictive values.

The ASQ is a 4-item screen used in a pediatric emergency department with patients aged 10 to 21, where a yes to any item is a positive screen; the development study reported sensitivity 97.6 percent, specificity 65.6 percent, and NPV 96.9 percent, while the implementation study reported 93.3 percent, 43.3 percent, and an NPV calculated at 98.98 percent.

Watch for

(E) reverses the finding. It was 53 percent of patients who screened positive who did NOT present with suicide-related complaints, and they were more likely to be male, African American, and carrying externalizing behavior diagnoses.

Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓A highly sensitive test is very good at identifying the disease in a patient
  • ✓A highly specific test is very good at identifying the patients without a disease
  • CSpecificity is the true-positive rate
  • ✓A likelihood ratio greater than 10 rules in the disease

A highly sensitive test is very good at identifying the disease and a highly specific test is very good at identifying patients without it; sensitivity is the true-positive rate and specificity is the true-negative rate. Watch for

(C) is the most common inversion in this section. Specificity is the true-NEGATIVE rate; the positive likelihood ratio is the ratio of true-positive to false-positive results and the negative likelihood ratio is the ratio of true-negative to false-negative results.

43. A Likert scale is written with six response options and no midpoint. What is this version called, and what is the argument against including a midpoint?

(A) borrows a reliability term. Parallel forms are two versions of an instrument used to test equivalence, not a response-option layout.
