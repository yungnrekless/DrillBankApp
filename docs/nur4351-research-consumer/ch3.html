qualitative approachesmatching approach to purpose
1. A nurse educator notices that new graduates are leaving her unit within six months and conducts a qualitative study to promote understanding of the experience from the perspective of the nurses living it and, if possible, to solve the problem. Which approach is this?
  • AGrounded theory research
  • BDescriptive phenomenological research
  • ✓Exploratory-descriptive qualitative research
  • DEthnographic research
Rationale

Exploratory-descriptive qualitative research is conducted to provide information that will promote understanding of an experience from the perspective of the persons living the experience and possibly solve a problem. It addresses an issue or problem in need of a solution and understanding.

Watch for

Every qualitative approach studies experience from the participant's perspective, so that phrase alone does not decide it. The give-away is the intent to solve a problem, which no other approach in this chapter claims as an outcome.

qualitative approachesgrounded theory
3. A researcher studies how nurses and families negotiate goals of care in the ICU, an area in which little previous research has been conducted, and ends the report with a diagram of concepts linked by relational statements. Which approach is this, and what does grounded refer to?
  • ✓Grounded theory; the theory was developed from the data the participants provided
  • BGrounded theory; the study was grounded in an established theoretical framework that it then tested
  • CEthnography; the description was grounded in a year of immersion in the unit's culture
  • DInterpretative phenomenology; the interpretation was grounded in a naive reading of the texts
Rationale

Grounded means the theory developed from the research, grounded in the real world and based on data provided by participants. Grounded theory is used most frequently in areas where little previous research has been conducted, and fully developed studies result in theoretical frameworks with relational statements between concepts, sometimes with a diagram.

Watch for

(B) inverts the direction of the logic. Grounded theory is inductive: it produces a theory rather than testing one.

qualitative approachesgrounded theory
4. Most grounded theory methodology is based on which theory, and who developed it?
  • AHermeneutic theory, developed by Martin Heidegger
  • BSymbolic interaction theory, developed by Glaser and Strauss
  • CPhenomenological theory, developed by Edmund Husserl
  • ✓Symbolic interaction theory, developed by George Herbert Mead
Rationale

Most grounded theory methodology is based on symbolic interaction theory, developed by the social psychologist George Herbert Mead in 1934, which explores how people define reality and how their beliefs are related to their actions.

Watch for

(B) names the right theory with the wrong authors. Glaser and Strauss developed the grounded theory method itself in 1967, through interviews to understand the perspectives of persons who were dying.

qualitative approachesethnography
5. An ethnographer joins a hospital night shift for a year and learns the staff's own terms, routines, and meanings as the staff themselves understand them, recognizing the uniqueness of each individual. Which approach is this, and is it the typical goal of ethnography?
  • AAn etic approach, and it is the typical goal
  • ✓An emic approach, and it is the typical goal
  • CAn emic approach, but the etic approach is the typical goal
  • DAn etic approach, but the emic approach is the typical goal
Rationale

The emic approach studies behaviors from within the culture and recognizes the uniqueness of the individual, and it is the typical goal of ethnography. Ethnography requires immersion; early researchers spent a year or longer in another culture.

Watch for

Morgan-Trimmer and Wood argue for using both emic and etic perspectives, which makes the two-sided options attractive, but that recommendation does not change which one the chapter names as typical.

6. A second researcher on the same night-shift study deliberately stands back and analyzes the unit's elements as a naive outsider would. This describes which approach?
  • ✓Etic
  • BEmic
  • CBracketing
  • DTriangulation
Rationale

The etic approach views the culture as a naive outsider and analyzes its elements as a researcher.

Watch for

(C) is tempting because bracketing also involves holding the researcher's own frame at a distance, but bracketing belongs to Husserlian phenomenology, not to ethnography, and the word naive appears in both definitions.

  • ✓Buildings used for cultural events
  • BSymbolic meaning and social customs that become apparent only over time
  • ✓Symbols

Material culture is all created or constructed aspects of culture, including buildings for cultural events, symbols, family traditions, networks of social relations, and beliefs reflected in social and political institutions.

phenomenologydescriptive versus interpretative
8. A researcher states that the aim of her study is to capture the experience of chemotherapy in its essence, without interpreting, explaining, or theorizing. Whose tradition is this, and which type of phenomenological research?
  • AHeidegger; interpretative phenomenological research
  • BHeidegger; hermeneutic phenomenological research
  • ✓Husserl; descriptive phenomenological research
  • DMead; descriptive phenomenological research
Rationale

Husserl focused on the phenomenon itself and aimed to capture experience in its essence without interpreting, explaining, or theorizing. He supports descriptive phenomenological research, whose purpose is to describe experiences as they are lived, capturing the lived experience.

phenomenologydescriptive versus interpretative
9. A study analyzes participants' interview texts beginning with a naive reading, looks for the hidden meaning in how participants gave meaning to their illness, and presents a rich word picture as interpreted by the researcher. This describes:
  • ADescriptive phenomenological research consistent with Husserl
  • ✓Interpretative phenomenological research using hermeneutics, consistent with Heidegger
  • CGrounded theory using symbolic interaction, consistent with Mead
  • DEthnographic research using an emic approach, consistent with anthropology
Rationale

Interpretative phenomenological research is consistent with Heidegger and involves analyzing the data and presenting a rich word picture of the phenomenon as interpreted by the researcher. Hermeneutics is an interpretative method involving textual analysis that begins with a naive reading of the texts.

Watch for

The phrase naive reading pulls toward Husserl, whose bracketing requirement is about describing the phenomenon in a naive way. But a naive reading of texts is the hermeneutic starting point, and hermeneutics is Heideggerian.

phenomenologybracketing
10. Before beginning interviews, a phenomenologist writes out her own assumptions about living with chronic pain and sets them aside so she can describe the phenomenon in a naive way. What is this, and whose requirement is it?
  • ATriangulation; a requirement of ethnographic fieldwork
  • BBracketing; a requirement of Heidegger's interpretative phenomenology
  • CSaturation; a requirement of grounded theory
  • ✓Bracketing; a requirement of Husserl's descriptive phenomenology
Rationale

Bracketing is setting aside one's own biases and preconceptions in order to describe the phenomenon in a naive way, and it is a Husserl requirement supporting descriptive phenomenological research.

Watch for

(B) is the classic swap the fact bank flags. Heidegger argued that it is impossible to set aside preconceptions and understand the world naively, so bracketing can never be attached to interpretative or hermeneutic work.

phenomenologybracketing
11. Which statements reflect Heidegger's position as the chapter presents it? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓The focus is how participants have interpreted or given meaning to their experiences
  • ✓The analysis looks for the hidden meaning in participants' interpretations
  • CResearchers must set aside their own biases before data collection begins
  • ✓It is impossible to set aside preconceptions and understand the world naively
  • ✓It supports interpretative phenomenological research
Rationale

Heidegger focused on how participants interpreted or gave meaning to their experiences and on the hidden meaning in those interpretations, argued that setting preconceptions aside is impossible, and supports interpretative phenomenological research.

Watch for

(C) is Husserl's bracketing requirement, and selecting it is the single most predictable error in this chapter. Heidegger did not soften bracketing; he denied it was possible.

qualitative samplingpurposeful sampling
12. A researcher recruits eight nurses specifically because each one worked a mass-casualty event, rather than drawing names at random from the staff roster. What sampling method is this, and what is the stated justification?
  • ✓Purposeful sampling; participants are recruited for their particular knowledge, experience, or views related to the study
  • BConvenience sampling; participants are recruited because they are the most easily available
  • CRandom sampling; it reduces selection bias in qualitative work
  • DPurposeful sampling; it guarantees the findings will apply to all emergency nurses
Rationale

Sampling in qualitative research is purposeful. The researcher recruits participants because of their particular knowledge, experience, or views related to the study.

Watch for

(D) names the right method and attaches a quantitative justification. Purposeful sampling is chosen for depth of experience, not to make findings apply to a whole population.

qualitative samplingpurposeful sampling
13. A grounded theory team deliberately recruits participants who differ in age, role, and years of experience. What kind of sample is this, and why is it chosen?
  • AA homogeneous sample, because the central focus is the phenomenon itself
  • BA homogeneous sample, because grounded theory requires similarity to reach saturation faster
  • ✓A heterogeneous sample, because participants with different characteristics support theory development
  • DA heterogeneous sample, because saturation cannot be reached with similar participants
Rationale

Heterogeneous samples, in which participants have different characteristics, are used frequently for grounded theory to support theory development. Homogeneous samples, in which participants have similar characteristics, are used when the central focus is the phenomenon.

Watch for

There is no blanket rule that qualitative samples must be homogeneous. Match the sample type to the method: phenomenon in focus means homogeneous, theory being built means heterogeneous.

qualitative samplingsample size and saturation
14. A doctoral student's phenomenological study has enrolled nine participants, and the three most recent interviews produced nothing the earlier interviews had not already covered. What should the student conclude?
  • AThe sample is too small, because qualitative studies require more than 30 participants
  • ✓Saturation has been reached, because additional participants are not providing new information
  • CA power analysis is needed to confirm whether the minimum sample size has been met
  • DData collection must continue until the planned enrollment target is reached
Rationale

Saturation occurs when additional participants or data sources do not provide new information. Phenomenological studies typically use 10 or fewer participants if the topic is narrow and participants provide adequate data.

Watch for

(D) defines saturation as hitting a planned number. Saturation is defined by no new information, not by a count, and the sizes the chapter gives are typical rather than rules.

15. Which statements about sample size in qualitative research are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Phenomenological studies typically use 10 or fewer participants if the topic is narrow and participants provide adequate data
  • ✓Grounded theory studies may require more than 30 participants to develop a robust theory
  • ✓Sampling continues until saturation
  • ✓Qualitative researchers deliberately recruit fewer participants but ensure that each one has experience with the research topic
  • EA power analysis determines the minimum sample size for a qualitative study
Rationale

The chapter gives 10 or fewer as typical for phenomenology and more than 30 as possible for grounded theory, states that sampling continues until saturation, and notes that qualitative researchers deliberately recruit fewer participants while ensuring each has experience with the topic.

Watch for

(E) is a quantitative tool. Power analysis determines the minimum sample size for a quantitative study; qualitative sample size is governed by saturation.

researcher and participant relationshipdegree of involvement
16. A quantitatively trained colleague criticizes a phenomenological study because the researcher became personally close to the participants over months of interviews. How does the chapter frame that involvement?
  • AIt is a source of bias in both quantitative and qualitative research
  • BIt is a threat to dependability that must be corrected with an audit trail
  • CIt is acceptable only if the researcher brackets the relationship after data collection
  • ✓It is considered a source of bias in quantitative research but is thought by qualitative researchers to be a critical element of the research process
Rationale

One of the important differences between quantitative and qualitative research lies in the degree of involvement of the researcher with the participants; involvement considered a source of bias in quantitative research is thought by qualitative researchers to be a critical element of the research process. The researcher influences the people being studied and in turn is influenced by them.

Watch for

(B) sounds rigorous and uses real vocabulary from the chapter, but the audit trail documents that methods were implemented appropriately. It is not a device for neutralizing researcher closeness.

17. Qualitative reports call the individuals studied participants rather than subjects. What reason does the chapter give?
  • AThey were not randomly assigned to groups
  • ✓The researcher and participants carry out the study cooperatively
  • CThey provide narrative data rather than measured data
  • DThe sample is purposeful rather than representative
Rationale

The term participants is used because the researcher and participants carry out the study cooperatively.

Watch for

(D) is a true statement about qualitative sampling but is not the reason the chapter gives for the terminology. The reason is the cooperative relationship, which fits with the researcher's involvement being treated as a critical element.

18. Which statements about qualitative data collection and analysis are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Data are collected through interviews, focus groups, observation, and examination of documents and media materials
  • ✓Data are words and images instead of numbers
  • CInterpretation is avoided so that the findings remain purely descriptive
  • ✓A code is a symbol or abbreviation used to classify words or phrases in the data
  • ✓Observation is a fundamental method of gathering data, especially for ethnographic studies
Rationale

Qualitative data are words and images rather than numbers, collected by interview, focus group, observation, and examination of documents and media; observation is fundamental, especially in ethnography. Analysis moves from codes to themes to interpretation.

Watch for

(C) stops the analysis one step short. Interpretation asks what the findings mean and may focus on the usefulness of the findings for clinical practice or move toward theorizing.

The chapter states that the extent to which findings are dependable, confirmable, credible, and transferable is the degree of rigor, assessed through design detail, careful data collection, and thorough analysis, with the audit trail assuring the reader that methods were implemented appropriately.

rigor and trustworthinesstrustworthiness criteria
20. A qualitative team interviews patients, interviews their family members, and reviews clinic documents, then converges these into one perspective on the topic. What is this strategy, and what does it support?
  • ✓Triangulation, a strategy for credibility
  • BBracketing, a strategy for dependability
  • CSaturation, a strategy for transferability
  • DMethodological congruence, a strategy for confirmability
Rationale

Triangulation means the researchers use at least two different perspectives to converge into a unique perspective on the topic, and it is named among the credibility strategies along with engaging family members, using multiple sources, and linking codes and themes with exemplar quotations.

Watch for

(D) borrows a real term from the chapter with the wrong meaning. Methodological congruence is the fit among purpose, questions, and methods that makes a study a cohesive whole, not a data-convergence strategy.

rigor and trustworthinesstransferability versus generalizability
21. A nurse manager reads a qualitative study of burnout conducted in a 200-bed rural hospital and asks whether she can apply the findings on her own similar rural unit. Which criterion is she invoking?
  • AGeneralizability, the desired outcome for qualitative studies
  • BCredibility, the truth value of the findings
  • CDependability, the appropriate implementation of the methods
  • ✓Transferability, the applicability of the findings to similar individuals
Rationale

Transferability is the applicability of the findings to similar individuals and is the desired outcome for qualitative studies; findings cannot be generalized but may be applied in other contexts or with other participants.

Watch for

(A) reverses the pair and mislabels it. Generalizability is not the desired outcome for qualitative studies at all.

rigor and trustworthinesstransferability versus generalizability
22. A quantitative study draws a sample that is representative of the target population so its results apply to that population. Which term names that desired outcome, and to which type of research does the chapter assign it?
  • ATransferability; qualitative research
  • BTransferability; quantitative research
  • ✓Generalizability; quantitative research
  • DGeneralizability; qualitative research
Rationale

Generalizability is a desired outcome for quantitative studies when the sample is representative of the target populations.

Watch for

This is the mirror image of the transferability item, and the pair is the one the fact bank flags most explicitly. An option praising a qualitative study for being generalizable to the population contradicts the chapter outright.
