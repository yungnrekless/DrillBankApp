research and evidence-based practicedefining research and nursing research
1. A nurse says that research is worthwhile only when it produces knowledge no one has had before. Which statement best corrects this using the text's definition of research?
  • AResearch is any careful observation of practice that is recorded and shared with colleagues
  • BResearch is limited to generating new knowledge through controlled experimentation
  • ✓Research validates and refines existing knowledge and also develops new knowledge
  • DResearch is the appropriation of knowledge from other disciplines to guide nursing practice
Rationale

Research is a diligent systematic inquiry or study that validates and refines existing knowledge and develops new knowledge. The definition includes both functions, and diligent systematic study indicates planning, organization, and persistence.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex because new knowledge is the memorable half of the definition; an option offering only that half is incomplete. (D) is the definition of borrowing.

research and evidence-based practicedefining research and nursing research
2. Which phrase in the text's definition of nursing research is what distinguishes it from the general definition of research?
  • ✓It generates knowledge that directly and indirectly influences nursing practice
  • BIt is conducted only by nurses prepared at the doctoral level
  • CIt uses numerical data to obtain information about the world
  • DIt validates existing knowledge but does not generate new knowledge
Rationale

Nursing research is a scientific process that validates and refines existing knowledge and generates new knowledge that directly and indirectly influences nursing practice.

Watch for

The word indirectly is the part that gets dropped. Studies of nursing education or administration are still nursing research even though they do not change bedside care immediately. (C) is the definition of quantitative research.

research and evidence-based practicecomponents of evidence-based practice
3. A unit adopts a new pressure-injury protocol based entirely on one high-quality meta-analysis, without consulting the staff nurses who deliver the care or asking patients about their preferences. Why does this fall short of the text's definition of evidence-based practice?
  • AA meta-analysis is not considered best research evidence
  • BEvidence-based practice requires that the synthesis be a systematic review rather than a meta-analysis
  • CEvidence-based practice requires that an expert consensus panel approve the evidence first
  • ✓Evidence-based practice is the integration of best research evidence with clinical expertise and the patient's circumstances and values
Rationale

EBP evolves from the integration of the best research evidence with clinical expertise and the patient's circumstances and values to produce quality health outcomes. Applying research findings is only one of the three parts.

Watch for

This is the single most testable idea in the chapter. Any option that calls applying research findings EBP by itself, or that names only two of the three parts, is the distractor.

5. The text defines best research evidence as which of the following?
  • AThe findings of the single most rigorous study available on a clinical problem
  • ✓The empirical knowledge generated from the synthesis of quality health studies to address a clinical problem
  • CThe recommendations issued by a panel of experts after they review the literature
  • DThe accumulated clinical judgment of the experienced nurses on a unit
Rationale

Best research evidence is the empirical knowledge generated from the synthesis of quality health studies to address a clinical problem.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex, but best research evidence is a synthesis product, not one study however strong. (C) describes evidence-based guidelines, which are built from best research evidence rather than being it.

ways of acquiring knowledgetradition, borrowing, and trial and error
6. A new graduate asks why the unit changes IV tubing on a set schedule and is told, "It is in the policy manual; that is how this unit has always done it." Which way of acquiring knowledge does this represent, and what limitation does the text attach to it?
  • AAuthority; the authority may be influencing behavior without a basis in research evidence
  • ✓Tradition; it can narrow and limit the knowledge sought for practice
  • CBorrowing; knowledge taken from another discipline may not fit nursing's focus
  • DRole modeling; imitating an expert transmits behavior without explanation
Rationale

Traditions are truths or beliefs based on customs and trends, carried in policy manuals and transferred from the past. The text says traditions can narrow and limit the knowledge sought for practice and often go unquestioned because they are supported by those with power and authority.

Watch for

(A) is close because the text mentions power and authority in the same breath. Authority as a way of knowing requires a specific person with expertise and power influencing opinion and behavior; here the source is the custom itself.

ways of acquiring knowledgetradition, borrowing, and trial and error
7. The text names a specific weakness of trial and error as a source of knowledge for practice. Which is it?
  • AIt cannot be used in situations of uncertainty
  • BIt produces knowledge that cannot be expressed numerically
  • CIt requires a person with expertise and power to interpret the result
  • ✓The knowledge gained is often not shared, so ineffective interventions continue to be used
Rationale

Trial and error is an approach with unknown outcomes used when other sources of knowledge are unavailable; the chapter's stated problem is that the knowledge gained is frequently not shared, so ineffective interventions persist.

Watch for

The reflex is to call trial and error simply unscientific. The text actually accepts it as legitimate under uncertainty and faults it for nondissemination.

ways of acquiring knowledgeintuition and personal experience
8. An expert nurse tells a colleague she "just knew" a patient was deteriorating before any vital sign changed. How does the text characterize this?
  • ATrial and error, because the outcome of acting on the feeling was unknown
  • BIntuition, which is a lack of knowing and therefore must be set aside in favor of objective data
  • ✓Intuition, which is not a lack of knowing but is instead a result of deep knowledge
  • DBorrowing, because the insight originates in psychology rather than nursing
Rationale

Intuition is an insight into or understanding of a situation as a whole that a person usually cannot explain logically, often described as a gut feeling or hunch. The text is explicit that intuition is not the lack of knowing but a result of deep knowledge.

Watch for

(B) inverts the chapter's own sentence and is the option a student picks when trying to sound scientific. Benner's work is the citation behind the correct reading.

ways of acquiring knowledgeintuition and personal experience
9. A nurse consistently views the patient as a whole and as a member of a family and community, and adjusts the plan of care to each patient's response. Which of Benner's five levels of experience does this describe?
  • AAdvanced beginner
  • BCompetent
  • ✓Proficient
  • DExpert
Rationale

The proficient nurse views the patient as a whole and as a member of a family and community and adjusts care to each patient's response. Benner's five levels came from a phenomenological qualitative study.

Watch for

(D) is the reflex for any description of skilled holistic practice. Expert is defined by grasping a situation intuitively with accuracy and speed; competent is defined by conscious, deliberate, organized planning toward long-range goals.

ways of acquiring knowledgeinductive and deductive reasoning
11. A doctoral student reviews the findings of 14 individual studies of early ambulation after surgery and combines them into a general statement about best practice. Which type of reasoning is this, and in which direction does it move?
  • ✓Inductive reasoning, from the specific to the general
  • BDeductive reasoning, from the general to the specific
  • CInductive reasoning, from the general to the specific
  • DDeductive reasoning, from the specific to the general
Rationale

Inductive reasoning moves from the specific to the general, combining particular instances into a larger whole. Synthesizing multiple studies into a best practice is the logic behind building an evidence-based practice.

Watch for

(C) and (D) attach the right label to the wrong direction, which is how this pair is usually missed. Fix the cue: synthesis of many studies is inductive.

ways of acquiring knowledgeinductive and deductive reasoning
12. A nurse begins from the premises that all humans experience loss and that all adolescents are humans, and concludes that all adolescents experience loss. Which type of reasoning is this?
  • AInductive reasoning
  • ✓Deductive reasoning
  • CIntuition
  • DTrial and error
Rationale

Deductive reasoning moves from the general to the specific, or from a general premise to a particular situation or conclusion. This is the chapter's own worked example.

Watch for

This is the mirror image of the synthesis item. The chapter's inductive example runs the other way: a headache is stressful and a terminal illness is stressful, therefore all altered levels of health are stressful.

research methodstypes of qualitative research
13. A researcher wants to describe the lived experience of parents who have lost a child. Which type of qualitative research fits this purpose?
  • AEthnographic research
  • BGrounded theory research
  • CExploratory-descriptive qualitative research
  • ✓Phenomenological research
Rationale

Phenomenological research is an inductive holistic approach used to describe an experience as it is lived by individuals; the chapter's own example is the lived experience of losing a child.

research methodstypes of qualitative research
14. A researcher interviews nurses in order to formulate, test, and refine a theory explaining how they manage interruptions during medication administration. Which type of qualitative research is this?
  • APhenomenological research
  • BEthnographic research
  • ✓Grounded theory research
  • DExploratory-descriptive qualitative research
Rationale

Grounded theory research is an inductive technique used to formulate, test, and refine a theory about a particular phenomenon.

Watch for

(B) is the near-neighbor when a study takes place inside a work setting, but ethnographic research was developed by anthropology to investigate cultures through in-depth study of members of the culture. The target has to be the culture itself.

research methodscomparing quantitative and qualitative research
17. A study is grounded in logical positivism, measures its variables with scales and physiological instruments, and sets out to test an existing theory. Which method is this, and what is its basis of knowing?
  • ✓Quantitative research; cause and effect relationships
  • BQualitative research; meaning, discovery, and understanding
  • COutcomes research; the results of care and changes in health status
  • DMixed methods research; complex health problems
Rationale

Table 1.3 assigns quantitative research a logical positivist origin, an objective researcher stance, numbers as data, statistical analysis, and a basis of knowing in cause and effect relationships. Quantitative research is a formal, objective, systematic process using numerical data.

This is the mirror of the qualitative item. Swapping logical positivism with naturalistic origins, or tests theory with develops theory, is the Table 1.3 reversal.

research methodsmixed methods research
18. A researcher studying a complex symptom problem collects numerical symptom scores and unstructured interview data from the same participants within a single study. Which method is this?
  • AOutcomes research
  • BMeta-analysis
  • CMixed-methods systematic review
  • ✓Mixed methods research
Rationale

Mixed methods research is an approach to inquiry that combines quantitative and qualitative research methods in a single study, and the text ties it to expanding understanding of complex health problems.

Watch for

(C) has almost the same name and is the distractor to beat. A mixed-methods systematic review synthesizes studies that already exist; mixed methods research combines the two approaches inside one primary study.

19. Which statement matches the text's definition of a nursing-sensitive outcome?

  • ✓An individual, family, or community state, behavior, or perception that is measured along a continuum in response to nursing interventions
  • CThe financial result of care that can be attributed to nurse staffing levels

A nursing-sensitive outcome is an individual, family, or community state, behavior, or perception measured along a continuum in response to nursing interventions. The Nursing Outcomes Classification standardizes this terminology across specialties and settings.

research synthesis strategiessystematic review and meta-analysis
21. A team combines the results of previous quantitative studies of an intervention into a single statistical analysis in order to estimate its effectiveness. Which synthesis strategy is this?
  • ✓Meta-analysis
  • BSystematic review
  • CMeta-synthesis
  • DMixed-methods systematic review
Rationale

A meta-analysis pools the results from previous quantitative studies into a single statistical analysis, and is strongest when pooling quasi-experimental and experimental studies.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex because the two words are used loosely as synonyms. A systematic review is the structured, comprehensive process of identifying, locating, appraising, and synthesizing evidence for one healthcare question; many systematic reviews contain a meta-analysis, but the terms are not interchangeable.

research synthesis strategiesmeta-synthesis and qualitative evidence
22. A student proposes conducting a meta-analysis of ten phenomenological studies of living with chronic pain. Why will this not work, and which strategy fits instead?
  • AThe studies are too few in number to pool; a systematic review is the correct strategy
  • BQualitative studies cannot be appraised for quality; a mixed-methods systematic review is the correct strategy
  • ✓Qualitative studies do not produce statistical findings and cannot be pooled; a meta-synthesis is the correct strategy
  • DQualitative studies rank below expert opinion on the evidence pyramid; an expert consensus panel is the correct strategy
Rationale

Because qualitative studies do not produce statistical findings, they cannot be included in a meta-analysis; meta-synthesis is the systematic compilation and integration of qualitative study results, and it focuses on interpretation rather than on combining results.

Watch for

(D) inverts the pyramid. Expert opinion is the base, below descriptive and qualitative studies, not above them.

levels of research evidencethe evidence pyramid
23. Which statements about the levels of research evidence in Fig. 1.3 are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Systematic reviews and meta-analyses of high-quality quasi-experimental and experimental studies sit at the top of the pyramid
  • ✓Among individual studies, experimental studies such as randomized controlled trials provide the strongest research evidence
  • ✓Descriptive and qualitative studies provide initial knowledge and a basis for further study
  • ✓Replication of studies with similar methodology increases the strength of the evidence
  • EThe opinions of expert committees and authorities occupy the level just below systematic reviews
Rationale

The levels form a continuum with the strongest evidence at the top: syntheses of quasi-experimental and experimental studies, then meta-analyses of correlational, quasi-experimental, experimental, and outcomes studies, then mixed-methods systematic reviews and meta-syntheses, then individual correlational and cohort studies, then descriptive and qualitative studies, with expert opinion at the base.

Watch for

(E) places expert opinion near the top. It is the weakest evidence at the base of the pyramid, which is the same idea as authority being a historical way of knowing that research evidence is meant to supersede.

levels of research evidenceevidence not ready for practice
24. A nurse proposes changing a unit protocol on the basis of one predictive correlational study. Using the levels of research evidence, what is the appropriate response?
  • AProceed, because correlational studies examine relationships and therefore establish intervention effectiveness
  • BProceed, because a single study conducted in a comparable setting counts as a synthesis
  • CDecline, because correlational studies rank below the opinions of expert committees on the pyramid
  • ✓Decline, because correlational, predictive correlational, and cohort studies provide direction for future research but are not ready for use in practice
Rationale

The chapter places individual correlational, predictive correlational, and cohort studies at the level that provides direction for future research but is not ready for use in practice.

Watch for

(C) inverts the pyramid, since expert opinion is the weakest level and sits below correlational studies. (A) confuses examining relationships with determining effectiveness, which is the job of quasi-experimental and experimental studies.

the nurse's research role by educationdoctoral preparation
26. A nursing student is comparing doctoral options. Which statement correctly distinguishes the two degrees as the text describes them?
  • ✓The DNP is practice-focused and translates scientific knowledge for use in practice, while the PhD is research-focused and generates nursing knowledge
  • BThe DNP is research-focused and generates nursing knowledge, while the PhD is practice-focused and translates it for use
  • CBoth degrees are research-focused, but only the PhD is permitted to conduct clinical studies
  • DBoth degrees are practice-focused, but only the DNP obtains research funding and coordinates research teams
Rationale

The DNP is practice-focused, representing the highest level of clinical expertise and translating scientific knowledge for use in practice; the PhD is research-focused, generating nursing knowledge as nurse scientists.

Watch for

(B) is the straight swap and is the likeliest item off this table. Anchor it by the other duties: PhD nurses obtain funding and coordinate research teams of BSN, MSN, and DNP nurses, while DNP nurses develop and revise protocols, policies, and guidelines.
