source typesprimary versus secondary sources
1. A student is appraising a research report published in a nursing journal and written by the three investigators who designed and conducted the study. How should the student classify this source?
  • ✓A primary source, because it was written by the people who generated the ideas being reported
  • BA secondary source, because it appears in a periodical rather than as the original data
  • CA secondary source, because a journal editor and peer reviewers shaped the final text
  • DA primary source only if the report includes the raw data set
Rationale

A primary source is written by the person who originated or is responsible for generating the ideas published, so a research report written by the researchers who conducted the study is a primary source.

Watch for

Students routinely reclassify journal articles as secondary because they are published. Primary versus secondary turns on who generated the ideas, not on the format or the editorial process.

source typesprimary versus secondary sources
2. A review cites a textbook chapter in which the author paraphrases the work of several researchers and theorists and presents her own interpretation of what they wrote. What is the source type, and what risk does the chapter attach to it?
  • APrimary source; the risk is that the author's credentials cannot be verified
  • BPrimary source; the risk is that book chapters are rarely peer-reviewed
  • ✓Secondary source; the information may be a misinterpretation of the primary authors' thoughts
  • DSecondary source; the information is by definition more than 5 years old
Rationale

A secondary source summarizes or quotes content from primary sources, and the chapter's stated risk is that the information may be a misinterpretation of the primary authors' thoughts.

Watch for

(D) fuses two separate standards. Source type and currency are independent dimensions, so a secondary source can be brand new and still carry the misinterpretation risk.

source typesprimary versus secondary sources
3. A faculty reviewer questions a student's use of a secondary source. According to the chapter, which conditions justify citing one? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓The primary source cannot be located
  • ✓The secondary source presents creative ideas not found in a primary source
  • ✓The secondary source offers a unique organization of information not found in a primary source
  • DThe secondary source is written more clearly than the primary source
  • EThe secondary source was published more recently than the primary source
Rationale

Secondary sources are used only when primary sources cannot be located, or when the secondary source provides creative ideas or a unique organization of information not found in a primary source.

Watch for

Readability and recency are not exemptions. Most references in a quality review are primary sources that are peer-reviewed, so the secondary source is a narrow exception rather than an interchangeable substitute.

source typespeer review
4. An author submitted a manuscript to a publication editor, who identified scholars familiar with the topic to review it; those scholars advised on whether it was accurate, met quality standards, and was appropriate for the journal. What does completing this process establish about the article?
  • AThat it is a primary rather than a secondary source
  • BThat it reports data-based rather than theoretical knowledge
  • CThat it is current, having been published within the past 5 years
  • ✓That it has undergone significant scrutiny and its content is considered trustworthy
Rationale

Peer review means the manuscript has undergone significant scrutiny and its content is considered trustworthy.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex answer because quality reviews cite sources that are both primary and peer-reviewed. They are separate dimensions — a secondary source can be peer-reviewed, and a primary source can appear in an outlet that never reviewed it.

source typestheoretical versus empirical literature
5. A student is sorting the sources cited in a review into empirical and theoretical literature. Which sources belong in the empirical category? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓A published report of a quasi-experimental study
  • ✓An unpublished master's thesis reporting a completed research project
  • ✓A doctoral dissertation reporting an original research project
  • DA published concept analysis of the concept of hope
  • EA conceptual framework proposed to explain self-care behavior
Rationale

Empirical literature is knowledge derived from research and is data-based, including published studies plus unpublished studies such as theses and dissertations. Concept analyses and conceptual frameworks are theoretical literature.

Watch for

Excluding unpublished theses and dissertations from the empirical category is the common error — publication status does not decide it, data do. Keep the degrees straight as well: a thesis reports a master's project, a dissertation is the final requirement for a doctorate.

source typestheoretical versus empirical literature
6. A researcher is developing the research framework for a planned study. Which body of literature provides the basis for that framework?
  • ✓Theoretical literature — concept analyses, models, theories, and conceptual frameworks
  • BEmpirical literature — data-based reports of completed studies
  • CSecondary sources that summarize several theorists at once
  • DUnpublished theses and dissertations on the same topic
Rationale

Theoretical literature consists of concept analyses, models, theories, and conceptual frameworks that support a selected research problem and purpose, and it provides the basis for the research framework.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex because empirical sources usually dominate the reference list. The framework is built from theoretical literature; the empirical literature documents what is known about the problem.

source currency and relevancecurrent sources
7. A faculty member tells a student that most of the sources in her synthesis paper must be current. How does the chapter define a current source?
  • APublished within 2 years before publication of the manuscript
  • ✓Published within 5 years before publication of the manuscript
  • CPublished within 10 years before publication of the manuscript
  • DPublished since the most recent revision of the relevant clinical guideline
Rationale

Current sources are those published within 5 years before publication of the manuscript.

Watch for

Currency is a separate test from relevance. A source published last month with no direct bearing on the problem of concern is current but not relevant.

source currency and relevanceseminal and landmark studies
8. A peer reviewer criticizes a literature review for citing a 1967 study, calling it out of date. That study was the first study on the topic and signaled the beginning of a new way of thinking about it. What is the best appraisal of the criticism?
  • AThe criticism is correct; sources older than 5 years should be removed from the review
  • BThe criticism is correct unless the 1967 study has been replicated since
  • ✓The criticism is unfounded; a review should be comprehensive as well as current, and seminal studies belong in it
  • DThe criticism is unfounded because the currency rule applies only to qualitative reviews
Rationale

Sources should be comprehensive as well as current, and because some problems have been studied for decades, reviews properly include seminal and landmark studies conducted years ago.

Watch for

The 5-year rule is real, but it is a currency standard, not a ban. An old citation is not automatically a weakness if it is seminal or landmark.

source currency and relevanceseminal and landmark studies
9. A review describes one cited study as a significant research project that generated knowledge influencing the discipline and sometimes society as a whole, and notes that it has been replicated frequently. Which term names this kind of study?
  • AA relevant study
  • BA current source
  • CA seminal study
  • ✓A landmark study
Rationale

Landmark studies are significant research projects that have generated knowledge influencing a discipline and sometimes society as a whole, and they are frequently replicated or used as the basis for further studies.

Watch for

(C) is the near-neighbor and the most-swapped pair in the chapter. Landmark means influential and discipline-shaping; seminal means first.

source currency and relevanceseminal and landmark studies
10. An author refers to a group of cited works as classic studies. Which term does the chapter treat as interchangeable, and what does that term denote?
  • ✓Seminal studies — the first studies on a topic, which signaled the beginning of a new way of thinking
  • BLandmark studies — studies that generated knowledge influencing an entire discipline
  • CRelevant studies — studies with a direct bearing on the problem of concern
  • DEmpirical studies — studies whose knowledge is derived from data
Rationale

Seminal studies are the first studies on a particular topic that signaled the beginning of a new way of thinking, and they are sometimes called classic studies.

Watch for

This is the mirror of the landmark item. Sort by the cue word in the stem: first or classic means seminal, influential or frequently replicated means landmark.

source currency and relevancestandards for a quality review
11. A student is appraising the reference list and scope of a published literature review. Which features indicate a sound review? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Most of the references are primary sources that are peer-reviewed
  • ✓The sources are relevant, having a direct bearing on the problem of concern
  • ✓The review is broad enough for the reader to become familiar with the research problem
  • ✓The review is narrow enough to include only the most relevant sources
  • EEvery source without exception was published within the past 5 years
Rationale

Most references in quality reviews are primary and peer-reviewed, relevant sources have a direct bearing on the problem, and the scope is broad enough to familiarize the reader yet narrow enough to include only the most relevant sources.

Watch for

(E) turns a currency guideline into an absolute. Reviews must be comprehensive as well as current, which is exactly why seminal and landmark studies survive the 5-year cutoff.

purpose of the literature reviewwhat the review accomplishes
12. According to the chapter, what does the literature review in a published research report do? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Describes the current knowledge of a practice problem
  • ✓Identifies the gaps in that knowledge base
  • ✓Explains how the study being reported has contributed to building knowledge in the area
  • DEstablishes whether the study's findings reached statistical significance
  • ESubstitutes for the study's research framework
Rationale

A review in a published report describes current knowledge of a practice problem, identifies gaps in that knowledge base, and explains how the reported study contributed to building knowledge in the area.

Watch for

(E) inverts the relationship. The framework is built from theoretical literature rather than replaced by the review, and both may be folded into the introduction without a separate heading.

13. A nurse wants to change a unit practice and asks a colleague where the process starts. What does the chapter identify as the first step in implementing evidence-based practice and identifying research problems?
  • AConvening a practice council to select an outcome measure
  • ✓Reviewing the literature
  • CSelecting a research framework
  • DCollecting baseline data on the unit
Rationale

Reviewing the literature is the first step in implementing evidence-based practice and in identifying research problems.

Watch for

(D) is attractive because baseline data feel like step one on a quality-improvement project. The chapter puts the review first, before anything is measured or framed.

literature review timing by research typequantitative research
14. A quantitative study report contains an extensive review written before data collection plus a shorter review of studies published since the study began 3 years earlier. How should this be appraised?
  • AA flaw, because a report should present a single unified review
  • BA flaw, because the later review should have replaced the earlier one rather than been added to it
  • CA flaw, because it shows the researchers did not adequately plan the study
  • ✓Appropriate, because the major review directs planning and execution and a limited review after completion identifies studies published since, especially when a year or longer has passed
Rationale

In quantitative research the major review is performed at the beginning to direct the planning and execution of the study, a limited review is conducted after completion — especially if a year or longer has passed — and both reviews appear in the report.

Watch for

An option describing the review as happening only after data collection is describing the qualitative pattern, not the quantitative one.

literature review timing by research typequalitative approaches
15. A phenomenological researcher deliberately delays the literature review until after data collection and initial analysis are complete. What is the best appraisal?
  • AA weakness, because the review must always precede data collection
  • BA weakness unique to phenomenology that reviewers should require the author to correct
  • ✓Consistent with the approach, because reviewing early could bias data collection, data analysis, and findings
  • DAcceptable only when fewer than five studies exist on the topic
Rationale

Phenomenologists are likely to delay the review until after data collection and initial analysis, because some qualitative researchers do not want their expectations about the topic to bias data collection, analysis, and findings.

Watch for

Calling a delayed review sloppiness is the reflex error. Delaying it is a deliberate methodological choice that keeps the researcher open to participants' perspectives.

literature review timing by research typequalitative approaches
16. Which statements about the timing of the literature review in qualitative research are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Phenomenologists are likely to delay the review until after data collection and initial analysis
  • ✓Grounded theory researchers conduct a minimal review at the beginning, merely to be aware of what has been done and that a problem exists
  • ✓In ethnography the review is handled similarly to the way it is handled in quantitative research
  • ✓A deeper review may be omitted because qualitative studies are often conducted on topics about which we know very little
  • EIn grounded theory the early review is used to direct data collection and theory development
Rationale

Phenomenology delays the review, grounded theory does a minimal review at the beginning, ethnography resembles quantitative research, and a deeper review may be omitted because little literature exists on topics about which we know very little.

Watch for

(E) reverses grounded theory's rule. The early review is explicitly not used to direct data collection or theory development; the literature is used afterward to explain, support, and extend the theory generated.

searching the literatureselecting a database
17. A nursing student needs clinical, theoretical, and research articles drawn from nursing and allied health journals. Which database is the best fit?
  • ✓CINAHL
  • BPsycARTICLES
  • CPubMed
  • DAcademic Search Complete
Rationale

CINAHL indexes nursing and allied health journals and includes clinical, theoretical, and research articles, many in full text.

Watch for

(C) is the reflex because PubMed is free and familiar. PubMed is free access to MEDLINE, which indexes biomedical journals deemed reputable by the National Library of Medicine — not a nursing-specific body of literature.

searching the literaturesearch strategy
18. A student is planning a database search for a synthesis paper. Which actions and cautions match the chapter's guidance? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Look beneath a useful article's abstract to see whether its search terms are listed, and reuse them as keywords
  • ✓Consult a librarian, who is an information specialist and may make searching more effective
  • ✓Assess sources retrieved through general search engines for quality, such as evidence of peer review
  • ✓Review the abstracts to identify relevant studies before reading full articles
  • ETreat Google Scholar results as equivalent to bibliographic database results, since both index scholarly work
Rationale

The chapter's process moves from selecting databases and search terms through searching, refining, reviewing abstracts to identify relevant studies, and reading the articles; it recommends reusing a good article's listed search terms and consulting a librarian.

Watch for

(E) is tempting because general search engines do return scholarly publications. They may also return sources that are not reliable and may not be the most current, which is why the peer-review check is required.

appraising review qualitysynthesis versus summary
19. A student's literature review is a series of paragraphs, each describing a single study, with no link drawn to the other studies being reviewed. What is the correct appraisal?
  • AIt is acceptable, because each study is described accurately and given its own paragraph
  • ✓It does not provide evidence of adequate analysis or synthesis of the literature
  • CIt is acceptable as long as the studies are arranged chronologically
  • DIt is acceptable as long as every study cited is a current primary source
Rationale

The chapter states directly that a review consisting of paragraphs each describing a single study, with no link to other studies, does not provide evidence of adequate analysis or synthesis of the literature.

Watch for

(D) is tempting because source quality is a separate standard the review may genuinely satisfy. Excellent sources arranged one per paragraph are still a summary; synthesis requires linking the studies to one another.

appraising review qualitylocating the review in the report
20. A student reports that a published quantitative article contains no literature review, because there is no heading labeled Review of Literature. What is the best response?
  • AThe student is correct; an article without that heading has no review
  • BThe student is correct unless the article reports a qualitative study
  • ✓The review section may not be labeled and may be integrated into the introduction, as the framework may also be
  • DThe review was almost certainly moved to the discussion section instead
Rationale

The literature review section may not be labeled and may be integrated into the introduction, and the same is true of the framework; citations may appear in all sections of the report.

Watch for

Judging a report by its headings is the reflex here, and (D) reinforces it by naming a real section with a different job — the discussion compares this study's findings with other studies' findings and states conclusions that synthesize previous research with the present findings. The absence of a labeled review section is a formatting choice, not evidence that the review was never done.
