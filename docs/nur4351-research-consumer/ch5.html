research problem and purposeproblem versus purpose
1. A study's introduction reads: "Little is known about how medical-surgical nurses recognize early sepsis in hospitalized older adults." The next paragraph reads: "The purpose of this study is to describe how medical-surgical nurses recognize early sepsis in adults older than 65." What does the first sentence represent?
  • AThe research purpose
  • ✓The research problem
  • CThe research objective
  • DThe research topic
Rationale

The research problem is an area of concern in which there is a gap in the knowledge needed for nursing practice; the purpose is a clear concise statement of the specific goal or focus of a study.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex answer because both sentences sit in the same introduction. The sentence naming what is not yet known is always the problem; the sentence naming what this particular study will do is the purpose.

research problem and purposeproblem versus purpose
2. Which statement correctly describes how a study's problem and purpose relate to each other?
  • AThe purpose identifies the gap in knowledge, and the problem narrows that gap to this study's goal
  • BThe problem and the purpose are two names for the same statement
  • ✓The problem is the gap in knowledge, and it provides the basis for developing the purpose
  • DThe purpose is written first, and the problem is derived from it
Rationale

The problem is the area of concern in which there is a gap in knowledge, and the chapter states that the problem provides the basis for developing the research purpose.

Watch for

(A) is the exact inversion of the pair and is the single most common error on this content. Direction of travel is problem to purpose, never the reverse.

research objectives and questionsdeclarative versus interrogative
4. A study report includes the statement: "This study describes the coping strategies used by family caregivers of persons with dementia." Which element is this, and what identifies it?
  • AA research question, because it names variables
  • ✓A research objective, because it is a declarative statement expressed in the present tense
  • CA hypothesis, because it names a population
  • DThe research problem, because it identifies what is not yet known
Rationale

A research objective, sometimes called an aim, is a clear, concise, declarative statement expressed in the present tense that identifies the goals of the study.

Watch for

(C) is tempting because the statement names both variables and a population, but a hypothesis must predict an expected relationship between variables. Nothing here predicts anything.

research objectives and questionsdeclarative versus interrogative
5. The same report includes the statement: "What coping strategies do family caregivers of persons with dementia use?" Which element is this, and what identifies it?
  • AA research objective, because objectives are interrogative
  • BThe research purpose, because it identifies the study's goal
  • CA nondirectional hypothesis, because no direction is predicted
  • ✓A research question, because it is an interrogative statement worded in the present tense
Rationale

A research question is a clear, concise, interrogative statement worded in the present tense that includes one or more variables and is expressed to guide the implementation of studies.

Watch for

(A) swaps the grammatical forms, which is the whole point of this pair. Objective and question are separated by declarative versus interrogative, not by tense — both are present tense.

research objectives and questionsquantitative and qualitative aims
6. Which statements about research objectives are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Objectives are sometimes called aims
  • BQualitative objectives are narrower in focus and more concrete than quantitative objectives
  • ✓Objectives are expressed in the present tense
  • ✓Objectives may be written to determine differences between two or more existing groups
  • ✓Quantitative objectives usually focus on one or two variables
Rationale

Objectives, sometimes called aims, are declarative present-tense statements that usually focus on one or two variables and may identify relationships or determine differences between existing groups such as females and males.

Watch for

(B) inverts the qualitative contrast. Qualitative objectives are commonly broader in focus and include concepts that are more complex and abstract, not narrower and more concrete.

hypothesis typesassociative versus causal
7. A researcher states: "Hours playing video games is related to body mass index in school-age children." How is this hypothesis classified?
  • ✓Simple, associative, nondirectional
  • BSimple, causal, directional
  • CComplex, associative, nondirectional
  • DSimple, associative, directional
Rationale

Two variables makes it simple; "is related to" proposes that the variables occur together without claiming that one causes the other; and no positive or negative nature is predicted.

Watch for

(D) is chosen by students who read any stated relationship as a direction. Direction requires words such as positive, negative, less, more, increase, decrease, greater, higher, or lower — none appears here.

hypothesis typesassociative versus causal
8. A researcher states: "Participants who perform brisk walking exercise combined with music exhibit higher sleep quality scores than participants who perform brisk walking alone." How is this hypothesis classified?
  • AAssociative, simple, nondirectional
  • ✓Causal, complex, directional
  • CCausal, simple, nondirectional
  • DAssociative, complex, nondirectional
Rationale

The chapter classifies this hypothesis as causal, complex because it involves three variables, and directional in the positive sense because it predicts higher sleep quality scores.

Watch for

(C) can be eliminated before you count anything. The chapter states that the independent variable increases or decreases each dependent variable, so all causal hypotheses are directional and "causal, nondirectional" is an impossible combination.

hypothesis typesassociative versus causal
9. Which statements about classifying hypotheses are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓All causal hypotheses are directional
  • ✓A hypothesis stating relationships among three or more variables is complex
  • CA nondirectional hypothesis predicts a negative relationship
  • ✓A complex hypothesis can be associative rather than causal
  • ✓Words such as greater, less, increase, and decrease signal a directional hypothesis
Rationale

Every hypothesis is classified simultaneously on relationship type, number of variables, direction, and form; simple means two variables and complex means three or more, and all causal hypotheses are directional.

Watch for

(C) confuses nondirectional with negative. A nondirectional hypothesis states only that a relationship exists and does not predict its nature at all; predicting a negative relationship is directional.

hypothesis typesdirectional versus nondirectional
10. A researcher states: "Parents will be more likely to misperceive the weight status of younger children." Which classification does the chapter assign?
  • ASimple, associative, nondirectional
  • BComplex, causal, directional
  • ✓Simple, associative, directional
  • DSimple, causal, directional
Rationale

Two variables makes it simple, no cause and effect is proposed, and "more likely" paired with "younger" specifies the nature of the relationship; the chapter classifies it as directional and negative.

Watch for

A directional hypothesis does not have to predict a positive relationship. Direction means the nature is specified, and this one is directional precisely because it predicts a negative relationship.

hypothesis typesresearch versus statistical null hypothesis
11. A published report states only a directional research hypothesis and then reports a t-test comparing group means. Which hypothesis was actually tested, and why?
  • AThe directional research hypothesis, because it is the one the researcher stated
  • BNeither one; the t-test tests the level of significance rather than a hypothesis
  • CThe research hypothesis, because null hypotheses apply only to associative relationships
  • ✓The statistical null hypothesis, which is implied because it is the converse of the research hypothesis
Rationale

Even if the null hypothesis is not stated, it is implied because it is the converse of the research hypothesis, and it is the statistical hypothesis that is tested to determine whether the independent variable produced a significant effect.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex because the printed hypothesis is the only one visible. What is printed and what is tested are different things in every quantitative report.

hypothesis typesresearch versus statistical null hypothesis
12. A hypothesis proposes that perceived social support and medication adherence occur together in adults with heart failure, with no claim that either causes the other. Which analysis matches this hypothesis?
  • ✓Correlational analyses examining the existence, type, and strength of the relationship
  • BA t-test comparing experimental and comparison group means
  • CAn ANOVA comparing three treatment group means
  • DA comparison of pretest and posttest means under an intervention protocol
Rationale

Associative hypotheses are tested with correlational analyses that examine the existence, type, and strength of the relationship, while causal hypotheses are tested with analyses of differences between means such as t-test or ANOVA.

Watch for

(B) and (C) are the causal-hypothesis tools. Match the analysis to the relationship the hypothesis claims, not to how sophisticated the study looks.

hypothesis quality and testabilityelements of a testable hypothesis
13. Which elements should a clearly stated hypothesis include? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓The population to be studied
  • BThe instrument that will measure the dependent variable
  • ✓The independent variables to be manipulated or measured
  • ✓The proposed outcomes or dependent variables to be measured
  • EThe level of significance that will be used
Rationale

A clearly stated hypothesis includes the independent variables to be manipulated or measured, the proposed outcomes or dependent variables to be measured, and the population to be studied.

Watch for

(B) and (E) look like rigor and are actually flaws. Naming an instrument limits the hypothesis to one study's measurement method, and determination of the level of significance is only a statistical technique applied to sample data.

hypothesis quality and testabilityelements of a testable hypothesis
14. A reviewer must flag one of these hypotheses as improperly written. Which one, and why?
  • ✓"Postoperative anxiety, measured by the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory, is lower in patients who receive guided imagery" — it names the measurement method
  • B"Patients who receive guided imagery report lower postoperative anxiety than patients who receive standard care" — it predicts a direction
  • C"Perceived stress is related to sleep quality in graduate nursing students" — it does not claim causation
  • D"Nurses with higher workloads report more missed nursing care" — it uses the comparative word higher
Rationale

Methodological phrases such as "measured by," "in a random sample of," and "using ANOVA" are inappropriate in a hypothesis because they limit it to one study's measurement methods, sample, or analysis techniques.

Watch for

(B) and (D) are correctly written directional hypotheses and (C) is a correctly written associative hypothesis. Predicting a direction and declining to claim causation are strengths, not defects.

hypothesis quality and testabilityelements of a testable hypothesis
15. A reviewer is judging whether a hypothesis is testable. Which feature is required?
  • AIt states whether the difference between the groups will be statistically significant
  • BIt names the sample and the analysis technique so the prediction can be checked
  • ✓It predicts a relationship among variables that the data can support or not support
  • DIt is written in null form so that it can be rejected
Rationale

A testable hypothesis clearly predicts relationships among variables, contains variables that are measurable or able to be manipulated, and predicts a relationship that can be supported or not supported by the data.

Watch for

(A) and (B) name the two things a hypothesis must not contain. (D) is unnecessary because the null hypothesis is implied as the converse of the research hypothesis whether or not it is written out.

hypothesis quality and testabilitywhich studies require hypotheses
16. A researcher is designing a quasi-experimental study testing whether a nurse-led education protocol reduces 30-day readmissions compared with usual care. What does the chapter say about hypotheses for a study like this?
  • AHypotheses are optional; research questions are preferred for intervention studies
  • ✓Quasi-experimental and experimental studies should include hypotheses to predict the study outcomes
  • CHypotheses are required only when the sample has been randomly selected
  • DHypotheses are appropriate only for descriptive and correlational studies
Rationale

Quasi-experimental and experimental studies should include hypotheses to predict the study outcomes, and predictive correlational studies that measure independent variables to predict a dependent variable often include hypotheses.

Watch for

(D) inverts the rule. Descriptive and correlational studies typically use objectives or questions, and hypotheses go on to shape the design, sampling method, data collection and analysis, and interpretation of findings.

study variablesindependent and dependent variables
17. A researcher assigns patients to either a nurse-led sleep hygiene protocol or usual care and measures sleep quality at 4 weeks. Which labeling is correct?
  • ASleep quality is the independent variable and the protocol is the dependent variable
  • BBoth are research variables because both are measured
  • CThe protocol is an extraneous variable and sleep quality is the dependent variable
  • ✓The protocol is the independent variable and sleep quality is the dependent variable
Rationale

The independent variable is the intervention or treatment manipulated or varied by the researcher to create an effect on the dependent variable, and the dependent variable is the outcome the researcher wants to predict or explain.

Watch for

(A) is the classic inversion. The independent variable — also called the intervention, treatment, or experimental variable — is what the researcher does, not what is measured as a result.

study variablesindependent and dependent variables
18. In a predictive correlational study, self-efficacy, social support, and health literacy are measured to predict medication adherence. Nothing is manipulated. What are the first three variables called?
  • AResearch variables, because none of them is manipulated
  • BExtraneous variables, because they are not controlled
  • ✓Independent variables, even though none of them is manipulated
  • DDependent variables, because all three are measured
Rationale

The chapter states explicitly that in predictive correlational studies, the variables measured to predict a single dependent variable are called independent variables.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex answer because nothing is being manipulated. This chapter is clear that "independent variable" does not always mean "manipulated intervention," so any option asserting that an independent variable must be manipulated is too absolute for this content.

study variablesresearch and extraneous variables
19. A nurse conducts a descriptive study measuring fatigue, appetite, and mood in patients receiving chemotherapy. No intervention is provided and no cause-and-effect relationship is examined. What are these three variables called?
  • ✓Research variables
  • BIndependent variables
  • CDependent variables
  • DExtraneous variables
Rationale

Research variables are the qualities, properties, or characteristics measured in a study as they exist in a natural setting without a treatment, and they appear in descriptive and correlational studies where no independent variables are manipulated.

Watch for

(C) is tempting because the three variables are outcomes being measured. Calling them dependent variables implies their changes are presumed to be caused by an independent variable, and there is no independent variable here.

study variablesresearch and extraneous variables
20. Which statements about extraneous variables are accurate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓They exist in all studies
  • ✓They are classified as recognized or unrecognized
  • ✓They are classified as controlled or uncontrolled
  • DThey are absent from descriptive and correlational studies
  • ✓They are a primary concern in intervention studies
Rationale

Extraneous variables exist in all studies and can affect the measurement of study variables and the relationships among them; they are classified as recognized or unrecognized and as controlled or uncontrolled, and they are a primary concern in intervention studies.

Watch for

(D) confuses "primary concern in intervention studies" with "only present in intervention studies." Some extraneous variables are not recognized until a study is in progress or completed, and researchers control them through specific designs, intervention protocols, and sample criteria, with environmental control an extremely important part of experimental studies.
