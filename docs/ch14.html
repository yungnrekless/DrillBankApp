health education in the communitydomains of learning
1. A nurse teaches a newly diagnosed diabetic what diabetes is and the facts of self-care. Which domain of learning does this activity target?
  • ✓Cognitive
  • BAffective
  • CPsychomotor
  • DAndragogical
Rationale

The cognitive domain involves thinking — memory, understanding, reasoning, and problem solving. Teaching the facts of a disease and self-care is the textbook cognitive example.

Watch for

"Andragogical" describes an approach to teaching adults, not a domain of learning — it is a category-error distractor.

  • ✓Affective; it is the hardest domain to change

14. A patient says, "Even if I did get lung disease, it wouldn't be that bad — I'd just deal with it." Which Health Belief Model component is this patient expressing low perceived level of?
  • APerceived susceptibility
  • ✓Perceived severity
  • CPerceived benefits
  • DSelf-efficacy
Rationale

Perceived severity asks "if it happens, is it a big problem?" This patient is minimizing how serious the consequence would be.

Watch for

Perceived susceptibility is about whether the event will happen at all ("will something happen to me?"), not how bad it would be if it did — the two are easy to swap.

15. A patient says, "I don't think quitting smoking will actually make a difference for someone like me at this point." Which Health Belief Model component does this reflect?
  • APerceived susceptibility
  • BPerceived barriers
  • ✓Perceived benefits
  • DCues to action
Rationale

Perceived benefits addresses "will doing this really help?" The patient is doubting that the behavior change will actually help.

Watch for

Perceived barriers concerns obstacles to acting (cost, unpleasantness), not doubt about whether the action would work if performed.

16. Which are the six components of the Health Belief Model? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Perceived susceptibility
  • ✓Perceived severity
  • ✓Perceived benefits
  • ✓Perceived barriers
  • ✓Cues to action
  • ✓Self-efficacy
  • GStages of change
Rationale

The HBM's six components are perceived susceptibility, perceived severity, perceived benefits, perceived barriers, cues to action, and self-efficacy. Stages of change belongs to the Transtheoretical Model, a different model entirely.

Watch for

Stages of change is the hallmark of the Transtheoretical Model (TTM), not the HBM — a common cross-model mix-up.

18. A community health nurse is forming a support group focused on helping individual members change a personal health behavior. What group size best supports this goal, and why?

  • BUp to 25 members, because larger groups generate more diverse input
  • C3–5 members, because smaller is always better for trust

Rationale

A group size of 8–12 members works best for individual health change; groups of up to 25 can work when the focus is community needs, and large groups should split into subgroups.

20. Members of a newly formed support group begin to disagree openly about how the group should proceed. The facilitator is considering whether to redirect the group away from the disagreement. What does the chapter say about this situation?
  • AConflict should be suppressed quickly to preserve group cohesion
  • ✓Conflict is normal and useful, signaling that differing viewpoints must be considered; a group that repeatedly avoids conflict becomes fragile
  • CConflict indicates the group is too large and should be split into subgroups
  • DConflict is a sign that trust has already failed to develop
Rationale

Conflict is normal and useful — it signals that differing viewpoints must be considered. A group that repeatedly avoids conflict becomes fragile and unable to adapt. Effective facilitation respects diverse opinions and promotes their expression.

Watch for

Suppressing conflict is the intuitive but wrong move — the fact bank is explicit that suppression damages the group, while trust is built progressively through cohesion and working through conflict, not by avoiding it.
