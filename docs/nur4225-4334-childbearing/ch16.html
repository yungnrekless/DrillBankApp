Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓The passenger, meaning the fetus and the placenta
  • ✓The passageway, meaning the birth canal
  • ✓The powers, meaning the contractions
  • DThe place of birth and the type of provider
  • ✓The position of the mother
  • ✓The psychologic response of the woman
Rationale

The chapter's own mnemonic is explicit: "At least five factors affect the process of labor and birth. These are easily remembered as the five Ps: passengers (fetus and placenta), passageway (birth canal), powers (contractions), position of the mother, and psychologic response."

(D) is the one to reject, and it pulls because the book does name those factors — "place of birth, preparation, type of provider ... nursing care, and procedures" — but files them as a separate list of other influences, deliberately outside the five. 2. A student asks whether the placenta should really be counted as a passenger, since it is delivered after the baby. Which response reflects the chapter?
  • ✓Yes, because it must pass through the birth canal, though it rarely interferes with a normal vaginal birth
  • BNo, because it is maternal tissue rather than fetal tissue
  • CYes, because it is the structure most likely to obstruct the descent of the fetus
  • DNo, because it is expelled in the third stage rather than the second
Rationale

"Because the placenta also must pass through the birth canal, it can be considered a passenger along with the fetus; however, the placenta rarely interferes with the process of labor in a normal vaginal birth. An exception is the case of placenta previa."

Watch for

(C) overstates the one exception into the rule. Placenta previa is named as the exception precisely because obstruction by the placenta is otherwise rare.

4. During a vaginal examination after rupture of membranes, the nurse palpates a diamond-shaped soft spot measuring roughly 3 cm by 2 cm at the junction of the sagittal, coronal, and frontal sutures. Which structure has been palpated, and what does finding it help establish?
  • AThe posterior fontanel, which helps establish fetal lie
  • ✓The anterior fontanel, which helps establish presentation, position, and attitude
  • CThe lambdoidal suture, which helps establish the degree of molding
  • DThe anterior fontanel, which helps establish the station of the presenting part
Rationale

The anterior fontanel is "diamond shaped," approximately 3 cm by 2 cm, at the junction of the sagittal, coronal, and frontal sutures. The chapter gives the clinical reason for finding it: "During labor after rupture of membranes, palpation of fontanels and sutures during vaginal examination reveals fetal presentation, position, and attitude."

Watch for

(D) is the tempting half-right answer because the same examination does establish station — but station is measured against the ischial spines, not read off a fontanel.

5. On the same examination the nurse palpates a triangular soft spot at the junction of the sutures of the two parietal bones and the occipital bone. Which fontanel is this, and when does it close?
  • AThe anterior fontanel, closing by 18 months
  • ✓The posterior fontanel, closing 6 to 8 weeks after birth
  • CThe posterior fontanel, closing by 18 months
  • DThe anterior fontanel, closing 6 to 8 weeks after birth
Rationale

The posterior fontanel is triangular, approximately 1 cm by 2 cm, at the junction of the sutures of the two parietal bones and the occipital bone, and closes 6 to 8 weeks after birth.

Watch for

(C) is the mirror error of the previous item — right fontanel, wrong closure time. The cue that survives a blank moment is that the bigger one takes longer to close: anterior is 3 by 2 cm and closes at 18 months, posterior is 1 by 2 cm and closes at 6 to 8 weeks.

6. Parents of a newborn born after a long second stage are distressed that their baby's head looks elongated and asymmetric. Which explanation does the chapter support?
  • ✓The skull bones are not firmly united, so they slide over one another during labor and most newborn heads regain their normal shape within 3 days
  • BThe bones have separated at the sutures under pressure and will reunite over the first 18 months
  • CThis represents fluid collected under the scalp and will resolve once the swelling is reabsorbed
  • DThe head has been compressed against an android pelvis and the shape change is usually permanent
Rationale

"Because the bones are not firmly united, slight overlapping or molding of the shape of the head occurs during labor. This capacity of the bones to slide over one another also permits adaptation to the various diameters of the maternal pelvis." The chapter adds that molding "can be extensive, but the heads of most newborns assume their normal shape within 3 days after birth."

Watch for

(B) sounds like a reasonable restatement but reverses the mechanism — the bones overlap rather than separate, and it is that overlap that lets the head adapt to the pelvis.

7. A term fetus is described as having the chin flexed on the chest, the thighs flexed on the abdomen, and the legs flexed at the knees. Which of the five Ps does this description belong to, and what is the finding called?
  • APosition; it describes an occiput anterior presentation
  • ✓Attitude; it describes general flexion, the normal attitude
  • CLie; it describes a longitudinal lie
  • DPresentation; it describes a vertex presentation
Rationale

"Attitude is the relation of the fetal body parts to one another," and the chapter's description of normal attitude is exactly this: "Normally the back of the fetus is rounded so that the chin is flexed on the chest, the thighs are flexed on the abdomen, and the legs are flexed at the knees."

Watch for

(C) pulls because a flexed fetus is usually also longitudinal, but lie compares the fetal spine to the maternal spine. Attitude compares the fetus to itself; nothing in this stem mentions the mother.

8. A vertex-presenting fetus is in complete flexion at the onset of labor. Which head diameter is presenting to the pelvis, and why does it matter?
  • ✓The suboccipitobregmatic, about 9.5 cm, the smallest and most critical diameter, which allows the head to pass easily through the true pelvis
  • BThe biparietal, about 9.25 cm, the smallest anteroposterior diameter of the fetal head
  • CThe occipitofrontal, which is the diameter that defines engagement
  • DThe occipitomental, which is the diameter presented when the head is fully flexed
Rationale

The suboccipitobregmatic diameter is "about 9.5 cm at term" and is "the smallest and the most critical" of the anteroposterior diameters. "When the head is in complete flexion, this diameter allows the fetal head to pass easily through the true pelvis."

Watch for

(B) is the classic swap: the biparietal is real and is 9.25 cm, but it is the largest transverse diameter, not an anteroposterior one. The two numbers are close enough that the roles get traded rather than the values.

9. A fetus presents with the head in marked extension, or deflection. Which diameter now presents to the inlet, and what does the chapter say about it?
  • AThe suboccipitobregmatic, which remains the presenting diameter regardless of attitude
  • BThe occipitofrontal, which is slightly larger but still permits entry
  • ✓The occipitomental, which is too large to permit the head to enter the pelvis
  • DThe biparietal, which widens as the head extends
Rationale

Fig. 16.5C shows "marked extension (deflection), so that largest diameter, which is too large to permit the head to enter the pelvis, is presenting" — the occipitomental. The governing rule is stated directly: "As the head is more extended, the anteroposterior diameter widens, and the head may not be able to enter the true pelvis."

Watch for

(B) is the mirror error of the next item and the reason this pair exists — moderate extension gives the occipitofrontal, marked extension gives the occipitomental. Both are larger than the flexed diameter, but only one is described as too large to enter at all.

10. A fetus is described as being in a military attitude, with the head neither flexed nor markedly extended. Which diameter is entering the pelvis?
  • AThe suboccipitobregmatic, because a neutral head is functionally flexed
  • ✓The occipitofrontal, because moderate extension presents that large diameter
  • CThe occipitomental, because any loss of flexion presents the largest diameter
  • DThe bitemporal, because the head is neither flexed nor extended
Rationale

Fig. 16.5B labels the military attitude "moderate extension (military attitude) so that large diameter enters" — the occipitofrontal.

Watch for

(C) is the mirror error of the previous item, jumping straight to the worst case. The chapter grades the three attitudes rather than treating any loss of flexion as equivalent: complete flexion gives 9.5 cm, moderate extension gives the occipitofrontal, marked extension gives the occipitomental.

11. An ultrasound report gives a biparietal diameter of 9.25 cm at term. Which statement about that measurement is accurate?
  • ✓It is the largest transverse diameter and an important indicator of fetal head size
  • BIt is the smallest anteroposterior diameter and the one flexion produces
  • CIt is the diameter that widens as the fetal head extends
  • DIt is measured from the suboccipital region to the bregma
Rationale

The biparietal diameter is "about 9.25 cm at term" and is "the largest transverse diameter and an important indicator of fetal head size. In a well-flexed cephalic presentation, the biparietal diameter is the widest part of the head entering the pelvic inlet." It is also the diameter whose passage through the inlet defines engagement.

Watch for

(B) and (D) both describe the suboccipitobregmatic, which is the other number students hold in this slot. Transverse versus anteroposterior is the distinction that keeps them apart: 9.25 cm side to side, 9.5 cm front to back with the head flexed.

fetal presentation and liewhat presents first
12. A term fetus is in a cephalic presentation with the occiput as the presenting part. How is this presentation noted, and how common is it?
  • ✓Noted as vertex; cephalic presentations account for approximately 97% to 98% of births
  • BNoted as vertex; cephalic presentations account for approximately 80% of births
  • CNoted as occiput anterior; cephalic presentations account for approximately 97% to 98% of births
  • DNoted as military; cephalic presentations account for approximately 90% of births
Rationale

"Presentation refers to the part of the fetus that enters the pelvic inlet first and leads through the birth canal during labor at term." Cephalic presentation occurs in "approximately 97% to 98% of births," the presenting part is usually the occiput, and "when the presenting part is the occiput, the presentation is noted as vertex."

Watch for

(C) confuses presentation with position. Occiput anterior is a position — a three-letter notation relating the occiput to a quadrant of the maternal pelvis — while vertex names what is presenting.

13. A fetus is found on examination to be presenting buttocks first, and a second fetus on the unit is lying with a shoulder over the inlet. Which presenting parts correspond to these two presentations?
  • ✓The sacrum for the breech and the scapula for the shoulder presentation
  • BThe scapula for the breech and the sacrum for the shoulder presentation
  • CThe mentum for the breech and the scapula for the shoulder presentation
  • DThe sacrum for the breech and the mentum for the shoulder presentation
Rationale

Breech presentation occurs in approximately 2% to 3% of births at term and the presenting part is the sacrum; shoulder presentation occurs in fewer than 1% of births and the presenting part is the scapula.

Watch for

(C) and (D) both reach for the mentum, which is the chin — it is the reference point for a face presentation, not for a breech or a shoulder. The four reference points are occiput, sacrum, mentum, and scapula, and they are easy to redistribute among the wrong presentations.

fetal presentation and lielie and its one absolute
14. An ultrasound at the onset of labor shows the fetal spine at a right angle to the mother's spine, and the fetus remains in that orientation. What does the chapter state about the outcome?
  • AThe lie is oblique and will usually convert during labor
  • ✓The lie is transverse, and vaginal birth cannot occur if the fetus stays in it
  • CThe lie is transverse, and vaginal birth is possible if the shoulder engages
  • DThe lie is longitudinal with a shoulder presentation
Rationale

"Lie is the relation of the long axis (spine) of the fetus to the long axis (spine) of the mother." In a transverse lie the fetal long axis is at a right angle to the mother's, and the chapter states the consequence without qualification: "Vaginal birth cannot occur when the fetus stays in a transverse lie."

Watch for

(A) confuses the two abnormal lies. An oblique lie is the one that "usually converts to a longitudinal or transverse lie during labor"; the transverse lie is the chapter's one absolute.

15. A fetus lies with its long axis parallel to the mother's long axis. Which statement follows from that finding alone?
  • AThe presentation is necessarily vertex
  • ✓The presentation is either cephalic or breech, depending on which fetal structure enters the pelvis first
  • CThe attitude is one of general flexion
  • DThe position is occiput anterior
Rationale

"Longitudinal lies are either cephalic or breech presentations, depending on the fetal structure that first enters the mother's pelvis." The chapter also names what settles the presenting part: "Factors that determine the presenting part include fetal lie, fetal attitude, and extension or flexion of the fetal head."

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error of the previous item — knowing the lie narrows the presentation to two options but does not pick between them. A longitudinal lie is equally compatible with a breech.

16. A cervical examination is documented as ROA. What does that notation state about the fetus?
  • ✓The occiput is the presenting part and is in the right anterior quadrant of the mother's pelvis
  • BThe fetus's own right side is anterior, with the occiput presenting
  • CThe occiput is the presenting part and is in the right anterior quadrant of the fetal pelvis
  • DThe sacrum is the presenting part and is in the right anterior quadrant of the mother's pelvis
Rationale

"Position is the relationship of a reference point on the presenting part (occiput, sacrum, mentum [chin], or sinciput [deflexed vertex]) to the four quadrants of the mother's pelvis." The book's own worked example reads: "ROA means that the occiput is the presenting part and is located in the right anterior quadrant of the maternal pelvis."

Watch for

(B) misassigns the reference frame, which is the single most common error with this notation. The first letter names the mother's right or left side, never the fetus's.

17. A second examination is documented as LSP. What does that notation state?
  • AThe occiput is presenting in the left posterior quadrant, a vertex presentation
  • ✓The sacrum is presenting in the left posterior quadrant, a breech presentation
  • CThe fetal spine lies in the left posterior quadrant
  • DThe scapula is presenting in the left posterior quadrant, a shoulder presentation

The book's second worked example: "LSP means that the sacrum is the presenting part and is located in the left posterior quadrant." The sacrum is the reference point for a breech.

(C) is the trap this notation sets every time — reading the middle S as "spine" instead of "sacrum." The middle letter always names the presenting part, and the four values are O for occiput, S for sacrum, M for mentum, and Sc for scapula.

19. A cervical examination places the lowermost portion of the presenting part 1 cm above the ischial spines. How is this station documented, and what does it indicate?
  • APlus 1, indicating the presenting part has descended past the spines
  • ✓Minus 1, indicating the presenting part has not yet reached the level of the spines
  • CZero, indicating engagement
  • DMinus 1, indicating the presenting part is 1 cm below the spines
Rationale

"Station is the relationship of the presenting fetal part to an imaginary line drawn between the maternal ischial spines and is a measure of the degree of descent." When the lowermost portion of the presenting part is 1 cm above the spines, "it is noted as being minus 1."

(A) inverts the sign, which is the reversal this measurement invites. Minus is above the spines and less descended; plus is below and further along.

22. Why does the chapter say station should be determined when labor begins?
  • ✓So that the rate of descent of the fetus during labor can be assessed accurately
  • BSo that the estimated fetal weight can be calculated
  • CBecause station cannot be assessed once the membranes have ruptured
  • DBecause station determines the pelvic type
Rationale

"The station of the presenting part should be determined when labor begins so that the rate of descent of the fetus during labor can be assessed accurately." A single station means little; the change between two stations is the finding.

Watch for

(C) reverses the relationship between station and membranes. Rupture of membranes makes palpation of fontanels and sutures possible, and descent is especially apparent after the membranes have ruptured.

station, engagement, and synclitismreading station
23. A nulliparous woman at 38 weeks is told at a prenatal visit that the baby has dropped, and a multiparous woman on the labor unit is found to have a freely movable head at 3 cm. Which statement about engagement fits both findings?
  • ABoth are abnormal and warrant evaluation for cephalopelvic disproportion
  • ✓Engagement often occurs in the weeks just before labor in nulliparas and may occur before or during labor in multiparas
  • CEngagement occurs only after labor is established, regardless of parity
  • DEngagement occurs earlier in multiparas because the abdominal musculature is more relaxed
Rationale

Engagement "often occurs in the weeks just before labor begins in nulliparas and may occur before or during labor in multiparas." The cardinal-movements section gives the reason: firmer abdominal muscles in nulliparous pregnancies direct the presenting part into the pelvis, while in multiparous pregnancies the more relaxed musculature often leaves the head freely movable above the brim until labor is established.

Watch for

(D) inverts the parity rule and is the mirror error to watch for, because relaxed abdominal muscles sound like they should let the head settle sooner. They do the opposite — they fail to direct it in.

24. A fetal head is noted to be deflected anteriorly in the pelvis rather than parallel to the anteroposterior plane. How should the nurse interpret this finding?
  • ✓As asynclitism, which frequently occurs and can facilitate descent by positioning the head to accommodate the pelvic cavity
  • BAs asynclitism, which is always pathologic and predicts cephalopelvic disproportion
  • CAs synclitism, the normal position at engagement
  • DAs a transverse arrest requiring immediate cesarean birth
Rationale

"The head usually engages in the pelvis in a synclitic position (i.e., one that is parallel to the anteroposterior plane of the pelvis)." Asynclitism, in which "the head is deflected anteriorly or posteriorly in the pelvis," "frequently occurs, which can facilitate descent because the head is being positioned to accommodate to the pelvic cavity."

Watch for

(B) treats a common and often helpful adaptation as a diagnosis, which is the reflex when an unfamiliar term appears in a stem.

25. A labor is arrested despite a pelvis measured as normal in size, and the head is found to be extremely deflected. What does the chapter say about this situation?
  • ✓Extreme asynclitism can cause cephalopelvic disproportion even in a normal-size pelvis, because the head is positioned so that it cannot descend
  • BCephalopelvic disproportion cannot occur when pelvic measurements are normal
  • CThe finding indicates an android pelvis regardless of the measurements
  • DThe finding is a normal variant and descent will resume without intervention
Rationale

"Extreme asynclitism can cause cephalopelvic disproportion, even in a normal-size pelvis, because the fetal head is positioned so that it cannot descend through the maternal pelvis."

Watch for

(D) is the mirror error of the previous item — having learned that asynclitism is usually helpful, the reflex is to dismiss the extreme form too. The chapter draws the line at extreme, and (B) fails for the same reason: disproportion is about fit, not about measurements alone.

bony pelvis planes and obstetric measurementsthe true pelvis and its three planes
26. A student asks which part of the bony pelvis the fetus actually passes through. Which response is correct?
  • AThe false pelvis, the part above the brim, which forms the birth canal
  • ✓The true pelvis, the part below the brim, divided into the inlet, the midpelvis, and the outlet
  • CBoth parts equally, since the brim is only an anatomic landmark
  • DThe true pelvis, which is divided into the inlet and the outlet only
Rationale

"The bony pelvis is separated by the brim, or inlet, into two parts: the false and the true pelves." And: "The false pelvis is the part above the brim and plays no part in childbearing. The true pelvis, the part involved in birth, is divided into three planes: the inlet, or brim; the midpelvis, or cavity; and the outlet."

Watch for

(D) drops the midpelvis, which is the plane most stems turn on — it is where the ischial spines sit, where station is measured, and where internal rotation begins.

27. A woman is found on examination to have a narrow subpubic angle. Why does this matter for labor?
  • AIt narrows the pelvic inlet, preventing engagement
  • ✓The fetus must first pass beneath the pubic arch, and a narrow subpubic angle is less accommodating than a rounded wide arch
  • CIt shortens the anterior wall of the midpelvis
  • DIt makes the coccyx immobile in the latter part of pregnancy
Rationale

"Because the fetus must first pass beneath the pubic arch, a narrow subpubic angle is less accommodating than a rounded wide arch." The outlet is bounded by the pubic arch anteriorly, the ischial tuberosities laterally, and the tip of the coccyx posteriorly.

Watch for

(D) contradicts the chapter, which notes that in the latter part of pregnancy "the coccyx is movable" — mobility at the back of the outlet is one of the accommodations that offsets a tight fit.

31. A woman's pelvis is described as having a transversely rounded brim, straight side walls, blunt and widely separated ischial spines, a deep curved sacrum, and a wide subpubic arch. Which pelvic type is this, and what does Table 16.2 associate it with?
  • ✓Gynecoid, associated with birth in the occiput anterior position
  • BAnthropoid, associated with birth in the occiput posterior position
  • CPlatypelloid, associated with transverse arrest
  • DAndroid, associated with an increased risk of cephalopelvic disproportion
Rationale

Table 16.2 gives the gynecoid pelvis a slightly ovoid or transversely rounded brim, straight side walls, blunt widely separated spines, a deep curved sacrum, and a wide subpubic arch, and names its effect on labor as the "classic female shape; associated with birth in OA position." It is the most common type at about 50%.

Watch for

(D) picks the type whose features are the near-opposite of this stem. Android features are the ones that read as tight: heart-shaped brim, convergent side walls, prominent spines with a narrow interspinous diameter, and a narrow arch.

Descent is "the progress of the presenting part through the pelvis," depending on four forces: pressure exerted by the amniotic fluid, direct pressure of the contracting fundus on the fetus, the force of the maternal diaphragm and abdominal muscles in the second stage, and extension and straightening of the fetal body. Descent accelerates in the active phase once the cervix has dilated to 5 to 6 cm and is especially apparent after the membranes have ruptured.

Watch for

(E) is the option to leave unselected, and it pulls because the pelvic floor does have a named role — it helps the fetus rotate anteriorly. Rotation, not descent, is its contribution.

68. Why must internal rotation occur, and where does it begin?
  • ✓Because the inlet is widest transversely and the outlet widest anteroposteriorly; it begins at the level of the ischial spines
  • BBecause the inlet is widest anteroposteriorly and the outlet widest transversely; it begins at the pelvic inlet
  • CBecause the fetal shoulders must align with the anteroposterior diameter; it begins after the head is born
  • DBecause the levator ani muscles contract asymmetrically; it begins at the outlet
Rationale

"The maternal pelvic inlet is widest in the transverse diameter; therefore the fetal head passes the inlet into the true pelvis in the occipitotransverse position. The outlet is widest in the anteroposterior diameter; for the fetus to exit, the head must rotate." Rotation "begins at the level of the ischial spines but is not completed until the presenting part reaches the lower pelvis. As the occiput rotates anteriorly, the face rotates posteriorly."

Watch for

(B) swaps the two widest diameters, which reverses the entire rationale for rotation. If the inlet were widest front to back, the head would need no turn at all.

69. Immediately after the head is born, it turns briefly to the position it occupied when it was engaged in the inlet, and then turns further. What are these two movements, and what is delivered first as they complete?
  • AInternal rotation and flexion, with the posterior shoulder delivered first
  • ✓Restitution and external rotation, with the anterior shoulder delivered first from under the pubic arch

Rationale

"After the head is born, it rotates briefly to the position it occupied when it was engaged in the inlet. This movement is referred to as restitution," and "the 45-degree turn realigns the infant's head with the back and shoulders." Then "external rotation occurs as the shoulders engage and descend.... The anterior shoulder descends first. When it reaches the outlet, it rotates to the midline and is delivered from under the pubic arch. The posterior shoulder is guided over the perineum."

Watch for

(D) reverses which shoulder goes where and is the mirror error of this pair. The anterior shoulder is the one that comes under the pubic arch; the posterior shoulder is guided over the perineum.

70. During extension, in what order do the parts of the fetal head emerge, and what passes under the symphysis pubis first?
  • AThe chin, then the face, then the occiput, with the chin passing under the symphysis first
  • ✓The occiput, then the face, then the chin, with the occiput passing under the lower border of the symphysis first
  • CThe occiput, then the chin, then the face, with the face passing under the symphysis first
  • DThe face and chin together, with the occiput delivered over the perineum first
Rationale

The head "emerges by extension: first the occiput, then the face, and finally the chin," and "the occiput passes under the lower border of the symphysis pubis first."

Watch for

(A) inverts the sequence, which is the error that follows from picturing the head as flexed at the moment of birth. Flexion is what got the head through the pelvis; extension is what delivers it, so the occiput leads and the chin comes last.
