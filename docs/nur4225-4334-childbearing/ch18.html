1. A nurse is reviewing the mechanisms by which fetal oxygen supply can decrease during labor. Which mechanisms does the chapter name? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Reduction of blood flow through the maternal vessels from hypertension, hypotension, or hypovolemia
  • ✓Reduction of the oxygen content in maternal blood from hemorrhage or severe anemia
  • ✓Alterations in fetal circulation from cord compression, placental separation, or head compression
  • ✓Reduction in blood flow to the intervillous space from uterine hypertonus or deterioration of the placental vasculature
  • EReduction in fetal hemoglobin production during the second stage of labor
Rationale

The chapter lists exactly four mechanisms by which fetal oxygen supply decreases, and they are the causal spine of every deceleration type: reduced maternal blood flow, reduced maternal oxygen content, altered fetal circulation, and reduced intervillous blood flow.

Watch for

(E) is invented physiology that sounds plausible because labor is a stress. Every real mechanism in the list is about delivery of oxygen to the fetus, not about the fetus making less hemoglobin.

basis for fetal monitoring and the compromise cascadethe compromise cascade
2. An abnormal FHR pattern persists uncorrected. In what order does the chapter describe fetal compromise progressing?
  • ✓Hypoxemia, then hypoxia, then metabolic acidosis, then acidemia
  • BHypoxia, then hypoxemia, then acidemia, then metabolic acidosis
  • CMetabolic acidosis, then hypoxemia, then hypoxia, then acidemia
  • DAcidemia, then hypoxemia, then hypoxia, then metabolic acidosis
Rationale

"Abnormal FHR patterns are associated with fetal hypoxemia, a deficiency of oxygen in the blood. If uncorrected, hypoxemia can deteriorate to severe fetal hypoxia, an inadequate supply of oxygen at the cellular level that can cause metabolic acidosis. Metabolic acidosis, in turn, can lead to acidemia or increased hydrogen ion content (decreased pH) in the blood."

Watch for

(B) swaps the first two terms, which is the common inversion. Hypoxemia is a blood finding and comes first; hypoxia is the cellular consequence.

3. A newborn's record documents metabolic acidemia at birth. What does the chapter say this may indicate?
  • AA transient finding of no clinical consequence
  • ✓Clinically significant interruption of fetal oxygenation, leading to cellular dysfunction, tissue dysfunction, or death
  • CDeficiency of oxygen in the blood without cellular effect
  • DAn inadequate supply of oxygen at the cellular level only
Rationale

"Metabolic acidemia may indicate clinically significant interruption of fetal oxygenation, leading to cellular dysfunction, tissue dysfunction, or death." It is the end of the cascade, not a step within it.

Watch for

(C) and (D) restate hypoxemia and hypoxia — the two earlier steps — which is the error that follows from holding the four terms as synonyms rather than as a sequence.

4. A nurse is asked why electronic monitoring remains nearly universal despite its known limits. Which statements does the chapter support? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓EFM is used with more than 80% of all laboring women in the United States
  • ✓Research has not shown that intrapartum FHR monitoring leads to a significant decrease in neonatal neurologic morbidity
  • ✓EFM has high sensitivity, so moderate variability with accelerations nearly always means a well-oxygenated fetus
  • ✓EFM has low specificity, with many false-positive results
  • EContinuous EFM has been associated with a reduction in cesarean births
Rationale

The chapter reports that EFM is used in more than 80% of United States labors, that research has not shown a significant decrease in neonatal neurologic morbidity, and from the Evidence-Based Practice box that EFM has high sensitivity and low specificity. It also attributes continued reliance on EFM to "staffing patterns, staffing mix, and increased defensive practices in a litigious environment."

Watch for

(E) reverses the finding that matters most in this box: "continuous EFM has been associated with an increase in cesarean births and instrumented vaginal births." The documented benefit is reduced neonatal seizures, with no clear difference in cerebral palsy or infant mortality.

5. A nurse is preparing to auscultate the FHR with a Doppler device. According to Box 18.2, what is the first step?
  • ACount the maternal radial pulse while listening to the FHR
  • ✓Perform Leopold maneuvers to identify fetal presentation and position
  • CPalpate the abdomen for the presence or absence of uterine activity
  • DCount the FHR for 30 to 60 seconds after a contraction
Rationale

Box 18.2 begins with Leopold maneuvers: "Perform Leopold maneuvers by palpating the maternal abdomen to identify fetal presentation and position." Locating the fetal back is what makes the listening device land on the area of maximal intensity.

Watch for

(A) is a real step in the same box and is the one most stems reach for, because differentiating the maternal from the fetal rate feels like the safety step. It is step 3, after the device has been placed.

6. A nurse auscultating the FHR documents "moderate variability with occasional variable decelerations." Why is this documentation inappropriate?
  • ✓Because those terms are visual descriptions of patterns produced on a monitor tracing and cannot be derived from auscultation
  • BBecause variability can only be documented with an internal spiral electrode
  • CBecause auscultation findings must be documented as a single number without qualifiers
  • DBecause those terms apply only to the second stage of labor
Rationale

The Nursing Alert states that when the FHR is auscultated it is "inappropriate to use the descriptive terms associated with EFM (e.g., moderate variability, variable deceleration) because most of the terms are visual descriptions of the patterns produced on the monitor tracing."

Watch for

(C) overcorrects. Auscultation supports a baseline number or range, a rhythm description, and abrupt or gradual changes — it is not reduced to a bare number.

7. Which findings can the nurse legitimately document from intermittent auscultation? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓A baseline number or range
  • ✓A regular or irregular rhythm
  • ✓Abrupt or gradual increases or decreases before, during, and after contractions
  • ✓Bradycardia or tachycardia, which are numerically defined
  • EMinimal baseline variability
Rationale

The Nursing Alert permits a "baseline number or range and a regular or irregular rhythm," the presence of "abrupt or gradual increases or decreases in FHR," and the numerically defined terms bradycardia and tachycardia. The chapter separately lists what the nurse can assess by IA: "the counted FHR, the rhythm, the presence of increases and decreases in the FHR, and whether these changes are abrupt or gradual."

Watch for

(E) is the one to leave unselected, and it is attractive because minimal variability has a number attached to it — 5 beats/min or less. The number does not rescue it: variability is an amplitude range judged by eye on a tracing, which auscultation does not produce.

8. A nurse is using intermittent auscultation as the primary method of fetal assessment when the unit census rises and another laboring woman is assigned to them. What does the chapter direct the nurse to do?
  • AContinue auscultation at half the usual frequency until staffing improves
  • ✓Inform the physician or nurse-midwife that continuous EFM will be used until staffing can be arranged to meet the standards
  • CDelegate auscultation to unlicensed assistive personnel
  • DDocument the staffing change and continue the current plan
Rationale

"When used as the primary method of fetal assessment, auscultation requires a one-to-one nurse-to-client staffing ratio. If acuity and census change so that auscultation standards are no longer met, the nurse must inform the physician or nurse-midwife that continuous EFM will be used until staffing can be arranged to meet the standards."

Watch for

(A) is the informal workaround and is exactly what the standard forbids — the frequencies are the standard, so stretching them means auscultation is no longer being performed to standard.

12. A woman with no risk factors is being monitored electronically in the active phase of the first stage, and a second woman with risk factors is in the second stage. How often should each tracing be evaluated?
  • ✓Every 30 minutes for the low-risk woman in the first stage, and at least every 5 minutes for the woman with risk factors in the second stage
  • BEvery 15 minutes for the low-risk woman in the first stage, and at least every 15 minutes for the woman with risk factors in the second stage
  • CEvery 30 minutes for both
  • DEvery 5 minutes for the low-risk woman in the first stage, and every 30 minutes for the woman with risk factors in the second stage
Rationale

With EFM, low-risk women are evaluated every 30 minutes in the active phase of the first stage and at least every 15 minutes in the second stage; when risk factors are present, the intervals are at least every 15 minutes and at least every 5 minutes.

Watch for

(B) uses real numbers from the same grid in the wrong cells, which is how these four values are usually missed. Risk doubles the frequency at each stage, and the second stage is always tighter than the first.

13. A tocotransducer is in place on a laboring woman's abdomen. Which uterine activity data can it provide?
  • ✓Frequency and duration only
  • BFrequency, duration, and intensity
  • CFrequency, duration, intensity, and resting tone in millimeters of mercury
  • DIntensity and resting tone only
Rationale

Table 18.2 states the tocotransducer "monitors frequency and duration of contractions by means of a pressure-sensing device applied to the maternal abdomen," and the text adds that it "can measure and record the frequency and approximate duration of UCs, but not their intensity."

Watch for

(C) describes the IUPC, which is the only device that reports intensity and resting tone in millimeters of mercury. Intensity is the single word that separates the two modes.

14. An intrauterine pressure catheter is placed. Which data does it add that the external mode could not provide?
  • AThe frequency of contractions
  • BThe duration of contractions
  • ✓The intensity of contractions and the uterine resting tone in millimeters of mercury
  • DThe fetal heart rate baseline
Rationale

"The IUPC can objectively measure the frequency, duration, and intensity of UCs and uterine resting tone," converting pressure at the catheter tip into millimeters of mercury.

(A) and (B) are the mirror error of the previous item — both modes give frequency and duration, so neither is what the internal catheter adds.

  • BMembranes intact and cervix dilated at least 4 cm
  • CMembranes ruptured and the woman in the second stage of labor

Internal monitoring requires all three conditions together: "the membranes must be ruptured, the cervix sufficiently dilated (at least 2 to 3 cm), and the presenting part ... low enough" to allow placement. The spiral electrode "penetrates into the fetal presenting part by 1.5 mm."

(B) is impossible as written — an option applying a spiral electrode through intact membranes describes something that cannot be done, and it is the distractor to recognize on sight.

17. A nurse is setting up external monitoring. Which placement and setup details match Box 18.8 and the chapter?
  • AThe ultrasound transducer over the fundus with gel, and the tocotransducer below the umbilicus without gel
  • ✓The ultrasound transducer over the point of maximal FHR intensity, usually below the umbilicus, with conductive gel, and the tocotransducer over the fundus without gel or paste
  • CBoth transducers over the fundus, both with conductive gel
  • DThe ultrasound transducer below the umbilicus without gel, and the tocotransducer over the fundus with gel
Rationale

The ultrasound transducer goes over the point of maximal FHR intensity, "usually below the umbilicus," with conductive gel; the tocotransducer is placed "over the fundus above the umbilicus" and, per Box 18.8, "applied without gel or paste."

(A) swaps the two placements, which is the error that produces an unreadable tracing at the bedside. Gel goes with sound waves, never with the pressure-sensing tocotransducer.

  • ✓Moderate at 50 mm Hg or greater; mild at less than 50 mm Hg
  • BModerate at less than 50 mm Hg; mild at 50 mm Hg or greater
  • CModerate at 80 mm Hg or greater; mild at 40 mm Hg
  • DModerate at 10 mm Hg; mild at 5 mm Hg
Rationale

Table 18.1 states that contractions palpated as mild "would likely peak at less than 50 mm Hg if measured internally, whereas contractions palpated as moderate or strong would likely peak at 50 mm Hg or greater."

(D) borrows the resting tone value of 10 mm Hg and uses it as a contraction peak, which is a magnitude error worth catching: resting tone is what the uterus reads between contractions, not during one.

20. How are Montevideo units calculated, and what monitoring mode do they require?
  • ABy adding the peak pressures of all contractions in a 10-minute window, with either external or internal monitoring
  • ✓By subtracting the baseline uterine pressure from the peak pressure of each contraction in a 10-minute window and adding those values, using internal monitoring only
  • CBy multiplying contraction frequency by average duration, using external monitoring
  • DBy averaging the resting tone over 10 minutes, using internal monitoring only
Rationale

"Montevideo units (MVUs) are calculated by subtracting the baseline uterine pressure from the peak contraction pressure for each contraction that occurs in a 10-minute window and then adding together the pressures generated by each contraction." Table 18.1 adds that "MVUs are used only with internal monitoring of contractions."

Watch for

(A) omits the subtraction of resting tone and, more importantly, allows external monitoring. A stem calculating MVUs from a tocotransducer tracing is describing something that cannot be done.

21. A woman's contractions are generating 110 MVUs. How should the nurse interpret this value?
  • AIt is below the range associated with initiating spontaneous labor
  • ✓It is in the range generally sufficient to initiate spontaneous labor, which is 80 to 120 MVUs
  • CIt exceeds the usual maximum for the first stage of labor
  • DIt is the expected value for the second stage of labor
Rationale

"Contraction intensities of 40 mm Hg or more and MVUs of 80–120 are generally sufficient to initiate spontaneous labor." First-stage activity usually ranges from 100 to 250 MVUs and "rarely exceeds 250 MVUs," while second-stage values may reach 300 to 400.

Watch for

(C) would require a value above 250, and the gap between 120 and 250 is where most of active labor actually sits — a value can be adequate to start labor and still be low for established labor.

baseline fetal heart ratederiving the baseline
22. A nurse is determining the baseline FHR from a 10-minute segment. What does the definition exclude?
  • ✓Periodic or episodic changes, periods of marked variability, and segments differing by more than 25 beats/min
  • BAccelerations only
  • CDecelerations only
  • DAll periods of moderate variability
Rationale

The baseline is "the average rate during a 10-minute segment that excludes periodic or episodic changes, periods of marked variability, and segments of the baseline that differ by more than 25 beats/min."

Watch for

(D) would exclude the very thing that makes a baseline normal. Moderate variability is a characteristic of the baseline itself, not an excursion from it.

23. A 10-minute tracing segment contains only 90 seconds of interpretable data. What does the chapter say about determining a baseline?
  • AA baseline can be determined from any amount of interpretable data
  • ✓There must be at least 2 minutes of interpretable baseline data in the 10-minute segment to determine the baseline
  • CThere must be at least 5 minutes of interpretable data
  • DThe baseline is determined from the preceding segment instead
Rationale

"There must be at least 2 minutes of interpretable baseline data in a 10-minute segment of tracing to determine the baseline FHR."

Watch for

(D) supplies a workaround the chapter does not offer, and it is tempting because it sounds like continuity of care. The rule is a data-sufficiency threshold, not a substitution rule.

baseline fetal heart ratederiving the baseline
24. Over a 10-minute segment the FHR varies between 130 and 140 beats/min. How is the baseline recorded?
  • A130 beats/min, taking the lowest rate
  • ✓135 beats/min, rounding the approximate mean to the closest 5 beats/min interval
  • C140 beats/min, taking the highest rate
  • DA range of 130 to 140 beats/min
Rationale

"After 10 minutes of tracing is observed, the approximate mean rate is rounded to the closest 5 beats/min interval." The chapter's own example uses these numbers and records the baseline as 135 beats/min.

Watch for

(D) is how an auscultated FHR may be documented — as a number or range — but a baseline derived from a tracing is a single rounded value.

baseline fetal heart rateautonomic control and normal range
25. A fetus has a baseline of 140 beats/min. What is the normal range, and what autonomic change would raise the rate?
  • ✓The normal range is 110 to 160 beats/min, and an increase in sympathetic response accelerates the FHR
  • BThe normal range is 120 to 160 beats/min, and an increase in parasympathetic response accelerates the FHR
  • CThe normal range is 110 to 160 beats/min, and an increase in parasympathetic response accelerates the FHR
  • DThe normal range is 100 to 150 beats/min, and an increase in sympathetic response slows the FHR
Rationale

"The normal FHR range is 110 to 160 beats/min." And: "An increase in sympathetic response results in acceleration of the FHR, whereas an increase in parasympathetic response produces a slowing of the FHR."

Watch for

(C) keeps the right range and inverts the autonomic rule, which matters downstream — decelerations are described as "caused by the dominance of a parasympathetic response," so holding this backwards breaks the mechanism of every deceleration.

fetal heart rate variabilitythe four categories
26. A tracing shows baseline fluctuations with an amplitude range detectable to the unaided eye but no greater than 5 beats/min. How is this variability classified?
  • AAbsent
  • ✓Minimal
  • CModerate
  • DMarked
Rationale

Minimal variability "has an amplitude range that is detectable to the unaided eye but is 5 beats/min or less." Absent variability "is defined as an amplitude range of the FHR fluctuations that is not detectable to the unaided eye."

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error and turns on one word: absent means not detectable at all, while minimal means detectable but small. A stem that says the fluctuations can be seen has already excluded absent.

fetal heart rate variabilitythe four categories
27. A tracing shows moderate variability. What does the chapter say this predicts?
  • ✓It reliably predicts a normal fetal acid-base balance, meaning the absence of fetal metabolic acidemia
  • BIt predicts imminent fetal compromise unless accelerations are also present
  • CIts significance is not known and it likely represents a normal variant
  • DIt predicts a normal outcome only when accompanied by early decelerations
Rationale

"Moderate variability is considered normal. Its presence reliably predicts a normal fetal acid-base balance (absence of fetal metabolic acidemia). Moderate variability indicates that FHR regulation is not affected significantly by interruption of fetal oxygenation."

Watch for

(C) is the chapter's statement about marked variability, not moderate. Mapping the significance line to the wrong category is the error this pair exists to catch.

28. A tracing shows marked variability. How should the nurse interpret it?
  • AAs an emergency requiring immediate delivery
  • ✓As a finding whose significance is not known and that in many cases likely represents a normal variant
  • CAs diagnostic of fetal metabolic acidemia
  • DAs equivalent to absent variability in its implications
Rationale

"The significance of marked variability is not known. In many cases, it likely represents a normal variant." Marked variability does place a tracing in category II, because category I requires moderate variability, but the chapter will not commit to more than that.

(A) is the exam's favorite move with this category — treating an unfamiliar extreme as an emergency. The book declines to.

fetal heart rate variabilitysinusoidal pattern
31. A tracing shows a regular, smooth, undulating wavelike pattern that has persisted for 25 minutes. What does this represent, and what does it classically indicate?
  • AMarked variability, indicating a normal variant
  • ✓A sinusoidal pattern, which classically occurs with severe fetal anemia
  • CModerate variability, indicating normal acid-base balance
  • DA prolonged deceleration, indicating cord compression
Rationale

"A sinusoidal pattern is a regular, smooth, undulating wavelike pattern that persists for at least 20 minutes," and "this uncommon pattern classically occurs with severe fetal anemia." Variants are described with chorioamnionitis, fetal sepsis, and opioid administration. It is also one of only two direct routes into category III.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex pick because both patterns look busy on a strip. The chapter settles it in one line: the sinusoidal pattern "is not included in the definition of FHR variability."

tachycardia and bradycardiathe definitions
32. A fetal baseline is 168 beats/min and has been for the past 12 minutes. How is this documented?
  • ✓Tachycardia, defined as a baseline greater than 160 beats/min for 10 minutes or longer
  • BA prolonged acceleration, since it exceeds 2 minutes
  • CMarked variability
  • DTachycardia, but only if it persists beyond 30 minutes
Rationale

"Tachycardia is a baseline FHR greater than 160 beats/min for 10 minutes or longer," and Table 18.3 repeats the threshold as "FHR >160 beats/min lasting >10 min."

Watch for

(B) misapplies the acceleration rules, and the chapter closes that door directly: "acceleration of the FHR for more than 10 minutes is considered a change in baseline rate." Past 10 minutes it is no longer an event, it is the baseline.

tachycardia and bradycardiathe definitions
33. A fetal baseline has been 104 beats/min for the past 14 minutes. How does this differ from a prolonged deceleration, and why does the distinction matter?
  • ✓It is bradycardia, a baseline below 110 for 10 minutes or longer; a prolonged deceleration lasts more than 2 but less than 10 minutes, and the causes and management of the two are very different
  • BIt is a prolonged deceleration, since any drop below baseline lasting over 10 minutes is prolonged
  • CThere is no meaningful difference; the terms are interchangeable
  • DIt is bradycardia only if variability is also absent
Rationale

"Bradycardia is a baseline FHR less than 110 beats/min for 10 minutes or longer." The chapter flags the pair explicitly: true bradycardia "must be distinguished from a prolonged deceleration because the causes and management of these two conditions are very different."

Watch for

(B) is the mirror error, and the 10-minute line is what settles it in both directions: below 10 minutes it is a prolonged deceleration, at or beyond 10 minutes it is a baseline change.

tachycardia and bradycardiacauses and clinical weight
35. A stem describes isolated tachycardia with moderate variability and no decelerations. What does the chapter say about its predictive value?
  • ✓Tachycardia alone poorly predicts fetal hypoxemia or acidemia, and becomes meaningful when accompanied by absent or minimal variability, recurrent decelerations, or both
  • BTachycardia alone is diagnostic of fetal metabolic acidemia
  • CTachycardia alone requires immediate cesarean birth
  • DTachycardia is never clinically significant
Rationale

"Tachycardia alone poorly predicts fetal hypoxemia or acidemia. It can indicate fetal hypoxemia or acidemia when accompanied by absent or minimal baseline variability, recurrent decelerations, or both." Elsewhere the chapter notes it can be an early sign of hypoxemia "especially when associated with late decelerations and minimal or absent variability."

Watch for

(D) overcorrects in the other direction. Neither extreme is the chapter's position: the accompanying variability is what gives a rate finding its weight.

  • BIt is listed as a cause of fetal bradycardia and is also used to augment labor
  • CIt causes late decelerations and is used to treat cord compression
  • DIt is contraindicated in labor for any purpose
Rationale

Table 18.3 lists "beta-sympathomimetic drugs e.g., terbutaline [Brethine]" among the causes of fetal tachycardia, and the chapter names terbutaline as the commonly used tocolytic for tachysystole and excessive contractions. Both statements are true at once.

Watch for

(B) misassigns the direction of the drug's effect. Whether a stem wants terbutaline as a cause or as a treatment depends on whether the drug is already running or is being considered.

72. A nurse reads a tracing as category III, and the obstetric provider disagrees and declines to come to the bedside. According to the Legal Tip, what is the nurse responsible for doing?
  • ADocumenting the disagreement and repeating the assessment in 15 minutes
  • ✓Initiating the institutional chain of command
  • CProceeding with the provider's interpretation, since interpretation is a medical decision
  • DObtaining a second nurse's interpretation and charting both
Rationale

The Legal Tip makes nurses "legally responsible for correctly interpreting FHR patterns, initiating appropriate nursing interventions based on those patterns, and documenting the outcomes of those interventions," for "timely notification of the obstetric health care provider," and for "initiating the institutional chain of command should differences in opinion arise among health care professionals."

Watch for

(A) and (D) are both charting responses to a clinical disagreement, and they are the reflex answers because documentation is genuinely one of the four responsibilities. When opinions differ, the named action is the chain of command.

73. Which components must be evaluated as the five essential components of the FHR tracing, and what follows if one is abnormal?
  • ✓Baseline rate, baseline variability, accelerations, decelerations, and changes or trends over time; corrective measures must be taken immediately
  • BBaseline rate, variability, uterine activity, maternal pulse, and maternal position; the provider is notified within an hour
  • CBaseline rate, variability, and decelerations only; the assessment is repeated in 30 minutes
  • DBaseline rate, accelerations, decelerations, and category; the tracing is filed
Rationale

The five essential components are "baseline rate, baseline variability, accelerations, decelerations, and changes or trends over time," and "whenever one of these five essential components is assessed as abnormal, corrective measures must be taken immediately."

Watch for

(B) imports uterine activity into the list. Uterine activity is required in documentation, but the five essential components of the FHR tracing are all FHR findings.

75. How soon should FHR and uterine activity documentation be recorded, and what terminology is required?
  • ✓Within 30 to 60 minutes of the assessment, procedure, or occurrence, using standardized FHR and uterine activity terminology
  • BAt the end of the shift, using whatever terminology the unit prefers
  • CWithin 24 hours, using standardized terminology
  • DImmediately and only in the electronic record, since handwritten charting is prohibited
Rationale

"Documentation should be recorded near the time of the assessment, procedure, or occurrence (i.e., within 30 to 60 minutes) according to agency policy," and "standardized FHR and UA terminology must be used in all documentation related to EFM. All documentation must be accurate. Legibility is also crucial, particularly if charting is handwritten."

Watch for

(D) overstates the legibility rule into a prohibition. The chapter anticipates handwritten charting and requires that it be legible.
