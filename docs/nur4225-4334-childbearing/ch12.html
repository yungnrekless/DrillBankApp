1. A nursing student asks which umbilical vessel carries oxygenated blood, and in which direction. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓The single umbilical vein carries oxygenated blood from the placenta to the fetus
  • BThe two umbilical arteries carry oxygenated blood from the placenta to the fetus
  • CThe single umbilical vein carries deoxygenated blood from the fetus to the placenta
  • DAll three vessels carry mixed blood in both directions
Rationale

"Two arteries carry blood from the embryo to the chorionic villi, and one vein returns blood to the embryo" (p. 238). The figure caption states the direction: "Deoxygenated blood leaves the fetus through the umbilical arteries and enters the placenta, where it is oxygenated. Oxygenated blood leaves the placenta through the umbilical vein" (p. 239).

Watch for

(B) is the single most-swapped fact in this chapter, and it is swapped because it is backwards from adult anatomy, where arteries carry oxygenated blood. In the fetus the naming follows direction of flow relative to the fetus, not oxygen content.

2. A newborn's cord is examined after birth. Which finding is normal, and what does the abnormal finding suggest?
  • ✓Two arteries and one vein is normal; a two-vessel cord occurs in about 1% and is associated with cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, and urinary tract anomalies
  • BTwo veins and one artery is normal; a three-vessel cord suggests a chromosomal abnormality
  • COne artery and one vein is normal; a third vessel indicates a vascular malformation
  • DThe vessel count varies normally and carries no clinical significance
Rationale

The normal cord has two arteries and one vein. "Approximately 1% of umbilical cords contain only two vessels: one artery and one vein," associated with "fetal cardiovascular, gastrointestinal (esophageal and anal atresia), and urinary tract anomalies" (p. 238).

Watch for

(B) inverts the vessel counts, which is the mirror of the previous item's error. The clinical point is that counting cord vessels at birth is a screening act: the missing artery is what prompts evaluation of three organ systems.

3. An ultrasound reports an amniotic fluid volume of less than 300 mL at 30 weeks. Which fetal problem should the nurse anticipate?
  • ✓A renal abnormality, since fetal urine forms a major part of amniotic fluid volume
  • BA gastrointestinal malformation, since the fetus cannot swallow the fluid
  • CA neural tube defect, since the fluid cushions the developing spine
  • DA cardiac anomaly, since fluid volume reflects fetal cardiac output
Rationale

"Having less than 300 mL of amniotic fluid (oligohydramnios) is associated with fetal renal abnormalities" (p. 237). Fetal "urine is excreted into the amniotic fluid and forms a major part of the amniotic fluid volume. Oligohydramnios is indicative of renal dysfunction" (p. 247).

Watch for

(B) is the mirror error, belonging to the opposite finding. The direction the fact bank gives is worth holding literally: the kidneys make the fluid and the gut removes it, so too little points at the kidneys and too much points at the gut.

4. A different ultrasound reports more than 2 L of amniotic fluid. Which fetal problem should the nurse anticipate?
  • ✓A gastrointestinal malformation, since fluid is removed by fetal swallowing
  • BA renal abnormality, since the fetus is producing excessive urine
  • CA pulmonary malformation, since lung liquid is the only source of the fluid
  • DA skeletal dysplasia, since the fetus is not moving enough to displace fluid
Rationale

"Having more than 2 L of amniotic fluid (hydramnios or polyhydramnios) is associated with gastrointestinal and other malformations" (p. 237). From the second trimester onward, volume "is regulated by a balance between fetal fluid production (lung liquid and urine) and fluid resorption through fetal swallowing" (p. 235).

Watch for

(B) is the mirror error of the previous item and completes the pair. Both findings are volume abnormalities, so only the direction tells you which side of the production-and-removal balance has failed.

5. A nurse is explaining what protects the umbilical vessels from compression as the fetus moves. Which structure should the nurse name?
  • ✓Wharton's jelly, a connective tissue surrounding the vessels
  • BThe vernix caseosa, which coats the cord along with the fetal skin
  • CThe chorionic villi, which anchor the cord to the placenta
  • DThe amnion alone, which is sufficient to prevent compression
Rationale

"Connective tissue called Wharton's jelly surrounds the vessels, preventing compression of the blood vessels and ensuring continued nourishment of the embryo or fetus" (p. 238). Compression is still possible if the cord lies between the fetal head and the maternal pelvis or is twisted around the fetal body.

Watch for

(B) borrows a real fetal substance from the integumentary section and gives it a mechanical job it does not have. Vernix protects fetal skin; Wharton's jelly protects the cord, and the distinction matters because cord compression is a fetal oxygenation emergency and vernix is not.

6. A patient asks at what point the placenta is structurally complete. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓The structure of the placenta is complete by the 12th week; it then grows wider until 20 weeks and afterward grows thicker
  • BThe structure is complete by 20 weeks, when it covers approximately half the uterine surface
  • CThe structure is complete by day 17, when the maternal-placental-embryonic circulation is established
  • DThe structure is not complete until 28 weeks, when placental hormone production peaks
Rationale

"The structure of the placenta is complete by the 12th week." The placenta "continues to grow wider until 20 weeks, when it covers approximately half of the uterine surface. It then continues to grow thicker" (p. 239).

Watch for

(B) is the fact bank's named error — a stem asking when the placenta is fully formed wants 12 weeks, not 20. The 20-week milestone is about surface area, and the two milestones are easy to merge because they sit in consecutive sentences.

7. A nurse is reviewing early placental development. By which day is the maternal-placental-embryonic circulation in place, and what else happens then?
  • ✓By day 17, when the embryonic heart starts beating
  • BBy day 7, at the moment of implantation
  • CBy day 28, when the neural tube closes
  • DBy the end of the eighth week, when the embryonic period ends
Rationale

"The maternal-placental-embryonic circulation is in place by day 17, when the embryonic heart starts beating" (p. 239). By the end of the third week, embryonic blood is circulating between the embryo and the chorionic villi.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick because the placenta does begin to form at implantation, so implantation feels like the moment circulation begins. Formation starts there; a working circulation takes another ten days, and the two events are separated deliberately in the text.

8. A nursing student is asked to describe the cotyledons of the placenta. Which description is accurate?
  • ✓There are 15 to 20 cotyledons, and each one is a functional unit
  • BThere are 15 to 20 cotyledons, which together make up one functional unit
  • CThere are approximately 200 cotyledons, each containing a single chorionic villus
  • DCotyledons are the maternal blood spaces between the villi
Rationale

"A third layer develops into anchoring septa, dividing the projecting decidua into separate areas called cotyledons. In each of the 15 to 20 cotyledons, the chorionic villi branch out, and a complex system of fetal blood vessels forms. Each cotyledon is a functional unit" (p. 239).

Watch for

(B) keeps the right count and destroys the point, which is that the placenta is built of many independent functional units rather than one. (D) names the intervillous spaces, which are where exchange happens but are not the same structure.

9. A nurse is explaining why placental exchange becomes more efficient as pregnancy advances. Which explanation is accurate?
  • ✓Permeability increases as the cytotrophoblast thins and disappears, leaving only the syncytium, the functional layer, by the fifth month
  • BPermeability increases because additional cell layers are added to the villous membrane over time
  • CPermeability is constant throughout pregnancy; only placental surface area changes
  • DPermeability increases because maternal blood pressure rises through pregnancy
Rationale

Early exchange "is minimal at this time because the two cell layers of the villous membrane are too thick. Permeability increases as the cytotrophoblast thins and disappears." "By the fifth month, only the single layer of syncytium is left between the maternal blood and the fetal capillaries. The syncytium is the functional layer of the placenta" (p. 239).

Watch for

(B) inverts the mechanism in the intuitive direction, since more tissue sounds like more capacity. The membrane gets thinner, not thicker, and the same syncytium is where the four placental hormones are produced.

placental hormonesgeneral
10. A patient's home pregnancy test is positive 10 days after ovulation. Which hormone did the test detect, and what is its primary function in early pregnancy?
  • ✓Human chorionic gonadotropin, which preserves the function of the ovarian corpus luteum
  • BHuman placental lactogen, which stimulates maternal metabolism to supply fetal nutrients
  • CEstriol, which reflects placental functioning
  • DProgesterone, which maintains the endometrium
Rationale

Human chorionic gonadotropin "can be detected in the maternal serum or urine by 8 to 10 days after ovulation, shortly after implantation. This hormone is the basis for pregnancy tests," and it "preserves the function of the ovarian corpus luteum, ensuring the continued supply of estrogen and progesterone needed to maintain the pregnancy" (p. 239). Its maximum level occurs at 60 to 70 days.

Watch for

(D) names a hormone with a real early-pregnancy role, but progesterone is not what a pregnancy test measures. The chain runs one step further back: the test detects the hormone that keeps the corpus luteum making progesterone.

11. A patient at 26 weeks has a positive glucose screen. Which placental hormone increases resistance to insulin and is implicated in this finding?
  • ✓Human placental lactogen, which is similar to a growth hormone and facilitates glucose transport across the placental membrane
  • BHuman chorionic gonadotropin, which peaks at 60 to 70 days and then declines
  • CEstriol, the major estrogen secreted by the placenta
  • DProgesterone, which decreases the contractility of the uterus
Rationale

Human placental lactogen, also known as human chorionic somatomammotropin, "is similar to a growth hormone" and "stimulates the maternal metabolism to supply nutrients needed for fetal growth." It "increases the resistance to insulin, facilitates glucose transport across the placental membrane, and stimulates breast development to prepare for lactation" (p. 239).

Watch for

(B) is the mirror error of the previous item — the two protein hormones are easily swapped because both are placental and both are abbreviated from long names. The insulin-resistance clause is the discriminator, and it is what gestational diabetes stems are built on.

12. A nurse is explaining why the uterus stays quiet through most of pregnancy and then does not. Which pair of statements is accurate?
  • ✓Progesterone decreases the contractility of the uterus, and estrogen stimulates myometrial contractility
  • BProgesterone stimulates myometrial contractility, and estrogen decreases it
  • CBoth hormones decrease contractility, and labor begins when both fall to zero
  • DNeither hormone affects contractility; labor onset is entirely mechanical
Rationale

Progesterone "maintains the endometrium, decreases the contractility of the uterus, and stimulates maternal metabolism and development of breast alveoli," while estrogen "stimulates uterine growth and uteroplacental blood flow" and "stimulates myometrial contractility" (pp. 239–240). One stated theory of labor onset is "the decline in the ratio of circulating levels of progesterone to the increased levels of estrogen."

Watch for

(C) overstates the theory in the way the fact bank flags: the book describes a shift in the ratio between the two hormones, not an absolute fall of progesterone to zero. Placental estrogen production increases greatly toward the end of pregnancy, so the ratio can shift while progesterone remains high.

13. A provider orders a maternal estriol level to evaluate placental function. Why is estriol the appropriate measure rather than estradiol?
  • ✓The major estrogen secreted by the placenta is estriol, whereas the ovaries produce mostly estradiol
  • BThe major estrogen secreted by the placenta is estradiol, and estriol is a metabolite of it
  • CEstriol is produced by the corpus luteum and therefore reflects early pregnancy maintenance
  • DEstriol is the only estrogen that crosses the placental membrane
Rationale

"The major estrogen secreted by the placenta is estriol, whereas the ovaries produce mostly estradiol. Estriol levels may be measured to determine placental functioning" (pp. 239–240). By 7 weeks the placenta is producing most of the maternal estrogens.

Watch for

(B) swaps the two estrogens between their two sources, which is the whole basis of the test. If the ovaries were the placental estrogen's source, the level would say nothing about how the placenta is working.

14. A patient at 7 weeks of gestation has a luteal phase defect and asks why this matters now rather than later. Which explanation is accurate?
  • ✓By around 9 weeks the corpus luteum regresses as the placenta takes over; miscarriage occurs if the corpus luteum stops functioning before the placenta produces sufficient estrogen and progesterone
  • BThe corpus luteum supports the pregnancy until term, so the risk continues throughout
  • CThe placenta produces sufficient progesterone from implantation onward, so corpus luteum function is never critical
  • DThe corpus luteum regresses at 20 weeks, when the placenta reaches its full width
Rationale

Progesterone comes from the corpus luteum early, and the placenta becomes the primary source; "by around 9 weeks, the corpus luteum regresses." "Miscarriage occurs if the corpus luteum stops functioning before the placenta is producing sufficient estrogen and progesterone" (pp. 239–240).

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick for anyone who knows the corpus luteum matters but not that there is a handoff with a date. Because there is a handoff, a luteal defect is an early-pregnancy risk specifically, and the window closes rather than persisting.

15. A patient asks how her antibodies reach her baby before birth. Which transfer mechanism carries maternal immunoglobulins across the placental membrane?
  • ✓Pinocytosis, which transfers large molecules such as albumin and gamma globulins
  • BActive transport against the concentration gradient
  • CSimple diffusion, as with water and small electrolytes
  • DFacilitated diffusion, as with glucose
Rationale

Pinocytosis is "a mechanism used for transferring large molecules such as albumin and gamma globulins across the placental membrane," and it "conveys the maternal immunoglobulins that provide early passive immunity to the fetus" (p. 240).

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick because active transport sounds like the mechanism that would handle something as important as immunity. Size is what decides here, not importance: the immunoglobulins are too large for the diffusion and carrier mechanisms, so the membrane engulfs them.

16. A nurse is sorting substances by how they cross the placenta. Select all the statements that are accurate.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Water and most electrolytes with a molecular weight less than 500 cross by simple diffusion
  • ✓Glucose is transported from mother to fetus by facilitated diffusion
  • ✓Amino acids and calcium are transported against the concentration gradient by active transport
  • ✓The majority of medications cross by passive diffusion
  • EOxygen is actively extracted from maternal blood by the placenta
Rationale

"Water and most electrolytes with a molecular weight less than 500 readily diffuse through the membrane." "Fetal demand for glucose is high, and it is transported from mother to fetus by facilitated diffusion." "Amino acids and calcium are transported against the concentration gradient." "The majority of medications cross the placenta through passive diffusion" (p. 240).

Watch for

(E) is the single wrong option, and it is the error the fact bank flags in the oxygenation section: the placenta moves gases by diffusion, not by active extraction. It is attractive because the fetus does obtain oxygen efficiently, but the efficiency comes from fetal adaptations rather than from placental work.

17. A patient with diabetes asks why her baby's blood sugar would be lower than hers if glucose crosses freely. Which explanation is accurate?
  • ✓The fetal concentration of glucose is lower because of rapid metabolism of glucose by the fetus
  • BThe fetal concentration is lower because the placenta consumes most of the glucose it transports
  • CThe fetal concentration is lower because glucose crosses only by active transport against a gradient
  • DThe fetal concentration is normally higher than maternal, so the premise is incorrect
Rationale

"The fetal concentration of glucose is lower than the glucose level in the maternal blood because of rapid metabolism of glucose by the fetus" (p. 240). Glucose crosses by facilitated diffusion, which follows the gradient rather than opposing it.

Watch for

(B) attributes the gradient to the placenta rather than to the fetus, which is the error the fact bank names. The distinction matters for the diabetes cascade: because the gradient is driven by fetal consumption, maternal hyperglycemia raises fetal glucose directly and drives fetal hyperinsulinemia.

placental transfer mechanismsgeneral
18. A nursing student is asked to name the metabolic functions of the placenta. Which set is correct?
  • ✓Respiration, nutrition, excretion, and storage
  • BRespiration, circulation, filtration, and immunity
  • CNutrition, excretion, thermoregulation, and hematopoiesis
  • DRespiration and nutrition only; the fetal kidneys handle excretion
Rationale

"The metabolic functions of the placenta are respiration, nutrition, excretion, and storage." Oxygen and carbon dioxide diffuse in opposite directions, so "the placenta functions as lungs for the fetus"; carbohydrates, proteins, calcium, and iron are stored in the placenta; and fetal metabolic wastes cross into maternal blood "for excretion by the maternal kidneys" (p. 240).

Watch for

(D) is the substantive error rather than a list error, and it inverts the chapter's central point. The fetus does not need functioning kidneys in utero precisely because the placenta acts as the organ of excretion, which is why renal failure declares itself at birth rather than before it.

19. Which fetal shunt allows blood to bypass the liver, and where does it carry blood?
  • ✓The ductus venosus, which carries blood from the umbilical vein into the inferior vena cava
  • BThe ductus arteriosus, which carries blood from the pulmonary artery into the aorta
  • CThe foramen ovale, which carries blood from the right atrium into the left atrium
  • DThe ductus venosus, which carries blood from the pulmonary artery into the aorta
Rationale

At the liver the umbilical vein "divides into two branches; one branch circulates some oxygenated blood through the liver. Most of the blood passes through the ductus venosus into the inferior vena cava" (p. 245). Of the three shunts, only the ductus venosus bypasses the liver.

Watch for

(D) keeps the correct shunt name and attaches the ductus arteriosus's route to it, which is the harder error because naming the shunt feels like answering the question. Both ducts share a word, and only one of them has anything to do with the liver.

20. A newborn's ductus arteriosus fails to close. Which connection has remained open, and what did it bypass in fetal life?
  • ✓The pulmonary artery to the aorta, bypassing the lungs
  • BThe right atrium to the left atrium, bypassing the lungs
  • CThe umbilical vein to the inferior vena cava, bypassing the liver
  • DThe aorta to the pulmonary vein, bypassing the left ventricle
Rationale

The ductus arteriosus runs from the pulmonary artery to the aorta, "distal to the point of exit of the arteries supplying the head and arms." Because the fetal lungs do not function for gas exchange, "a special circulatory pathway, the ductus arteriosus, bypasses the lungs" (p. 245). A small amount of blood circulates through the resistant lung tissue, but the majority follows the lower-resistance path through the duct.

Watch for

(B) describes the foramen ovale, and it is attractive because both structures bypass the lungs. The fact bank's discrimination is worth holding exactly: two shunts bypass the lungs and only one bypasses the liver, so "bypasses the lungs" cannot by itself identify a shunt.

21. A nurse is teaching about the three fetal shunts. Select all the statements that are accurate.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓The ductus venosus bypasses the liver
  • ✓The foramen ovale carries blood from the right atrium to the left atrium
  • ✓The ductus arteriosus carries blood from the pulmonary artery to the aorta
  • ✓Both the foramen ovale and the ductus arteriosus allow blood to bypass the lungs
  • EThe ductus venosus bypasses the lungs
Rationale

The figure caption states that "three shunts permit most of the blood to bypass the liver and lungs: (1) ductus venosus, (2) foramen ovale, and (3) ductus arteriosus" (p. 245). The ductus venosus runs from the umbilical vein to the inferior vena cava, bypassing the liver; the foramen ovale opens from the right atrium into the left atrium; and the ductus arteriosus runs from the pulmonary artery to the aorta.

(E) is the single wrong option, and it is the one the fact bank predicts will be checked — an item naming all three shunts plus "ductus venosus bypasses the lungs" is testing precisely this. The word "ductus" appears in two shunt names, which is what makes their destinations easy to trade.

22. Why does fetal circulation deliver the most oxygen-rich blood to the heart, head, neck, and arms rather than distributing it evenly?
  • ✓This pattern enhances cephalocaudal, or head-to-rump, development of the fetus
  • BThe lower body has no arterial supply until after birth
  • CThe liver requires the least oxygen and is therefore supplied last
  • DThe pattern is incidental and has no stated developmental purpose
Rationale

Blood passing through the foramen ovale into the left ventricle is squeezed into the aorta, "where the arteries supplying the heart, head, neck, and arms receive most of the oxygen-rich blood." "This pattern of supplying the highest levels of oxygen and nutrients to the head, neck, and arms enhances the cephalocaudal (head-to-rump) development of the embryo/fetus" (p. 245).

Watch for

(D) is the pick for a learner who has memorized the route without the reason, and the reason is what makes later material cohere — head-sparing in fetal growth restriction is the same principle under stress.

23. Deoxygenated blood returning from the fetal head and arms enters the right atrium through the superior vena cava. Where does the majority of it go next?
  • ✓Into the right ventricle, out the pulmonary artery, and through the ductus arteriosus into the aorta
  • BThrough the foramen ovale into the left atrium and out the aorta
  • CInto the pulmonary circulation, where it is oxygenated by the fetal lungs
  • DDirectly into the umbilical vein for return to the placenta
Rationale

"Deoxygenated blood returning from the head and arms enters the right atrium through the superior vena cava. This blood is directed downward into the right ventricle, where it is squeezed into the pulmonary artery." "A small amount of blood circulates through the resistant lung tissue, but the majority follows the path with less resistance through the ductus arteriosus into the aorta" (p. 245).

Watch for

(B) applies the foramen ovale's route to the wrong stream. Blood arriving from the inferior vena cava crosses the foramen ovale; blood arriving from the superior vena cava is directed to the right ventricle instead, and keeping the two streams separate is what the fetal heart is arranged to do. (D) reverses the umbilical vein's direction.

24. A nurse is explaining how the fetus obtains enough oxygen across a placenta rather than through lungs. Select all the special characteristics the chapter names.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Fetal hemoglobin has a high affinity for oxygen and carries 20% to 30% more oxygen than maternal hemoglobin
  • ✓The hemoglobin concentration of the fetus is approximately 50% greater than that of the mother
  • ✓The fetal heart rate of 110 to 160 beats per minute gives a cardiac output per unit of body weight higher than an adult's
  • DThe fetus has a larger lung surface area relative to body weight than an adult
Rationale

"The following three special characteristics enable the fetus to obtain sufficient oxygen from the maternal blood": fetal hemoglobin's higher affinity and 20% to 30% greater carrying capacity, a hemoglobin concentration approximately 50% greater than the mother's, and a fetal heart rate of 110 to 160 beats per minute producing higher cardiac output per unit of body weight (p. 246).

Watch for

(D) is the single wrong option and it is wrong in a revealing way: the fetal lungs are not participating in gas exchange at all, so no lung property can be one of the adaptations. The three real adaptations are all about the blood and the pump, which is the pattern worth holding.

26. A patient asks why her baby is not harmed by having oxygen delivered through the placenta instead of lungs. Which statement about fetal hemoglobin is accurate?
  • ✓It has a high affinity for oxygen and carries 20% to 30% more oxygen than maternal hemoglobin
  • BIt has a lower affinity for oxygen than maternal hemoglobin, which allows easier release to the tissues
  • CIt carries the same amount of oxygen as maternal hemoglobin but is present in lower concentration
  • DIt is identical to adult hemoglobin and confers no advantage
Rationale

"Fetal hemoglobin has a high affinity for oxygen and carries 20% to 30% more oxygen than maternal hemoglobin," and "the hemoglobin concentration of the fetus is approximately 50% greater than that of the mother" (p. 246).

Watch for

(B) is the plausible-sounding inversion, since a lower affinity genuinely would help unload oxygen at the tissues. (B) raises a correctly remembered threshold to a wrong number, which is the standard way this item is missed. The 1.2:1 value that also appears in this chapter is the 30-to-31-week figure, not a target.

28. A student asks why fetal lung maturity is expressed as a ratio rather than as a single surfactant level. Which explanation is accurate?
  • ✓Lecithin is detectable at approximately 21 weeks and increases after week 24, whereas sphingomyelin remains constant, so the ratio tracks the rising component against a fixed reference
  • BSphingomyelin rises steadily while lecithin remains constant, so the ratio tracks sphingomyelin
  • CBoth phospholipids rise together, and the ratio corrects for amniotic fluid volume
  • DThe ratio corrects for maternal blood contamination of the specimen
Rationale

Lecithin is "the most critical alveolar surfactant required for postnatal lung expansion. It is detectable at approximately 21 weeks and increases in amount after week 24," while sphingomyelin "remains constant in amount" (p. 246).

(B) swaps which phospholipid is the variable one, and the fact bank names this as the test of whether the ratio is understood or merely memorized. If the denominator were the rising value, the ratio would move the wrong way as the lungs matured.

29. Besides the lecithin/sphingomyelin ratio, which findings indicate fetal lung maturity?
  • ✓Phosphatidylglycerol, detectable at about 36 weeks, indicating very low risk for respiratory distress syndrome, and a lamellar body count above 50,000
  • BPhosphatidylglycerol detectable at about 24 weeks, and a lamellar body count below 50,000
  • CAn absent phosphatidylglycerol level with a rising sphingomyelin level
  • DThe presence of meconium in the amniotic fluid
Rationale

Phosphatidylglycerol is detectable "at about 36 weeks," and "its presence indicates very low risk for respiratory distress syndrome." A lamellar body count above 50,000 "indicates lung maturity"; lamellar bodies are "surfactant-containing particles within the alveoli" (p. 246).

(B) inverts the lamellar body threshold, turning a floor into a ceiling. (D) is a real amniotic fluid finding attached to the wrong question — meconium in the fluid points to breech presentation or fetal hypoxia, not to lung maturity.

30. A patient with gestational diabetes at 37 weeks has an estimated fetal weight above the 90th percentile. What should the nurse anticipate about this fetus's lungs?
  • ✓They may be immature despite the large size, because hyperinsulinemia blocks lung maturation
  • BThey are certainly mature, because lung maturity tracks fetal size
  • CThey are accelerated in maturity, because maternal diabetes stresses the fetus
  • DLung maturity cannot be affected by maternal metabolic conditions
Rationale

"Gestational diabetes and chronic glomerulonephritis can inhibit fetal lung maturity" (p. 246). In fetuses of mothers with uncontrolled diabetes, maternal hyperglycemia produces fetal hyperglycemia, "stimulating hyperinsulinemia and islet cell hyperplasia. This results in a macrosomic (large) fetus. Hyperinsulinemia also blocks lung maturation, placing the neonate at risk for respiratory distress and hypoglycemia when the maternal glucose source is lost at birth" (p. 247).

Watch for

(B) is the trap the fact bank names outright — assuming a macrosomic infant must have mature lungs. Size and maturity are driven by the same hormone in opposite directions here, so the infant can be large and immature at once. (C) applies the acceleration rule, which belongs to maternal hypertension, placental dysfunction, infection, and corticosteroid use, not to diabetes.

31. A neonate born by scheduled cesarean at 38 weeks without labor develops tachypnea and has crackles. Which explanation does the chapter give?
  • ✓Neonates born by scheduled cesarean before 39 weeks without labor have an increased risk of transient tachypnea of the newborn, with crackles for the first 24 to 48 hours
  • BThe absence of thoracic compression during vaginal birth is now understood to be the sole cause of retained lung fluid
  • CLung fluid is produced only after birth, so early cesarean birth cannot affect it
  • DCrackles at this age indicate pneumonia until proven otherwise
Rationale

"Neonates born by scheduled cesarean birth before 39 weeks' gestation without labor have an increased risk of transient tachypnea of the newborn and are likely to have crackles in their lungs for the first 24 to 48 hours." Lung fluid clearance "was originally attributed to the thoracic compression during vaginal birth; current thinking is that ... it begins well in advance of term birth" (p. 246).

Watch for

(B) states the older explanation as though it were current, and the fact bank flags exactly this: do not attribute clearance solely to the thoracic squeeze. The distinction matters because clearance beginning well before birth is what makes gestational age, not mode of birth alone, the operative variable.

32. A nursing student is asked where blood formation first occurs in the developing human and how the site changes. Which sequence is correct?
  • ✓Yolk sac in the third week, fetal liver from the sixth week, then bone marrow, spleen, thymus, and lymph nodes seeded in weeks 8 to 11
  • BFetal liver in the third week, then yolk sac, then bone marrow
  • CBone marrow from the third week onward, with no earlier site
  • DPlacenta in the third week, then fetal liver from the sixth week
Rationale

Hematopoiesis, "the formation of blood, occurs in the yolk sac ... beginning in the third week." Hematopoietic stem cells seed the fetal liver in the fifth week and hematopoiesis begins there in the sixth, which "accounts for the relatively large size of the liver between the seventh and ninth weeks." Stem cells seed the bone marrow, spleen, thymus, and lymph nodes in weeks 8 to 11 (p. 246).

Watch for

(C) is the reflex pick from adult physiology, where marrow is the only answer. The sequence matters clinically because it explains the disproportionately large fetal liver at 7 to 9 weeks, which is otherwise a puzzling finding on early imaging.

33. An Rh-negative patient has a miscarriage at 8 weeks of gestation. Is she at risk for isoimmunization?
  • ✓Yes; blood-type antigens are present on fetal erythrocytes soon after the sixth week, so she is at risk in any pregnancy lasting longer than 6 weeks after fertilization
  • BNo; isoimmunization can occur only after a term birth
  • CNo; fetal red cells do not carry antigens until the third trimester
  • DYes, but only if the pregnancy was ectopic rather than intrauterine
Rationale

"The antigenic factors that determine blood type are present in the erythrocytes soon after the sixth week. For this reason an Rh-negative woman is at risk for isoimmunization in any pregnancy that lasts longer than 6 weeks after fertilization" (p. 246).

Watch for

(B) is the error the fact bank names, and it is the one with real consequences — confining the risk to term birth misses every early-loss scenario, including miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy, and induced abortion, all of which are prophylaxis indications.

34. A patient asks what "viability" means for her fetus. Which response reflects both definitions the chapter gives?
  • ✓A standard statutory definition is 20 weeks of gestation with a birth weight of 350, 400, or 500 g and varies by state, while clinically infants at 22 to 25 weeks are on the threshold of viability
  • BViability is uniformly defined nationally as 24 weeks of gestation
  • CViability is defined solely by birth weight, without reference to gestational age
  • DViability begins at 28 weeks, when surfactant production is sufficient
Rationale

Viability is "the capability of the fetus to survive outside the uterus and is usually defined by fetal weight and pregnancy duration for statistical and legal purposes." "A standard definition is 20 weeks' gestation and birth weight of 350, 400, or 500 g, but this varies by state. Nurses must be aware of the laws in their states," while "infants who are 22 to 25 weeks of gestation are on the threshold of viability" (p. 241). The limits on survival are "CNS function and the oxygenation capability of the lungs."

Watch for

(B) supplies the false comfort of a single national number, and the source says the opposite — the definition varies by state, which is why the chapter tells nurses to know their own state's law. The two definitions serve different purposes, and the stem decides which is being asked for.

35. A patient at 30 weeks is exposed to a substance known to be toxic. Which type of harm is most consistent with an exposure at this stage?
  • ✓Functional damage to the central nervous system, since from 9 weeks the fetus is less vulnerable to teratogens except those affecting CNS function
  • BMajor structural malformation of the limbs and heart, since these organs are still forming
  • CNo harm is possible after the embryonic period ends
  • DImmediate fetal demise, since the fetus has no remaining adaptive capacity
Rationale

"The stage of the fetus begins at 9 weeks," and during this period the fetus "is less vulnerable to teratogens except for those affecting functioning of the central nervous system" (p. 241). Stressors "cause damage to the CNS long after the vulnerable embryonic time for malformations in other organ systems," resulting in "cerebral palsy, neuromuscular impairment, intellectual disability, and learning disabilities" (p. 247).

Watch for

(B) applies the embryonic-period rule to a fetal-period exposure. Structural malformations belong to weeks 3 through 8, when organs are forming; a third-trimester exposure item is a functional item, and mixing the two windows is what the distinction is there to prevent.

38. A newborn receives prophylactic vitamin K. Which reasoning chain explains why?
  • ✓The sterile fetal gut cannot synthesize vitamin K, so coagulation factors II, VII, IX, and X cannot be synthesized in the fetal liver, and the deficiency persists for several days after birth
  • BThe fetal liver is too immature to synthesize any coagulation factors until several weeks after birth
  • CVitamin K is destroyed by the placenta, so fetal stores are depleted at birth
  • DCoagulation factors V and VIII cannot be synthesized because of maternal antibody transfer
Rationale

"Coagulation factors II, VII, IX, and X cannot be synthesized in the fetal liver because of the lack of vitamin K synthesis in the sterile fetal gut. 39. A nurse is explaining why newborns, especially preterm ones, are prone to jaundice. Which explanation is accurate?
  • ✓In fetal life unconjugated bilirubin is cleared by the placenta, so the glucuronyl transferase enzyme needed for conjugation is present in amounts less than those required after birth
  • BThe fetal liver conjugates bilirubin efficiently, but the newborn gut reabsorbs it
  • CBilirubin is produced only after birth, when fetal hemoglobin begins to break down
  • DThe placenta produces glucuronyl transferase, which the newborn must begin making at birth
Rationale

"During fetal life the liver does not have to conjugate bilirubin for excretion because the unconjugated bilirubin is cleared by the placenta. Therefore the glucuronyl transferase enzyme needed for conjugation is present in amounts less than those required after birth. This predisposes the neonate, especially the preterm infant, to hyperbilirubinemia" (p. 247).

Watch for

(D) relocates the enzyme to the placenta, which sounds plausible because the placenta is doing the clearing. The enzyme is fetal and simply under-produced, since there has been no demand for it — an instance of the chapter's central pattern, in which a placental function becomes a newborn problem the moment the cord is cut.

40. A term newborn becomes hypothermic and has an increased work of breathing. Which hepatic store is the major source of energy under this stress, and how large is it?

  • BIron, which at term is stored at twice adult levels
  • CGlycogen, which at term is stored at half adult levels
  • DBrown fat, which is stored in the liver beginning at week 9
Rationale

The fetus stores glycogen in the liver beginning at week 9 or 10, and "at term, glycogen stores are twice those of an adult. (D) names a real store laid down for exactly this purpose, but brown fat is deposited between 26 and 30 weeks in preparation for extrauterine cold stress and is not a hepatic glycogen store. 42. A nurse is explaining why fetal renal failure may not be apparent before birth. Which statement is accurate?
  • ✓The placenta acts as the organ of excretion and maintains fetal water and electrolyte balance, so the fetus does not need functioning kidneys in utero, but the kidneys are required immediately at birth
  • BThe fetal kidneys are the primary organ of excretion from the end of the first trimester onward
  • CFetal renal failure always presents as hydramnios before birth
  • DThe newborn kidney concentrates urine as effectively as an adult kidney, so the transition is uneventful
Rationale

"Because the placenta acts as the organ of excretion and maintains fetal water and electrolyte balance, the fetus does not need functioning kidneys while in utero. However, at birth, the kidneys are required immediately for excretory and acid-base regulatory functions." At term "the GFR is low, and the kidneys lack the ability to concentrate urine," making the newborn "more susceptible to both overhydration and dehydration" (p. 247).

Watch for

(C) inverts the amniotic fluid finding: absent or reduced fetal urine output produces oligohydramnios, not hydramnios, so renal failure that does declare itself before birth does so as too little fluid. (D) contradicts the stated limitation that makes newborn fluid management delicate.

43. A patient is told she is carrying dizygotic twins. Which statement about the membranes and placentas is always true?
  • ✓There are always two amnions, two chorions, and two placentas that may be fused
  • BThere is always one chorion and one placenta shared by both fetuses
  • CThe membrane arrangement depends on when the zygote divided
  • DThere is always one amnion shared by both fetuses
Rationale

Dizygotic twins arise when multiple mature ova are "fertilized by separate sperm," and there "are always two amnions, two chorions, and two placentas that may be fused" (p. 248). They can be the same sex or different sexes and are genetically "no more alike than siblings born at different times."

Watch for

(C) applies the monozygotic rule to the dizygotic case. Membrane arrangement is a timing question only when a single zygote divides; with two separate zygotes there was never a shared structure to divide, so the arrangement is fixed rather than variable.

44. A patient carrying monozygotic twins asks why some identical twins share a placenta and others do not. Which explanation is accurate?
  • ✓The arrangement depends on when division occurs: soon after fertilization gives two amnions, two chorions, and two placentas, while division between 4 and 8 days, the most common timing, gives two amnions, one chorion, and one placenta
  • BAll monozygotic twins share one placenta and one chorion regardless of timing
  • CThe arrangement depends on the sex of the fetuses
  • DDivision after the eighth day gives two separate placentas with no shared membranes
Rationale

Division "soon after fertilization" produces "two embryos, two amnions, two chorions, and two placentas that can be fused"; division "between 4 and 8 days after fertilization," which is most common, produces "two embryos, two amnions, one chorion, and one placenta"; division after the eighth day produces "two embryos within a common amnion and a common chorion with one placenta," which "often causes circulatory problems because the umbilical cords may tangle together" (p. 249). Division 13 days or later produces conjoined twins.

Watch for

(D) inverts the latest arrangement, and it inverts the one with the highest stakes — division after day 8 gives the most sharing, not the least, and that is the arrangement carrying cord-entanglement risk.

45. A nurse is reviewing risk factors for twinning. Select all the statements that are accurate.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Dizygotic twinning occurs in families and increases with maternal age, peaking at 37 years
  • ✓Dizygotic twinning can follow discontinuation of oral contraceptives as follicle-stimulating hormone levels rebound
  • ✓Monozygotic twinning has no association with race, heredity, maternal age, or parity
  • ✓Assisted reproductive technology increases the incidence of monozygotic twinning by more than tenfold
  • EMonozygotic twinning runs in families in the same way dizygotic twinning does
Rationale

Dizygotic twinning "occurs in families," is more common among Black women than White women and least common among Asian women, and increases "with maternal age, peaking at 37 years; with parity; with maternal family history of twins; with maternal obesity; and with the use of assisted reproductive technology," as well as after "discontinuation of oral contraceptives as FSH levels rebound." Monozygotic twinning has "no association with race, heredity, maternal age, or parity," though assisted reproductive technology "increases the incidence ... by more than tenfold" (pp. 248–249).

Watch for

(E) is the single wrong option, and it is the clause the fact bank singles out — running in families belongs only to dizygotic twinning, which makes sense mechanically, since what is inherited is the tendency to release more than one ovum. Assisted reproductive technology is the one risk factor that raises both, which is why it cannot be used to tell the two types apart.
