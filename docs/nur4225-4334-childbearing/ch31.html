postpartum blues versus depressiontelling them apart
1. A woman 4 days after birth has mood swings, tearfulness, difficulty sleeping, and loss of appetite, but is caring for her infant and herself without difficulty. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • ✓As postpartum blues, a mild and transient depression occurring in 50% to 70% of women, in which functioning is not usually impaired and symptoms typically subside within a few days to 2 weeks; treatment is not needed
  • BAs postpartum depression requiring immediate medication
  • CAs postpartum psychosis
  • DAs a normal finding needing no follow-up at all
Rationale

Postpartum blues is "characterized by mood swings, feelings of sadness and anxiety, crying, difficulty sleeping, and loss of appetite; the woman's functioning is not usually impaired." It occurs "in 50% to 70% of women after birth," symptoms "typically subside within a few days to 2 weeks," and "treatment is not needed."

Watch for

(D) is close but drops the surveillance, and the whole chapter turns on the follow-up: symptoms that persist past 2 weeks or that impair functioning are a different diagnosis.

postpartum blues versus depressiontelling them apart
2. What distinguishes postpartum depression from postpartum blues?
  • ✓Depression involves an intense and pervasive sadness with labile mood swings that can be severe, is more serious and persistent, lasts more than 2 weeks, and impairs functioning, affecting approximately 9% to 24% of women
  • BDepression is milder and shorter
  • CDepression occurs only after the sixth week
  • DThey are the same condition with different names
Rationale

Postpartum depression is "an intense and pervasive sadness with labile mood swings that can be severe," is "more serious and persistent," lasts "more than 2 weeks," and is "experienced by approximately 9% to 24% of women," with a recent meta-analysis reporting "a 12% incidence ... among healthy women who gave birth to healthy infants."

Watch for

(D) collapses a distinction with different treatment implications — blues need nothing, depression needs support, psychotherapy, or medication by severity.

3. Why does the chapter say a new mother's emotional distress often goes unaddressed by those around her?
  • ✓Because birth is usually thought to be a happy event, so a new mother's emotional distress can puzzle and immobilize family and friends — when she most needs caring attention, they may either criticize or withdraw
  • BBecause families are indifferent
  • CBecause women rarely have symptoms
  • DBecause symptoms are always obvious and acted on
Rationale

"Because birth is usually thought to be a happy event, a new mother's emotional distress can puzzle and immobilize family and friends. When she most needs the caring attention of loved ones, they may either criticize or withdraw." The chapter states the stakes plainly: failure to assess, intervene, and refer "can result in tragic consequences such as suicide or infanticide."

Watch for

(B) attributes to indifference what the chapter attributes to confusion, and the difference matters for how the nurse involves the family.

screening tools for perinatal mood disordersthe edinburgh scale
5. What is the most widely used screening tool in perinatal populations, and how is it structured and scored?
  • ✓The Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Screen, comprising 10 statements about common symptoms of depression, with the woman choosing the response closest to how she has felt for the past week; the maximum score is 30, and women scoring 12 or higher may possibly have depression and need further assessment
  • BThe Patient Health Questionnaire–9, with a maximum score of 27 and a cutoff of 5
  • CThe Edinburgh scale, with 20 items and a cutoff of 20
  • DA single question about mood
Rationale

"In perinatal populations the most widely used screening tool is the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Screen (EPDS)." It "comprises 10 statements about the common symptoms of depression. The woman is asked to choose the response that is closest to describing how she has felt for the past week." And: "A maximum score on the EPDS is 30; women with scores of 12 or higher may possibly have depression and need further assessment."

Watch for

(C) inflates both the item count and the cutoff, and the cutoff is what determines who gets further assessment.

postpartum depression risk and etiologyetiology
13. What hormonal change does the chapter identify as an important etiologic factor in postpartum depression?
  • ✓The change from the high levels of estrogen and progesterone at the end of pregnancy to the much lower levels of both after birth
  • BRising estrogen after birth
  • CRising progesterone after birth
  • DHormones play no role
Rationale

"The change from the high levels of estrogen and progesterone at the end of pregnancy to the much lower levels of both hormones that are present after birth is an important etiologic factor." Other contributors named are "changes in cortisol, oxytocin, melatonin, and thyroid hormone after birth; neuroinflammatory processes; and abnormal levels or activity of neurotransmitters (dopamine, norepinephrine, serotonin)," plus possible deficiencies of vitamin D, vitamin B12, folate, and omega-3 fatty acids.

Watch for

(B) and (C) reverse the direction of the postpartum hormone shift, which Chapter 20 establishes as a steep fall reaching its lowest point about a week after birth.

14. Which symptoms are common in postpartum depression?
  • ✓Fatigue, sleep disturbance, appetite disturbance, and irritability, with anxiety often prominent — either as a symptom of the depression or as a sign of a comorbid anxiety disorder
  • BEuphoria and increased energy
  • CHallucinations in most cases
  • DIsolated physical complaints with no mood change
Rationale

"Fatigue, sleep disturbance, appetite disturbance, and irritability are common. Anxiety is often a prominent symptom of PPD, or it can be a sign of a comorbid anxiety disorder." The chapter notes that "a majority of women have comorbidities such as generalized anxiety disorder."

(B) describes mania, and the chapter requires that "all women who present with depressive symptoms should be screened for bipolar disorder" precisely so that this is not missed.

  • BAs evidence that she should not care for the infant

Rationale

"Feelings of detachment toward the newborn or not feeling love for the newborn are common symptoms of PPD.   • BAny thought of harming the infant indicates psychosis
  • CWomen with depression act on their thoughts and women with psychosis do not
  • DThe two cannot be distinguished clinically
Rationale

"A woman with PPD may have obsessive thoughts about harming the infant, and this can be very frightening to her. (B) is the response that punishes disclosure and drives the thoughts underground, and it is the reason the distinction is stated so explicitly.

anxiety disorders and ptsdthe anxiety disorders
19. A postpartum woman worries constantly and excessively about her infant's well-being, with muscle tension, restlessness, insomnia, and difficulty concentrating. Which disorder does this describe?
  • ✓Generalized anxiety disorder, characterized by excessive and pervasive worrying, restlessness, inability to relax, difficulty concentrating, distress about making decisions, and worry out of proportion to the impact of the event
  • BPanic disorder
  • CObsessive-compulsive disorder
  • DPosttraumatic stress disorder
Rationale

Generalized anxiety disorder is "characterized by excessive and pervasive worrying, restlessness, inability to relax, difficulty concentrating, distress about making decisions, and obsession over things that are out of proportion to the impact of the event." The chapter adds: "The issues of concern to people with GAD are realistic, but the level of worry is much more intense than appropriate. Postpartum women may worry excessively about the well-being of their infants." Physical symptoms include muscle tension, irritability, restlessness, insomnia, fatigue, headache, nausea, diarrhea, and abdominal pain.

Watch for

(B) is a different pattern entirely — episodic rather than continuous, with "the rapid onset of intense fear (typically peaking at 10 minutes)."

anxiety disorders and ptsdthe anxiety disorders
20. A woman has episodes of sudden intense fear with palpitations, sweating, shortness of breath, dizziness, and a fear of losing control or dying, and has begun staying home. What has developed?
  • ✓Panic disorder, with agoraphobia — fear of certain places and situations — which develops when panic disorder is untreated, so that women tend to remain at home
  • BGeneralized anxiety disorder
  • CPostpartum psychosis
  • DObsessive-compulsive disorder
Rationale

Panic symptoms are "characterized by the rapid onset of intense fear (typically peaking at 10 minutes)," with "palpitations, sweating, shortness of breath, choking, nausea, abdominal discomfort, dizziness, unsteadiness, numbness or tingling, chills, or hot flashes," plus "a fear of losing control or fear of dying. There may or may not be an identifiable trigger." Untreated, women "may experience agoraphobia ..., and as a result, they tend to remain at home."

Watch for

(C) escalates an anxiety disorder to a psychotic one, and the presence of an identifiable panic pattern with intact reality testing is what separates them.

  • BOnly stillbirth
  • COnly cesarean birth

Rationale

"PTSD has been found to be more prevalent in women who have experienced severe complications in pregnancy or during birth such as preterm birth, preeclampsia with severe features, obstetric hemorrhage, emergency cesarean birth, stillbirth, or an infant in the neonatal intensive care unit."
