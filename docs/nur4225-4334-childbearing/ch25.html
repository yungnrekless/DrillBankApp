1. Which breastfeeding rates does the chapter report for infants born in 2019?
  • ✓83.2% ever breastfed, 55.8% receiving any breast milk at 6 months, and 24.9% exclusively breastfed through 6 months
  • B50% ever breastfed and 10% at 6 months
  • C95% ever breastfed and 60% exclusively at 6 months
  • D83.2% ever breastfed and 83.2% still breastfeeding at 1 year
Rationale

The chapter gives 83.2% ever breastfed, 78.6% receiving some breast milk at 1 month, 55.8% at 6 months, 35.3% at 12 months, 45.3% exclusive through 3 months, and 24.9% exclusive through 6 months. Commercial infant formula was fed to 19.2% of breastfed newborns during the first 2 days of life.

Watch for

(D) holds the initiation rate steady through a year, and the gap between initiation and duration is the whole reason the Healthy People 2030 targets exist — 42.4% exclusive through 6 months and 54.1% breastfed at 1 year.

2. Which groups does the chapter identify as having the lowest breastfeeding rates?
  • ✓Non-Hispanic Blacks, lower income families, families living in rural areas, women 20 to 29 years of age, and women participating in the WIC program
  • BWomen over 30 in urban areas
  • CWomen with previous breastfeeding experience
  • DNo disparities are identified
Rationale

"The lowest breastfeeding rates continue to be among non-Hispanic Blacks, lower income families, and families living in rural areas. Younger women (20 to 29 years of age) are less likely to breastfeed than women who are 30 years of age or older. Women participating in the Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants and Children (WIC) have lower breastfeeding rates than women who were eligible but did not participate and women who were ineligible."

(D) erases a set of disparities the chapter names precisely, and those groups are where support changes outcomes most.

6. A parent says formula is basically the same as breast milk. How should the nurse respond, based on the chapter?
  • AAgree, since formulas are nutritionally complete
  • ✓Explain that although many consider commercial formula equivalent, this belief is erroneous: human milk is the gold standard for infant nutrition and is nutritionally superior to infant formula
  • CExplain that formula is superior for weight gain
  • DAvoid the topic
Rationale

"Although many consider commercial infant formula to be equivalent to breast milk, this belief is erroneous. Human milk is the gold standard for infant nutrition; it is nutritionally superior to infant formula." Formulas "are usually adequate in providing nutrition to maintain infant growth and development within normal limits, but they are not equivalent to human milk."

Watch for

(A) accepts a premise the chapter states plainly as wrong, and the qualifier matters: formula is adequate, not equivalent.

7. What does the chapter mean by saying human milk is species specific and dynamic?
  • ✓That it is a dynamic substance whose composition changes to meet the changing nutritional and immunologic needs of the growing infant, and that milk produced by mothers of preterm infants differs in composition from that of mothers who give birth at term
  • BThat its composition is fixed from birth
  • CThat it varies only by maternal diet
  • DThat it is identical across all mammals
Rationale

"Human milk is species specific," "it is a dynamic substance with composition that changes to meet the changing nutritional and immunologic needs of the growing infant," and "the milk produced by mothers of preterm infants differs in composition from that of mothers who give birth at term." It is also "more easily digested" and has "greater bioavailability of vitamins, minerals, active enzymes, and hormones."

Watch for

(B) treats milk as a fixed formulation, which is exactly the property that formula has and milk does not.

8. Which immune components does the chapter name in human milk?
  • ✓Secretory IgA as the major immunoglobulin, with IgG, IgM, IgD, and IgE also present, plus T and B lymphocytes, epidermal growth factor, cytokines, interleukins, bifidus factor, complement, and lactoferrin
  • BIgG as the major immunoglobulin, with no cellular components
  • COnly antibodies, with no cells or growth factors
  • DNo immune components
Rationale

"The major immunoglobulin (Ig) in human milk is secretory IgA; IgG, IgM, IgD, and IgE are also present. Human milk also contains T lymphocytes and B lymphocytes, epidermal growth factor, cytokines, interleukins, bifidus factor, complement (C3 and C4), and lactoferrin."

Watch for

(B) names the major immunoglobulin of the placental transfer rather than of milk, and it drops the living cells that no formula contains.

  • BAfter the newborn's first bath and vitamin K injection
  • CAfter the first 4 hours of transition
  • DOnly after the mother has rested
Rationale

"The ideal time to begin breastfeeding is within the first hour after birth," and "a newborn without complications should be allowed to remain in direct skin-to-skin contact with the mother until he or she is able to breastfeed for the first time. 18. A mother worries that her newborn is taking very little at each feeding on day 1. What should the nurse explain?
  • ✓That babies normally consume small amounts during the first 3 days of life, with intake increasing from 5 to 15 mL per feeding in the first 24 hours to 60 to 90 mL by the end of the first week
  • BThat the infant should be taking 60 mL per feeding from the first day
  • CThat supplementation is needed immediately
  • DThat small intake indicates poor latch in every case

"Babies normally consume small amounts of milk with feedings during the first 3 days of life," and as the digestive tract clears of meconium, "milk intake increases from 5 to 15 mL per feeding in the first 24 hours to 60 to 90 mL by the end of the first week."

22. A mother asks how many minutes she should feed on each side. What should the nurse teach?
  • AExactly 10 minutes per side
  • ✓To look for signs that the baby has finished — the sucking and swallowing pattern has slowed, the breast is softened, the baby appears content and may fall asleep or release the nipple — rather than feeding for a set number of minutes, since time at the breast is not a reliable indicator of the amount of milk consumed
  • CTo feed for 5 minutes on each side to prevent nipple soreness
  • DTo feed until exactly 30 minutes have passed
Rationale

(C) is the old advice for preventing soreness, and it is the shortest route to the hindmilk problem in the next item.

23. A baby feeds effectively with adequate urine output and bowel movements, but weight gain is not satisfactory. What does the chapter suggest, and why?
  • ✓That the mother may be switching to the second breast too soon; feeding on the first breast until it softens ensures the baby triggers multiple milk ejection reflexes to provide the higher-fat hindmilk, which usually results in increased weight gain
  • BThat formula supplementation should begin immediately
  • CThat the infant needs a pacifier between feedings
  • DThat feedings should be shortened
Rationale

"If a baby seems to be feeding effectively and urine output and bowel movements are adequate, but the weight gain is not satisfactory, the mother may be switching to the second breast too soon. Watch for

(B) adds formula to a problem that is a technique issue, and the chapter's alternating rule is the other half of the answer: "the first breast offered should be alternated at each feeding to ensure that each breast receives equal stimulation and emptying."

Rationale

Mastitis "is characterized by the sudden onset of influenza-like symptoms including fever, chills, malaise, body aches, headache, nausea, and vomiting. The woman usually has localized breast pain and tenderness and a hot, reddened area on the breast." It "most commonly occurs in the upper outer quadrant of the breast; one or both breasts can be affected. Watch for

(B) is a bilateral, systemic-symptom-free condition of the early days, and the influenza-like symptoms with a localized red area are what separate mastitis from it.

Treatment is "bedrest, antibiotics (dicloxacillin [Dynapen], cephalexin [Keflex], or clindamycin [Cleocin]), measures to reduce pain and swelling (antiinflammatory medications and cold compresses), and continued lactation." Watch for

(A), (C), and (D) all reduce emptying of the affected breast, which is the condition that caused the mastitis in the first place — stopping makes it worse and is the most common reason breastfeeding ends prematurely.
