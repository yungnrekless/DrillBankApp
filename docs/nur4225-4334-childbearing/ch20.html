1. Six hours after a vaginal birth, the nurse palpates the fundus one fingerbreadth above the umbilicus. How should this be interpreted?
  • AAs abnormal, requiring immediate provider notification
  • ✓As expected, since within 12 hours the fundus can rise to approximately 1 cm above the umbilicus before beginning to descend
  • CAs a sign of a full bladder only
  • DAs evidence of retained placental fragments
Rationale

The fundus is "in the midline, approximately 2 cm below the level of the umbilicus" at the end of the third stage, and "within 12 hours the fundus can rise to approximately 1 cm above the umbilicus." After that, "the fundus descends 1 to 2 cm every 24 hours."

Watch for

(A) is the reflex response to a fundus above the umbilicus, and the timing is what settles it. The same finding on postpartum day 3 would be abnormal, because by then the fundus should be several centimeters lower.

2. Where should the fundus be on the sixth postpartum day, and when should it no longer be palpable abdominally?
  • ✓Halfway between the umbilicus and the symphysis pubis on day 6; not palpable abdominally after 2 weeks
  • BAt the umbilicus on day 6; not palpable after 6 weeks
  • CAt the symphysis pubis on day 6; not palpable after 1 week
  • DOne centimeter above the umbilicus on day 6; not palpable after 4 weeks
Rationale

"By the sixth postpartum day the fundus is normally located halfway between the umbilicus and the symphysis pubis," and "after 2 weeks the uterus should not be palpable abdominally." By 6 weeks it "should have returned to its nonpregnant location."

Watch for

(B) holds the fundus at its day-1 position for a week, which would mean no involution at all — the finding that defines subinvolution.

3. A woman asks how her uterus shrinks back to its former size. Which explanation reflects the chapter?
  • AThe muscle cells added during pregnancy are all destroyed, returning the uterus to its exact prepregnancy size
  • ✓Falling hormone levels cause autolysis — the self-destruction of excess hypertrophied tissue — while the additional cells laid down during pregnancy remain, which accounts for the slight increase in uterine size after each pregnancy
  • CThe uterus shrinks by expelling endometrial tissue only
  • DInvolution is complete within 1 week
Rationale

Pregnancy growth came from "both hyperplasia (an increase in the number of muscle cells) and hypertrophy (an enlargement of the existing cells)," and after birth "the decrease in these hormones causes autolysis," the self-destruction of excess hypertrophied tissue. "The additional cells laid down during pregnancy remain and account for the slight increase in uterine size after each pregnancy." The uterus weighs about 500 g at 1 week, 300 g at 2 weeks, and about 100 g at 4 weeks.

Watch for

(A) misses the parity detail that explains why a multipara's uterus never quite returns to a nullipara's size.

4. A woman on postpartum day 10 has a uterus that remains enlarged and boggy with continued rubra bleeding. What is this called, and what are the most common causes?
  • ✓Subinvolution, most commonly caused by retained placental fragments and infection
  • BNormal involution
  • CEndometriosis
  • DUterine atony, which occurs only in the first hour
Rationale

"Subinvolution is the failure of the uterus to return to a nonpregnant state as a result of ineffective uterine contractions," and "the most common causes of subinvolution are retained placental fragments and infection." Persistent lochia rubra "suggests continued bleeding as a result of retained fragments of the placenta or membranes."

Watch for

(D) confines atony to the immediate postpartum period, when the problem here is a failure of involution over days — which is why the two most common causes are things left behind or an infection, not a single relaxed moment.

5. How is postpartum hemostasis primarily achieved?
  • ABy platelet aggregation and clot formation at the placental site
  • ✓By compression of intramyometrial blood vessels as the uterine muscle contracts, rather than by platelet aggregation and clot formation
  • CBy vasoconstriction from circulating catecholamines
  • DBy fibrin deposition in the endometrium
Rationale

"Postpartum hemostasis is achieved primarily by compression of intramyometrial blood vessels as the uterine muscle contracts rather than by platelet aggregation and clot formation." Oxytocin "strengthens and coordinates these uterine contractions, compressing blood vessels and promoting hemostasis."

Watch for

(A) is the ordinary mechanism of hemostasis everywhere else in the body, and it is why a boggy uterus bleeds despite a normal platelet count — and why the intervention is massage and oxytocin rather than clotting factors.

afterpains and uterine contractionswho has them and when
6. A multiparous woman reports strong uterine cramping that intensifies when she breastfeeds. How should the nurse respond?
  • ✓Explain that afterpains are more common in subsequent pregnancies and that breastfeeding intensifies them because it stimulates uterine contractions, and that they typically resolve in 3 to 7 days
  • BNotify the provider, since cramping during breastfeeding is abnormal
  • CAdvise her to stop breastfeeding until the cramping resolves
  • DExplain that afterpains are strongest in first-time mothers
Rationale

"In primiparous women, the uterine tone is good, the fundus generally remains firm, and the woman usually perceives only mild uterine cramping. Periodic relaxation and vigorous contractions are more common in subsequent pregnancies" and cause afterpains, which "typically resolve in 3 to 7 days." Both breastfeeding and oxytocic medication "usually intensify afterpains because both stimulate uterine contractions."

Watch for

(D) reverses the parity relationship, and (C) would remove the intervention that most reduces bleeding — breastfeeding "increases the release of oxytocin, which promotes uterine contractions, therefore decreasing blood loss and reducing the risk for postpartum hemorrhage."

7. After which births are afterpains most noticeable?
  • ✓Births in which the uterus was overdistended, such as with a large infant, multifetal gestation, or polyhydramnios
  • BPrecipitous births only
  • CCesarean births only
  • DFirst births
Rationale

Afterpains are "more noticeable after births in which the uterus was overdistended (e.g., large infant, multifetal gestation, polyhydramnios)."

Watch for

(D) is the mirror error of the previous item. Overdistention and parity both work in the same direction, and both run opposite to the intuition that a first birth hurts most afterward.

8. Why is exogenous oxytocin usually administered immediately after expulsion of the placenta?
  • ✓Because during the first 1 to 2 postpartum hours uterine contractions can decrease in intensity and become uncoordinated, and it is vital that the uterus remain firm and well contracted
  • BBecause oxytocin prevents infection
  • CBecause oxytocin promotes milk production
  • DBecause oxytocin relieves afterpains
Rationale

"During the first 1 to 2 postpartum hours, uterine contractions can decrease in intensity and become uncoordinated. Because it is vital that the uterus remain firm and well contracted, exogenous oxytocin (Pitocin) is usually administered intravenously or intramuscularly immediately after expulsion of the placenta." With active third-stage management it may be given after the birth but before the placenta is expelled, and "the uterus is very sensitive to oxytocin during the first week or so after birth."

Watch for

(C) assigns oxytocin the job that belongs to prolactin, and (D) reverses its effect on afterpains, which it intensifies rather than relieves.

9. What does the chapter say about healing at the placental site?
  • ✓Upward growth of the endometrium causes sloughing of necrotic tissue and prevents the scar formation characteristic of normal wound healing, which enables the endometrium to resume its usual cycle and allow implantation in future pregnancies
  • BThe site heals by scar formation like any other wound
  • CThe site never fully heals
  • DHealing is complete within 3 days
Rationale

"Upward growth of the endometrium causes sloughing of necrotic tissue and prevents the scar formation characteristic of normal wound healing. This unique healing process enables the endometrium to resume its usual cycle of changes and to allow implantation and placentation in future pregnancies." Endometrial regeneration begins within 3 days and is complete by the third week, with regeneration at the placental site complete by the sixth week.

Watch for

(B) applies ordinary wound healing to the one site in the body that deliberately avoids it — and a scarred placental site would prevent future implantation there.

  • CLochia alba, containing white blood cells only

Rationale

Table 20.1 gives lochia rubra as bright red, 1 to 3 days after birth, containing "blood from placental site; trophoblastic tissue debris, vernix, lanugo, meconium." It is the only stage whose contents include fetal debris.

(B) has the right idea and the wrong window: serosa is pinkish-brown from days 4 to 10, and alba is whitish-yellow from days 10 to 14 and can last 3 to 6 weeks.

11. A woman is 12 days postpartum and reports whitish-yellow discharge. She asks how much longer it will last. What should the nurse say?
  • ✓That this is lochia alba, which typically appears at 10 to 14 days and can last 3 to 6 weeks, and that most women experience lochia for 4 to 6 weeks after birth
  • BThat discharge at this point indicates infection
  • CThat lochia should have stopped by day 10
  • DThat this is lochia serosa and will become rubra again
Rationale

Table 20.1 gives lochia alba as whitish-yellow at 10 to 14 days, lasting 3 to 6 weeks, and the chapter states that "most women experience lochia for 4 to 6 weeks after birth."

(D) reverses the progression. Lochia moves rubra to serosa to alba and does not go back — a return to rubra is what suggests retained fragments.

12. A woman who stood up from bed reports a sudden gush of dark blood. Her fundus is firm and the flow quickly lessens to a trickle. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • AAs hemorrhage requiring immediate intervention
  • ✓As lochia that pooled in the vagina while she was lying down, which should not be confused with hemorrhage
  • CAs a cervical laceration
  • DAs retained placental fragments
Rationale

"Lochia tends to pool in the vagina when the woman is lying in bed; the woman then can experience a gush of blood when she stands. This gush should not be confused with hemorrhage." Box 20.1 adds that a gush appearing with fundal massage, if dark, "has been pooled in the relaxed vagina, and the amount soon lessens to a trickle of bright red lochia."

Watch for

(C) is what the same volume of blood would suggest if it spurted while the uterus was firmly contracted — the direction and pattern of the flow, not the volume, is the discriminator.

13. A woman 10 days postpartum reports a sudden increase in bleeding. What does the chapter say, and what is the time limit for acting?
  • ✓It is not uncommon to have a sudden brief increase in bleeding 7 to 14 days after birth when sloughing of eschar over the placental site occurs; if it does not subside within 1 to 2 hours, she needs evaluation for possible retained placental fragments
  • BAny increase in bleeding after day 7 is abnormal and requires immediate admission
  • CIncreased bleeding at this point always indicates endometritis
  • DShe should be told to expect this for the next 2 weeks
Rationale

"It is not uncommon for women to experience a sudden, but brief, increase in bleeding 7 to 14 days after birth when sloughing of eschar over the placental site occurs. If this increase in bleeding does not subside within 1 to 2 hours, the woman needs to be evaluated for possible retained placental fragments."

Watch for

(D) drops the time limit, which is the part that makes this teachable — the event is normal, and its persistence past 1 to 2 hours is not.

  • ✓As within normal limits, since during the first 24 hours the temperature can increase to 38°C as a result of the dehydrating effects of labor
  • BAs puerperal sepsis
  • CAs requiring blood cultures immediately
  • DAs mastitis
Rationale

Table 20.2 gives the normal finding: "during first 24 h, temperature can increase to 38°C (100.4 °F) as a result of dehydrating effects of labor. After 24 h, the woman should be afebrile."

Watch for

(B) applies the postpartum sepsis criterion to a reading inside the normal window. The same number after 24 hours, recurring or persisting for 2 days, is what suggests puerperal sepsis — along with mastitis, endometritis, urinary tract infection, or other systemic infection.

16. A woman's temperature is 38.2°C (100.8 °F) on postpartum day 2 and again on day 3. What does this suggest?
  • ANormal postpartum thermoregulation
  • ✓Infection: a temperature of 38°C or higher after the first 24 hours that recurs or persists for 2 days suggests puerperal sepsis, with mastitis, endometritis, urinary tract infection, or other systemic infection as possible causes
  • CEngorgement alone
  • DDehydration from labor
Rationale

"A diagnosis of puerperal sepsis is suggested if an increase in maternal temperature to 38°C (100.4 °F) or higher is noted after the first 24 h after birth and recurs or persists for 2 days." The chapter adds that a temperature of 38°C or higher during the first 10 days postpartum can indicate infection.

Watch for

(C) names a real cause of mild temperature elevation — engorged breasts "are hard and uncomfortable, and mild temperature elevation can occur" — but a recurrent fever over 2 days meets the sepsis criterion and is evaluated.

17. A woman's pulse is 48 beats/min on postpartum day 3. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • AAs bradycardia requiring cardiac evaluation
  • ✓As puerperal bradycardia, which is common for 6 to 10 days after birth with heart rates of 40 to 50 beats/min
  • CAs a sign of hypovolemia
  • DAs an effect of the epidural
Rationale

"Puerperal bradycardia is common for 6 to 10 days after birth, with the heart rate decreasing to 40 to 50 beats/min." The finding that matters is the opposite: "a rapid pulse rate or one that is increasing can indicate hypovolemia due to hemorrhage."

Watch for

(C) inverts the postpartum pattern. In this period tachycardia is the warning and bradycardia is the expectation, which reverses ordinary adult assessment.

18. A woman becomes dizzy and faint when she stands for the first time after birth. Her blood pressure is unchanged. What explains this?
  • ✓Orthostatic hypotension from the splanchnic engorgement that can occur after birth, which can develop in the first 48 hours
  • BHemorrhage
  • CPreeclampsia
  • DEpidural-induced hypotension persisting for days
Rationale

Table 20.2 describes "orthostatic hypotension, as indicated by feelings of faintness or dizziness immediately after standing up," which "can develop in the first 48 h as a result of the splanchnic engorgement that can occur after birth." Blood pressure normally shows "a transient increase of approximately 5% during the first few days."

Watch for

(B) is the diagnosis to rule out rather than the explanation here, and the table warns that falling blood pressure is "a late sign" of hemorrhage, with other symptoms usually present first.

19. A woman's blood pressure is 148/94 mm Hg on two readings 4 hours apart on postpartum day 2. How should this be interpreted?
  • AAs a normal transient postpartum increase
  • ✓As possible gestational hypertension or preeclampsia, which should be evaluated
  • CAs an expected effect of breastfeeding
  • DAs orthostatic hypotension
Rationale

Table 20.2 states that "elevated blood pressure (>140/90 mm Hg on two occasions 4 h apart) can be a sign of gestational hypertension or preeclampsia and should be evaluated." An increased reading can also result from excessive use of vasopressor or oxytocic medications.

Watch for

(A) stretches the normal 5% transient increase past the threshold the same table sets. Preeclampsia can appear for the first time after birth, which is why the postpartum blood pressure is not a formality.

20. About half of women experience shivering episodes shortly after birth. What does the chapter say about this?
  • ✓The exact cause is unknown and usually no treatment is needed, although pharmacologic treatment may be needed if the shivering is related to the effects of anesthesia
  • BIt always indicates infection
  • CIt indicates hemorrhage
  • DIt requires immediate warming measures and a provider notification
Rationale

"As many as 50% of women experience shivering episodes during the first few minutes up to the first hour after birth. The exact cause is unknown, and usually no treatment is needed"; if it is related to anesthesia, pharmacologic treatment may be needed.

Watch for

(B) treats a common, self-limited event as a rigor. Fever criteria in this period are specific, and shivering alone does not meet them.

21. How much blood loss is expected with a vaginal birth and with a cesarean birth?
  • ✓300 to 500 mL for a vaginal birth, about 10% of blood volume; 500 to 1000 mL for a cesarean birth, 15% to 30% of blood volume
  • B100 to 300 mL for a vaginal birth; 300 to 500 mL for a cesarean birth
  • C500 to 1000 mL for a vaginal birth; 1000 to 1500 mL for a cesarean birth

Rationale

"The average blood loss for a vaginal birth of a single fetus ranges from 300 to 500 mL (10% of blood volume)," and "the typical blood loss for women who give birth by cesarean is 500 to 1000 mL (15% to 30% of blood volume)."

(D) collapses a distinction that changes the interpretation of the same number: a 700-mL loss is expected after a cesarean and excessive after a vaginal birth.

22. Why can most women tolerate the blood loss of birth, and what compensations does the chapter name? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Pregnancy-induced hypervolemia, an increase in blood volume of 40% to 45% above prepregnancy levels
  • ✓Elimination of uteroplacental circulation, which reduces the size of the maternal vascular bed by 10% to 15%
  • ✓Loss of placental endocrine function, which removes the stimulus for vasodilation
  • ✓Mobilization of extravascular water stored during pregnancy
  • EA rapid rise in erythrocyte production within the first 24 hours
Rationale

"Pregnancy-induced hypervolemia ... allows most women to tolerate considerable blood loss during birth," and the chapter lists three compensations: reduction of the vascular bed by 10% to 15%, loss of the vasodilating stimulus, and mobilization of stored extravascular water. (E) invents a marrow response on a timescale the chapter does not support: hemoglobin and hematocrit "decrease slightly during the first 24 hours, plateau by 3 to 4 days, and then begin to increase, reaching nonpregnant levels by 4 to 8 weeks."

23. What happens to cardiac output immediately after birth?
  • AIt falls by half because of blood loss
  • ✓It increases by 60% to 80% over prelabor values and returns to prelabor values within 1 hour
  • CIt remains unchanged for 24 hours
  • DIt increases gradually over 2 weeks
Rationale

"Typically, cardiac output is increased immediately after birth by 60% to 80% over prelabor values; it returns to prelabor values within 1 hour," because of "the compensatory influx of nearly 500 mL of blood into the maternal system from the uteroplacental bed, a rapid decrease in uterine blood flow, and mobilization of extracellular fluid." By 2 weeks it decreases by 30%, reaching prepregnant levels by 6 to 8 weeks.

Watch for

(A) reasons from the blood loss alone and misses the autotransfusion — this is also the physiology behind the warning that the immediate postbirth period is the most dangerous window for a woman with cardiac disease.

24. A postpartum woman's white blood cell count is 26,000/mm3. How should the nurse interpret this, and what consequence does the chapter draw?

(A) uses a value that is not diagnostic in this population, which is exactly the problem the chapter names — infection has to be diagnosed on other grounds here.

25. How long does the chapter say the postpartum hypercoagulable state increases thromboembolic risk?

26. A woman on postpartum night 2 wakes drenched in sweat and reports voiding large amounts. What should the nurse do?
  • AObtain blood cultures for suspected infection
  • ✓Provide a gown change and reassurance, since urine output of 3000 mL or more per day and profuse diaphoresis, especially at night, are common for the first 2 to 3 days and account for a weight loss of 2 to 3 kg
  • CRestrict fluids to reduce the diuresis
  • DInsert an indwelling catheter to measure output accurately
Rationale

"Within 12 hours of birth, women begin to lose excess tissue fluid accumulated during pregnancy," driven by decreased estrogen levels, removal of increased venous pressure in the lower extremities, and loss of the pregnancy-induced blood volume increase. "Urine output of 3000 mL or more each day during the first 2 to 3 days after birth is common," and "profuse diaphoresis often occurs, especially at night, for the first 2 to 3 days," together accounting for "a weight loss of 2 to 3 kg (5 to 6.6 lb.) during the early puerperium."

Watch for

(A) works up a normal physiologic event, and (D) adds a catheter that would raise the risk of urinary tract infection in a woman already at increased risk.

  • ✓Lactogenesis II, occurring by about 72 to 96 hours after birth
  • BLactogenesis I, occurring in the first 24 hours
  • CLet-down, occurring with each feeding
  • DEngorgement, which is always pathologic
Rationale

"The breasts gradually become fuller and heavier as the colostrum transitions to mature milk by about 72 to 96 hours after birth; this is known as lactogenesis II" — often called the milk coming in. 32. A breastfeeding woman has hard, uncomfortable, engorged breasts on day 3 with a mild temperature elevation, and her infant is having trouble latching. What should the nurse teach?
  • ✓That engorgement is from an increase in blood and lymphatic fluid as milk production increases, that the fullness of the nipple tissue can make latching difficult, and that with frequent breastfeeding and proper care it typically lasts only 24 to 48 hours
  • BThat she should stop breastfeeding until the swelling resolves
  • CThat the temperature elevation indicates mastitis
  • DThat engorgement lasts about 2 weeks
Rationale

"Some women experience engorgement at this time owing to an increase in blood and lymphatic fluid as milk production increases. Engorged breasts are hard and uncomfortable, and mild temperature elevation can occur; the fullness of the nipple tissue can make it difficult for the infant to latch on and feed." And: "with frequent breastfeeding and proper care, engorgement is a temporary condition that typically lasts only 24 to 48 hours."

Watch for

(B) removes the treatment. Frequent feeding is what resolves engorgement in a lactating woman, and stopping would worsen it.

33. A woman who is not breastfeeding has distended, firm, tender breasts on postpartum day 3 and asks whether she should express some milk for relief. What should the nurse tell her?
  • AYes, expressing milk will relieve the pressure
  • ✓No — breast distention is caused primarily by temporary congestion of veins and lymphatics rather than by an accumulation of milk; milk is present but should not be expressed, and the engorgement resolves spontaneously with discomfort decreasing usually within 24 to 36 hours
  • CShe should pump every 3 hours until the swelling resolves
  • DShe should apply heat and massage the breasts to move the milk
Rationale

In nonbreastfeeding women, "breast distention is caused primarily by the temporary congestion of veins and lymphatics rather than by an accumulation of milk. Milk is present but should not be expressed." Engorgement "resolves spontaneously, and discomfort decreases usually within 24 to 36 hours," and "lactation ceases within a few days to 1 week."

(A) and (C) both stimulate the breast and prolong lactation, which is the opposite of what this woman wants — and it is the most tempting advice because it works for the breastfeeding woman two items earlier.
