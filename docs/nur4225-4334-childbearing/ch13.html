named signs of early pregnancygeneral
1. At a first prenatal visit at 6 weeks, the examiner notes softening of the cervical tip. Which sign does this describe?
  • ✓Goodell sign
  • BHegar sign
  • CChadwick sign
  • DBallottement
Rationale

"In a normal, unscarred cervix, softening of the cervical tip can be observed about the beginning of the 6th week. This probable sign of pregnancy, the Goodell sign, is due to increased vascularity, slight hypertrophy, and hyperplasia" (p. 253).

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick because both signs are softening findings at about the same gestational age. The discriminator is which structure: Goodell is the cervix, and Hegar is the lower uterine segment. Two of these three signs are about texture and one is about color, which is the fastest way to sort them.

2. At the same visit, the examiner notes softening and compressibility of the lower uterine segment. Which sign does this describe, and what symptom does the resulting uterine position produce?
  • ✓Hegar sign, which produces exaggerated uterine anteflexion so the fundus presses on the bladder, causing urinary frequency
  • BGoodell sign, which produces cervical friability and postcoital spotting
  • CChadwick sign, which produces leukorrhea
  • DHegar sign, which produces painless irregular contractions
Rationale

"At approximately 6 weeks of gestation, softening and compressibility of the lower uterine segment (uterine isthmus) occurs (Hegar sign)," which "results in exaggerated uterine anteflexion during the first 3 months. In this position, the uterine fundus presses on the urinary bladder, causing the woman to experience urinary frequency" (p. 252).

Watch for

(B) is the mirror error of the previous item, trading the two softening signs. This item also connects the sign to a symptom the patient reports, which is why first-trimester urinary frequency is a mechanical finding rather than an infection until dysuria appears.

3. A patient at 7 weeks has a violet-blue discoloration of the vaginal mucosa and cervix. Which sign is this, and what causes it?
  • ✓Chadwick sign, caused by increased vascularity
  • BGoodell sign, caused by softening of the cervical tip
  • CHegar sign, caused by compressibility of the isthmus
  • DMelasma, caused by increased melanotropin
Rationale

"Increased vascularity results in the violet-blue color of the vaginal mucosa and cervix, known as the Chadwick sign. This is evident at 6 to 8 weeks of pregnancy" (p. 253).

(B) and (C) are the two texture signs offered against the one color sign, which is how this trio usually appears on an exam. (D) is a real pigmentation change from a later chapter section, but melasma is on the face and appears after week 16, so both site and timing are wrong.

4. During a bimanual examination at 17 weeks, the examiner taps gently upward on the cervix, and the fetus rises and then sinks back against the examining finger. What is this technique called, and who perceives it?
  • ✓Ballottement, perceived by the examiner
  • BQuickening, perceived by the woman
  • CBallottement, perceived by the woman
  • DBraxton Hicks contractions, perceived by both
Rationale

Ballottement is the "passive movement of the unengaged fetus," a "technique of palpating a floating structure by bouncing it gently and feeling it rebound," generally possible between 16 and 18 weeks. The examiner "places a finger within the vagina and taps gently upward on the cervix, causing the fetus to rise. The fetus then sinks, and a gentle tap is felt on the finger" (p. 253).

Watch for

(C) keeps the right term and gives it to the wrong person, which is the distinction that matters: ballottement is an examiner's finding and quickening is the woman's. Both occur in roughly the same window, so timing alone cannot separate them.

5. A nulliparous patient at 17 weeks is worried because she has not yet felt fetal movement. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓Quickening occurs at 18 weeks or later in a nulliparous woman, so this is expected
  • BQuickening occurs at 14 to 16 weeks regardless of parity, so evaluation is needed
  • CQuickening occurs at 14 to 16 weeks in a nulliparous woman and later in a multiparous woman
  • DAbsence of movement at 17 weeks indicates fetal demise until proven otherwise
Rationale

Quickening, "the first recognition of fetal movements," occurs at "14 to 16 weeks" in the multiparous woman and at "the 18th week or later" in the nulliparous woman. It is "commonly described as a flutter and is difficult to distinguish from peristalsis," and "the week in which quickening occurs provides a tentative clue in dating the duration of gestation" (p. 253).

Watch for

(C) inverts the parity relationship, which is the error worth catching because it runs the same direction as lightening later in pregnancy — the experienced woman recognizes the finding earlier, and the first-time mother later. A nurse who has the relationship backwards will reassure the wrong patient and alarm the right one.

6. A patient at 20 weeks asks what is making her uterus grow at this stage. Which explanation is accurate?
  • ✓After the third month, uterine enlargement is primarily the result of mechanical pressure of the growing fetus
  • BUterine enlargement throughout pregnancy is driven entirely by estrogen and progesterone
  • CUterine enlargement after the third month results from continued hyperplasia of new muscle fibers
  • DUterine enlargement is caused by increasing amniotic fluid volume alone
Rationale

"High levels of estrogen and progesterone stimulate significant uterine growth in the first trimester" through increased vascularity, hyperplasia, hypertrophy, and development of the decidua. But "after the third month, uterine enlargement is primarily the result of mechanical pressure of the growing fetus" (p. 252).

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick because hormones explain so much else in this chapter, and it is wrong specifically because the driver changes partway through. The chapter's two explanatory categories, hormones and mechanical pressure, hand off to each other here, which is why the timing clause matters.

7. A nurse palpating a pregnant abdomen notes that the uterus is rotated to the right. How should this be interpreted?
  • ✓It is expected, probably because of the presence of the rectosigmoid colon on the left side
  • BIt is abnormal and suggests a uterine anomaly or an adnexal mass
  • CIt is expected, caused by the greater weight of the right-sided placenta
  • DIt is abnormal and suggests fetal malposition
Rationale

"As the uterus enlarges and rises in the abdomen, it rotates to the right, probably because of the presence of the rectosigmoid colon on the left side" (p. 252).

Watch for

(B) treats a normal asymmetry as pathology. The same rightward displacement explains why the renal pelvis and ureter dilate more on the right, which is why right-sided pyelonephritis is the classic presentation in pregnancy — one anatomic fact with two consequences.

8. At which gestational age would the nurse expect to palpate the fundus at the level of the umbilicus?
  • ✓20 to 22 weeks
  • B12 to 14 weeks
  • C28 to 30 weeks
  • D36 to 38 weeks
Rationale

The fundus is palpated above the symphysis pubis between the 12th and 14th weeks and "rises gradually to the level of the umbilicus" at 20 to 22 weeks, nearly reaching the xiphoid process at term (p. 252).

Watch for

(B) names the landmark from one row earlier in the same sequence, which is the reliable way this item is missed. The three landmarks are worth learning as a set with their weeks attached, because fundal height is used as a rough dating check at every visit.

9. A nulliparous patient at 38 weeks reports that she is breathing more easily and that her abdomen looks lower. What does this indicate?
  • ✓Lightening, which in a nullipara occurs approximately 2 weeks before the onset of labor
  • BLightening, which indicates that labor has begun
  • CAn abnormal decrease in fundal height requiring immediate evaluation
  • DBraxton Hicks contractions pulling the fundus downward
Rationale

"Between weeks 38 and 40" the fundal height decreases "as the fetus begins to descend into the pelvis (lightening)." "In general, lightening occurs in the nullipara approximately 2 weeks before the onset of labor and in the multipara at the start of labor" (p. 252).

Watch for

(B) applies the multipara's timing to a nullipara, which would send a woman to the hospital two weeks early. This is the same parity-dependent pattern as quickening: in a first pregnancy the landmark arrives with more warning, and in a later one it arrives closer to the event.

10. A patient at 30 weeks reports irregular, painless tightening of her abdomen that stops when she walks around. Which finding would distinguish these from true labor contractions?
  • ✓True labor contractions increase in intensity and duration and cause cervical dilation; Braxton Hicks contractions do not
  • BTrue labor contractions are painless, whereas Braxton Hicks contractions are painful
  • CTrue labor contractions cease with walking, whereas Braxton Hicks contractions intensify with walking
  • DOnly the presence of bloody show can distinguish them
Rationale

Braxton Hicks contractions are "irregular and painless" and "usually cease with walking or exercise." They "can be mistaken for true labor; however, they do not increase in intensity or duration or cause cervical dilation" (p. 253).

Watch for

(C) reverses which contractions stop with activity. The chapter's stated discriminators are progression and cervical change, and cervical change is the one that settles it, because a patient's report of intensity is subjective and the cervix is not.

11. A patient at 30 weeks reports regular abdominal tightening. She has had no cervical examination. What is the priority nursing action?
  • ✓Evaluate for cervical change, because preterm labor contractions can be mistaken for Braxton Hicks contractions, leading to a delay in seeking treatment
  • BReassure her that these are Braxton Hicks contractions, which are normal after the fourth month
  • CInstruct her to walk, since Braxton Hicks contractions cease with exercise
  • DSchedule her for evaluation at her next routine prenatal visit
Rationale

The chapter states the danger in both directions: Braxton Hicks contractions can be mistaken for true labor, and "conversely, preterm labor contractions can be mistaken for Braxton Hicks contractions, which can lead to a delay in seeking treatment" (p. 253).

Watch for

(B) is the plausible reassurance, and it is exactly the error the source names. At 30 weeks the cost of wrongly reassuring is preterm birth, so the reasoning has to run toward evaluation rather than toward the reassuring explanation, however common that explanation is.

12. On auscultation of a pregnant abdomen the nurse hears a rushing sound synchronous with the maternal pulse. What is this, and what would a sound synchronous with the fetal heart rate be?
  • ✓The uterine souffle, maternal blood flowing through the uterine arteries; a funic souffle is fetal blood coursing through the umbilical cord
  • BThe funic souffle, maternal blood in the uterine arteries; a uterine souffle is fetal blood in the cord
  • CThe uterine souffle, fetal blood in the umbilical cord; a funic souffle is maternal in origin
  • DA systolic ejection murmur transmitted from the maternal heart
Rationale

The uterine souffle is "a rushing or blowing sound of maternal blood flowing through uterine arteries to the placenta that is synchronous with the maternal pulse," while the funic souffle "is synchronous with the fetal heart rate and is caused by fetal blood coursing through the umbilical cord" (p. 253).

Watch for

(B) swaps the two names, which is the entire content of this item — the sounds are told apart by which pulse they match, and matching the wrong one reverses whose circulation is being heard.

13. A patient at 24 weeks has a blood pressure lower than her prepregnancy baseline, although her cardiac output has increased substantially. Which explanation is accurate?
  • ✓Systemic vascular resistance decreases by 30% to 50% because of the vasodilatory effects of progesterone, prostaglandins, and relaxin
  • BCardiac output does not actually rise in pregnancy; the measurement is unreliable
  • CBlood pressure falls because blood volume decreases in the second trimester
  • DBlood pressure falls because maternal heart rate decreases in pregnancy
Rationale

"Although CO increases significantly during pregnancy, maternal blood pressure remains the same or decreases slightly. This is due to reduced SVR caused primarily by the vasodilatory effects of progesterone, prostaglandins, and relaxin." Systemic vascular resistance "decreases by 30% to 50% and is lowest at 16 to 34 weeks" (p. 256).

Watch for

(C) and (D) both invert a real pregnancy change: blood volume rises 40% to 45% and heart rate rises 10 to 20 beats per minute. The apparent paradox resolves only through resistance, and the diastolic nadir at 24 to 32 weeks is why a "normal" second-trimester reading can conceal developing hypertension.

14. A patient's blood pressure readings have varied across three prenatal visits. Which factor should the nurse consider first?
  • ✓Position, since brachial blood pressure is highest sitting, lowest in the lateral recumbent position, and intermediate supine
  • BTime of day only, since circadian rhythm is the dominant influence
  • CCuff size only, since arm circumference changes little in pregnancy
  • DPosition is irrelevant to brachial readings in pregnancy
Rationale

"Brachial blood pressure is highest when the woman is sitting, lowest when she is lying in the lateral recumbent position, and intermediate when she is supine" (p. 256). Age, activity level, health problems, circadian rhythm, alcohol, smoking, anxiety, and pain also influence readings.

Watch for

(D) is the assumption behind most inconsistent prenatal blood pressures. Because the three positions produce a predictable ordering, a reading taken in a different position from the last one can look like a trend when it is only a posture change.

15. A patient at 30 weeks becomes lightheaded and faint several minutes after lying flat for an ultrasound. Which finding would the nurse expect, and what is the intervention?
  • ✓A reflex bradycardia with cardiac output reduced by half; reposition her to the left lateral position
  • BA reflex tachycardia with cardiac output increased; elevate her legs while she remains supine
  • CA rise in blood pressure from aortic compression; place her in a sitting position
  • DNo physiologic change; her symptoms are most likely anxiety
Rationale

"After 4 to 5 minutes, a reflex bradycardia is noted, CO is reduced by half, and the woman feels faint. This condition is called supine hypotensive syndrome or vena cava syndrome." Cardiac output falls "as much as 25% to 30%" when a pregnant woman turns from left lateral recumbent to supine, and some women drop more than 30 mm Hg systolic (p. 256).

Watch for

(B) substitutes tachycardia for bradycardia, which is the compensation a reader expects rather than the one the book describes. Getting this backwards matters because a bradycardic, faint pregnant patient who is lying flat needs repositioning, not a workup.

16. A patient at 34 weeks reports ankle edema, leg varicosities, and hemorrhoids. Which explanation should the nurse give?
  • ✓Compression of the iliac veins and inferior vena cava by the uterus raises venous pressure and reduces blood flow in the legs except in the lateral position
  • BThese findings indicate preeclampsia and require immediate evaluation
  • CThese findings result from increased systemic vascular resistance in the third trimester
  • DThese findings indicate venous thromboembolism until proven otherwise
Rationale

"Compression of the iliac veins and inferior vena cava by the uterus causes increased venous pressure and reduced blood flow in the legs except when the woman is in the lateral position," producing "dependent edema, varicose veins in the legs and vulva, and hemorrhoids" and contributing to "the increased risk for venous thromboembolism" (p. 256).

Watch for

(B) escalates a mechanical, positional finding into a hypertensive disorder. Dependent edema in the legs is expected; what would change the picture is hypertension with proteinuria, which this stem does not supply.

17. On auscultation of a patient at 28 weeks, the nurse hears splitting of the first and second heart sounds, a third heart sound, and a systolic ejection murmur at the left sternal border. How should these be interpreted?
  • ✓All are expected findings in pregnancy and are transient, usually disappearing shortly after birth
  • BA systolic ejection murmur is always pathologic and requires echocardiography
  • CA third heart sound in pregnancy indicates fluid overload requiring diuresis
  • DThese findings indicate cardiac decompensation and require immediate referral
Rationale

"By the end of the first trimester, there is an audible splitting" of the first and second heart sounds. "The majority of pregnant women also have a third heart sound after midpregnancy resulting from rapid diastolic filling. Approximately 96% of women develop systolic ejection murmurs that are most audible over the left sternal border." "The auscultatory changes are transient and usually disappear shortly after birth" (p. 256).

Watch for

(C) is the reflex from adult cardiac assessment, where a third heart sound suggests failure. In pregnancy it is present in the majority of women from rapid diastolic filling, and treating it as overload could lead to a diuretic — which this chapter separately warns causes severe hypovolemia and reduced placental perfusion.

18. A patient at 26 weeks has a hemoglobin of 10.8 g/dL. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • ✓It is within normal limits for the second trimester, where anemia is defined as hemoglobin less than 10.5 g/dL
  • BIt indicates anemia, since the threshold is 11 g/dL in every trimester
  • CIt indicates anemia and requires transfusion
  • DIt cannot be interpreted without a serum ferritin level
Rationale

A pregnant woman is anemic with "hemoglobin less than 11 g/dL or hematocrit less than 33%" in the first or third trimester, and with "hemoglobin less than 10.5 g/dL or hematocrit less than 32%" in the second trimester (p. 257).

Watch for

(B) applies the first- and third-trimester cutoff to a second-trimester patient, which labels a normal value as anemia. The threshold is lower in the second trimester precisely because that is when hemodilution is most pronounced — the physiology and the cutoff move together.

19. A patient asks why her hemoglobin is lower than before pregnancy if she is being told her blood count is normal. Which explanation is accurate?
  • ✓Plasma volume increases faster than red blood cell production, producing hemodilution called physiologic anemia; red cell mass itself rises 20% to 30%
  • BRed blood cell production decreases in pregnancy, which is why the hemoglobin falls
  • CThe hemoglobin falls because iron is diverted entirely to the fetus
  • DThe finding is always pathologic and indicates iron deficiency
Rationale

"Because the plasma increase is greater than the increase in RBC production, there is a decrease in the normal hemoglobin and hematocrit values. This state of hemodilution is referred to as physiologic anemia of pregnancy," which is "more noticeable during the second trimester." Red cell mass increases "250 to 450 mL, or approximately 20% to 30% over prepregnancy values," and the percentage of increase "depends on the amount of iron available" (p. 257).

Watch for

(B) is the intuitive misreading of a falling hemoglobin — that the marrow must be making fewer cells. Red cell mass genuinely rises; it is simply outpaced by plasma, which is why the finding is a dilution rather than a production problem.

20. A pregnant patient at 30 weeks has a white blood cell count of 14,000 per cubic millimeter. She is afebrile with no localizing symptoms. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • ✓It is within the expected range for pregnancy, where the white count ranges from 5000 to 15,000
  • BIt indicates infection and requires cultures and antibiotics
  • CIt indicates a hematologic malignancy and requires referral
  • DIt is below the expected pregnancy range and suggests immunosuppression
Rationale

Table 13.2 gives a pregnant white blood cell range of 5000 to 15,000 per cubic millimeter, against a nonpregnant 5000 to 10,000. The count "begins to increase as early as the 2nd month," primarily in granulocytes, nonclassic monocytes, and polymorphonuclear leukocytes, while "the lymphocyte count stays approximately the same" (p. 257).

Watch for

(B) applies a nonpregnant reference range, which is the practical error this table exists to prevent. An elevated white count is not by itself evidence of infection in pregnancy, so the febrile and localizing findings, absent here, are what would carry the diagnosis.

22. A patient asks why pregnancy increases her risk of blood clots. Which explanation is accurate?
  • ✓Clotting factors VII, VIII, IX, and X and fibrinogen increase, inhibitors such as protein S decrease, and fibrinolytic activity is depressed, producing a five- to sixfold increased risk
  • BPlatelet count and function increase markedly, producing a hypercoagulable state
  • CClotting factors II and V increase while all other factors remain unchanged
  • DPregnancy is a hypocoagulable state, and clot risk rises only after birth
Rationale

"Pregnancy is considered a hypercoagulable state in which women are at a five- to sixfold increased risk for thromboembolic problems," through "increases in various clotting factors (i.e., factors VII, VIII, IX, and X and fibrinogen) and decreases in factors that inhibit coagulation (e.g., protein S)," while "fibrinolytic activity ... is depressed." This "provides a protective function to decrease the chance of bleeding but also makes the woman more vulnerable to thrombosis, especially after cesarean birth" (p. 257).

Watch for

(C) names the wrong factors, which the fact bank flags as the checkable error in this list. (B) reaches for platelets, which the same table says do not significantly change — the hypercoagulability is a plasma-factor phenomenon, not a platelet one.

23. A nurse is reviewing expected respiratory changes in pregnancy. Select all the statements that are accurate.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Respiratory rate is unchanged or only slightly increased
  • ✓Tidal volume increases by approximately 40%
  • ✓Minute ventilation increases by 30% to 50%
  • ✓Expiratory reserve volume decreases by approximately 20%
  • EVital capacity decreases markedly as the diaphragm rises
Rationale

Table 13.3 gives respiratory rate as "unchanged or slightly increased," tidal volume increased 40%, vital capacity "unchanged," inspiratory capacity increased 6%, expiratory reserve volume decreased 20%, total lung capacity "unchanged to slightly decreased," minute ventilation increased 30% to 50%, and oxygen consumption increased 20% to 40% (p. 258).

(E) is the single wrong option and it is the one that intuition supplies: the diaphragm does rise as much as 4 cm, so vital capacity ought to fall. 24. A patient at 28 weeks reports feeling short of breath at rest, which began in the second trimester. She has no orthopnea, chest pain, or edema beyond mild ankle swelling. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • ✓This is consistent with physiologic dyspnea of pregnancy, which occurs with mild exertion or at rest and begins in the first or second trimester
  • BDyspnea at rest is never physiologic and always indicates cardiac decompensation
  • CPhysiologic dyspnea begins only in the third trimester, so this requires evaluation
  • DThis indicates pulmonary embolism until proven otherwise
Rationale

"Pregnancy-related dyspnea is common, beginning in the first or second trimester. This physiologic dyspnea occurs with mild exertion or at rest." The chapter adds that it is "important to differentiate physiologic from pathologic dyspnea, such as may be caused by cardiac decompensation" (p. 257).

Watch for

(B) is the reflex escalation, and it is wrong on the specific point that separates the two patterns — physiologic dyspnea does occur at rest, so "at rest" is not the discriminator. Onset in the first or second trimester without other cardiac findings is the reassuring pattern.

25. An arterial blood gas on a healthy pregnant patient shows a decreased PaCO2, decreased bicarbonate, and a slightly increased pH. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • ✓A compensated respiratory alkalosis, which is the expected acid-base state of pregnancy
  • BA metabolic alkalosis caused by vomiting earlier in pregnancy
  • CA respiratory acidosis caused by upward displacement of the diaphragm
  • DA laboratory error, since pregnancy does not alter acid-base balance
Rationale

"Pregnancy is a state of chronic mild hyperventilation with reduced arterial carbon dioxide and increased oxygen over nonpregnant levels." "Progesterone may be responsible for increasing the sensitivity of the respiratory center receptors so that PaCO2 decreases, the base excess (bicarbonate) decreases, and pH increases slightly. These alterations in acid-base balance create a state of respiratory alkalosis" (p. 257).

Watch for

(C) reasons from the mechanical change to the wrong direction: the diaphragm does rise, but ventilation increases rather than falls, so carbon dioxide is blown off rather than retained. Naming it metabolic, as (B) does, misses that the primary change is in carbon dioxide.

26. What purpose does the maternal respiratory alkalosis of pregnancy serve for the fetus?
  • ✓It facilitates transport of carbon dioxide from the fetus to the mother and release of oxygen from the mother to the fetus
  • BIt increases maternal oxygen storage for use during labor
  • CIt prevents fetal respiratory acidosis by raising fetal carbon dioxide
  • DIt has no fetal purpose and is an incidental consequence of progesterone
Rationale

The acid-base changes "also facilitate the transport of CO2 from the fetus to the mother and O2 release from the mother to the fetus" (p. 257). Maternal oxygen consumption rises steadily, reaching 40% above nonpregnant levels by term.

Watch for

(D) is the pick for anyone who has learned the change without the reason. The gradient the alkalosis creates is what allows a placenta working by diffusion to move gases in both directions efficiently, which connects this chapter to the fetal oxygenation adaptations in Chapter 12.

29. A patient at 7 weeks reports nausea that is worst in the evening. She asks whether this means something is wrong, since it is called morning sickness. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓Only about half of women have symptoms in the morning; for some it is worse in the evening and for others constant, and it typically peaks at 8 to 12 weeks and subsides by the end of the first trimester
  • BEvening nausea is atypical and suggests a gastrointestinal disorder
  • CNausea beginning at 7 weeks is late and should be evaluated
  • DNausea that is not worst in the morning indicates hyperemesis gravidarum
Rationale

Up to 80% of pregnant women experience nausea with or without vomiting. "Although ... commonly referred to as 'morning sickness,' only about half of the women experience the symptoms in the morning. For some, it is worse in the evening, and others experience symptoms constantly." It "generally appears at 4 to 6 weeks of gestation, peaks by 8 to 12 weeks, and usually subsides by the end of the first trimester" (p. 258).

Watch for

(D) reaches for the complication on the strength of an atypical timing that the source explicitly calls typical. The escalation criteria are different ones entirely, and confusing "not in the morning" with "severe" would send a normally progressing patient for an unnecessary workup.

30. Which pattern of nausea and vomiting in pregnancy warrants further evaluation?
  • ✓Vomiting that is severe, persists beyond the first trimester, or is accompanied by fever, pain, or weight loss
  • BAny vomiting occurring more than once daily in the first trimester
  • CNausea that is worse in the evening than in the morning
  • DNausea accompanied by an increased appetite in the second trimester
Rationale

"Rarely does NVP have harmful effects on the embryo, fetus, or woman. When the vomiting is severe or persists beyond the first trimester or when it is accompanied by fever, pain, or weight loss, further evaluation is necessary, and medical intervention is often needed. Watch for

(D) describes a normal finding — "by the end of the second trimester, the appetite increases in response to increasing metabolic needs" — dressed up as a warning sign. The three escalation criteria are duration, severity, and the accompanying findings, and none of them is frequency alone.

"In the latter part of pregnancy, the renal pelvis and ureter dilate more on the right side than on the left because the heavy uterus is displaced to the right by the sigmoid colon." (D) inverts the renal change — glomerular filtration rate increases by 50% during the first trimester and remains elevated. The susceptibility comes from stasis in dilated collecting structures, not from reduced filtration.
