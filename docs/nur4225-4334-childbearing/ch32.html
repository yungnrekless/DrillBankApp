tocolytic therapy selection and contraindicationsmatching the drug to the woman
1. A woman in preterm labor has a history of myasthenia gravis. Which tocolytic does the medication guide direct the nurse to question?
  • ✓Magnesium sulfate, which is not given to women with myasthenia gravis, hypocalcemia, or renal failure
  • BTerbutaline, which is not given to women with neuromuscular disease
  • CIndomethacin, which is not given to women with neuromuscular disease
  • DNifedipine, which is not given to women with neuromuscular disease
Rationale

The magnesium sulfate guide states plainly: "Do not give to women with myasthenia gravis, hypocalcemia, or renal failure." Magnesium is a CNS depressant that relaxes smooth muscle, and it worsens neuromuscular blockade.

Watch for

(B) is the tempting pick because terbutaline carries the longest contraindication list of the four, but that list is cardiac, metabolic, and neurologic in a different sense — heart disease, diabetes, severe preeclampsia or eclampsia, hyperthyroidism, glaucoma, and seizure disorder.

2. A woman with pregestational diabetes and a history of hyperthyroidism is in preterm labor. Which tocolytic is contraindicated for her?
  • AMagnesium sulfate
  • ✓Terbutaline
  • CIndomethacin
  • DNifedipine
Rationale

Terbutaline "should not be used in women with known or suspected heart disease, pregestational or gestational diabetes, preeclampsia with severe features or eclampsia, hyperthyroidism, glaucoma, or seizure disorder." It is a beta-2-adrenergic agonist, and the guide notes that "myocardial infarction leading to death has been reported after use."

Watch for

(C) is contraindicated in renal or hepatic disease, active peptic ulcer disease, poorly controlled hypertension, asthma, and coagulation disorders — a different list entirely, and the one that catches an aspirin-sensitive woman rather than a diabetic one.

3. A woman receiving magnesium sulfate for tocolysis has an order added for nifedipine. Why should the nurse question this?
  • ✓Administering nifedipine and magnesium sulfate simultaneously can cause skeletal muscle blockade
  • BThe combination causes fetal ductal constriction
  • CNifedipine inactivates magnesium sulfate
  • DThe combination has no interaction and may be given together
Rationale

The Safety Alert is explicit: "Administering nifedipine and magnesium sulfate simultaneously can cause skeletal muscle blockade. In addition, nifedipine should not be given along with or immediately following a beta2-adrenergic agonist (e.g., terbutaline) because of effects on maternal heart rate and blood pressure."

Watch for

(B) names indomethacin's fetal effect and attaches it to the wrong drug pair. Ductal constriction is a prostaglandin-inhibitor problem, not a calcium channel blocker one.

4. A woman is discharged after an episode of preterm labor with a prescription for a terbutaline pump for maintenance tocolysis. What does the chapter say about this plan?
  • AIt is the standard of care for maintenance tocolysis after 34 weeks
  • ✓Long-term oral or subcutaneous administration as maintenance therapy has not been proven effective and terbutaline should not be used for this purpose
  • CIt is acceptable if the dose does not exceed 0.25 mg every 4 hours
  • DIt is acceptable only with concurrent corticosteroids
Rationale

"Long-term oral or subcutaneous administration (e.g., terbutaline pump) as maintenance therapy to suppress preterm labor has not been proven to be effective in reducing prematurity or neonatal morbidity, so terbutaline should not be used for this purpose." It also "has the potential to cause serious maternal cardiac problems and death."

Watch for

(C) supplies the correct acute dose — 0.25 mg subcutaneously every 4 hours, for no longer than 24 hours — and uses it to justify the wrong indication. The dose is right and the duration is what makes the plan wrong.

magnesium sulfate tocolysis monitoringthe stop-the-infusion findings
5. A woman receiving magnesium sulfate for tocolysis has a respiratory rate of 10 breaths/min and absent deep tendon reflexes. What do these findings represent?
  • AExpected adverse effects that subside once the loading dose is complete
  • ✓Intolerable adverse effects, meaning the infusion must be stopped
  • CFindings that require only an increase in the infusion rate
  • DSigns of hypocalcemia requiring additional magnesium
Rationale

The medication guide lists among intolerable adverse effects a respiratory rate under 12 breaths/min, pulmonary edema, absent DTRs, chest pain, severe hypotension, altered level of consciousness, extreme muscle weakness, urine output under 25 to 30 mL/h or under 100 mL in 4 hours, and a serum magnesium of 10 mEq/L or greater.

Watch for

(A) applies a real note from the same guide — "some reactions may subside when loading dose is completed" — to findings that are on the intolerable list. Hot flushes and nausea may subside; absent reflexes stop the drug.

magnesium sulfate tocolysis monitoringthe stop-the-infusion findings
6. Which assessments does the Safety Alert direct the nurse to perform frequently during magnesium sulfate administration, and why?
  • ✓Respiratory status, deep tendon reflexes, and level of consciousness, because magnesium sulfate depresses central nervous system function
  • BBlood glucose and potassium, because magnesium causes hyperglycemia and hypokalemia
  • CAmniotic fluid volume and fetal ductus arteriosus function
  • DMaternal heart rate and blood pressure only
Rationale

"Because magnesium sulfate depresses the function of the central nervous system, the nurse must frequently assess the woman's respiratory status, deep tendon reflexes, and level of consciousness to identify signs that the serum level of magnesium sulfate is reaching toxic levels."

Watch for

(B) belongs to terbutaline, whose guide directs assessment of glucose and potassium before treatment, and (C) belongs to indomethacin. Each of the four tocolytics has its own monitoring set, and they are not interchangeable.

  • AWithin the therapeutic range of 5 to 8 mg/dL, with calcium gluconate available to reverse toxicity
  • ✓Above the therapeutic range of 5 to 8 mg/dL, with calcium gluconate available to reverse toxicity
  • CBelow the therapeutic range, requiring a second loading dose

Rationale

The guide gives the therapeutic range as "4–7.5 mEq/L or 5–8 mg/dL" and directs the nurse to "ensure that calcium gluconate is available for emergency administration to reverse magnesium sulfate toxicity."

magnesium sulfate tocolysis monitoringdosing and the other magnesium indications
8. Magnesium sulfate is ordered for a woman at 28 weeks whose preterm birth now appears inevitable within 24 hours. What is the purpose of this order?
  • ATocolysis, to stop the labor
  • ✓Neuroprotection, to reduce or prevent neonatal neurologic morbidity such as cerebral palsy
  • CSeizure prophylaxis for preeclampsia
  • DPromotion of fetal lung maturity
Rationale

"When preterm birth appears inevitable (i.e., is expected to occur within the next 24 hours), magnesium sulfate may be administered to reduce or prevent neonatal neurologic morbidity (e.g., cerebral palsy)." It is given at 24 to less than 32 weeks and "should not be continued longer than 24 hours if birth has not occurred."

Watch for

(A) is the same drug at overlapping doses for a different purpose, which is what makes this a real bedside confusion. Tocolysis tries to stop labor; neuroprotection assumes labor will not be stopped.

10. Which limits does the chapter place on indomethacin tocolysis?
  • ✓Use only if gestational age is under 32 weeks, administer for 48 hours or less, and check amniotic fluid volume and ductus arteriosus function before starting and within 48 hours of stopping
  • BUse only after 34 weeks and continue until birth
  • CUse at any gestational age for up to 7 days
  • DUse only in women with asthma, since it is a prostaglandin inhibitor
Rationale

The guide directs: "Use only if gestational age is <32 weeks," "administer for 48 h or less," and "determine amniotic fluid volume and function of fetal ductus arteriosus before initiating therapy and within 48 h of discontinuing therapy." The chapter adds that therapy should be limited "to no more than 2 to 3 days."

Watch for

(D) inverts a contraindication into an indication. Indomethacin is not given in asthma, and specifically not to aspirin-sensitive clients. The guide also warns that the drug "can mask maternal fever," which matters when chorioamnionitis is in the differential.

11. A woman receiving nifedipine for tocolysis wants to get up to the bathroom. Which instruction does the Safety Alert support?
  • ARise quickly to minimize the time spent standing
  • ✓Change position slowly from supine to upright, then sit until any dizziness disappears before standing, and maintain adequate fluid balance
  • CRemain on bed rest for the duration of therapy
  • DTake the next dose sublingually before ambulating
Rationale

"Because using a calcium channel blocker can result in orthostatic hypotension and dizziness, women must be instructed to change position slowly from supine to upright and then sit until any dizziness disappears before standing. In addition, maintaining adequate fluid balance is important to reduce the drop in blood pressure that can occur with the drug-related vasodilation."

Watch for

(D) adds a route the guide forbids outright: "do not use sublingual route of administration." Nifedipine's contraindications are intrauterine infection, hypertension, and cardiac disease.

14. How long after the first injection does the fetus receive optimal benefit, and why does that timing matter?

obesity in labor and birthclassification and risk
16. A woman's prepregnancy BMI is 41. How is this classified, and which labor and birth risks does the chapter associate with it?
  • AObese; an increased risk for cesarean birth but not for emergency cesarean birth
  • ✓Severely obese; increased risks for induction, longer labor, cesarean birth in general, and emergency cesarean birth
  • COverweight; an increased risk for postterm pregnancy only
  • DSeverely obese; a decreased risk for postpartum hemorrhage because of larger blood volume
Rationale

"People with a BMI of 25 or greater are categorized as overweight, people with a BMI of 30 or greater are considered obese, and people with a BMI of 40 or greater are classified as severely obese." The chapter lists an increased incidence of postterm pregnancy and longer labor, a greater likelihood that labor will need to be induced, and increased risks for cesarean birth in general and emergency cesarean birth.

(D) reverses the hemorrhage risk, which the chapter states in the other direction and explains mechanically in the next item.

obesity in labor and birthclassification and risk
17. Why does the chapter give obese women a higher risk for postpartum hemorrhage?
  • ✓Because of an increased likelihood of induction, prolonged labor, birth of a macrosomic infant, and caregiver difficulty locating the uterine fundus to provide effective fundal massage
  • BBecause adipose tissue produces anticoagulants
  • CBecause oxytocin is less effective in obese women
  • DBecause obese women are more likely to refuse uterotonic medication
Rationale

The chapter gives a causal chain rather than a single factor: the higher risk follows from "an increased likelihood of induction, resulting in prolonged labor, birth of a macrosomic infant, and caregiver difficulty with locating the uterine fundus to provide effective fundal massage."

Watch for

(B) invents a mechanism. The mechanical item at the end of the chain — difficulty finding the fundus to massage it — is the one most often missed and the one a nurse can act on.

obesity in labor and birthcare management
18. What does the chapter identify as the central safety problem in caring for an obese woman in labor?
  • ✓That routine procedures require more time and effort, which can slow essential interventions such as an emergency cesarean birth
  • BThat obese women cannot receive regional anesthesia
  • CThat external fetal monitoring is always impossible
  • DThat obese women should be delivered by scheduled cesarean birth
Rationale

"Even routine procedures require more time and effort.... This can slow essential interventions and increase risks to the mother and fetus, such as when an emergency cesarean birth is needed." Concrete examples include repeated IV attempts sometimes by multiple people, difficult transfers especially once regional anesthesia is in effect, and delays from difficulty establishing an epidural or spinal block or accomplishing intubation.

Watch for

(C) overstates a real difficulty: continuous external FHR and contraction monitoring "can be extremely difficult if not impossible to perform," which is not the same as always impossible.

bishop score and cervical ripenessreading the score
24. A woman's Bishop score is 9. What does this indicate about her induction?
  • AThat the cervix is unfavorable and ripening is required first
  • ✓That the likelihood of vaginal birth is similar whether labor is spontaneous or induced
  • CThat induction is contraindicated
  • DThat the maximum possible score has been reached
Rationale

"A score of 8 or more on this 13-point scale indicates that the cervix is soft, anterior, 50% or more effaced, and dilated 2 cm or more and that the presenting part is engaged. When the Bishop score totals 8 or more, the likelihood of vaginal birth is similar whether labor is spontaneous or induced."

Watch for

(D) assumes 9 is at the top. The maximum is 13, because cervical consistency and cervical position max out at 2 while dilation, effacement, and station each reach 3.

32. A balloon catheter is inserted for cervical ripening. How does it work, and what should the nurse expect?
  • ✓The balloon is inflated above the internal os with 30 to 50 mL of sterile water, producing pressure, stretching, and release of endogenous prostaglandins, and it usually falls out within 8 to 12 hours at about 3 cm of dilation
  • BThe balloon is inflated below the external os with 10 mL of saline and is removed after 2 hours
  • CThe balloon delivers prostaglandin gel directly into the cervical canal
  • DThe balloon must be removed before the woman ambulates
Rationale

The catheter balloon "is inflated above the internal cervical os with 30 to 50 mL of sterile water," producing "pressure and stretching of the lower uterine segment and the cervix as well as the release of endogenous prostaglandins," and "usually falls out within 8 to 12 hours, when cervical dilation reaches approximately 3 cm." It is especially helpful for women who cannot receive exogenous prostaglandins and carries a reduced risk of tachysystole.

Watch for

(C) confuses the mechanical device with a chemical one. The balloon releases the woman's own prostaglandins; nothing is instilled through it.
