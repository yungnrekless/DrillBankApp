family planning terminology and framinggeneral
1. A patient tells the nurse that she and her partner have decided they want to wait four years before trying to conceive, and are weighing what to do in the meantime. Which term names the decision she has just described?
  • ✓Family planning
  • BContraception
  • CBirth control
  • DFertility awareness
Rationale

Family planning is "the conscious decision on when to conceive or to avoid pregnancy throughout the reproductive years" (p. 143). The patient has described a decision about timing across her reproductive years, not a method.

Watch for

(C) is the reflex pick because the three terms are used interchangeably in ordinary speech. The fact bank gives the cue that separates them: if the option describes a choice or a plan it is family planning, and if it describes a thing you use it is birth control.

2. The same patient then asks the nurse to explain what the phrase "birth control" specifically refers to. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓The device or practice used to decrease the risk of conceiving or bearing offspring
  • BThe conscious decision on when to conceive or to avoid pregnancy
  • CThe intentional prevention of pregnancy during sexual intercourse
  • DThe percentage of users expected to conceive during the first year of use
Rationale

Birth control is "the device and/or practice used to decrease the risk of conceiving or bearing offspring" (p. 143). It names the thing used, not the decision behind it or the outcome sought.

Watch for

(B) is the mirror error of the previous item, handing back the decision when the device was asked for. (D) defines the contraceptive failure rate, which is a separate term from the same page cluster.

family planning terminology and framinggeneral
3. A nursing student is asked which term names the intentional prevention of pregnancy during sexual intercourse. Which term is it?
  • ✓Contraception
  • BFamily planning
  • CBirth control
  • DTherapeutic abortion
Rationale

Contraception is "the intentional prevention of pregnancy during sexual intercourse" (p. 143). The three terms are a graded set rather than synonyms: family planning names the decision, contraception the intent and outcome, and birth control the device or practice.

Watch for

This is the third angle on the same trio, and it is deliberate. A learner who has the set backwards will get one of the three right by elimination; asking from all three ends is what exposes it.

4. A nurse is preparing a community education session and wants to open with the scale of the problem. Which statement reflects the chapter's framing?
  • ✓Nearly half of all US pregnancies are not planned, and Healthy People 2030 aims to reduce unintended pregnancies from 43.0% to 36.5%
  • BNearly one in ten US pregnancies is unplanned, and the rate has been rising steadily
  • CUnintended pregnancy is now rare in the US, which is why modern contraception is called a public health achievement
  • DHealthy People 2030 aims to eliminate unintended pregnancy entirely by increasing access to sterilization
Rationale

Modern contraception "is considered one of the 10 greatest public health achievements of the 20th century," and "nevertheless, nearly half of all US pregnancies are not planned." The Healthy People 2030 goal is to "decrease the percentage of unintended pregnancies from 43.0% to 36.5% by increasing the use of effective birth control" (p. 143).

Watch for

(C) is the seductive misreading of the achievement sentence — the chapter names contraception an achievement and then immediately says nearly half of pregnancies are still unplanned. The two statements sit next to each other precisely because the second qualifies the first.

5. A nurse is preparing to counsel a patient about contraceptive options. Which arrangement of the visit best reflects the chapter's guidance?
  • ✓A private setting with distractions minimized and samples of birth control devices available for interactive teaching
  • BA group session in the waiting area, so that patients can learn from one another's questions
  • CThe examination room during the pelvic examination, so that anatomy can be pointed out directly
  • DA telephone call after discharge, so that the patient can consult her partner first
Rationale

Contraceptive counseling is "best conducted in a private setting. The woman should feel safe and free to communicate. Distractions are minimized, and samples of birth control devices for interactive teaching are available" (pp. 143–144).

Watch for

(C) sounds efficient and clinically grounded, which is what makes it attractive. A patient in the lithotomy position mid-examination is not in a setting where she is free to communicate, which is the condition the source actually specifies.

6. A patient has chosen a diaphragm. After explaining how it is used, what should the nurse do next?
  • ✓Ask her to perform a return demonstration and assess her understanding
  • BDocument that teaching was completed and schedule her annual visit
  • CProvide written instructions and end the session, since repetition can overwhelm
  • DRefer her to the physician to confirm that she has understood
Rationale

"Education is the cornerstone of the plan of care." After the method is taught, the nurse asks "the woman to perform a return demonstration of how a device is used, if appropriate, and assess[es] her understanding," then provides written instructions, a list of reliable websites, and contact information (pp. 143–144).

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick for anyone who treats documentation as the closing step of teaching. Documenting that teaching occurred is not the same as verifying that it worked, and for a device method the source names the return demonstration specifically.

7. A patient is leaving with her first prescription for combined oral contraceptives. The chapter singles out one piece of information as very important to provide. Which is it?
  • ✓Information about backup methods of birth control and emergency contraception
  • BThe theoretic effectiveness rate of the method to the nearest tenth of a percent
  • CA schedule of the long-term cancer risks associated with hormonal methods
  • DInstructions to discontinue the method if any side effect occurs
Rationale

"It is very important to provide the woman and her partner with information about backup methods of birth control and emergency contraception (EC)" (pp. 143–144). This pairs with the chapter's Safety Alert, which is that a woman using a new method needs a backup method readily available during the initial learning phase.

Watch for

(D) is the dangerous distractor because it sounds cautious. Blanket discontinuation on any side effect is not what the chapter teaches; the specific stop-and-report list is the warning-signs mnemonic, and stopping for a minor effect without a backup is how an unintended pregnancy happens.

contraceptive counseling and assessmentgeneral
8. While assessing a patient who is considering a fertility-awareness-based method or a diaphragm, the nurse includes an appraisal that is not part of a routine history. Which assessment does the chapter name?
  • ✓The woman's level of comfort and willingness to touch her genitals and cervical mucus
  • BThe woman's household income and insurance status
  • CThe partner's contraceptive history with previous partners
  • DThe woman's willingness to undergo laboratory testing for hormone levels
Rationale

Assessment "begins with the following appraisals," among them "assessing the woman's level of comfort and willingness to touch her genitals and cervical mucus," alongside her knowledge about contraception, her partner's commitment, frequency of coitus, number of partners, objections to any methods, misconceptions, religious and cultural factors, and her reproductive life plan (pp. 143–144).

Watch for

(B) and (C) are plausible-sounding intake items that the source does not name, and the second is not the nurse's to collect from this patient. The appraisal that distinguishes this assessment is the one that decides whether a method requiring self-touch is workable at all.

9. A nurse counseling a patient about an intrauterine device explains the advantages of the method and its success rates. Which element of the BRAIDED framework has the nurse addressed?
  • ✓Benefits
  • BRisks
  • CExplanations
  • DAlternatives
Rationale

In BRAIDED, B is Benefits — "information about advantages and success rates" — while R is Risks, "information about disadvantages and failure rates" (p. 144).

Watch for

(C) is the reflex pick for anyone who reads any act of explaining as the Explanations element. Explanations is narrower than that: it is information about the method and how it is used. Success rates belong to Benefits, and failure rates to Risks.

informed consent and the braided frameworkgeneral
10. A nurse documents that a patient was told she could take more time and could change her mind about sterilization at any point before the procedure. Which BRAIDED element does this satisfy, and which one does it not?
  • ✓It satisfies Decisions; it does not satisfy Documentation, which requires recording the information given and her understanding of it
  • BIt satisfies Documentation; Decisions refers to the provider's decision about candidacy
  • CIt satisfies both, since recording the conversation is what Decisions means
  • DIt satisfies neither, since BRAIDED does not apply to sterilization
Rationale

BRAIDED contains two different D elements: Decisions is the "opportunity to decide or to change her mind," and Documentation is "information given and client's understanding" (p. 144). The nurse "often has the responsibility to document information provided and the understanding of that information by the client."

Watch for

(C) collapses the two D elements into one, which is the standard corruption of this mnemonic. Giving a patient room to change her mind and recording what she understood are separate obligations, and a chart entry can satisfy one without touching the other.

11. A nursing student lists the BRAIDED elements as Benefits, Risks, Alternatives, Inquiries, Decisions, Effectiveness, and Documentation. Which correction is needed?
  • ✓The E is Explanations, information about the method and how it is used; effectiveness is covered under Benefits and Risks
  • BThe E is Effectiveness, and the student is correct
  • CThe E is Evaluation, meaning assessment of the client's understanding after teaching
  • DThe E is Emergency contraception, which must be offered with every method
Rationale

E in BRAIDED stands for Explanations — "information about method and how it is used" (p. 144). Success rates sit under Benefits and failure rates under Risks, so effectiveness is already accounted for elsewhere in the mnemonic.

Watch for

(B) is the error the fact bank names, and it is attractive because effectiveness genuinely is something a patient must be told. The point is that it is told twice already, under two other letters, which is why the E is free to mean something else.

12. A patient is scheduled for a tubal ligation. Which statement about informed consent for this procedure is accurate?
  • ✓The BRAIDED framework applies, since the source names informed consent as vital in educating a client about contraception or sterilization
  • BBRAIDED applies only to reversible methods, so a separate surgical consent framework is used
  • CBRAIDED applies, but the Alternatives element is omitted for permanent methods
  • DInformed consent for sterilization is the surgeon's responsibility alone, and the nurse has no documentation role
Rationale

"Informed consent is a vital component in educating a client about contraception or sterilization," and the nurse "often has the responsibility to document information provided and the understanding of that information by the client" (p. 144).

Watch for

(C) is the subtle version of the error and it inverts the priority. Alternatives matter most, not least, when the method under discussion is intended to be permanent — a patient consenting to sterilization is precisely the one who needs to hear what the reversible options are.

contraceptive effectiveness and failure ratesgeneral
14. A package insert reports how effective a method is when it is used exactly as directed every time. Which term names this figure?
  • ✓Theoretic effectiveness
  • BTypical effectiveness
  • CThe contraceptive failure rate
  • DThe method's safety profile
Rationale

Theoretic effectiveness is "how effective the method is with perfect use," and typical effectiveness is "how effective the method is with typical use" (p. 144).

Watch for

(C) is the pick for anyone who treats every effectiveness number as a failure rate. The failure rate is a distinct measure with a defined denominator and a one-year window, not a restatement of effectiveness under ideal conditions.

15. A counselor wants to quote the number that reflects how the method performs in the hands of real users, including inconsistent ones. Which term names this figure?
  • ✓Typical effectiveness
  • BTheoretic effectiveness
  • CPerfect-use effectiveness
  • DThe ideal contraceptive standard
Rationale

Typical effectiveness is "how effective the method is with typical use," as against theoretic effectiveness, which is perfect use (p. 144).

(B) is the mirror error of the previous item, and (C) is the same error wearing the correct term's own definition as its name — perfect use is the theoretic figure, so an option calling it perfect-use effectiveness is naming theoretic effectiveness in different words.

contraceptive effectiveness and failure ratesgeneral
16. A patient asks what the "7% failure rate" printed in her pill pamphlet actually counts. Which explanation is accurate?
  • ✓The percentage of users expected to have an unplanned pregnancy during the first year of use, even when they use the method consistently and correctly
  • BThe percentage of users who will misuse the method at some point during the first year
  • CThe percentage of pills in a pack that fail to suppress ovulation
  • DThe lifetime percentage of users who will eventually conceive while using the method
Rationale

The contraceptive failure rate is "the percentage of contraceptive users expected to have an unplanned pregnancy during the first year of use, even when they use a method consistently and correctly" (p. 144).

Watch for

(B) inverts the definition in the way the fact bank flags: the failure rate is not a misuse rate. The "even when they use a method consistently and correctly" clause is the whole point — these are pregnancies that occur despite correct use, which is why no method is quoted at zero.

17. A nurse notes that a method's failure rate is lower in the second year of use than in the first, and a student asks why. Which explanation does the chapter give?
  • ✓Users gain experience and use the method more appropriately, and less effective users stop using the method
  • BThe method becomes more potent with continued exposure
  • CFertility declines measurably over a single year of contraceptive use
  • DSecond-year figures exclude any user who has become pregnant
Rationale

"Failure rates decrease over time, either because a user gains experience and uses a method more appropriately or because less effective users stop using the method" (p. 144).

Watch for

(B) is the error the fact bank names — attributing the decline to the method rather than to the population using it. Both stated reasons are about users: one improves, and the other leaves the denominator.

18. A nurse is explaining why two patients using the same method may have different real-world results. Select all the factors the chapter names as affecting contraceptive method effectiveness.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Frequency of intercourse
  • ✓Motivation to prevent pregnancy
  • ✓Understanding of how to use the method
  • ✓Consistent use of the method
  • EThe manufacturer's brand of the method chosen
Rationale

Box 8.1 lists frequency of intercourse, motivation to prevent pregnancy, understanding of how to use the method, adherence to correct use, provision of short- or long-term protection, likelihood of pregnancy for the individual woman, and consistent use of the method (p. 144). contraceptive safety and risk weighinggeneral
19. A patient with well-controlled type 1 diabetes asks whether contraception is simply too risky for someone with a chronic illness. Which response reflects the chapter's position?
  • ✓In most instances pregnancy would be more dangerous for a woman with medical problems than a particular contraceptive method would be
  • BWomen with any chronic medical condition should use only barrier methods
  • CContraceptive risk always exceeds pregnancy risk when a chronic illness is present
  • DContraceptive safety is unaffected by medical history, so her condition is not relevant
Rationale

Safety "may be affected by a woman's medical history (e.g., thromboembolic problems and contraceptive methods containing estrogen)," but "in most instances, pregnancy would be more dangerous for a woman with medical problems than a particular contraceptive method" (p. 144).

Watch for

(D) overcorrects a true reassurance into a false one — medical history does matter, and the chapter names the thromboembolic-plus-estrogen combination as its example. The answer is that risk is weighed against the risk of pregnancy, not that risk is absent.

  • ✓Barrier methods such as the male condom offer some protection from acquiring sexually transmitted infections, and oral contraceptives lower the incidence of ovarian and endometrial cancer
  • BOral contraceptives offer protection from sexually transmitted infections, and condoms lower cancer risk
  • CNo contraceptive method has effects beyond preventing pregnancy

"Barrier methods, such as the male condom, offer some protection from acquiring STIs, and oral contraceptives lower the incidence of ovarian and endometrial cancer" (p. 144).

(B) swaps the two benefits between the two method classes, which is the version most likely to be chosen quickly. (D) overstates a real benefit by extending it to every reproductive cancer, when the source names exactly two.

21. A patient is starting a new contraceptive method today. Which teaching point should the nurse prioritize?
  • ✓She needs a backup method of birth control readily available during the initial learning phase
  • BShe should watch for long-term metabolic side effects over the coming year
  • CShe should return in twelve months to reassess the method's typical effectiveness
  • DShe should discontinue the method if she experiences any breakthrough bleeding
Rationale

The chapter's Safety Alert states that when a woman is using a new method, "it is important that she understands the need to have a backup method of birth control readily available during the initial learning phase" (p. 144).

(B) and (C) are plausible-but-adjacent, and the fact bank names exactly this pattern: on a "what does the nurse teach first" item about a newly prescribed method, the backup-method answer beats options about long-term effects. The risk that a new method addresses is immediate, and the learning phase is when it is highest.

22. A patient asks the nurse to describe what an ideal contraceptive would look like. Which response reflects the chapter's list?
  • ✓Effective, safe, easily available, economical, acceptable, simple to use, and promptly reversible, although no method may ever have all of these characteristics
  • BEffective and permanently reversible, with all other characteristics being matters of preference
  • CEffective and inexpensive, since those two characteristics determine adherence
  • DEffective and free of side effects, a standard several current methods already meet
Rationale

The ideal contraceptive "should be effective, safe, easily available, economical, acceptable, simple to use, and promptly reversible. Although no method may ever have all of these characteristics..." (p. 144).

(D) is attractive because it flatters current practice, and the source says the opposite in the same sentence that lists the criteria — the list is explicitly framed as a standard no method fully meets, which is why method choice is a matter of trade-offs.

oral contraceptive warning signs and contraindicationsgeneral
23. A patient taking combined oral contraceptives is taught the ACHES warning signs. Select all the symptoms for which she should stop taking the pill and report immediately.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Abdominal pain
  • ✓Chest pain or shortness of breath
  • ✓Sudden or persistent headaches
  • ✓Severe leg pain
  • EMild nausea during the first cycle of use
Rationale

The instruction is to "stop taking the pill and to report" any ACHES symptom "immediately": abdominal pain, suggesting a liver or gallbladder problem; chest pain or shortness of breath, suggesting a clot in the lungs or heart; headaches, suggesting cerebrovascular accident or hypertension; eye problems, with the same two explanations; and severe leg pain, suggesting a thromboembolic process (pp. 155–156).

Watch for

(E) is the single wrong option and it is the one a cautious learner adds anyway. The mnemonic exists to separate the symptoms that mean stop the pill now from the ordinary early side effects that do not, and treating every symptom as an emergency defeats the sorting the list was written to do.

24. A patient taking combined oral contraceptives calls to report new visual disturbances. Which explanation should the nurse associate with this symptom?
  • ✓A vascular accident or hypertension
  • BA liver or gallbladder problem
  • CA thromboembolic process in the leg
  • DA clot within the lungs or heart
Rationale

In ACHES, eye problems indicate a "vascular accident or hypertension," the same two explanations given for headaches. Abdominal pain points to the liver or gallbladder, chest pain or shortness of breath to a clot in the lungs or heart, and severe leg pain to a thromboembolic process (pp. 155–156).

Watch for

(B) is the error the fact bank names — pairing eye problems with gallbladder is drawing the explanation from the wrong letter. The structural cue worth holding is that headaches and eye problems share their two explanations, which is why they can be learned as a pair.

25. Four patients request combined oral contraceptives. Which one meets a contraindication exactly as the chapter states it?
  • ✓A 36-year-old who smokes one pack per day
  • BA 30-year-old who smokes one pack per day
  • CA 38-year-old with migraine headaches without aura
  • DA 25-year-old with irritable bowel syndrome in remission
Rationale

Box 8.3 lists "smoking 15 or more cigarettes per day at age 35 or older" as a contraindication, along with a history of thromboembolic disorders, cerebrovascular or cardiovascular disease, and migraine with aura (p. 155).

(B) and (C) each satisfy one half of a two-part criterion, which is why both are attractive. The smoking contraindication requires both the quantity and the age, so a 30-year-old smoking the same amount does not meet it; and it is migraine with aura that is contraindicated, not migraine generally.

26. A patient taking combined oral contraceptives reports that she recently started an herbal supplement and has had breakthrough bleeding. Which supplement does the chapter name as able to alter the effectiveness of these pills?
  • ✓St. John's wort
  • BEchinacea
  • CGinkgo biloba
  • DSaw palmetto
Rationale

The Medication Alert states that "over-the-counter medications as well as some herbal supplements (such as St. John's wort) can alter the effectiveness of COCs," and women should be asked about their use when combined oral contraceptives are prescribed (p. 155).

The other three are real supplements with real interactions elsewhere, which makes them plausible, but St. John's wort is the one this chapter names. induced abortion definitions and lawgeneral
27. A nursing student is asked what makes a procedure a legal induced abortion under the CDC definition the chapter quotes. Select all the required elements.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓It is performed by a licensed clinician
  • ✓It is performed within the limits of state regulations
  • ✓It is intended to terminate a suspected or known ongoing intrauterine pregnancy
  • ✓It does not result in a live birth
  • EIt is performed before 12 weeks of gestation
Rationale

The CDC defines legal induced abortion as "an intervention performed by a licensed clinician (e.g., a physician, certified nurse-midwife, nurse practitioner, physician assistant) within the limits of state regulations that is intended to terminate a suspected or known ongoing intrauterine pregnancy and that does not result in a live birth" (p. 161).

Watch for

(E) is the single wrong option and it imports a gestational limit the definition does not contain. It is attractive because roughly 90% of abortions are in fact first-trimester, so a true statistic gets mistaken for a defining criterion. The word "intrauterine" is the element most often overlooked, and it is what separates this from an ectopic pregnancy.

30. A patient asks the nurse what the legal status of abortion is now. Which response reflects the chapter?
  • ✓The 2022 Dobbs vs. Jackson Women's Health decision overturned Roe vs. Wade, so each state can determine its own abortion legislation
  • BThe 2022 Dobbs decision made abortion illegal nationwide
  • CRoe vs. Wade, decided in 1973, remains the controlling federal precedent
  • DAbortion law has been determined by individual states continuously since before 1970
Rationale

"In January 1973, the US Supreme Court set aside previous antiabortion laws and legalized abortion as a result of the Roe vs. Wade decision." "In June 2022, the US Supreme Court overturned Roe vs. Wade by its decision in favor of Dobbs vs. Jackson Women's Health. As a result of this decision, each state can determine legislation regarding abortion," and about half the states have moved to outlaw it (p. 161).

Watch for

(B) overstates the holding in the direction most people assume, and the fact bank flags it: the decision returned the question to the states rather than banning anything nationally. The practical consequence for the nurse is the Legal Tip that follows — know the law in your own state of practice before offering abortion counseling or care.

32. A nurse holds a personal moral objection to abortion and is assigned a patient seeking one. What is the nurse's ethical responsibility as the chapter states it?
  • ✓To counsel the woman about her options and to make appropriate referrals
  • BTo decline the assignment and leave the unit until another nurse is available
  • CTo provide care while explaining the personal objection to the patient
  • DTo ask the patient to transfer to another facility
Rationale

"Nurses who provide care to women seeking abortion have an ethical responsibility to counsel women about their options and to make appropriate referrals" (pp. 161–163), regardless of personal views.

34. A patient who has just learned she is pregnant is undecided about what to do. Which nursing approach reflects the chapter's guidance?
  • ✓A calm, matter-of-fact approach that offers information about all options, including abortion, referral to adoption agencies, and support services if she chooses to keep her baby
  • BPresenting abortion as the option most consistent with her stated circumstances
  • CDeferring all discussion until she has decided, to avoid influencing her
  • DProviding information about adoption and parenting only, unless she raises abortion herself
Rationale

"A calm, matter-of-fact approach on the part of the nurse can be helpful," using clarifying, restating, and reflecting statements, open-ended questions, and feedback. "Information about all options, including abortion, referral to adoption agencies, or to support services if a woman chooses to keep her baby, should be provided," and "the decision to have an abortion should be the woman's, based on her own values and situation" (pp. 161–163).

(D) is the version that feels respectful and is not — withholding one option until the patient names it is not neutrality, because a patient cannot ask about what she has not been told exists. (C) makes the same error by deferring the information she needs in order to decide.

  • BDepression following abortion is well established and affects most patients within the first year

  • DAny distress after the procedure indicates that the decision was made in error
Rationale

"Psychologic sequelae of induced abortion are rare with no evidence of a correlation between abortion and mental health issues," and afterward "most people report relief, but some have temporary distress or mixed emotions" (pp. 161–163).

Watch for

(B) contradicts the source directly, and the fact bank names it as the trap in this section. (D) is the more damaging error clinically: it converts the temporary distress the source explicitly describes as possible into evidence of a wrong decision, which is neither accurate nor supportive.

(C) inverts the ideal window, turning a ceiling into a floor. (D) borrows dilation and evacuation's defining statistic, which is the swap this section invites because both procedures share the same basic technique.

39. The second drug in the same regimen works by binding to progesterone receptors and blocking the action of progesterone. Which drug is this, and how is the regimen sequenced?
  • ✓Mifepristone, formerly known as RU 486, given orally first, followed by buccal or vaginal misoprostol
  • BMisoprostol, formerly known as RU 486, given first, followed by oral mifepristone
  • CMifepristone, given after misoprostol has softened the cervix
  • DMethotrexate, given as a single agent without a second drug
Rationale

Mifepristone, "formerly known as RU 486, works by binding to progesterone receptors and blocking the action of progesterone, which is necessary for maintaining pregnancy." The FDA-approved regimen is "mifepristone orally, followed by buccal (in the pouch of the cheek) or vaginal misoprostol," and the combination is 95% effective in resulting in a complete abortion over a 24- to 72-hour period (p. 162).

Watch for

(C) is the mirror error of the previous item, and it is attractive because softening the cervix first sounds like the logical order. The sequence runs the other way: the progesterone blockade comes first, and the prostaglandin follows. Note also that the side effects of nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, headache, dizziness, fever, and chills are attributed to misoprostol, not to mifepristone.

40. A patient at 15 weeks of gestation is scheduled for a dilation and evacuation. Which statements about this procedure are accurate?
  • ✓It accounts for almost all second-trimester abortions, requires more cervical dilation because the products of conception are larger, and is preceded by osmotic dilators or misoprostol applied to the cervix
  • BIt is performed most commonly between 17 and 20 weeks and requires no cervical preparation
  • CIt carries fewer complications and lower costs than a first-trimester procedure
  • DIt uses a smaller cannula than aspiration because the cervix is already dilated
Rationale

"D&E accounts for almost all second-trimester abortions performed in the United States," is most commonly performed between 13 and 16 weeks, and "the cervix requires more dilation because the products of conception are larger." Osmotic dilators such as laminaria "are inserted several hours or several days before the procedure, or misoprostol can be applied to the cervix," and a larger cannula is used. An ultrasound examination should be performed before a second-trimester abortion is done (p. 162).

Watch for

(C) inverts the risk gradient, and it is the inversion that matters for counseling: second-trimester abortion is associated with more complications and higher costs, which is the reason timing is discussed at all. (D) reverses the cannula size, which follows from the same fact that drives the extra dilation.

Select all that apply · scored all or nothing

43. A patient returns 4 days after an aspiration abortion with prolonged heavy vaginal bleeding. What is the most likely cause, and what management should the nurse anticipate?
  • ✓Retained products of conception, managed with evacuation of the uterus, uterine massage, and administration of oxytocin or methylergonovine or both
  • BNormal postprocedure bleeding, managed with reassurance and iron supplementation
  • CEndometritis, managed with prophylactic antibiotics alone
  • DA coagulation disorder, managed with transfusion and hematology referral
Rationale

"Excessive vaginal bleeding and infection, such as endometritis or salpingitis, are the most common complications of elective abortion. Management may require "evacuation of the uterus, uterine massage, and administration of oxytocin or methylergonovine (Methergine) or both" (pp. 161–162).

Watch for

(C) names the other common complication and attaches it to the wrong presentation — infection is common, but retained tissue is what the source names as the primary cause of this specific finding. The reasoning chain the fact bank draws is heavy bleeding to retained tissue to evacuation, not simply observe and reassure.

44. A patient who is D negative with negative Coombs test results has just undergone an induced abortion. Which intervention is indicated?
  • ✓Administer Rh D immune globulin within 72 hours after the abortion
  • BAdminister Rh D immune globulin only if she has had a previous pregnancy
  • CNo prophylaxis is needed, since isoimmunization occurs only after a term birth
  • DAdminister Rh D immune globulin within 2 weeks, once Coombs results are repeated
Rationale

"If the woman is Rh negative, she is a candidate for prophylaxis against Rh isoimmunization. (D) misses the window, which is the error with real consequences — prophylaxis given late does not prevent sensitization. "All methods of contraception may be started immediately after surgical abortion." After medical abortion, oral and other hormonal methods are started "the day of the first pill," while "insertion of an IUD and tubal ligation may occur once it is known the woman is no longer pregnant" (p. 162).
