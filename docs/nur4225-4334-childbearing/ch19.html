stages of labor frameworkthe four stages and their boundaries
1. A nurse documents that a woman has entered the second stage of labor. Which finding justifies that entry?
  • AThe cervix is 6 cm dilated and contractions are 2 to 3 minutes apart
  • ✓The cervix is fully dilated at 10 cm with complete effacement
  • CThe woman reports an irresistible urge to push
  • DThe fetal head is visible at the perineum
Rationale

The second stage "begins with full cervical dilation (10 cm) and complete effacement (100%) and ends with the baby's birth." The chapter is explicit that full dilation is "the only certain objective sign that the second stage of labor has begun."

Watch for

(C) is the reflex pick because the urge to push is the most visible marker of the second stage, and Box 19.10 does list it among the signs that suggest onset. Those signs "can appear earlier in labor," and women labouring without regional anesthesia can feel an irresistible urge as early as 5 cm, most often because the presenting part has descended below the ischial spines.

2. Which sequence correctly describes where the third and fourth stages begin and end?
  • AThird stage begins with full dilation and ends with the birth; fourth stage begins with the birth
  • ✓Third stage begins with the birth of the baby and ends when the placenta is expelled; fourth stage begins with expulsion of the placenta
  • CThird stage begins when the placenta separates and ends when the fundus is firm; fourth stage begins one hour after birth
  • DThird stage begins with the birth and ends one hour later; fourth stage covers the next 24 hours
Rationale

"The third stage of labor lasts from the birth of the baby until the placenta is expelled. It is generally by far the shortest stage of labor." The fourth stage "begins with the expulsion of the placenta and lasts until the woman is stable in the immediate postpartum period, usually within the first hour after birth."

Watch for

(A) shifts every boundary one stage earlier, which is the most available error because the birth itself feels like the natural end of labor rather than a midpoint. The placenta, not the baby, ends the third stage.

3. A woman's placenta was delivered at 0340. At 0450 the nurse charts that she remains in the fourth stage. Which statement about this period is accurate?
  • AThe fourth stage ends at exactly one hour after birth regardless of maternal status
  • ✓The stage usually lasts within the first hour after birth, but the immediate postpartum recovery period usually includes at least the first 2 hours, based on maternal status
  • CThe fourth stage lasts 24 hours and ends with the first ambulation
  • DThe fourth stage has no defined endpoint
Rationale

The chapter gives two numbers that are easy to collapse into one. The stage itself lasts "usually within the first hour after birth," while "the immediate postpartum recovery period lasts longer. It usually includes at least the first 2 hours after birth, based on maternal status."

Watch for

(A) treats the hour as a hard boundary when the chapter attaches the recovery period to maternal status rather than the clock. The two-number structure is the testable part.

stages of labor frameworkthe two phases of the first stage
4. A nursing student describes a laboring woman as being "in transition at 9 cm." Which correction reflects the current edition?
  • ✓The first stage now comprises two phases, latent and active, and the transition phase is no longer used
  • BTransition now begins at 7 cm rather than 8 cm
  • CTransition is now considered part of the second stage
  • DThe description is correct as stated
Rationale

"Traditionally the first stage of labor was considered to comprise three phases: the latent phase (0 through 3 cm of dilation), the active phase (4 to 7 cm of dilation), and the transition phase (8 to 10 cm of dilation). However, these definitions have changed based on research." The contemporary division is latent and active only, with active beginning at 6 cm and ending at complete dilation.

Watch for

(B) and (D) both preserve a phase this edition retired, and the pull is strong because "transition" remains in common clinical speech and in older course materials. The chapter attributes the change to contemporary women being older and heavier, progressing more slowly in the active phase and experiencing longer labors than Friedman's half-century-old curves predicted.

5. A multiparous woman is 5 cm dilated. Her nurse tells the oncoming shift she is in active labor because multiparas enter the active phase earlier. Which correction applies?
  • AMultiparas enter the active phase at 4 cm; nulliparas enter at 6 cm
  • ✓Active labor begins at 6 cm for both nulliparous and multiparous women; only after 6 cm do multiparas progress more rapidly
  • CMultiparas enter active labor at 6 cm and nulliparas at 4 cm
  • DParity does not affect the rate of progress at any point
Rationale

"Active labor begins at 6 cm for both nulliparous and multiparous women." The parity effect appears only afterward: "During the early phase of the first stage of labor, nulliparous and multiparous women progress at similar rates. After reaching a cervical dilation of 6 cm, however, multiparous women progress more rapidly."

Watch for

(A) inverts where the parity difference lives, and it is the intuitive inversion because multiparous labors really are faster overall, so students assume the speed advantage starts at the beginning. It starts at 6 cm. (D) discards the parity effect entirely.

6. A woman at 39 weeks calls to report contractions. Which description most strongly suggests true labor?
  • ✓The contractions become more intense when she walks
  • BThe contractions stop when she changes position
  • CThe contractions are felt in the abdomen above her umbilicus
  • DA warm shower relieves the contractions
Rationale

The Teaching for Self-Management box states that true labor contractions "become more intense with walking or position change," while false labor contractions "often stop with walking or position change." What activity does to the contraction is one of the two cleanest discriminators the box offers.

Watch for

(B) and (D) are both false-labor findings, and (D) in particular pulls because relief from comfort measures reads as good news rather than as diagnostic information. True labor contractions "continue despite use of comfort measures."

7. A second woman calls with contractions that stop when she walks the hallway and are felt in her abdomen above the umbilicus. Which pattern is she describing?
  • ATrue labor, because the contractions are palpable and located anteriorly
  • ✓False labor, because the contractions stop with walking and are felt above the umbilicus
  • CTrue labor entering the latent phase
  • DCannot be determined without a contraction monitor
Rationale

False labor contractions "occur irregularly or become regular only temporarily," "often stop with walking or position change," "can be felt in the back or the abdomen above the umbilicus," and "can often be stopped through the use of comfort measures." True labor pain is "usually felt in the lower back, radiating to the lower portion of the abdomen."

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error of the previous item, and the location clue is the one most often reversed: above the umbilicus is the false-labor location, while the lower back radiating forward is the true-labor one.

8. A woman arrives with contractions she describes as strong and regular. Her cervix is unchanged from her examination three hours earlier. Which statement is the definitional basis for calling this false labor?
  • AHer contractions are not painful enough to indicate labor
  • ✓Her contractions are not causing cervical dilation
  • CShe is nulliparous and therefore not yet in labor
  • DHer contractions are more than five minutes apart
Rationale

The arbiter is cervical change, not contraction quality. The chapter names the emotional cost of this exact conversation: a woman "may feel discouraged, angry, or confused on learning that the contractions that feel so strong and regular to her do not indicate true labor because they are not causing cervical dilation."

Watch for

(A) is the reflex pick because contraction quality is what both the woman and the nurse can feel, and the teaching box itself is built on contraction characteristics. Those characteristics are screening clues; the cervix is the definition.

10. Which statement about when nulliparous and multiparous women typically present for admission is accurate?
  • ANulliparous women typically present in the active phase; multiparous women in the latent phase
  • BBoth groups typically present in the active phase
  • ✓Nulliparous women may seek admission in the latent phase; multiparous women may not present until the active phase
  • DPresentation timing is unrelated to parity
Rationale

"Many nulliparous women planning to give birth in a hospital or birth center may seek admission in the latent (early) phase because they have not experienced labor before and are unsure of the 'right' time to come in. Multiparous women may not present to the birth center or hospital until they are in the active phase of the first stage of labor."

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error and it is available because experience reads as caution — students expect the woman who has done this before to arrive earlier. Experience does the opposite: she waits.

telephone triage and emtala obligationsgeneral
11. A woman calls the labor unit at 0200 describing contractions and asks the nurse whether she is in labor and should come in. Which response reflects the chapter's guidance?
  • AAssess her contraction pattern by phone and advise her whether this is true labor
  • ✓Advise her to call her obstetric health care provider or to come to the hospital if she feels the need to be checked, and document the call
  • CTell her to wait until contractions are five minutes apart for one hour before calling back
  • DInstruct her to time contractions for two hours and call back with the results
Rationale

"Many hospitals, however, discourage the nurse from giving advice by telephone regarding what to do because of legal liability. Nurses are often instructed to tell women who call with questions to call their obstetric health care provider (HCP) or to come to the hospital if they feel the need to be checked." The nature of the conversation, "including any advice or instructions given and the person's response to the instructions, should be documented in the client's record."

Watch for

(A) and (C) both diagnose or triage over the phone, which is exactly the liability the chapter names. (C) is especially tempting because the five-one-one rule is widely taught, and it substitutes a rule for the assessment the woman is entitled to.

12. An uninsured woman presents to obstetric triage reporting contractions. The unit is full. Which action is required under the Emergency Medical Treatment and Active Labor Act?
  • ✓She must be assessed, stabilized, and treated at the hospital where she presents, regardless of insurance status or ability to pay
  • BShe may be redirected to a public hospital if the unit has no beds
  • CShe must be assessed but may be transferred before stabilization if another facility accepts her
  • DShe may be discharged by the triage nurse if the cervix is unchanged
Rationale

The Legal Tip states that "pregnant women who present with contractions or who may be in labor are considered unstable and must be assessed, stabilized, and treated at the hospital where they present regardless of their insurance status or ability to pay." The act is "a federal regulation enacted to protect pregnant women during an emergency regardless of their insurance status or ability to pay for care."

Watch for

(B) frames a capacity problem as a legitimate reason to redirect, which is the most plausible-sounding violation because it does not turn on insurance at all. (D) is the next question's error.

13. A triage nurse assesses a woman whose cervix is closed and whose contractions are irregular. What limits the nurse's ability to discharge her?
  • ANothing; a closed cervix rules out labor
  • ✓She is presumed to be in true labor until a qualified health care provider certifies that she is not
  • CShe may be discharged after a second examination two hours later
  • DOnly the charge nurse may authorize the discharge
Rationale

The Legal Tip states the presumption and its direction: "A pregnant woman presenting to an obstetric triage area is presumed to be in 'true' labor until a qualified HCP certifies that she is not." Named urgent problems that bring women to triage include labor, decreased fetal movement, rupture of membranes, and recent trauma.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex pick because a closed cervix genuinely is strong evidence against labor, and the nurse's own assessment feels sufficient. The presumption runs toward labor, and lifting it requires a qualified provider's certification, not a nurse's finding.

14. Four women arrive in obstetric triage simultaneously. Using the Maternal Fetal Triage Index, which one is Priority 1, STAT?
  • AA woman at 38 weeks with regular contractions and spontaneous rupture of membranes
  • BA woman at 32 weeks reporting decreased fetal movement
  • ✓A woman with fetal parts visible on the perineum and active bearing-down efforts
  • DA woman at 39 weeks with a temperature of 100.6°F and no other complaint
Rationale

Priority 1 captures abnormal STAT vital signs, the need for immediate lifesaving intervention, or imminent birth, and the index lists "fetal parts visible on the perineum" and "active maternal bearing-down efforts" as imminent birth. Priority 1 also covers maternal cardiac compromise, severe respiratory distress, seizing, hemorrhaging, acute mental status change, signs of placental abruption or uterine rupture, and prolapsed cord.

Watch for

(B) pulls because decreased fetal movement sounds like a fetal emergency; the index places it under Priority 2, urgent. (D) is a Priority 3 finding, since a temperature above 100.4°F sits in the prompt tier while 101.0°F or higher moves it to urgent.

15. The Maternal Fetal Triage Index lists a maternal heart rate threshold at more than one priority level. Which pairing is correct?
  • APriority 1 at a heart rate above 120; Priority 2 at above 130
  • ✓Priority 1 at a heart rate below 40 or above 130; Priority 2 at above 120 or below 50
  • CPriority 1 at a heart rate below 50; Priority 2 at below 40
  • DThe heart rate appears only at Priority 2
Rationale

The STAT tier lists maternal heart rate below 40 or above 130, apnea, oxygen saturation below 93%, systolic pressure at or above 150 or diastolic at or above 110 or a pressure below 60 or palpable only, no fetal heart rate detected by doppler, and a fetal heart rate below 110 for more than 60 seconds. The urgent tier widens to a heart rate above 120 or below 50, temperature at or above 101.0°F, respiratory rate above 26 or below 12, and saturation below 95%.

Watch for

(A) and (C) both narrow the STAT range inward, which is the intuitive error because the more urgent tier feels like it should trigger sooner. The STAT thresholds are the more extreme numbers at both ends; the urgent tier catches the values on the way there. The index's own disclaimer notes that its vital signs "are suggested values" that each clinical team adjusts for its population and region.

16. A low-risk nulliparous woman in latent labor lives 45 minutes away, has no car, and has a history of a 3-hour labor with her first birth. Which factor most supports admitting her rather than sending her home?
  • AHer low-risk status
  • BHer nulliparity
  • ✓Her lack of transportation, distance, and history of rapid labor
  • DHer request to stay
Rationale

The chapter sorts on practical logistics rather than on the cervix. Women who live near the facility with adequate support and transportation may be sent home; those who live "at a considerable distance," lack "adequate support and transportation," or have "a history of rapid labors" are admitted in latent labor.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex pick because low risk sounds like a discharge criterion, and it is the criterion for the ideal of laboring at home. Low risk permits going home only when the logistics permit it too. Note that the stem's history is internally inconsistent with "nulliparous," which is why (B) cannot be the reasoning either.

labor triage acuity and dispositionwho is admitted and who goes home
17. A hospital operates an early labor lounge. What does it accomplish?
  • AIt admits women in latent labor to a lower-acuity bed
  • ✓It allows women in early labor to remain on site as outpatients until they are admitted or discharged, so clients are not admitted until active labor
  • CIt provides antepartum testing for high-risk women
  • DIt replaces obstetric triage for low-risk women
Rationale

The early labor lounge is "a room for women who are in early labor and wish to remain on site. Women using the early labor lounge remain there as outpatients until they are admitted or discharged." It gives providers "an alternative to discharging clients who are reluctant to leave," and "clients are therefore not admitted until they are in active labor." Labor triage units similarly "offer time without official hospital admission to evaluate for labor or other concerns."

Watch for

(A) is the reflex pick because a woman occupying a room in the hospital reads as admitted. The whole design of the lounge turns on her outpatient status; that is what keeps the latent-phase admission from happening.

confirming rupture of membranesgeneral
18. A nitrazine test on vaginal fluid turns the tape deep blue. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • AMembranes are probably intact, because the fluid is acidic
  • ✓Membranes are probably ruptured, because amniotic fluid is alkaline
  • CThe result is inconclusive without a fern test
  • DThe result indicates infection rather than rupture
Rationale

Nitrazine dye "differentiates amniotic fluid, which is slightly alkaline, from urine and purulent material [pus], which are acidic." Blue-green at pH 6.5, blue-gray at 7.0, and deep blue at 7.5 all read as membranes probably ruptured. Results are reported as positive or negative.

Watch for

(A) inverts the pH direction, which is the single most invertible fact in this section. The anchor to hold is that amniotic fluid is the alkaline one, so the blue end of the tape is the positive result.

19. A nitrazine test on a second woman's vaginal fluid reads olive-green. What does this indicate?
  • AMembranes are probably ruptured
  • ✓Membranes are probably intact, at a pH of about 6.0
  • CThe specimen was contaminated with blood
  • DThe test is positive and the fern test may be omitted
Rationale

Yellow at pH 5.0, olive-yellow at 5.5, and olive-green at 6.0 all identify "vaginal and most body fluids that are acidic," reading as membranes probably intact.

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error of the previous item. Note also that false results are possible "because of presence of bloody show or semen or insufficient amount of amniotic fluid" — and two of those three push the result toward a false positive, so a blue reading in a woman with bloody show deserves confirmation.

confirming rupture of membranesgeneral
20. A fern test on vaginal fluid shows a frond-like crystalline pattern. A student says this proves the membranes are ruptured. Which caution does the chapter add?
  • ✓Ferning also occurs in the cervical mucus test when estrogen levels are high
  • BFerning appears only if the specimen is examined while still wet
  • CFerning is unreliable after 37 weeks
  • DFerning indicates infection rather than amniotic fluid
Rationale

The procedure is to spread a drop of fluid on a clean glass slide, "allow the fluid to dry," and examine it under the microscope for "ferning (a frond-like crystalline pattern)." The chapter names the decoy: "Do not confuse this with the cervical mucus test, when high levels of estrogen also cause ferning."

Watch for

(B) inverts the procedure — the specimen must dry before it will fern, so examining it wet produces a false negative. Absence of ferning is also not proof of intact membranes: it "alerts staff to possibility that amount of specimen was inadequate or that specimen was urine, vaginal discharge, or blood."

nursing care after rupture of membranesgeneral
21. A laboring woman's membranes rupture spontaneously with a large gush of clear fluid. What is the nurse's first action?
  • AChange the underpad and assess the odor and amount of fluid
  • BTake the woman's temperature
  • ✓Assess the fetal heart rate and pattern for several minutes
  • DNotify the obstetric health care provider
Rationale

The SAFETY ALERT names both the action and the reason: "The umbilical cord may prolapse when the membranes rupture. The FHR and pattern should be assessed for several minutes immediately after ROM to determine fetal well-being, and the findings should be documented."

Watch for

(A) and (B) are both real parts of the assessment and both are wrong as a first action, because neither would detect a prolapsed cord in the window that matters. Documentation after rupture must include the time of rupture, the fetal heart rate immediately before and after, and the color, estimated amount, and odor of the fluid.

22. Which statement about spontaneous rupture of membranes at term is accurate?
  • ARupture of membranes at term always means labor has begun
  • ✓Labor is initiated at term by spontaneous rupture in approximately 25% of women, and a lag rarely exceeding 24 hours may precede contractions
  • CLabor is initiated by rupture in approximately 75% of women at term
  • DRupture during labor occurs most commonly in the latent phase
Rationale

"Labor is initiated at term by SROM in approximately 25% of pregnant women," and "a lag period, rarely exceeding 24 hours, may precede the onset of labor." Rupture during labor "most commonly occurs in the active phase of the first stage of labor."

Watch for

(A) is the reflex equation of rupture with labor onset, and it fails on both halves — the fraction is a minority, and even when rupture does start things, contractions may lag by up to a day.

nursing care after rupture of membranesgeneral
23. A woman's membranes ruptured six hours ago. Which nursing measures does the chapter specify to identify infection?
  • ✓Limit vaginal examinations and monitor maternal temperature and vaginal discharge at least every 2 hours
  • BObtain a vaginal culture every 4 hours
  • CPerform a vaginal examination hourly to track progress
  • DRestrict oral intake and begin prophylactic antibiotics
Rationale

"When membranes rupture, microorganisms from the vagina can then ascend into the amniotic sac, causing chorioamnionitis and placentitis to develop. For this reason, vaginal examinations must be limited, and maternal temperature and vaginal discharge must be monitored frequently (at least every 2 hours) to quickly identify signs of infection."

Watch for

(C) is exactly the practice the chapter restricts, and it pulls because tracking progress feels like attentive nursing. The chapter also closes the reassuring loophole: "Even when membranes are intact, however, microorganisms can ascend and cause infection."

nursing care after rupture of membranesgeneral
24. A woman in uncomplicated active labor at 7 cm is progressing normally. The provider suggests amniotomy to speed things up. Which consideration applies?
  • AAmniotomy reliably shortens labor and should be performed
  • ✓Amniotomy is discouraged if there is no medical reason, because it can increase the sensation of pressure and pain and is not necessary for a normal birth
  • CAmniotomy is contraindicated after 6 cm
  • DAmniotomy eliminates the risk of cord prolapse
Rationale

Artificial rupture of membranes, or amniotomy, "is discouraged if there is no medical reason for it because it can increase the laboring woman's sensation of pressure and pain and is not necessary for a normal birth to occur." It is performed by the obstetric provider using a plastic AmniHook or a surgical clamp.

Watch for

(D) inverts the risk in the most dangerous direction — rupture, spontaneous or artificial, is precisely when the cord may prolapse, which is why the fetal heart rate is assessed for several minutes afterward either way.

25. A woman is in the early phase of the first stage of labor. According to Table 19.2, how often are her blood pressure, pulse, and respirations assessed?
  • AEvery 15 to 30 minutes
  • ✓Every 30 to 60 minutes
  • CEvery hour
  • DEvery 5 to 15 minutes
Rationale

Table 19.2 sets maternal vital signs and presence of bloody show at every 30 to 60 minutes in the early phase, tightening to every 15 to 30 minutes in the active phase. Uterine activity and fetal heart rate and pattern are assessed every 15 to 30 minutes in both phases.

Watch for

(A) is the active-phase interval and is the most available wrong answer because 15 to 30 minutes appears three times in the table. The vital-sign row is the one that changes between phases; uterine activity and fetal heart rate do not.

26. The same table specifies an assessment whose interval tightens more sharply between the early and active phases than the vital signs do. Which is it?
  • AFetal heart rate and pattern
  • BUterine activity
  • ✓Changes in maternal appearance, mood, affect, energy level, and involvement of the partner
  • DTemperature

The psychosocial row moves from every 30 minutes in the early phase to every 5 to 15 minutes in the active phase, a sharper tightening than the vital signs' shift from 30-to-60 to 15-to-30 minutes. Uterine activity and fetal heart rate stay at 15 to 30 minutes in both phases.

(A) and (B) pull because they are the assessments that feel most clinically urgent, and both intervals are in fact unchanged between the phases. The row that intensifies most is the one about how the woman is coping.

  • ✓Every 2 hours
  • CEvery hour
  • DEvery 30 minutes

Table 19.2 states the rule as a conditional: temperature "every 4 h until membranes are ruptured, then every 2 h." Temperature is monitored to identify signs of infection or a fluid deficit such as dehydration from inadequate intake.

29. Before performing Leopold maneuvers, which preparation steps does the chapter specify?
  • ✓Ask the woman to empty her bladder and place a rolled towel under one hip
  • BPosition the woman flat and supine with legs extended
  • CHave the woman lie on her left side with knees drawn up
  • DPerform the maneuvers during a contraction for better definition
Rationale

The preparation is hand hygiene, asking the woman to empty her bladder, positioning her supine with one pillow and knees slightly flexed, and placing "a small rolled towel under the woman's right or left hip to displace the uterus off major blood vessels (prevents supine hypotensive syndrome)."

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick because abdominal palpation seems to require a flat abdomen, and it recreates the aortocaval compression the rolled towel exists to prevent. The bladder step is easy to skip and is itself testable.

30. The nurse palpates the uterine fundus and finds a part that is round, firm, and freely movable. Which conclusion follows?
  • AThe fetus is in a breech presentation with the buttocks in the fundus
  • ✓The head is in the fundus, suggesting a breech presentation
  • CThe fetal back is anterior
  • DThe presenting part is engaged
Rationale

The first maneuver palpates the fundal contents, and the chapter gives the tactile discriminator: "The head feels round, firm, and freely movable; the breech feels less regular and softer." A head in the fundus means the breech is presenting. This maneuver identifies fetal lie, longitudinal or transverse, and presentation, cephalic or breech.

Watch for

(A) makes the right diagnosis with the wrong anatomy, which is the reflex error — students correctly hear "breech presentation" but then place the buttocks at the top instead of the bottom. What is in the fundus is the opposite of what is presenting.

31. On the fourth maneuver, the nurse finds the cephalic prominence on the same side as the fetal small parts. What does this indicate?
  • AThe head is extended and the face is presenting
  • ✓The head is flexed and the vertex is presenting
  • CThe fetus is in a transverse lie
  • DThe presenting part is not yet engaged
Rationale

The fourth maneuver, performed facing the woman's feet, outlines the fetal head and palpates the cephalic prominence. Prominence "on the same side as the small parts means the head is flexed and the vertex presents."

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error and it is the one most often held backwards, because there is no intuitive reason why the prominence should sit opposite the back when the head is flexed. Documentation after the maneuvers records presentation, position, and lie, and whether the presenting part is flexed or extended, engaged, or free-floating.

33. A nurse is locating the point of maximal intensity to place the ultrasound transducer. In a vertex presentation, where is the fetal heart rate usually heard loudest?
  • AAbove the maternal umbilicus in the midline
  • ✓Below the maternal umbilicus, in either the right or left lower quadrant
  • CAt the level of the umbilicus on the side opposite the fetal back
  • DAt the fundus
Rationale

The point of maximal intensity "is the location on the maternal abdomen at which the FHR is heard the loudest. It is usually directly over the fetal back." In a vertex presentation the fetal heart rate "can usually be heard below the mother's umbilicus in either the right or the left lower quadrant." The point of maximal intensity "is where the nurse places the ultrasound transducer when an electronic fetal monitor is used."

(C) places the transducer opposite the back, which is exactly backwards — the sound transmits through the fetal back, which is why the point of maximal intensity sits over it.

68. A different provider watches for signs of separation, instructs the woman to push to expel the placenta, and then massages the fundus and gives oxytocin. Which approach is this, and what is the expected timing?
  • AActive management; the placenta is expelled within 5 minutes
  • ✓Expectant, or passive, management; the placenta is usually expelled within 15 minutes after the birth
  • CActive management; the placenta is expelled within 15 minutes
  • DExpectant management, which is the approach recommended by professional societies
Rationale

Passive management is "patiently watching for signs that the placenta has separated from the uterine wall spontaneously and monitoring for spontaneous expulsion"; the woman is instructed to push, "the placenta is usually expelled within 15 minutes after the birth of the baby," and "as soon as the placenta is expelled, the uterine fundus is massaged, and medication to contract the uterus (usually oxytocin [Pitocin]) is administered."

Watch for

(D) is the mirror error on the recommendation. Active management is what the professional society recommends, and its benefits are "a shorter duration of third-stage labor, less risk for postpartum hemorrhage, and decreased risk for anemia" for both the woman and the newborn.

(B) and (D) are the general shock interventions a student imports from a medical-surgical context, and neither addresses the cause. A full bladder displaces the uterus and prevents it contracting, which is why emptying it comes before anything else.

71. A laceration extends through the external anal sphincter and into the rectal mucosa. How is it classified, and why does the classification matter?
  • AThird degree; it determines the suture material used
  • ✓Fourth degree; third- and fourth-degree tears must be carefully repaired so the woman retains fecal continence
  • CSecond degree; it extends into the perineal body
  • DThird degree; it involves the sphincter but spares the mucosa
Rationale

A first-degree laceration is "confined to the skin," a second "extends into the perineal body," a third "involves injury to the external anal sphincter muscle," and a fourth "extends completely through the anal sphincter and the rectal mucosa." Third- and fourth-degree lacerations "must be carefully repaired so that the woman will retain fecal continence."

Watch for

(A) and (D) both stop at the sphincter, which is the discriminator between third and fourth degree. Once the rectal mucosa is involved the injury is fourth degree, and that boundary is the one the classification exists to mark.

73. A woman asks whether an episiotomy will be performed routinely at her birth. Which response reflects current practice as the chapter describes it?
  • ✓Routine episiotomy has no role in modern obstetric care and should be avoided whenever possible, though it may still be indicated in specific situations
  • BEpisiotomy is routine for all nulliparous women to prevent uncontrolled tearing
  • CEpisiotomy is indicated whenever a laceration appears likely
  • DEpisiotomy produces less blood loss and less postpartum pain than an intact perineum
Rationale

"Routine episiotomy has no role in modern obstetric care and should be avoided whenever possible. Indicated episiotomy, however, may still be performed in specific situations, such as the need to hasten birth when FHR abnormalities are present." Giving birth over an intact perineum yields "less blood loss, less risk for infection, and less postpartum pain."

Watch for

(D) inverts which outcome favors which, and it survives on the intuition that a clean incision must heal better than a tear. The chapter attributes the better outcomes to the intact perineum. Prevention measures named include upright positioning, gentle open glottis pushing that coincides with the woman's natural urges, and massage with warm compresses during the second stage.
