1. A patient recovering from a vaginal infection asks the nurse why the vagina does not become infected more often, given what it is exposed to. Which property of normal vaginal secretions should the nurse describe?
  • ✓They are slightly acidic, at a pH of 4 to 5, which limits susceptibility to infection
  • BThey are slightly alkaline, at a pH of 8 to 9, which neutralizes invading organisms
  • CThey are neutral, at a pH of 7, which prevents irritation of the mucosa
  • DThey are strongly acidic, at a pH of 2 to 3, which sterilizes the vaginal canal
Rationale

Vaginal secretions are "slightly acidic (pH 4 to 5) so that vaginal susceptibility to infections is limited" (p. 48). The acidity is a defense, and it operates by limiting susceptibility rather than by sterilizing anything.

Watch for

(B) inverts the source outright, and it is the intuitive inversion because students associate antiseptics with alkalinity. (D) overshoots a true direction into a false magnitude, which is the harder distractor: the pH is acidic, but 2 to 3 is gastric, not vaginal.

2. A 58-year-old patient reports vaginal dryness and discomfort. On examination the nurse practitioner notes that the vaginal walls appear thin and smooth rather than folded. Which explanation fits this finding?
  • ✓Estrogen deprivation has caused thinning of the walls and smoothing of the rugae
  • BRepeated childbirth has permanently stretched the rugae beyond their elastic limit
  • CThe finding is abnormal at any age and requires biopsy of the vaginal wall
  • DProgesterone withdrawal has caused the transverse mucosal folds to fuse
Rationale

During the reproductive years the vaginal mucosa is arranged in transverse folds called rugae. Estrogen deprivation "that occurs after birth, during lactation, and at menopause causes dryness and thinning of the vaginal walls and smoothing of the rugae" (p. 48).

Watch for

(D) is the reflex pick for anyone who remembers that a hormone withdrawal is responsible but not which one — the source names estrogen deprivation specifically, and names three settings for it, only one of which is menopause.

3. During a bimanual examination the examiner palpates the internal pelvic organs through the thin-walled pockets that surround the cervix. Which structures make this possible?
  • ✓The fornices, anterior, posterior, and lateral pockets surrounding the cervix
  • BThe rugae, transverse mucosal folds along the length of the vaginal canal
  • CThe cul-de-sac of Douglas, a single recess anterior to the cervix
  • DThe transformation zone, where squamous and columnar epithelium meet
Rationale

"Anterior, posterior, and lateral pockets called fornices (singular: fornix) surround the cervix. The internal pelvic organs can be palpated through the thin walls of these fornices" (p. 48).

Watch for

(C) names a real structure and gets its relationship to the cervix backwards — the cul-de-sac of Douglas is a deep pouch posterior to the cervix, formed by the posterior ligament, not an anterior one, and it is one recess rather than the set of pockets used for palpation.

4. A nursing student palpating a nonpregnant cervix during a supervised examination asks the preceptor what a normal cervix should feel like, and how it can possibly stretch during birth. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓It feels firm, like the end of a nose, and it is mostly fibrous connective and elastic tissue, which is what allows it to stretch
  • BIt feels soft, like the lips, and it is mostly smooth muscle, which contracts to open during birth
  • CIt feels firm, like the end of a nose, because it is mostly smooth muscle in a figure-eight arrangement
  • DIt feels boggy and indistinct, and it is mostly glandular tissue that liquefies at term
Rationale

The cervix "feels firm (like the end of a nose) with a dimple in the center that marks the external os," and it is "made up of mostly fibrous connective tissue and elastic tissue, making it possible for the cervix to stretch during vaginal birth" (p. 49).

Watch for

(C) pairs the correct texture with the wrong tissue, which is why it is the most attractive distractor — the figure-eight muscle arrangement is real but belongs to the middle myometrial layer of the uterine corpus, not to the cervix.

5. A patient asks why the Papanicolaou test requires scraping cells from one specific spot just inside the cervical os rather than sampling the cervix broadly. Which rationale should the nurse give?
  • ✓That spot is the squamocolumnar junction, the most common site for neoplastic changes
  • BThat spot is the only part of the cervix that can be reached with a spatula
  • CThat spot is where cervical mucus is produced, so it yields the most cells
  • DThat spot is the internal os, which marks the boundary of the uterine cavity
Rationale

Where squamous and columnar epithelium meet "is usually located just inside the cervical os. This junction is also called the transformation zone and is the most common site for neoplastic changes. Cells from this site are scraped for the Papanicolaou (Pap) test" (p. 49).

Watch for

(B) is the reflex answer of convenience, and the fact bank flags it by name — a stem asking why the Pap samples that specific spot is answered by the neoplasia sentence, not by ease of access. (D) confuses the two openings: the internal os connects the uterine cavity to the endocervical canal, while the transformation zone sits at the external end.

6. A nurse reviewing pelvic anatomy is asked to name the dome-shaped top of the uterus, the region where the uterine tubes enter. Which division does this describe?
  • ✓The fundus
  • BThe isthmus
  • CThe corpus
  • DThe cervix
Rationale

The fundus is the "dome-shaped top of the uterus" and is the "site at which the uterine tubes (fallopian tubes) enter" the uterus (p. 48).

Watch for

(C) is the near miss rather than a wrong region — the corpus is the upper triangular portion as a whole, and the fundus is its dome-shaped top. A stem that specifies where the tubes enter is asking for the more precise of the two.

8. Immediately after birth the myometrium contracts and bleeding from the placental site slows sharply, even though large vessels were torn open. Which myometrial layer accounts for this?
  • ✓The middle layer, whose fibers run in all three directions in a figure-eight pattern encircling large blood vessels
  • BThe outer layer, whose longitudinal fibers are found mostly in the fundus
  • CThe inner layer, whose fibers encircle the internal cervical os
  • DThe endometrial basal layer, which regenerates the lining after separation
Rationale

The middle myometrial layer is made of "fibers from all three directions, which form a figure-eight pattern encircling large blood vessels," and "these fibers assist in ligat[ing]" those vessels (pp. 48–49). It is the living ligature that controls bleeding at the placental site.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick because the outer longitudinal layer is the one associated with the fundus, and the fundus is where the nurse assesses tone after birth. Assessing there does not make it the layer that ligates — the outer layer assists in expelling the fetus.

9. A nurse is explaining why menstrual blood does not normally flow backward into the uterine tubes, and why the cervix stays closed through pregnancy. Which myometrial layer performs both functions?
  • ✓The inner layer, whose fibers surround where the tubes enter and encircle the internal cervical os
  • BThe middle layer, whose figure-eight fibers encircle the large blood vessels
  • CThe outer layer, whose longitudinal fibers are concentrated in the fundus
  • DThe peritoneal layer, which covers the uterus externally
Rationale

The inner myometrial fibers, arranged around where the tubes enter and around the internal cervical os, "help keep the cervix closed during pregnancy" and "prevent menstrual blood from flowing back into the uterine tubes during menstruation" (pp. 48–49).

Watch for

(C) is the mirror error of the previous item's trap — the outer longitudinal layer expels the fetus, which is the opposite of keeping the cervix closed. All three layers are plausible only until you match each to its stated function.

10. A patient asks how the uterine lining can be shed every month and still be there the next month. Select all that are true of the endometrium during and after menstruation.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓The endometrium is made up of three layers
  • ✓The functional two-thirds, the compact and spongy layers, is what is shed
  • ✓The basal layer is always retained
  • ✓Regeneration of the lining begins near the end of the cycle
  • EThe entire endometrium is shed and is rebuilt from the myometrium
Rationale

The endometrium is a "highly vascular lining made up of three layers, the outer two of which are shed during menstruation" (p. 48). During the menstrual phase, shedding of "the functional two-thirds of the endometrium (the compact and spongy layers)" occurs, while "the basal layer is always retained, and regeneration begins near the end of the cycle" (p. 53).

Watch for

(E) is the single wrong option and it is the one the fact bank flags — a distractor saying the entire endometrium sheds is wrong, and it is attractive because "shedding the lining" is how the process gets described in plain language. Nothing regenerates from the myometrium; the retained basal layer is the source.

11. A patient with a history of tubal surgery asks where fertilization normally takes place. Which section of the uterine tube should the nurse name?
  • ✓The ampulla, one of the middle portions of the tube
  • BThe infundibulum, the section closest to the ovary
  • CThe interstitial portion, the section closest to the uterus
  • DThe isthmus, the section immediately distal to the interstitial portion
Rationale

"The ovum is usually fertilized by the sperm in the ampulla portion of one of the tubes" (p. 49). The four sections run, uterus to ovary: interstitial, isthmus, ampulla, infundibulum.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick because the infundibulum is the end that captures the ovum, so it feels like the place where sperm and ovum would first meet. Capture and fertilization happen at different points along the tube, and the order of the four sections is what this item is testing.

12. The same patient asks what actually draws the released ovum into the tube in the first place. Which structure and section should the nurse name?
  • ✓The fimbriae, the fringed ends of the infundibulum
  • BThe fimbriae, the fringed ends of the ampulla
  • CThe cilia of the interstitial portion, which sweep the ovum inward
  • DThe rhythmic contractions of the isthmus, which create suction at the ovary
Rationale

"The infundibulum has fimbriated (fringed) ends, which pull the ovum into the tube" (p. 49). Once inside, the ovum is moved "by rhythmic contractions of muscles of the tubes and by the current produced by the movement of the cilia that line the tubes."

Watch for

(B) is the mirror error of the previous item, attaching the right structure to the wrong section. (C) and (D) name the two real transport mechanisms but assign them the job of capture, which belongs to the fimbriae alone.

13. On pelvic examination of a healthy 24-year-old, the ovaries are described as nodular rather than smooth. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • ✓It is an expected finding after menarche, caused by repeated rupture of follicles at ovulation
  • BIt is an abnormal finding that suggests polycystic change and warrants imaging
  • CIt is an expected finding before menarche and should resolve with regular cycles
  • DIt is an expected finding only after menopause, when the ovaries diminish in size
Rationale

"Before menarche, each ovary has a smooth surface; after menarche, they are nodular because of repeated ruptures of follicles at ovulation" (p. 49).

(B) is the reflex pick because "nodular" reads as pathology everywhere else in nursing assessment, and the fact bank flags exactly that: do not read nodular as pathology in this context. (C) inverts the marker — smooth is the pre-menarcheal state, not the post-menarcheal one.

14. A nursing student is sorting reproductive hormones by the organ that secretes them. Select all the hormones that the ovaries themselves produce.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Estrogen
  • ✓Progesterone
  • ✓Androgen
  • DFollicle-stimulating hormone
  • EGonadotropin-releasing hormone
Rationale

The ovaries have two functions, "ovulation and hormone production," and the hormones they produce are "estrogen, progesterone, and androgen" (p. 49). Follicle-stimulating hormone is an anterior pituitary product and gonadotropin-releasing hormone comes from the hypothalamus (p. 53).

Watch for

(D) is the strongest wrong option because follicle-stimulating hormone is named for the ovarian structure it acts on, which invites the reader to file it as ovarian. The ovary is its target tissue, not its source. Androgen is the keyed option most often dropped, since it is easy to think of the ovary as producing only the two hormones named in every cycle diagram.

15. A mother tells the school nurse that her daughter "reached puberty last month, when she got her first period." Which correction should the nurse offer?
  • ✓The first menstrual period is menarche; puberty is the entire transitional stage between childhood and sexual maturity
  • BThe first menstrual period is puberty; menarche is the point at which ovulatory cycles begin
  • CThe two terms are interchangeable, and either is correct for a first period
  • DThe first menstrual period is the climacteric; puberty refers only to breast development
Rationale

"The term menarche denotes first menstruation," while puberty is "a broader term that denotes the entire transitional stage between childhood and sexual maturity" (p. 51).

Watch for

(B) is the classic swap the fact bank names — a distractor that defines puberty as the first menstrual period. The two words are not a wide term and a narrow term for the same event; one is a single event inside the other.

17. A 12-year-old whose periods began four months ago tells the nurse that her cycles have been irregular and unpredictable, and asks whether that means she cannot get pregnant yet. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓Early cycles are commonly irregular and anovulatory, but pregnancy can occur at any time after the onset of menses
  • BIrregular cycles in the first year confirm that ovulation is not occurring, so pregnancy is not possible yet
  • CCycles are regular from menarche onward, so irregularity should be evaluated
  • DOvulatory cycles begin only after age 16, so contraception is unnecessary before then
Rationale

"Initially, menstrual periods are irregular and unpredictable and anovulatory (no ovum is released from the ovary)," and after "1 or more years" a hypothalamic-pituitary rhythm develops. The chapter states the teaching point outright: "All young adolescents of both sexes would benefit from knowing that pregnancy can occur at any time after the onset of menses" (p. 51).

Watch for

(B) is the dangerous distractor, and it is dangerous precisely because its first half is true. Early cycles are commonly anovulatory; the source still says pregnancy can occur at any time after menses begin, so "commonly anovulatory" can never be converted into "cannot conceive."

18. A 49-year-old reports that she has had no menstrual period for the past six months and asks whether she has gone through menopause. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓Menopause can be dated with certainty only 1 year after menstruation ceases, so she is currently perimenopausal
  • BSix months without a period confirms menopause, since the average age is 51.4 years
  • CMenopause is confirmed by a single hormone level rather than by elapsed time
  • DAt 49 she is too young for menopause, since the age range begins at 51
Rationale

Menopause "refers only to the last menstrual period," and "in contrast to menarche, menopause can be dated with certainty only 1 year after menstruation ceases" (p. 54). Perimenopause is the roughly 4-year period preceding menopause, during which ovarian function declines.

Watch for

(D) misreads 51.4 years as a lower bound when it is the average; the stated range is 35 to 60 years, so 49 is unremarkable. The deeper point is that menopause cannot be diagnosed prospectively at all — it is named in retrospect, which is what separates it from menarche.

20. Three patients report irregular, unpredictable bleeding: a 13-year-old whose periods began eight months ago, a 28-year-old with previously regular cycles, and a 48-year-old whose cycles have been shortening. Which patient's finding most warrants evaluation rather than reassurance?
  • ✓The 28-year-old, because anovulatory irregular bleeding is expected only at the two ends of the reproductive years
  • BThe 13-year-old, because bleeding should be regular within six months of menarche
  • CThe 48-year-old, because irregular bleeding is never expected before menopause is confirmed
  • DAll three, because irregular bleeding is abnormal at any age
Rationale

Irregular, unpredictable, anovulatory cycles are normal just after menarche, where periods are "irregular and unpredictable and anovulatory" (p. 51), and again in perimenopause, where "menstrual cycles may be anovulatory, resulting in irregular bleeding" (p. 54). The same finding in a 28-year-old falls outside both windows.

Watch for

This is the fact bank's "anovulatory bleeding bookends the reproductive years" note turned into a discrimination. Age is the discriminating cue, not the bleeding pattern, which is identical in all three patients. (D) is the reflex pick for a reader who treats irregular bleeding as a finding rather than as a finding in a life stage.

23. A patient is alarmed that her menstrual blood does not seem to clot the way blood from a cut does, and asks whether something is wrong with her clotting. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓Menstrual blood that does not appear to clot is reported in about 50% of women and is an expected finding
  • BAbsent clotting in menstrual blood indicates a coagulation disorder and requires testing
  • CMenstrual blood never clots in any woman, because it is not true blood
  • DAbsent clotting suggests blood loss is above 80 mL and warrants evaluation
Rationale

The chapter's flow characteristics note that menstrual blood does not appear to clot in about 50% of women (pp. 51–53), which makes it a common normal variant rather than a sign of a clotting problem.

24. Menstruation is described as beginning approximately 14 days after ovulation and as being controlled by a feedback system of three simultaneous cycles. Which three cycles are these?
  • ✓Endometrial, hypothalamic-pituitary, and ovarian
  • BEndometrial, cervical, and ovarian
  • CFollicular, luteal, and ischemic
  • DProliferative, secretory, and menstrual
Rationale

26. An endometrial biopsy shows rapid growth with the surface restored and the lining thickened eight- to tenfold, and growth appears to be leveling off. Which phase does this describe, and which hormone drives it?
  • ✓The proliferative phase, driven by estrogen from the ovarian follicles
  • BThe secretory phase, driven by progesterone from the corpus luteum
  • CThe ischemic phase, driven by falling estrogen and progesterone
  • DThe menstrual phase, driven by withdrawal of both hormones
Rationale

The proliferative phase runs "from about the fifth day to the time of ovulation." The endometrial surface is restored in about 4 days, then thickens "8- to 10-fold, with a leveling off of growth at ovulation," and the phase "depends on estrogen stimulation derived from ovarian follicles" (p. 53).

Watch for

(B) is the pick for anyone who reads "growth" as the whole story and forgets which hormone builds versus which hormone enriches. The fact bank's rule settles it: if the stem names progesterone or the period after ovulation, it cannot be the proliferative phase — and here the leveling off at ovulation says the phase is ending, not beginning.

27. An endometrial specimen taken 8 days after ovulation is described as edematous and vascular, with the texture of heavy, soft velvet and luxuriant with blood and glandular secretions. Select all that correctly characterize this phase.

  • ✓Large amounts of progesterone are produced during it
  • ✓It creates a suitable protective and nutritive bed for a fertilized ovum

The secretory phase "extends from the day of ovulation to about 3 days before the next menstrual period." After ovulation "large amounts of progesterone are produced. 28. Two days before an expected period, the spiral arteries go into spasm, blood supply to the functional endometrium is blocked, and necrosis develops. Which phase is this, and what triggers it?
  • ✓The ischemic phase, triggered by the rapid decrease in progesterone and estrogen as the corpus luteum regresses
  • BThe menstrual phase, triggered by the surge of luteinizing hormone
  • CThe ischemic phase, triggered by rising progesterone from a maturing corpus luteum
  • DThe secretory phase, triggered by withdrawal of estrogen alone
Rationale

In the ischemic phase the corpus luteum regresses, and "with the rapid decrease in progesterone and estrogen levels, the spiral arteries go into spasm." The blood supply "is blocked and necrosis develops," the functional layer separates from the basal layer, and bleeding begins, "marking day 1 of the next cycle" (p. 53).

Watch for

(C) keeps the right phase and reverses its trigger, which is the harder error to catch because naming the phase feels like the whole question. Ischemia here is a withdrawal event: both hormones fall, and it is the fall that causes the spasm.

29. A patient asks what is actually happening in the uterus on the first two days of her period. Which description is accurate?
  • ✓The functional two-thirds of the endometrium is shed after periodic vasoconstriction in the upper layers, while the basal layer is retained
  • BThe full thickness of the endometrium separates, and regeneration begins only after bleeding stops
  • CThe basal layer is shed first, and the compact and spongy layers follow
  • DThe endometrium is digested by glandular secretions released at the end of the secretory phase
Rationale

In the menstrual phase, "shedding of the functional two-thirds of the endometrium (the compact and spongy layers) is initiated by periodic vasoconstriction in the upper layers," and "the basal layer is always retained, and regeneration begins near the end of the cycle" (p. 53).

(C) inverts which layer goes and which stays, and it is the inversion that matters clinically: the retained basal layer is what makes the next cycle possible. (B) gets the timing of regeneration wrong as well, since regeneration begins near the end of the cycle rather than after bleeding has finished.

30. A patient's cycles consistently run 35 days rather than 28. Which phase accounts for the extra week?
  • ✓The follicular phase, since almost all variation in ovarian cycle length comes from variation in its length
  • BThe luteal phase, since it lengthens to accommodate a longer cycle
  • CBoth phases lengthen proportionally, so each contributes about half
  • DNeither phase; the extra days are added to the menstrual phase
Rationale

The follicular, or preovulatory, phase "varies in length from woman to woman. Almost all variations in ovarian cycle length are the result of variations in the length of the follicular phase" (p. 53). The luteal phase "usually requires 14 days (range of 13 to 15 days)."

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick and the fact bank says a distractor lengthening the luteal phase is always wrong. The luteal phase is the fixed one: a 35-day cycle is a 21-day follicular phase plus a 14-day luteal phase.

31. A pathology report describes a secretory endometrium. Select all that are true of the ovarian phase occurring at the same time.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓It is the luteal phase
  • ✓It begins immediately after ovulation
  • ✓It ends with the start of menstruation
  • ✓The corpus luteum reaches its peak of functional activity 8 days into it
  • EIts length accounts for almost all variation in ovarian cycle length

The luteal, or postovulatory, phase "begins immediately after ovulation and ends with the start of menstruation," usually requiring 14 days with a range of 13 to 15. 32. A patient with a regular 35-day cycle asks when she ovulates. Which calculation should the nurse use?
  • ✓About day 21, counting back 14 days from the expected next period
  • BAbout day 17, counting forward 14 days plus the extra 3 days of cycle length
  • CAbout day 14, since ovulation occurs on day 14 regardless of cycle length
  • DAbout day 28, since ovulation occurs 7 days before the next period
Rationale

Menstruation "begins approximately 14 days after ovulation" (p. 52), and the luteal phase is the fixed 14-day interval, so ovulation is located by counting back from the next expected period rather than forward from the last one. In a 35-day cycle that places ovulation at about day 21.

Watch for

(C) is the near-universal reflex, because day 14 is true only of the 28-day cycle used to illustrate the diagram. (B) is the subtler error: counting forward from day 1 is the wrong direction entirely, and it happens to give day 17 here, which is close enough to look defensible.

33. Fourteen days after ovulation, with no fertilization, a patient's progesterone and estrogen levels fall and her period begins. What has happened to the structure that had been producing those hormones?
  • ✓The corpus luteum has regressed, having peaked in function 8 days after ovulation
  • BThe corpus luteum has been shed with the functional endometrium
  • CThe graafian follicle has failed to rupture, trapping the ovum
  • DThe corpus luteum has become the corpus albicans immediately at ovulation
Rationale

"The corpus luteum reaches its peak of functional activity 8 days after ovulation, secreting the steroids estrogen and progesterone." Watch for

(B) confuses the two things that end at the same time — the corpus luteum is an ovarian structure and is not part of what the uterus sheds. (C) describes the prostaglandin-deficit picture from later in the chapter, in which the ovum stays trapped in the follicle, which means no corpus luteum forms at all.

34. At the very end of a cycle, blood levels of estrogen and progesterone are low. According to the chapter, what does this low level trigger?
  • ✓It stimulates the hypothalamus to secrete gonadotropin-releasing hormone, which stimulates anterior pituitary secretion of follicle-stimulating hormone
  • BIt suppresses the hypothalamus, allowing the anterior pituitary to release luteinizing hormone directly
  • CIt stimulates the anterior pituitary to secrete gonadotropin-releasing hormone, which acts on the ovary
  • DIt stimulates the ovary to secrete follicle-stimulating hormone, which matures the next follicle
Rationale

"Low blood levels of these ovarian hormones stimulate the hypothalamus to secrete gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH). In turn, GnRH stimulates anterior pituitary secretion of FSH. FSH stimulates development of ovarian graafian follicles and their production of estrogen" (p. 53).

(B) is the reflex pick for a reader who assumes every falling hormone level must be inhibitory.   • DOvulation occurs at the moment of the lowest temperature, which was three days ago
Rationale

(B) is attractive because "ripening" sounds like softening and softening sounds like relaxation. Prostaglandins do the opposite on the muscle side: they potentiate the response to oxytocic stimulation rather than blocking it, which is also why they are not a neutral cervical agent.

  • DProstaglandins from semen thicken cervical mucus to protect the sperm
