bonding versus attachmentthe two definitions
1. A nurse documents that a 2-day-old infant has "good attachment" with the mother. Why is this the wrong term?
  • ✓Because attachment is a two-way interaction that develops during the first year of the infant's life, and the quality of attachment is not typically observable before 9 months of age
  • BBecause attachment applies only to fathers
  • CBecause attachment cannot be documented by nurses
  • DBecause attachment and bonding are identical terms
Rationale

"Attachment is a two-way interaction between the mother (or primary caregiver) and the infant that develops during the first year of the infant's life," and "the quality of attachment is not typically observable before 9 months of age." What is observable in the first days is bonding: "bonding behaviors may be observed in the early moments, days, weeks, and months after birth."

Watch for

(D) is the usage the chapter opens by correcting: "the term bonding should be considered separately from the term attachment, as they refer to different but related processes."

bonding versus attachmentthe two definitions
2. When does bonding begin, and how does it relate to attachment developmentally?
  • AAt birth, and it follows attachment
  • ✓During pregnancy, continuing to develop into childhood; parent-infant bonding precedes attachment developmentally
  • CAt 9 months, along with attachment
  • DOnly once the infant can respond socially
Rationale

Bonding is "the emotions and feelings experienced by the mother (or parent) in relation to the infant," and "it begins during pregnancy and continues to develop into childhood." The chapter states the order: "Parent-infant bonding precedes attachment developmentally."

Watch for

(A) starts the process at birth and reverses the order, which would make prenatal attachment-promoting care look pointless.

3. An infant cries, and the parent comes to the crib; the infant then roots and grasps the parent's finger. Which behaviors are these, and what does each accomplish?
  • ✓Crying is a signaling behavior that initiates contact and brings the caregiver to the child; rooting and grasping are executive behaviors that maintain the contact
  • BCrying is an executive behavior; rooting is a signaling behavior
  • CBoth are signaling behaviors
  • DBoth are reflexes with no role in bonding
Rationale

"The infant displays signaling behaviors such as crying, smiling, and cooing that initiate the contact and bring the caregiver to the child," and "executive behaviors such as rooting, grasping, and postural adjustments that maintain the contact."

Watch for

(B) reverses the pair. The cue that survives is that signaling calls the parent over and executive behavior holds on once they arrive.

bonding versus attachmentmutuality and the infant's contribution
4. What does the chapter say about how an infant's own characteristics affect bonding?
  • AInfant characteristics have no effect
  • ✓The infant's behaviors and characteristics elicit a corresponding set of parental behaviors, and bonding occurs more readily with an infant whose temperament, social capabilities, appearance, and sex fit the parent's expectations
  • COnly the parent's characteristics matter
  • DIrritable infants bond more strongly because they demand more care
Rationale

"The concept of bonding includes mutuality; that is, the infant's behaviors and characteristics elicit a corresponding set of parental behaviors and characteristics." And plainly: "Most caregivers are attracted to an alert, responsive, cuddly infant but find it less desirable to interact with an irritable, apparently disinterested infant. Bonding occurs more readily with the infant whose temperament, social capabilities, appearance, and sex fit the parent's expectations."

Watch for

(A) makes bonding entirely a parental achievement, which both misplaces responsibility and misses why an irritable or sedated infant is a bonding risk factor.

acquaintance and claiminghow the infant is claimed
9. In what order does the claiming process proceed?
  • AUniqueness first, then differences, then likeness
  • ✓The child is first identified in terms of likeness to other family members, then in terms of differences, and finally in terms of uniqueness
  • CDifferences first, then likeness
  • DThere is no established order
Rationale

"The child is first identified in terms of likeness to other family members, then in terms of differences, and finally in terms of uniqueness. The unique infant newcomer is thus incorporated into the family." Parents become acquainted through "eye contact, touching, talking, and exploring."

Watch for

(A) reverses the sequence, and the order matters because a family still at the likeness stage on day 1 is doing exactly what the chapter describes.

(D) reads the first step of claiming — identifying likeness — as a requirement rather than one route among several.

11. A mother says her newborn "cries just to spite me" and "has been nothing but pain since the day he was born," and she holds him at a distance. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • AAs normal postpartum fatigue
  • ✓As negative claiming: some parents claim the infant in terms of the discomfort or pain the infant causes, interpret the infant's normal responses as being negative toward them, and react with dislike or indifference
  • CAs an expected first-day response in all mothers
  • DAs a cultural practice
Rationale

"Some parents react negatively to their newborns. They claim the infant in terms of the discomfort or pain the infant causes. A parent might interpret the infant's normal responses as being negative toward the parents, and the parent may react to the child with dislike or indifference, not holding the child close or touching the child to be comforting."

Watch for

(A) is the explanation that lets an inhibiting pattern pass unremarked, and fatigue is a real risk factor for it — but the behavior described is an assessment finding in its own right.

acquaintance and claimingwhen claiming goes wrong
12. What is the fantasy infant, and why does it matter?
  • AAn imaginary sibling the older child invents
  • ✓The image of the ideal infant that parents develop during pregnancy and often before conception; at birth the fantasy infant becomes the real infant, and how closely the dream child resembles the real child influences the bonding process
  • CA term for the infant's own developing self-image
  • DThe infant as described on ultrasound
Rationale

"During pregnancy and often even before conception, parents develop an image of the ideal or fantasy infant. At birth, the fantasy infant becomes the real infant. How closely the dream child resembles the real child influences the bonding process." A parental task is to "reconcile the actual child with the fantasy and dream child," and "if the real child differs greatly from the fantasy child, parents may delay the acceptance of the child."

Watch for

(D) narrows a psychological construct to an image on a screen, and misses why an infant's sex, temperament, or appearance can slow acceptance.

16. A father avoids sustained direct eye contact with his newborn. What should the nurse consider before interpreting this?
  • AThat eye contact is universally a bonding indicator
  • ✓That in North American culture eye contact appears to reinforce the development of a trusting relationship, but in other cultures sustained direct eye contact is considered rude, immodest, and dangerous — a danger that may arise from the mal de ojo, or evil eye, resulting from excessive admiration
  • CThat avoidance of eye contact always indicates depression

early and extended contactwhat skin-to-skin does and does not do
21. A mother whose infant was taken to the NICU immediately after birth is distressed that she missed the first hour. What should the nurse tell her?
  • AThat the critical bonding window has passed
  • ✓That early contact may facilitate bonding but a delay does not necessarily mean bonding will be inhibited, that no scientific evidence has demonstrated immediate contact is essential for the human parent-child relationship, and that the parent-infant relationship is a process that develops over time
  • CThat bonding is now unlikely without intensive intervention
  • DThat she should not hold the infant until discharge
Rationale

"Although a delay in contact does not necessarily mean that bonding will be inhibited, additional psychologic energy may be necessary to achieve the same effect. To date, no scientific evidence has demonstrated that immediate contact after birth is essential for the human parent-child relationship." Parents who could not have early contact "can be reassured that such contact is not essential for optimal parent-infant interactions," and nurses "need to stress that the parent-infant relationship is a process that develops over time."

Watch for

(A) is the message an over-enthusiastic reading of skin-to-skin teaching sends, and it adds guilt to a separation the mother did not choose.

30. The chapter uses two metaphors for reciprocal interaction. What are they, and what does synchrony achieve?

Rationale

"While all infants attach to their parents, the quality of attachment varies from secure to insecure and disorganized." Secure attachment is built on reciprocal and synchronous serve and return interactions, with the parent staying close and responding consistently, providing "a secure base for the infant's exploration and a safe haven from distress." Quality can be assessed from about 9 months, and "secure attachment is linked to a host of optimal physical and mental health outcomes over the lifespan."

(E) confuses whether attachment forms with how good it is — the attachment system "maintains the proximity of the infant to the caregiver, thus helping to ensure the survival of the otherwise helpless infant," and it operates regardless.
