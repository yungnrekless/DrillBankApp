1. A nurse is classifying risk factors for a pregnant woman. Which definition matches the biophysical category?
  • ✓Factors that originate within the pregnant woman or fetus and affect the development or functioning of either one or both
  • BMaternal behaviors and adverse life events that have a negative effect on health
  • CFactors arising from the context in which the woman and family live
  • DHazards in the workplace and the woman's general environment
Rationale

"Biophysical risks include factors that originate within the pregnant woman or fetus and affect the development or functioning of either one or both," with examples of genetic disorders, nutritional and general health status, and medical or obstetric illnesses.

Watch for

(B), (C), and (D) define the psychosocial, sociodemographic, and environmental categories respectively. The phrase "originate within" is what makes the biophysical category answerable without memorizing every example.

4. A woman has three separate identified risk factors, each described as minor. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • AMinor risks can be disregarded individually and collectively
  • ✓Risk factors are interrelated and cumulative in their effects
  • COnly the most severe factor determines her risk
  • DRisk is determined by counting factors, with three or more defining high risk
Rationale

The chapter states it in one sentence: "Risk factors are interrelated and cumulative in their effects." Categories exist because risk was formerly "evaluated only from a medical standpoint," and the broader grouping reflects threats to health and pregnancy outcome.

(D) turns a qualitative principle into a counting rule, which the chapter does not support — the interaction among factors, not the tally, is the point.

18. A woman is scheduled for a transvaginal ultrasound at 8 weeks. What preparation is needed, and why is this route chosen?
  • AA full bladder is required to displace the uterus upward
  • ✓No full bladder is needed; the transvaginal route is optimal in the first trimester and allows pelvic anatomy to be evaluated in greater detail with earlier diagnosis of intrauterine pregnancy
  • CA full bladder is needed only if the woman is obese
  • DBowel preparation is required
Rationale

The transvaginal approach "does not require that the woman have a full bladder," is "optimally used in the first trimester to detect ectopic pregnancies, monitor the developing embryo, help identify abnormalities, and help establish gestational age," and "allows pelvic anatomic features to be evaluated in greater detail and intrauterine pregnancy to be diagnosed earlier."

Watch for

(A) is the preparation for an abdominal scan, where a full bladder displaces the uterus upward for a better image — and giving that instruction before a transvaginal scan is an uncomfortable and pointless error.

19. Why might a transvaginal scan be chosen for a woman with obesity later in pregnancy?
  • ABecause it is faster
  • ✓Because it is especially useful in obese women whose thick abdominal layers cannot be penetrated adequately
  • CBecause abdominal scanning is contraindicated in obesity
  • DBecause it requires less gel
Rationale

The transvaginal route is "especially useful in obese women whose thick abdominal layers cannot be penetrated adequately." The abdominal approach is "more useful after the first trimester when the pregnant uterus becomes an abdominal organ," and it allows the screen to be positioned so the woman and her partner can watch.

Watch for

(C) overstates a practical limitation into a contraindication. The procedure itself "is not physically painful, although the woman feels pressure as the probe is moved," and a protective cover and water-soluble gel are used.

doppler and amniotic fluid volumemeasuring amniotic fluid
34. An amniotic fluid index of 4 cm is reported. How should this be interpreted, and what would the same number mean as a single deepest pocket?
  • ✓An AFI under 5 cm indicates oligohydramnios; as a single deepest pocket, 4 cm would be normal, since oligohydramnios by that method is less than 2 cm
  • BAn AFI of 4 cm is normal; a single pocket of 4 cm indicates oligohydramnios
  • CBoth indicate polyhydramnios
  • DBoth indicate oligohydramnios
Rationale

"An AFI of less than 5 cm indicates oligohydramnios," where the AFI sums "the vertical depths (in centimeters) of the largest pocket of amniotic fluid in all four quadrants." By the single-pocket method, oligohydramnios is a maximum vertical pocket of less than 2 cm, and polyhydramnios is a pocket greater than 8 cm; by AFI, polyhydramnios is 25 cm or more.

Watch for

(D) applies one system's threshold to the other's number, which is the manufactured error these two measurement systems invite.

  • ✓Amniotic fluid volume, fetal breathing movements, fetal movements, fetal tone, and FHR reactivity by nonstress test; each is scored 2 or 0
  • BThe same five components, each scored 0, 1, or 2
  • CFour ultrasound measures only, each scored 2 or 0
  • DAmniotic fluid volume, placental grade, fetal weight, tone, and heart rate
Rationale

The BPP's components are "AFV, FBMs, fetal movements, and fetal tone determined by ultrasound and fetal heart rate (FHR) reactivity determined by means of the nonstress test (NST)." Table 26.2 scores each variable 2 or 0, so totals are always even and range from 0 to 10. The chapter calls the BPP "a physical examination of the fetus including determination of vital signs."

Watch for

(B) allows a score of 1, which the table never awards — a distractor offering a total of 7 or 9 is impossible by construction and is a free elimination.

38. Why does the chapter distinguish amniotic fluid volume from the other four biophysical profile components?
  • ABecause fluid volume is harder to measure
  • ✓Because FHR reactivity, breathing movements, fetal movement, and tone reflect current CNS status, whereas amniotic fluid volume demonstrates the adequacy of placental function over a longer period of time
  • CBecause fluid volume is scored differently
  • DBecause fluid volume is optional
Rationale

The chapter states it directly: "FHR reactivity, FBMs, fetal movement, and fetal tone reflect current CNS status, whereas the AFV demonstrates the adequacy of placental function over a longer period of time." The BPP is "based on acute and chronic markers of fetal disease."

Watch for

(A) is true — the chapter concedes that measuring fluid accurately is difficult — and it is not the reason for the distinction. The split is about what each marker reports, not how hard it is to obtain.

39. A fetus at 35 weeks scores 8 on the biophysical profile, with the 2 points lost on amniotic fluid volume. How should this be interpreted?
  • AAs the desired result, since a score of 8 is normal
  • ✓As requiring attention, because the desired result is a BPP of 8 or 10 with a normal amniotic fluid volume, and low fluid indicates placental function failing over time
  • CAs requiring repeat testing in 1 week regardless
  • DAs indistinguishable from a score of 8 with normal fluid
Rationale

"A BPP of 8 or 10 with a normal AFV is the desired test result." When the lost points are the fluid points, the acute markers are reassuring while the marker of placental function over time is not — and Table 26.3's score-6 row makes the priority explicit: "deliver if oligohydramnios is present."

Watch for

(D) treats the total as the whole answer, which is exactly what the acute-chronic split forbids. The number does not say which point was lost.

40. A fetus at 37 weeks has a biophysical profile score of 4. What does Table 26.3 direct?
  • ARepeat testing in 1 week
  • ✓Proceed to birth, since the gestational age is 36 weeks or greater
  • CRepeat the score, since the fetus is under 32 weeks
  • DExtend testing time to 120 minutes
Rationale

Table 26.3 interprets a score of 4 as "suspect chronic asphyxia," directing that "if ≥36 weeks of gestation, proceed to birth; if <32 weeks of gestation, repeat score."

Watch for

(D) is the management for a score of 0 to 2, where testing is extended to 120 minutes and "if persistent score ≤4, proceed to birth, regardless of gestational age." That final phrase is the cue that overrides gestational age at the bottom of the ladder.

41. How does the modified biophysical profile differ from the full biophysical profile, and what is the desired result?
  • ✓It combines the nonstress test with measurement of a single deepest pocket of amniotic fluid rather than the four-quadrant AFI; the desired result is a reactive NST and a single deepest vertical pocket greater than 2 cm
  • BIt uses the four-quadrant AFI with the nonstress test
  • CIt omits the nonstress test and uses only ultrasound measures
  • DIt scores all five variables in half the time
Rationale

The modified profile is "a way to shorten the testing time required for the complete BPP by assessing the components that are most predictive of perinatal outcome" — "the NST, which assesses the current fetal condition," plus "measurement of the quantity of amniotic fluid, an indicator of placental function over a longer period of time." The fluid "is determined by measuring a single deepest pocket of fluid instead of using the AFI," and the desired result is "a reactive NST and a single deepest vertical pocket of amniotic fluid that is more than 2 cm."

Watch for

(B) changes the one measurement detail that distinguishes the modified profile, and (C) removes the component that reports the fetus's present condition — leaving only the chronic marker.

42. How does magnetic resonance imaging compare with computed tomography and ultrasound in obstetrics?
  • ✓Like CT it gives excellent soft tissue images but uses no ionizing radiation and needs no iodinated contrast; like ultrasound it is noninvasive and multiplanar, but with no interference from skeletal, fatty, or gas-filled structures and no need for a full bladder
  • BIt uses ionizing radiation but less than CT
  • CIt requires a full bladder like abdominal ultrasound
  • DIt cannot evaluate the placenta
Rationale

"Similar to computed tomography (CT), MRI provides excellent pictures of soft tissue. In contrast to CT, ionizing radiation is not used," and vascular structures can be evaluated "without injecting an iodinated contrast medium." Compared with sonography, "no interference occurs from skeletal, fatty, or gas-filled structures, and imaging of deep pelvic structures does not require a full bladder." It evaluates fetal structure and growth, the placenta, amniotic fluid quantity, and maternal structures.

Watch for

(B) attributes CT's central drawback to the modality chosen to avoid it.

43. A woman is scheduled for fetal MRI. What should the nurse tell her about the procedure and its main limitation?
  • ✓She will lie supine with one hip elevated if possible, for 20 to 60 minutes, staying perfectly still except for short breaks; the main limitation is fetal motion obscuring anatomic detail, which is only reliably prevented by sedating the woman — an approach reserved for selected cases
  • BThe procedure takes 5 minutes and requires no stillness
  • CThe main limitation is a documented decrease in fetal heart rate and movement
  • DShe must fast for 12 hours beforehand
Rationale

The woman "is placed on a table in the supine position with one hip elevated if possible," and "the procedure may take from 20 to 60 minutes, during which time the woman must be perfectly still except for short breaks." The fetus "will probably move, which will obscure anatomic details," and the only reliable solution is a maternal sedative "reserved for selected cases."

Watch for

(C) contradicts the chapter's reassurance: "MRI has little effect on the fetus. Concerns that the FHR or fetal movement would decrease have not been supported by evidence."

44. A woman asks the nurse about having a keepsake three-dimensional ultrasound at a shopping-mall studio. What position do ACOG and AIUM take, and why?
  • ✓They strongly discourage the practice, because exposure of the fetus to high-frequency sound waves without a clear medical indication should be avoided, and because casual ultrasonography by unqualified people could give false reassurance or reveal abnormalities in settings not conducive to discussion and follow-up
  • BThey endorse it as a bonding activity
  • CThey discourage it because ultrasound uses ionizing radiation
  • DThey take no position
Rationale

Both organizations "have published statements that strongly discourage this practice," for the two stated reasons. Diagnostic ultrasound exposure "appears to be safe for the fetus," but "because there is the possibility that unrecognized harm exists, ultrasound should be used only by qualified health professionals to provide medical benefit to clients."

(C) supplies a mechanism ultrasound does not have — that is CT's problem — and it would make the objection easy to dismiss once the woman learns there is no radiation.
