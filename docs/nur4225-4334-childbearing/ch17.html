neuraxial block preparation and labsthe pre-block laboratory and medication review
1. A woman at 5 cm requests an epidural. Before the anesthesia provider arrives, the nurse reviews the chart. Which laboratory result does the chapter direct the nurse to check, and for what reason?
  • AA recent hemoglobin, because anemia deepens the hypotension the block causes
  • ✓A recent platelet count, because thrombocytopenia could cause a hematoma
  • CA type and screen, because an accidental dural puncture may require transfusion
  • DA serum glucose, because the preload fluid can cause maternal hypoglycemia
Rationale

The chapter names exactly one lab and one reason: "A recent platelet count should be reviewed before administering a spinal or epidural, as thrombocytopenia could cause a hematoma." A hematoma in the epidural space can compress the cauda equina or the spinal cord, which is why the count is checked and not merely noted.

Watch for

(A) and (C) are adjacent labs that feel like reasonable pre-procedure workup, and hemoglobin in particular pulls because hemorrhage is separately a contraindication. The chapter names only the platelet count here.

2. A woman who has taken low-dose aspirin daily throughout this pregnancy asks whether she can still have an epidural in labor. Which response reflects the chapter's rule?
  • ALow-dose aspirin is not considered a blood thinner and requires no change
  • BAspirin must be stopped 7 days before neuraxial anesthesia
  • ✓Blood thinners, including low-dose aspirin, should be stopped 12 to 24 hours before neuraxial anesthesia
  • DAspirin is acceptable at any time as long as the platelet count is normal
Rationale

"Women on blood thinners (including low-dose aspirin) should stop the medication 12 to 24 hours before neuraxial anesthesia." The contraindication list states the related rule for low-molecular-weight heparin separately, at a last dose within 12 hours.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex pick because low-dose aspirin is over the counter and feels too small to matter, and (D) substitutes a normal lab for the medication history. The chapter deliberately writes aspirin into the blood-thinner rule rather than exempting it.

3. While cleansing the back of a laboring woman in preparation for an epidural, the nurse notes a large tattoo covering the lower lumbar area. What is the significance of this finding?
  • AIt is cosmetic only and does not affect the procedure
  • BIt requires additional antiseptic scrubbing but nothing further
  • CIt means the block should be placed one interspace higher without further discussion
  • ✓The block should not be initiated at a site where the woman has a tattoo
Rationale

The chapter states it flatly: spinal or epidural anesthesia or analgesia "should not be initiated in a woman who has a tattoo at the site where the needle would be inserted."

Watch for

(A) is the intuitive answer because a tattoo is not an infection, a bleeding risk, or a lab value, and nothing about it feels like a contraindication. It is stated once, concretely, which is exactly what makes it testable.

neuraxial block preparation and labsthe intravenous fluid preload
4. The nurse is preparing a woman for an epidural block. Which intravenous order matches the chapter's preload?
  • ✓500 to 1000 mL of lactated Ringer's or normal saline, 15 to 30 minutes before induction
  • B250 mL of normal saline immediately after the catheter is taped in place
  • C1000 to 2000 mL of lactated Ringer's over the hour preceding induction
  • D500 mL of dextrose 5% in lactated Ringer's, 15 to 30 minutes before induction
Rationale

"500 to 1000 mL of lactated Ringer's solution or normal saline solution may be administered 15 to 30 minutes before induction of the anesthetic to decrease the potential for hypotension caused by sympathetic blockade." The mechanism the chapter gives is vasodilation with pooling of blood in the lower extremities, which decreases cardiac output.

Watch for

(B) inverts the timing — the whole point of a preload is that it precedes the sympathetic blockade rather than chasing it. (D) has the right volume and the right timing but the wrong fluid, which is the next question.

neuraxial block preparation and labsthe intravenous fluid preload
5. An order reads "D5LR 1000 mL bolus prior to epidural placement." The nurse questions the order. Which consequence supports the nurse's concern?
  • ADextrose-containing fluid worsens maternal hypotension after the block
  • ✓Dextrose-containing fluid could contribute to neonatal hypoglycemia
  • CDextrose-containing fluid increases the risk of post-dural puncture headache
  • DDextrose-containing fluid interferes with the epidural test dose
Rationale

The chapter gives one reason and it is a fetal one: fluid used for the bolus "should not contain dextrose, which could contribute to neonatal hypoglycemia." The chapter's second qualifier on the bolus is that "the initiation of spinal anesthesia should not be delayed to deliver a fixed volume of fluid," so the preload never outranks the block itself.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex pick because hypotension is what the bolus exists to prevent, so students look for a maternal hemodynamic consequence. The harm of dextrose here lands on the newborn, not the mother.

maternal hypotension after neuraxial blockrecognizing the drop
6. Ten minutes after an epidural is dosed, a woman whose preblock blood pressure was 118/72 now reads 98/56. Using the chapter's criteria, how should the nurse classify this?
  • ANot hypotension, because the systolic pressure has not fallen below 90 mm Hg
  • BNot hypotension, because the drop is less than 20% of baseline
  • ✓Hypotension, because the systolic pressure is at or below 100 mm Hg
  • DHypotension only if fetal bradycardia is also present
Rationale

The chapter defines it twice and identically: maternal hypotension is a "20% decrease from preblock baseline level or 100 mm Hg systolic or less." A systolic of 98 meets the second criterion on its own; the fetal signs are consequences the nurse looks for next, not part of the definition.

Watch for

(A) imports a 90 mm Hg cutoff from general adult practice, and it is the most common wrong number here. (D) makes the maternal criterion dependent on a fetal finding, when the box lists maternal hypotension and the fetal changes as three separate signs.

7. A different woman had a preblock blood pressure of 142/88. After her spinal block her pressure reads 110/64 and the fetal heart rate shows minimal variability. How should the nurse classify this?
  • ANot hypotension, because the systolic pressure remains above 100 mm Hg
  • ✓Hypotension, because the systolic pressure has fallen more than 20% below her preblock baseline
  • CNot hypotension, because both criteria must be met before the emergency protocol applies
  • DHypotension, but only because of the fetal heart rate change
Rationale

Twenty percent of a 142 mm Hg baseline is roughly 28 mm Hg, so anything at or below about 114 meets the percentage criterion. Either criterion alone is sufficient, and this woman's pressure never approaches 100. Absent or minimal fetal heart rate variability is one of the box's listed accompanying signs.

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error of the previous item — there the fixed number fired and the percentage did not, here the percentage fires and the fixed number does not. (C) is the reflex of a reader who saw "or" and read "and"; requiring both criteria would miss both of these patients.

maternal hypotension after neuraxial blockthe emergency sequence
8. A woman becomes hypotensive with fetal bradycardia three minutes after her epidural is dosed. Which action does the nurse take first?
  • AAdminister ephedrine 5 to 10 mg intravenously
  • BElevate the woman's legs
  • CNotify the obstetric and anesthesia care providers
  • ✓Turn the woman to the lateral position or place a wedge under one hip
Rationale

Position is the first intervention in the Emergency box, and the chapter supplies the mechanism: with the woman supine, the uterus compresses the ascending vena cava and descending aorta, which "impair[s] venous return, reduce[s] cardiac output and blood pressure, and decrease[s] placental perfusion." Displacing the uterus corrects the cause rather than masking the number.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex pick because a vasopressor is the intervention that most obviously raises a blood pressure. The box places it last and conditions it: give a vasopressor "if previous measures have been ineffective," which means four steps come before it.

9. The nurse is applying oxygen to a woman with hypotension and fetal bradycardia after a neuraxial block. Which delivery matches the Emergency box?
  • A2 L/min by nasal cannula
  • B6 L/min by simple face mask
  • ✓10 to 12 L/min by nonrebreather face mask
  • D4 L/min by nasal cannula, titrated to a saturation above 95%
Rationale

The box specifies "oxygen by nonrebreather face mask at 10–12 L/min or per protocol." The same device and range reappear in the NURSING ALERT covering epidural opioids, where a nonrebreather mask is used for a respiratory rate under 12 or a saturation under 89%.

Watch for

(A) is the everyday order a student has seen most often on a medical floor, and it is both the wrong device and the wrong flow for an intrauterine resuscitation. This emergency is about placental perfusion, not comfort.

10. Previous measures have not restored the woman's blood pressure and the provider orders a vasopressor. Which order is consistent with the chapter?
  • AEphedrine 50 to 100 mcg intravenously
  • BPhenylephrine 5 to 10 mg intravenously
  • ✓Ephedrine 5 to 10 mg or phenylephrine 50 to 100 mcg intravenously
  • DEphedrine 5 to 10 mcg or phenylephrine 50 to 100 mg intravenously
Rationale

The box names both agents with their own units: "ephedrine 5–10 mg or phenylephrine 50–100 mcg per protocol if previous measures have been ineffective." Milligrams for ephedrine, micrograms for phenylephrine.

Watch for

(A) and (B) each swap the two drugs' units onto the wrong agent, which is a plausible near-miss precisely because the numbers 5-to-10 and 50-to-100 are both correct and only the pairing is wrong. Read the unit, not the number.

11. Select all interventions the Emergency box for maternal hypotension with decreased placental perfusion directs the nurse to perform.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • APlace the woman in high Fowler position to improve ventilation
  • ✓Turn the woman to the lateral position or place a pillow or wedge under one hip
  • ✓Maintain the intravenous infusion at the rate specified, or increase the rate per protocol
  • ✓Elevate the woman's legs
  • ✓Remain with the woman and monitor blood pressure and fetal heart rate every 5 minutes until stable
Rationale

The box's seven interventions are, in order: reposition laterally or wedge the hip, maintain or increase the intravenous rate, oxygen by nonrebreather at 10 to 12 L/min, elevate the legs, notify the obstetric and anesthesia providers, give a vasopressor if previous measures failed, and remain with the woman monitoring every 5 minutes.

(A) is the one wrong option and it inverts two of the box's interventions at once — sitting the woman up neither displaces the uterus nor elevates the legs, and it worsens the venous return problem the whole sequence exists to correct.

12. A woman in the first stage of labor at 4 cm asks about her options for pain relief. According to Box 17.4, which method is listed only for cesarean birth and is therefore not among them?
  • AEpidural block analgesia
  • ✓General anesthesia
  • CNitrous oxide
  • DCombined spinal-epidural analgesia
Rationale

Box 17.4 sorts methods by stage of labor and method of birth, and general anesthesia appears in one row only — cesarean birth. That single placement is the reason the chapter carries a general-anesthesia section at all. Combined spinal-epidural appears in the first stage, the second stage, and vaginal birth, but notably not in the cesarean row.

Watch for

(D) pulls because combined spinal-epidural sounds like the most invasive option on the list and students expect the most invasive method to be the cesarean one. The box places it everywhere except cesarean, and reserves general anesthesia for cesarean alone.

neuraxial technique selection in laborspinal versus epidural
13. A woman is scheduled for a repeat cesarean birth and receives a subarachnoid block. Which description matches what was done?
  • ✓Anesthetic was injected through the third, fourth, or fifth lumbar interspace, where it mixes with cerebrospinal fluid
  • BA catheter was threaded into the peridural space between the fourth and fifth lumbar vertebrae and taped in place
  • CA large-bore 16- to 18-gauge needle was left in place for intermittent redosing
  • DAnesthetic was infused continuously by pump through an indwelling plastic catheter
Rationale

Spinal anesthesia injects "through the third, fourth, or fifth lumbar interspace into the subarachnoid space," where the solution "mixes with cerebrospinal fluid." It is usually a single injection; the effect is complete and fixed within 5 to 10 minutes and lasts 1 to 3 hours.

Watch for

(B), (C) and (D) all describe the epidural, which is the catheter technique. The pairing to hold is that the spinal enters the subarachnoid space and mixes with cerebrospinal fluid, while the epidural stops short of the dura in the peridural space and leaves a catheter behind.

14. After threading an epidural catheter, the anesthesia provider injects a small amount of medication and waits. The woman asks what that was for. Which explanation is accurate?
  • AIt confirms that the analgesic dose will be adequate for her level of pain
  • ✓It confirms the catheter has not been accidentally placed in the subarachnoid space or in a blood vessel
  • CIt confirms the catheter tip has reached the T10 dermatome
  • DIt confirms she is not allergic to the local anesthetic agent
Rationale

The test dose exists "to make sure that the catheter has not been accidentally placed in the subarachnoid (spinal) space or in a blood vessel." It is a check on catheter position, and it screens for two specific misplacements.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex reading of the word "test" — students hear a dose being tested for effect rather than a location being excluded. (D) borrows the allergy question from a different part of the chapter; allergy to the anesthetic drug is a listed contraindication, not what the test dose detects.

neuraxial technique selection in laborspinal versus epidural
15. A woman in early labor at 3 cm asks for "the strongest thing you have." Which statement about spinal anesthesia is accurate?
  • ✓Low spinal anesthesia may be used for vaginal birth but is not suitable for labor
  • BSpinal anesthesia is suitable for all stages of labor and all types of birth
  • CSpinal anesthesia is preferred in the first stage because its onset is faster than an epidural
  • DSpinal anesthesia is the only neuraxial technique that preserves the pushing reflex
Rationale

The chapter states it directly: "Low spinal anesthesia (block) may be used for vaginal birth, but it is not suitable for labor." The epidural is the technique described as suitable for all stages of labor and types of birth, because it is titratable through an indwelling catheter while a spinal is a single fixed injection lasting 1 to 3 hours.

Watch for

(C) is the trap built out of a true fact: a spinal really does set up faster, complete and fixed within 5 to 10 minutes. Speed of onset does not make it suitable for a labor of unknown length, which is what the fixed duration decides.

16. An epidural is being placed for a woman who plans a vaginal birth. Which block level does the chapter specify for relieving the discomfort of labor and vaginal birth?
  • AT6 to S1
  • BT4 to T10
  • ✓T10 to S5
  • DT8 to S1
Rationale

For "relieving the discomfort of labor and vaginal birth," the chapter specifies "a block from T10 to S5." The sacral extension is what covers the perineum for the second stage.

Watch for

(D) is the cesarean level and is the single most available wrong answer, because both begin with a thoracic dermatome and differ by two numbers at each end. The rule to hold is directional: the higher the upper level, the more it is a surgical block.

17. The same woman's labor arrests and a cesarean birth is planned using her existing epidural catheter. Which block level is now required?
  • ✓At least T8 to S1
  • BT10 to S5, which is already established
  • CT12 to S5
  • DAt least T4 to L1
Rationale

For cesarean birth the chapter specifies "a block from at least T8 to S1," which is higher than the T10 to S5 labor block and is why the catheter must be redosed rather than simply continued. Diffusion is determined by the location of the catheter tip, the dose and volume of anesthetic, and the woman's position.

Watch for

(B) is the mirror error of the previous item — assuming a working labor epidural is automatically a surgical block. The equivalent spinal levels move the same direction: T10 at the hips for vaginal birth, up to T6 at the nipple line for cesarean.

epidural block nursing caregeneral
18. A woman with a well-functioning epidural suddenly reports intense rectal pressure and says she feels like she needs to move her bowels. Which interpretation is correct?
  • AThe epidural has failed and the anesthesia provider should rebolus the catheter
  • BThe catheter has migrated into a blood vessel
  • ✓This is expected; epidurals do not remove the pressure sensations of fetal descent, and the nurse should assess for second-stage progress
  • DThis indicates local anesthetic toxicity and the infusion should be stopped
Rationale

The NURSING ALERT is explicit: epidural anesthesia "effectively relieves the pain caused by uterine contractions," but for most women "it does not completely remove the pressure sensations that occur as the fetus descends in the pelvis." Pressure in a woman with a working epidural is a descent finding.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex pick because a patient who was comfortable now reports a new sensation, which reads as a block wearing off. Distinguishing contraction pain from descent pressure is exactly what the alert exists to teach, and treating descent as block failure delays recognizing the second stage.

19. A woman with a continuous epidural infusion reaches full dilation. Which nursing action does the chapter specify?
  • ADiscontinue the infusion so she can feel the urge to push
  • ✓Instruct her when to bear down, because she is unable to sense her contractions
  • CRely on her report of the urge to push to time each effort
  • DPlace her supine to maximize her expulsive effort
Rationale

"Because the woman is unable to sense her contractions, she must be instructed when to bear down during a vaginal birth." The same reasoning drives the monitoring plan: the fetal heart rate, contraction pattern, and labor progress must be watched carefully "because the woman may not be aware of changes in the strength of the uterine contractions or the descent of the presenting part."

Watch for

(C) is the reflex pick for anyone taught to follow the woman's own urge, which is correct for an unmedicated second stage and fails here. (D) contradicts the chapter's positioning rule and reintroduces the aortocaval compression the lateral position exists to prevent.

20. A woman with an epidural in place asks to walk to the bathroom. Which response reflects the chapter?
  • AAmbulation is contraindicated for the duration of any epidural block
  • BShe may ambulate freely once she can lift both legs off the bed
  • ✓Upright positions and ambulation may be possible depending on motor impairment, but never without assistance
  • DShe may ambulate only after the infusion has been off for one hour
Rationale

"Upright positions and ambulation may be possible depending on the degree of motor impairment. However, ambulation should never be attempted without assistance." While in bed, the woman is positioned preferably on her side, and her position is "alternated from side to side every hour" so the uterus does not compress the vena cava and aorta.

Watch for

(A) overcorrects into a blanket prohibition the chapter does not make, and it is the safer-sounding option, which is why it pulls. The chapter permits ambulation and conditions it; the absolute in the sentence attaches to "without assistance," not to walking itself.

epidural block nursing caregeneral
21. An epidural has just been dosed. How often does the chapter direct the nurse to assess and document maternal and fetal status, and for how long?
  • ✓Every 5 to 10 minutes for the first 15 minutes
  • BEvery 15 minutes for the first hour
  • CEvery 5 minutes for the first hour
  • DEvery 30 minutes until the block level is fixed
Rationale

Maternal "blood pressure, pulse, and respirations as well as FHR and pattern must be assessed and documented every 5 to 10 minutes for the first 15 minutes." The chapter adds that more frequent monitoring for longer may be necessary depending on the maternal-fetal condition and response.

Watch for

(B) and (D) are both too slow for the window in which sympathetic blockade produces its hypotension, and (B) is the interval a student may carry over from routine labor monitoring. Note that the assessment is explicitly both maternal and fetal, not vital signs alone.

epidural and intrathecal opioidsgeneral
22. A woman receives fentanyl alone through her epidural catheter, with no local anesthetic. Which set of findings should the nurse expect?
  • AHypotension, dense motor block, and loss of the urge to push
  • ✓Vital signs unaffected, contractions felt but not painful, and the pushing reflex preserved
  • CHypotension requiring a fluid preload, with motor power intact
  • DComplete loss of sensation from T10 to S5 with preserved motor function
Rationale

Opioids given this way "do not cause hypotension or affect vital signs but rapidly produce pain relief. Contractions are often felt, but they are not painful. The ability to bear down during the second stage of labor is preserved because the pushing reflex is not lost and motor power remains intact."

Watch for

(A) describes the local-anesthetic block and is the reflex pick because "epidural" and "hypotension" are welded together by everything else in this chapter. The route is the same; what determines the hemodynamic profile is which drug went down the catheter.

23. A second woman receives bupivacaine through her epidural catheter. Which set of findings should the nurse expect, in contrast?
  • AVital signs unaffected, with the pushing reflex fully preserved
  • BNo fluid preload needed, because sympathetic tone is unchanged
  • ✓Hypotension from sympathetic blockade, motor block, and reduced ability to sense contractions
  • DRapid analgesia with no effect on the second stage
Rationale

The local anesthetic is what blocks the sympathetic fibers, which produces the vasodilation and pooling that the 500 to 1000 mL preload is given to offset, and it is what produces motor block and the loss of contraction awareness that makes coached pushing necessary.

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error of the previous item — carrying the opioid-only profile onto the local-anesthetic block. The one honest complication is that opioids alone "usually do not provide adequate analgesia," which is why in practice the two are combined.

24. An order combines fentanyl with bupivacaine in the epidural infusion. A student asks why both are used. Which explanation is correct?
  • AThe combination deepens the motor block and produces a more complete anesthetic
  • ✓The combination reduces the dose of anesthetic required, preserving a greater degree of motor function
  • CThe combination eliminates the need for a fluid preload
  • DThe combination shortens the second stage of labor
Rationale

"The combination of an opioid with the local anesthetic agent reduces the dose of anesthetic required, thereby preserving a greater degree of motor function," and the chapter connects that directly to pushing: it "reduces the degree of motor function loss, enhancing the woman's ability to push effectively."

Watch for

(A) inverts the purpose, and it is the intuitive inversion because adding a second drug reads as adding depth. The opioid is there so that less local anesthetic is needed, which means less motor block, not more. (D) is contradicted by Box 17.5, which lists longer second-stage labor as a side effect of neuraxial anesthesia.

epidural and intrathecal opioidsgeneral
25. A woman received epidural morphine after a cesarean birth. The catheter was removed in the recovery room. How should the nurse plan respiratory monitoring?
  • ✓Assess respiratory status hourly for 24 hours, because respiratory depression can be delayed
  • BAssess respiratory status until the catheter is removed, after which the risk ends
  • CAssess respiratory status every 4 hours for 24 hours
  • DAssess respiratory status only if the woman reports pruritus or nausea
Rationale

"Respiratory depression is a serious concern; for this reason the woman's respiratory status should be assessed and documented every hour for 24 hours or as designated by hospital protocol." The chapter names delayed respiratory depression among the side effects of epidural and intrathecal opioids and notes that these effects "are more common when morphine is administered."

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick because removing the catheter feels like ending the exposure. The whole reason the monitoring rule exists is that the depression is delayed, so it can appear well after the drug stopped being given. Breakthrough pain in that first 24 hours is treated with a nonsteroidal antiinflammatory drug such as ketorolac rather than another opioid.

26. Naloxone is given to a newly delivered woman for respiratory depression after intravenous opioid analgesia. Her respiratory rate improves promptly. Which nursing implication follows?
  • AThe reversal is definitive and routine monitoring may resume
  • ✓The duration of naloxone is shorter than that of most opioids, so she must be monitored for a return of respiratory depression
  • CThe duration of naloxone exceeds that of most opioids, so oversedation from the antagonist is now the concern
  • DA second dose should be given routinely at 30 minutes regardless of her status
Rationale

"The duration of action of naloxone is shorter than that of most opioids. Therefore close monitoring for the return of opioid depression when the effects of naloxone are gone is needed. Additional doses of naloxone may be necessary to maintain reversal." Adult dosing is 0.4 to 2 mg intravenously, repeated at 2- to 3-minute intervals to a maximum of 10 mg.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex pick because a visible clinical improvement reads as a problem solved. (C) inverts the relative durations, which is the one fact that makes this drug a monitoring problem rather than a one-time fix.

opioid antagonists in laborgeneral
27. A woman with a documented opioid use disorder receives intravenous analgesia in labor and becomes sedated with a respiratory rate of 9. Which consideration applies?
  • ANaloxone is the drug of choice and should be given immediately at full dose
  • ✓Naloxone is contraindicated because it may precipitate abstinence syndrome, and butorphanol and nalbuphine are excluded for the same reason
  • CNaloxone is safe here, but butorphanol and nalbuphine would not have been
  • DNaloxone should be given only after the newborn is delivered
Rationale

The MEDICATION ALERT states that an opioid antagonist "is contraindicated for opioid-dependent women because it may precipitate abstinence syndrome (withdrawal symptoms). For the same reason, opioid agonist-antagonist analgesics such as butorphanol (Stadol) and nalbuphine (Nubain) should not be given to opioid-dependent women."

Watch for

(C) is the near-miss that gets the second half right and the first half backwards. The rule is one rule covering both drug groups, and extending it past naloxone to the agonist-antagonists is what makes it testable. Maternal withdrawal presents as weakness, fatigue and drowsiness, nausea and vomiting, diarrhea and abdominal cramps, and bone and muscle pain with muscle spasms and kicking movements.

28. A newborn is depressed at birth after the mother received meperidine in labor. Which statement about naloxone applies here?
  • ANaloxone will fully reverse the effect, because meperidine is a pure opioid agonist
  • ✓Naloxone cannot reverse the effects of normeperidine, an active metabolite of meperidine
  • CNaloxone is the recommended first-line treatment for any depressed newborn
  • DNaloxone should be given to the mother instead, which will reverse the newborn's depression
Rationale

The chapter states that "naloxone cannot reverse the effects of normeperidine, an active metabolite of meperidine," and notes that both meperidine and normeperidine have long half-lives. Separately, naloxone in newborns has "insufficient evidence to evaluate the safety and efficacy," with reported concerns including pulmonary edema, cardiac arrest, and seizures.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex pick because meperidine is an opioid and naloxone is the opioid antagonist, so the reversal looks automatic. (C) states a practice the chapter explicitly does not endorse for the newborn.

29. A woman who received an opioid agonist an hour ago is now being given naloxone for respiratory depression. What should the nurse tell her to expect?
  • AHer pain relief will continue while her breathing improves
  • ✓The pain that was relieved by the opioid will return with the antagonist
  • CShe will feel more comfortable, because naloxone has its own analgesic effect
  • DHer pain will return gradually over the next several hours
Rationale

"The woman should be told that the pain that was relieved with the use of the opioid analgesic will return with the administration of the opioid antagonist." The chapter adds a second cost that compounds it: the antagonist "counters the effect of the stress-induced levels of endorphins," so her own endogenous relief is blocked too.

Watch for

(A) is a reassurance that feels kind and is wrong on the chapter's own terms, and it is the option a nurse reaches for when the patient is frightened. Preparing her for the return of pain is the teaching the chapter actually requires.

30. A woman with epidural fentanyl in place has a respiratory rate of 10 breaths/min and an oxygen saturation of 87%. Which set of actions matches the NURSING ALERT?
  • AContinue monitoring and reassess in 15 minutes
  • BStop the infusion and reposition her laterally as the sole intervention
  • ✓Have naloxone available and administer it, apply oxygen by nonrebreather face mask, and notify the anesthesia care provider
  • DGive oxygen by nasal cannula at 2 L/min and notify the anesthesia care provider
Rationale

"Naloxone should be readily available for use if the respiratory rate decreases to less than 12 breaths/min or if the oxygen saturation rate decreases to less than 89%. Administration of oxygen by a nonrebreather face mask can also be initiated, and the anesthesia care provider should be notified." Both of this woman's numbers are below the stated thresholds.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex pick for anyone who reads a rate of 10 as merely low rather than as below a stated action threshold. Note that 12 breaths/min is an adult number and does not transfer to the newborn, whose normal range is 30 to 60.

opioid antagonists in laborgeneral
31. Which statement about naloxone's pharmacology and use matches the Medication Guide?
  • ✓It blocks both mu and kappa opioid receptors from the effects of opioid agonists, and may be used to reverse pruritus from epidural opioids
  • BIt is a partial agonist that competes with opioids at the mu receptor only
  • CIt is given intramuscularly as first choice, with intravenous administration reserved for failure
  • DBreastfeeding may resume immediately after administration
Rationale

The Medication Guide classifies naloxone as an opioid antagonist whose action is to block "both μ and κ opioid receptors from the effects of opioid agonists," and its indication includes reversing opioid-induced respiratory depression and pruritus from epidural opioids. Adverse effects listed for the laboring woman include hypotension or hypertension, tachycardia, hyperventilation, nausea and vomiting, sweating, and tremulousness.

Watch for

(C) inverts the route hierarchy — intravenous is first, with intramuscular or subcutaneous used only "if IV route is unavailable." (D) contradicts the guide's instruction to delay breastfeeding until the medication is out of her system, approximately 2 hours after the last dose.

32. Two days after an epidural, a postpartum woman reports a severe headache. Which description would most support post-dural puncture headache?
  • AThe headache is worst when she lies flat and eases when she sits up
  • ✓The headache begins when she sits or stands and is relieved when she lies down
  • CThe headache is constant regardless of position and worsens with light
  • DThe headache is worst in the morning and improves through the day
Rationale

The positional signature is the diagnostic feature: "assuming an upright position triggers or intensifies the headache, whereas assuming a supine position achieves pain relief." Onset is within 2 days of the puncture and it may persist for days or weeks. Associated auditory problems such as tinnitus and visual problems such as blurred vision and photophobia are common.

Watch for

(A) reverses the direction and describes a different kind of headache entirely. The mechanism makes the direction predictable: cerebrospinal fluid leaks out through the dural hole, so upright posture is what drops the pressure and hurts.

33. A nurse is surprised that a woman who received an epidural, not a spinal, has developed what the unit calls a spinal headache. Which explanation is accurate?
  • AThe terms are interchangeable and the technique does not affect the risk
  • BAny neuraxial block carries an identical risk of dural puncture
  • ✓The epidural needle is much larger in gauge, so an accidental dural puncture creates a bigger opening and a greater loss of cerebrospinal fluid
  • DThe epidural catheter erodes the dura over the hours it remains in place
Rationale

The epidural needle is a large-bore 16-, 17-, or 18-gauge needle, and "has a much larger gauge than the one used for spinal anesthesia and thus creates a bigger opening in the dura, resulting in a greater loss of CSF." Post-dural puncture headache is "much more likely to occur when the dura is accidentally punctured during the process of administering an epidural block" — the wet tap.

Watch for

(A) rests on the drug-store name rather than the mechanism, and the name is genuinely misleading: the headache called a spinal headache is more likely after an attempted epidural than after a spinal. Prevention on the spinal side is a small-gauge needle.

34. A woman with a post-dural puncture headache has not improved after 24 hours of oral analgesics and caffeine. Which intervention does the chapter identify as the most rapid, reliable, and beneficial relief measure?
  • AStrict bed rest in the prone position with aggressive oral hydration
  • ✓An autologous epidural blood patch, using 20 mL of the woman's own blood
  • CAn increased dose of methylxanthines
  • DRepositioning the epidural catheter and redosing it
Rationale

"An autologous epidural blood patch is the most rapid, reliable, and beneficial relief measure." Twenty milliliters of the woman's blood is injected slowly into the lumbar epidural space, "creating a clot that patches the tear or hole in the dura mater." Oral analgesics and methylxanthines such as caffeine are conservative management; caffeine works by constricting the cerebral blood vessels.

Watch for

(A) is the intervention that feels most nursing-appropriate and least invasive, and the chapter specifically retires it: hydration and bed rest in the prone position "have been recommended as preventive measures but have proven to be of little value."

  • AObserve for 15 minutes, then encourage early ambulation
  • ✓Observe for 1 hour for altered vital signs, pallor, clammy skin, and cerebrospinal fluid leakage, and instruct her to avoid coughing or straining for the first day

(C) reimports the prone bed rest the chapter has already dismissed as ineffective for prevention, which makes it the most familiar-sounding wrong answer here. The observation window is one hour, not one day.

38. A woman with a placental abruption and ongoing bleeding requests an epidural. Which reasoning explains why the block is contraindicated?

The chapter gives the mechanism with the contraindication: "Acute hypovolemia leads to increased sympathetic tone to maintain the blood pressure. 40. A woman in prolonged latent-phase labor is exhausted and asks for something to help with the pain. The provider orders zolpidem. What should the nurse understand about this order?
  • AZolpidem is a barbiturate and will provide analgesia along with sedation
  • ✓Sedatives relieve anxiety and induce sleep but do not provide analgesia; zolpidem is given here to provide rest
  • CZolpidem will potentiate any opioid she receives later
  • DZolpidem is contraindicated in labor because it crosses the placenta
Rationale

"Sedative medications relieve anxiety and induce sleep, but they do not provide analgesia." Zolpidem is "not related to barbiturates" but has the sedative effect, and is "sometimes given to women with prolonged latent phase labor or hypertonic uterine dysfunction to provide rest."

Watch for

(A) makes both available errors at once — misclassifying zolpidem with the barbiturates and assuming a drug given for discomfort must relieve pain. Barbiturates such as secobarbital cross the placenta, have a long half-life, and potentiate respiratory depression, which is why they are seldom used in obstetrics.

41. A provider asks the nurse to add a medication to potentiate the effect of an opioid analgesic in labor. Which agent does the chapter support, and which does it warn against?
  • APromethazine is supported; metoclopramide has no evidence behind it
  • BBoth promethazine and metoclopramide are supported equally
  • ✓Metoclopramide may be a better choice; routine promethazine with opioids should be avoided to prevent excessive sedation
  • DNeither should be used, because no antiemetic potentiates an analgesic
Rationale

Promethazine does not relieve pain, and although it was historically added to opioids, "there is no research evidence to support this practice. The routine use of promethazine along with opioids should be avoided to prevent excessive sedation." Metoclopramide, an antiemetic, "has been found to effectively potentiate the effects of analgesics. Therefore it may be a better choice than promethazine."

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error and it is the one long practice produces, because the promethazine-plus-opioid combination was standard for years. The chapter retires it by name and supplies the replacement.

42. A benzodiazepine is proposed for a woman in active labor. Which reason does the chapter give for avoiding it?
  • ✓Benzodiazepines cause significant maternal amnesia
  • BBenzodiazepines cause maternal respiratory depression more than any other class
  • CBenzodiazepines have no effect on nausea and vomiting
  • DBenzodiazepines fail to cross the placenta and are therefore ineffective
Rationale

Given with an opioid, benzodiazepines "seem to enhance pain relief and reduce nausea and vomiting. Because benzodiazepines cause significant maternal amnesia, however, their use should be avoided during labor." The separate newborn harm is thermal: diazepam "disrupts thermoregulation in newborns," making them less able to maintain body temperature.

Watch for

(B) borrows the barbiturates' stated problem, and it is the reflex answer because respiratory depression is the sedative harm students memorize first. The reason to withhold a benzodiazepine in labor is that the woman will not remember her birth. (C) contradicts the chapter, which credits them with reducing nausea and vomiting.

The three components are that the procedure and its advantages and disadvantages "must be thoroughly explained in a manner and language the woman can understand," that she "must agree with the plan of pain management as it was explained," and that her consent "must be given freely without coercion or manipulation from her health care provider."

(A) is a comprehension check, and comprehension is already covered by the first component; the third component is about freedom, not understanding, and confusing the two collapses three requirements into two.
