1. Which two hemorrhagic disorders does the chapter name as having the highest incidence of maternal mortality?
  • AMiscarriage and cervical insufficiency
  • ✓Ruptured ectopic pregnancy and placental abruption
  • CMolar pregnancy and placenta previa
  • DPlacenta previa and disseminated intravascular coagulation
Rationale

"Ruptured ectopic pregnancy and abruptio placentae (placental abruption) have the highest incidence of maternal mortality." The chapter also states that "hemorrhagic disorders in pregnancy are medical emergencies."

Watch for

(C) pairs the two disorders that bleed most visibly. Visible bleeding and mortality are not the same ranking — a ruptured ectopic and a severe abruption can both bleed internally.

2. A woman at 22 weeks reports light vaginal bleeding and feels well otherwise. What does the chapter's key point direct?
  • ✓Vaginal bleeding during pregnancy should always be regarded as a warning sign, and determining the cause is a priority
  • BLight bleeding after 20 weeks is a normal variant requiring no workup
  • CBleeding is significant only when accompanied by pain
  • DBleeding is significant only in the first trimester
Rationale

The chapter's key point reads: "Vaginal bleeding during pregnancy should always be regarded as a warning sign, and determining the cause is a priority." Blood loss decreases oxygen-carrying capacity and puts the woman at risk for hypovolemia, anemia, infection, and preterm labor, and the fetus at risk for hypoxemia, hypoxia, anoxia, and preterm birth.

Watch for

(C) is a reasonable-sounding filter that would miss placenta previa entirely, since previa bleeding is characteristically painless.

4. Where are the vast majority of ectopic pregnancies located, and which part of that structure is most common?
  • ✓The uterine tube, with approximately 70% in the ampulla
  • BThe ovary, with most on the surface
  • CThe abdominal cavity, with most near the omentum
  • DThe cervix, at the external os
Rationale

Ectopic pregnancies "are often called tubal pregnancies because the vast majority are located in the uterine (fallopian) tube," and "of all tubal ectopic pregnancies, approximately 70% are located in the ampulla, or largest portion of the tube." Ectopic pregnancies account for about 2% of all pregnancies in the United States and 3% of pregnancy-related maternal deaths.

Watch for

(C) names a real but rare site. Fig. 28.3 gives the frequency order — ampulla, isthmus, interstitium, fimbria, tuboovarian ligament, ovary, abdominal cavity, cervix — and abdominal pregnancies are "thought to be the result of early tubal rupture, followed by reimplantation."

6. A woman presents with dull right lower quadrant pain, a period that was 10 days late and lighter than usual, and dark brown intermittent spotting. What should the nurse suspect?
  • ✓Ectopic pregnancy, which classically presents with abdominal pain, delayed menses, and abnormal vaginal bleeding
  • BPlacenta previa
  • CMolar pregnancy
  • DNormal early pregnancy
Rationale

The three most classic symptoms are abdominal pain, delayed menses, and abnormal vaginal bleeding or spotting. "Abdominal pain occurs in almost every case. It usually begins as a dull, lower quadrant pain on one side," and most women report a period "delayed 1 to 2 weeks or lighter than usual, or an irregular period," with mild-to-moderate dark red or brown intermittent bleeding.

Watch for

(D) is what this presentation is most often mistaken for, which is why the chapter sets a screening rule: "every woman with abdominal pain, vaginal spotting or bleeding, and a positive pregnancy test should undergo screening for ectopic pregnancy."

7. A woman with a suspected ectopic pregnancy develops sharp shoulder pain along with lower abdominal pain. What does the shoulder pain indicate?
  • AReferred pain from a musculoskeletal strain
  • ✓Diaphragmatic irritation caused by blood in the peritoneal cavity, indicating rupture
  • CA cardiac event
  • DNormal round ligament pain
Rationale

"Referred shoulder pain results from diaphragmatic irritation caused by blood in the peritoneal cavity." It is a sign of rupture, appearing with generalized or deep lower quadrant acute abdominal pain.

(D) is the benign explanation a nurse might reach for in early pregnancy, and here it would delay recognition of an intraabdominal hemorrhage.

8. A woman with a ruptured ectopic pregnancy is faint and dizzy but has only scant vaginal bleeding. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • AHer symptoms are disproportionate and probably anxiety-related
  • ✓Signs of shock relate to the amount of bleeding in the abdominal cavity and not necessarily to frank vaginal bleeding
  • CScant bleeding rules out significant hemorrhage
  • DShock cannot occur without visible bleeding
Rationale

The woman "may exhibit signs of shock such as faintness and dizziness related to the amount of bleeding in the abdominal cavity and not necessarily related to frank vaginal bleeding." Cullen sign — "an ecchymotic blueness around the umbilicus" — indicates hemoperitoneum, and signs of internal bleeding include vertigo, shoulder pain, hypotension, and tachycardia.

Watch for

(C) uses visible blood as the measure of blood loss, which is the same error that makes a severe concealed abruption look stable later in this chapter.

9. A woman with abdominal pain has a beta-hCG level of 2200 milli-international units/mL, and transvaginal ultrasound shows no intrauterine pregnancy. How should this be interpreted?
  • ✓An ectopic pregnancy is very likely, because a normal intrauterine pregnancy should be visible above the discriminatory level
  • BThe pregnancy is too early to visualize and no action is needed
  • CThe findings confirm a molar pregnancy
  • DThe beta-hCG is too low to interpret
Rationale

"The term discriminatory zone addresses the concept that there is a beta-hCG level above which a normal intrauterine pregnancy should be visible on ultrasound," given as greater than 1500 to 2000 milli-international units/mL. Above 1500 with no intrauterine pregnancy seen, "an ectopic pregnancy is very likely." Serial levels may be redrawn every 48 hours to determine viability.

Watch for

(B) is the correct reasoning below the discriminatory level and the wrong reasoning above it — which is the entire point of having a threshold.

10. A serum progesterone level returns at 4 ng/mL in a woman being evaluated for possible ectopic pregnancy. What does this suggest, and what value would point the other way?
  • ✓It suggests an ectopic or abnormal intrauterine pregnancy; a level greater than 25 ng/mL almost always rules out an ectopic pregnancy
  • BIt rules out ectopic pregnancy; a level greater than 25 ng/mL would confirm one
  • CIt is a normal value with no diagnostic meaning
  • DIt confirms a viable intrauterine pregnancy
Rationale

A progesterone level "less than 6 ng/mL suggests either an ectopic pregnancy or an abnormal intrauterine pregnancy," while a level "greater than 25 ng/mL almost always rules out the presence of an ectopic pregnancy."

Watch for

(B) inverts the two thresholds. High progesterone is reassuring here, which runs against the instinct that a higher number means a worse result.

14. A woman treated with methotrexate asks for a stronger pain medication than acetaminophen. What does the Safety Alert direct, and why?

  • DNo analgesia is permitted at all
Rationale

"Women receiving methotrexate to treat an ectopic pregnancy should refrain from taking any analgesic stronger than acetaminophen. Stronger analgesics can mask symptoms of tubal rupture." 16. What should the nurse teach about methotrexate excretion after an intramuscular dose?
  • ✓Urine contains drug metabolite at levels considered toxic for approximately 72 hours, highest during the first 8 hours; avoid getting urine on the toilet seat and double flush with the lid down; stools may contain residual drug for up to 7 days
  • BThe drug is fully excreted within 2 hours and no precautions are needed
  • COnly stool precautions are needed, for 24 hours
  • DPrecautions are required for 30 days
Rationale

Box 28.2 teaches that "urine contains levels of drug metabolite that could be considered toxic for approximately 72 hours after receiving methotrexate. (B) treats an intramuscular dose as though it were gone once the injection is over. Methotrexate is a hazardous, high-alert drug, which is also why it is prepared in the pharmacy under a biologic safety cabinet and given with two pairs of gloves and an independent double check.

17. What follow-up does the chapter specify after methotrexate treatment, and how long does resolution take?
  • ✓A beta-hCG level on posttreatment days 4 and 7 and then weekly, until undetectable; complete resolution usually occurs within 7 weeks
  • BA single beta-hCG level at 6 weeks
  • CMonthly beta-hCG levels for 1 year
  • DNo follow-up is needed once pain resolves
Rationale

"It is common to check a beta-hCG level on posttreatment days 4 and 7 and then weekly thereafter to ensure that it continues to drop steadily until it becomes undetectable. Complete resolution of an ectopic pregnancy usually occurs within 7 weeks." The pregnancy itself "is absorbed by the body over the next 4 to 6 weeks."

Watch for

(C) is the molar-pregnancy schedule, which does run about a year. The two hCG follow-ups are different lengths for different reasons: ectopic resolution versus surveillance for gestational trophoblastic neoplasia.

18. A woman with an unruptured tubal pregnancy who wants future children is scheduled for surgery. Which procedure fits, and what is done with the incision?
  • ASalpingectomy, removing the entire tube, with the incision closed by primary intention
  • ✓Salpingostomy, with an incision made over the pregnancy site and the products of conception removed, and the incision left to close by secondary intention because this results in less scarring
  • CSalpingostomy, with the incision closed with fine sutures
  • DHysterectomy
Rationale

"If the tube has not ruptured and the woman desires future fertility," a salpingostomy is performed: "an incision is made over the pregnancy site in the tube, and the products of conception are gently and very carefully removed," and the incision is "not sutured but left to close by secondary intention instead, given that this method results in less scarring."

(C) is the surgically intuitive answer and the specific detail the chapter contradicts. Surgical choice depends on "the location and cause of the ectopic pregnancy, the extent of tissue involvement, and the woman's desires regarding future fertility."

19. What perioperative nursing care does the chapter specify for a woman with a ruptured ectopic pregnancy?
  • ✓Vital signs every 15 minutes or as needed according to the severity of bleeding, preoperative blood type and Rh status, complete blood count and quantitative beta-hCG, and Rh immune globulin postoperatively if appropriate
  • BVital signs every 4 hours and a type and screen only
  • CNo preoperative laboratory work is needed in an emergency
  • DRh immune globulin is given only if the woman is Rh positive
Rationale

"Before surgery, vital signs (pulse, respirations, and blood pressure [BP]) are assessed every 15 minutes or as needed, according to the severity of the bleeding and the woman's condition." Preoperative labs are blood type and Rh status, complete blood cell count, and serum quantitative beta-hCG, and the nurse "administers Rh immune globulin postoperatively if appropriate."

(D) reverses the Rh indication. Immune globulin protects an Rh-negative woman from sensitization; an Rh-positive woman has nothing to be sensitized to.

21. An ultrasound shows a snowstorm pattern with no fetus, placenta, amniotic membranes, or fluid, and karyotyping shows 46XX. Which type of molar pregnancy is this, and how did it arise?
  • ✓A complete mole, from fertilization of an egg whose nucleus has been lost or inactivated, with the sperm nucleus duplicating itself
  • BA partial mole, from one ovum fertilized by two or more sperm
  • CA complete mole, from one ovum fertilized by two sperm
  • DA partial mole, from an empty egg
Rationale

"The complete mole results from fertilization of an egg in which the nucleus has been lost or inactivated," with the sperm nucleus duplicating itself to the diploid 46XX or 46XY, or an empty egg fertilized by two normal sperm. "The complete mole contains no fetus, placenta, amniotic membranes, or fluid," and the mole "resembles a bunch of white grapes."

Watch for

(B) describes the partial mole. The cue that survives is to count the sperm and look for fetal parts: one duplicated sperm and no fetus is complete.

22. Karyotyping of a molar pregnancy shows triploidy, 69XXY, and the specimen contains embryonic parts and an amniotic sac. Which type is this, and what accompanies it?
  • AA complete mole, with a high risk for persistent gestational trophoblastic neoplasia
  • ✓A partial mole, in which congenital anomalies, severe growth restriction, or both are usually present, with a lower risk for persistent GTN than a complete mole
  • CA complete mole with an unusual karyotype
  • DA normal twin pregnancy
Rationale

"In a partial mole, one apparently normal ovum is fertilized by two or more sperm," producing "triploidy (69XXY) or quadraploidy (92XXXY)." "Partial moles often have embryonic or fetal parts and an amniotic sac," with congenital anomalies and severe growth restriction usually present, and "the risk for persistent GTN is less than with a complete mole."

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error of the previous item, and the karyotype settles it: a diploid 46 chromosome count is complete, triploid or quadraploid is partial.

24. What does the chapter say about the incidence and risk factors for hydatidiform mole?
  • ✓It occurs in 1 in 1000 pregnancies in the United States, with higher risk after a prior molar pregnancy, at the extremes of reproductive age, and among Asian, Hispanic, and Native American women
  • BIt occurs in 1 in 100 pregnancies, with risk limited to women over 40
  • CIt occurs in 1 in 10,000 pregnancies, with no identified risk factors
  • DIt occurs only in first pregnancies
Rationale

"Hydatidiform mole occurs in 1 in 1000 pregnancies in the United States." 28. A molar pregnancy is diagnosed and evacuation is planned. Which method is used, and which is contraindicated?
  • ✓Suction curettage is used; induction of labor with oxytocic agents or prostaglandin is not recommended because of the increased risk for embolization of trophoblastic tissue

  • CHysterectomy is required in all cases

Rationale

"Although most moles abort spontaneously, suction curettage offers a safe, rapid, and effective method of evacuating a hydatidiform mole if necessary," and "induction of labor with oxytocic agents or prostaglandin is not recommended because of the increased risk for embolization of trophoblastic tissue." (C) takes a discussed option and makes it universal. Hysterectomy is an alternative for a woman who wants sterilization, not the standard evacuation.

29. What beta-hCG follow-up schedule does the chapter specify after evacuation of a molar pregnancy?

  • BA single level at 6 weeks
  • CWeekly levels for 3 weeks only
  • DMonthly levels for 3 months
Rationale

The schedule is "weekly measurements of the beta-hCG level until the level decreases to normal and remains normal for 3 consecutive weeks. Monthly measurements are then taken for 6 to 12 months. 30. Why must pregnancy be avoided during the molar follow-up period, and which contraceptive does the chapter prefer?
