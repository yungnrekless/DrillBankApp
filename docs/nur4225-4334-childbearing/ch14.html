classifying signs of pregnancywho perceives the sign
1. A woman reports amenorrhea, breast tenderness, nausea, and urinary frequency. How are these findings classified, and what do they establish?
  • ✓Presumptive signs: subjective changes experienced by the woman that suggest the possibility of pregnancy
  • BProbable signs: objective changes perceived by an examiner that strongly suggest pregnancy
  • CPositive signs: objective changes that indicate proof of pregnancy
  • DPresumptive signs, which confirm pregnancy when four or more are present
Rationale

Table 14.1 defines presumptive signs as "subjective changes experienced by the woman that suggest the possibility of pregnancy." Every one of these findings has another possible cause in the table's third column — premenstrual changes, stress, gastrointestinal virus, infection.

Watch for

(D) treats accumulation as proof, which the classification does not allow. No number of presumptive signs becomes a positive sign; only detection of the fetus itself does.

2. An examiner detects fetal heart tones with a Doppler ultrasound stethoscope at 10 weeks. How is this classified, and what makes it that class?
  • AProbable, because Doppler findings can have other causes
  • ✓Positive, because the finding can be attributed only to the presence of a fetus and has no other cause
  • CPresumptive, because the woman has not yet felt movement
  • DProbable, because positive signs require visualization rather than sound
Rationale

Positive signs are "objective changes observed/perceived by an examiner that indicate proof of pregnancy," and every positive row in Table 14.1 reads "no other causes" in its third column. Fetal heart tones by Doppler at 8 to 17 weeks is one of those rows.

Watch for

(D) invents a requirement. Visualization by ultrasound, fetal heart tones by any of three methods, and fetal movements palpated or seen by the examiner are all positive — the common element is the fetus itself, not the modality.

3. A woman brings in a positive home pregnancy test and says it confirms she is pregnant. How should the nurse classify this finding?
  • APositive, because a pregnancy test detects hCG produced only by a pregnancy
  • ✓Probable, because a hydatidiform mole or choriocarcinoma can produce a positive test, and a urine test can be falsely positive from pelvic infection or tumors
  • CPresumptive, because the woman performed the test herself
  • DPositive, if the test is repeated and remains positive
Rationale

Table 14.1 lists both serum and urine pregnancy tests as probable signs, with "hydatidiform mole, choriocarcinoma" and "false-positive result may be caused by pelvic infection, tumors" in the other-possible-cause column.

Watch for

(C) reclassifies by who ran the test rather than by what the finding proves. A serum test run in a clinic is also probable — the class is set by the possibility of another cause, not by the setting.

4. A woman at 18 weeks reports feeling fluttering movements, and at her next visit the examiner palpates fetal movement. How are these two findings classified?
  • ABoth are presumptive
  • BBoth are positive
  • ✓Quickening felt by the woman is presumptive; fetal movement palpated by the examiner is positive
  • DQuickening felt by the woman is probable; fetal movement palpated by the examiner is positive
Rationale

Quickening at 16 to 20 weeks is a presumptive sign whose other possible causes are gas and peristalsis. Fetal movements palpated by an examiner at 19 to 22 weeks is a positive sign with no other causes.

Watch for

(A) treats the phenomenon as fixed and the class as following from it. The same movement changes category depending on who perceives it, which is the rule this chapter is built on.

classifying signs of pregnancythe probable signs by name
5. An examiner documents softening of the cervix at 5 weeks in one woman, a bluish-purple discoloration of the vulva and vagina in another, and softening of the lower uterine segment in a third. Which signs are these?
  • ✓Goodell, Chadwick, and Hegar
  • BChadwick, Goodell, and Hegar
  • CHegar, Chadwick, and Goodell
  • DGoodell, Hegar, and Chadwick
Rationale

Goodell sign is cervical softening at about 5 weeks, Chadwick sign is the vulvar and vaginal color change at 6 to 8 weeks, and Hegar sign is softening of the lower uterine segment at 6 to 12 weeks. All three are probable signs, and all three list pelvic congestion as another possible cause.

Watch for

(C) reverses the first and third, which is the usual mix-up because both are described as softening. The exam's own wording is the cue: "assessed by observing the cervix" is Goodell, "softening of the lower uterine segment" is Hegar.

6. A woman's last menstrual period began on December 10, 2022. Using the Naegele rule, what is her estimated date of birth?
  • ✓September 17, 2023
  • BSeptember 3, 2023
  • CMarch 17, 2023
  • DSeptember 17, 2022
Rationale

Box 14.1's own worked example uses these dates: subtract 3 from the month, add 7 to the day, add 1 to the year. December 10, 2022 becomes September 17, 2023.

Watch for

(B) performs the month subtraction and forgets the 7 days, which is the most common arithmetic slip. Fig. 14.1's caption gives a second pair to check against: a last period beginning February 21, 2022 gives an estimated date of birth of November 29, 2022.

7. A woman with irregular cycles ranging from 35 to 45 days asks how reliable her Naegele-rule due date is. What assumption limits it?
  • ✓It assumes a 28-day cycle with fertilization on the 14th day
  • BIt assumes conception occurred on the first day of the last menstrual period
  • CIt assumes a 30-day cycle with fertilization on the 16th day
  • DIt assumes the woman has been pregnant before
Rationale

"It assumes that the woman has a 28-day cycle and that fertilization occurred on the 14th day." A woman whose cycles run longer ovulates later, so the rule dates her pregnancy as further along than it is.

Watch for

(B) confuses the starting point of the calculation with the assumed timing of conception. The rule counts from the last menstrual period precisely because conception is assumed to follow it by 14 days.

8. Which method does the chapter identify as the most accurate assessment of the estimated date of birth?
  • AThe Naegele rule applied to an accurately recalled last menstrual period
  • ✓Ultrasound measurement of the embryo or fetus during the first trimester
  • CA gestational wheel
  • DFundal height measured at 20 weeks
Rationale

"The most accurate assessment of the EDB is based on ultrasound measurement of the embryo or fetus during the first trimester of pregnancy." The estimated date of birth is determined from both the last menstrual period and the first accurate ultrasound examination.

Watch for

(A) is the method the chapter teaches in most detail, which is why it gets promoted to most accurate. Teaching emphasis and accuracy are different things here.

9. A woman reaches her estimated date of birth and has not given birth. What should the nurse teach her about the meaning of that date?
  • ✓Only about 5% of women give birth spontaneously on the estimated date, and most births occur from 7 days before to 7 days after it
  • BMost women give birth within 24 hours of the estimated date
  • CPassing the estimated date by any amount means the pregnancy is postterm
  • DThe estimated date is accurate to within 1 day when calculated by the Naegele rule
Rationale

"Only about 5% of women give birth spontaneously on the EDB as determined by the Naegele rule; most births occur within the time period from 7 days before to 7 days after the EDB."

Watch for

(C) confuses the estimated date with the postterm boundary, which is 42 0/7 weeks — about 2 weeks past the estimated date, not a day past it.

gravidity parity and gtpalwhat each term counts
10. A woman pregnant for the second time gave birth to twins at 36 weeks in her first pregnancy; both children are living. What is her gravidity and parity using the 2-digit system?
  • AGravida 2, para 2, because she has given birth to two children
  • ✓Gravida 2, para 1, because parity counts pregnancies reaching 20 weeks, not the number of fetuses born
  • CGravida 3, para 2, because a twin pregnancy counts twice
  • DGravida 2, para 0, because the birth was preterm
Rationale

"Parity: The number of pregnancies in which the fetus or fetuses have reached 20 weeks of gestation or more, not the number of fetuses (e.g., twins) born." Gravida counts pregnancies including the present one, so she is gravida 2, para 1.

Watch for

(A) counts babies, which is the error this definition is written against, and (D) treats preterm birth as not counting toward parity — the boundary is 20 weeks, not 37.

11. A woman at 24 weeks in her third pregnancy had a stillbirth at 22 weeks and a miscarriage at 10 weeks. How does the stillbirth affect her parity?
  • AIt does not count, because the fetus showed no signs of life
  • ✓It counts toward parity, because parity is not affected by whether the fetus is born alive or is stillborn
  • CIt counts as an abortion in GTPAL because there is no living child
  • DIt counts toward parity only if the fetus weighed more than 500 g
Rationale

"Parity is not affected by whether the fetus is born alive or is stillborn (i.e., showing no signs of life at birth)." The 22-week stillbirth reached 20 weeks, so it counts toward parity and belongs in the preterm column of GTPAL; the 10-week loss is an abortion.

Watch for

(C) uses the living-children column to decide the preterm column. Those are separate letters in GTPAL, and a pregnancy can count as preterm with no living child to show for it.

13. A pregnancy ends at 38 weeks. In which GTPAL column does it belong, and why?
  • AThe preterm column, because 38 weeks is not full term
  • ✓The term column, because term includes early-term, full-term, late-term, and postterm births at 37 weeks 0 days and beyond
  • CThe abortion column, because it did not reach 39 weeks

14. Which pregnancies are counted in the abortion column of GTPAL?
  • AOnly elective terminations
  • ✓Pregnancies ending in miscarriage or elective termination before 20 weeks, or in which the fetus weighed less than 500 g at birth
  • CAny pregnancy loss at any gestational age
  • DOnly pregnancies ending before 12 weeks

Box 14.3 defines the abortion column as "the number of pregnancies that ended in miscarriage (spontaneous abortion) or elective termination (therapeutic abortion) before 20 weeks or weighed less than 500 g at birth."

15. A pregnancy reaches 38 weeks 2 days. How is this gestational age classified?
  • AFull term
  • ✓Early term
  • CLate preterm
  • DTerm, with no further subdivision
Rationale

The chapter's ladder is exact: early term is 37 weeks 0 days to 38 weeks 6 days, and full term begins at 39 weeks 0 days and runs to 40 weeks 6 days.

Watch for

(A) uses the everyday meaning of full term, which covers everything past 37 weeks. A stem offering 38 weeks and the phrase "full term" is using ordinary usage against the book's definition — and this is also the boundary behind the 39-week rule for elective induction.

16. Match the remaining gestational-age categories to their boundaries. Which statement is correct?
  • ✓Late preterm is 34 weeks 0 days to 36 weeks 6 days; late term is 41 weeks 0 days to 41 weeks 6 days; postterm is 42 weeks 0 days and beyond
  • BLate preterm is 32 weeks 0 days to 36 weeks 6 days; late term is 40 weeks; postterm is 41 weeks and beyond

Preterm is 20 weeks 0 days to before 37 weeks 0 days, late preterm is 34 weeks 0 days to 36 weeks 6 days, late term is 41 weeks 0 days to 41 weeks 6 days, and postterm is 42 weeks 0 days and beyond.

17. Parents of an infant born at 23 weeks ask whether their baby was viable. What does the chapter say about viability?
  • AViability begins at 24 weeks 0 days
  • ✓Viability is the capacity to live outside the uterus, with no clear limits of gestational age or weight; infants born at 22 to 25 weeks are considered to be on the threshold of viability
  • CViability begins at 20 weeks, matching the parity boundary
  • DViability is defined by a birth weight of 500 g
Rationale

"Viability: The capacity to live outside the uterus; there are no clear limits of gestational age or weight. Infants born at 22–25 weeks of gestation are considered to be on the threshold of viability and are especially vulnerable to brain injury if they survive."

Watch for

(C) borrows the 20-week line that governs parity and applies it to viability. The two boundaries are near each other and answer different questions — one is a counting rule, the other a clinical threshold with no fixed value.

  • AA checklist of closed questions to ensure nothing is missed
  • ✓A broad open-ended question such as "How have you been feeling?", with the woman's chief concerns recorded in her own words
  • CQuestions directed to the woman's partner to reduce her anxiety

  • BThat it is brief, with the detailed history deferred to the second trimester
  • CThat it consists only of laboratory testing

Rationale

"The first prenatal visit is longer and more detailed than future visits," and the initial evaluation includes "a comprehensive health history emphasizing the current pregnancy, previous pregnancies, the family, a psychosocial profile, a physical assessment, diagnostic testing, and an overall risk assessment." immunizations in pregnancythe live-virus rule
24. A woman at 12 weeks is found to be nonimmune to rubella and varicella. When should she be vaccinated?
  • AImmediately, since nonimmunity poses a fetal risk
  • ✓As part of postpartum care, because immunization with live or attenuated live viruses is contraindicated during pregnancy due to potential teratogenicity
  • CIn the third trimester, when organogenesis is complete
  • DNever, since these vaccines are contraindicated in women of childbearing age
Rationale

"Immunization with live or attenuated live viruses is contraindicated during pregnancy because of potential teratogenicity; recommended vaccination with these agents should be part of postpartum care." The live-virus vaccines named are measles (rubeola and rubella), varicella, mumps, and some influenza vaccines.

Watch for

(C) treats the first trimester as the only vulnerable window. The contraindication runs through the whole pregnancy, and the postpartum visit is where these vaccines are given.

26. A woman who received Tdap during her last pregnancy 2 years ago asks whether she needs it again. What should the nurse tell her?
  • ANo, because immunity from the previous dose persists
  • ✓Yes, because maternal antipertussis antibodies are short-lived, so Tdap is administered during each pregnancy regardless of prior vaccination history
  • CYes, but only if her partner is unvaccinated
  • DNo, unless she has had a known pertussis exposure
Rationale

"Maternal antipertussis antibodies are short-lived and antibody levels drop significantly during the first year after vaccination. As a result, it is unlikely that a Tdap vaccination in one pregnancy will transfer passive immunity from mother to infant in a subsequent pregnancy. Therefore the recommendation is to administer Tdap to a pregnant woman during each pregnancy regardless of her prior vaccination history."

Watch for

(A) applies adult-booster logic to a strategy whose purpose is passive transfer to this infant. Her own protection is not the point of the dose.

28. A grandmother who will be caring for a newborn asks whether she needs anything before the baby comes home. What does the Safety Alert direct?
  • ANo vaccination is needed for household contacts
  • ✓Adolescents and adults who will have close contact with an infant under 12 months and are not up to date with pertussis vaccination should receive a single dose of Tdap at least 2 weeks before contact with the infant
  • CHousehold contacts should receive Tdap on the day of the birth
  • DOnly health care personnel need pertussis vaccination
Rationale

The Safety Alert states the cocooning rule: "Adolescents and adults (parents, grandparents, siblings, childcare workers, and health care personnel) who will have close contact with an infant less than 12 months of age and are not up to date with pertussis vaccination should receive a single dose of Tdap at least 2 weeks before contact with the infant." (C) gets the vaccine right and the timing wrong, which leaves the contact unprotected during the window that matters — the 2-week interval is what makes the dose useful.

29. An Rh-negative woman with a negative antibody screen is at 28 weeks. What does the chapter direct?
  • ✓A dose of 300 mcg of Rh immune globulin, which is routinely administered at 26 to 30 weeks to all Rh-negative women without evidence of anti-D alloimmunization
  • BNo treatment unless the fetus is known to be Rh positive
  • CA dose of 300 mcg only after birth
  • DA dose at 36 weeks, closer to the anticipated birth
Rationale

"A dose of 300 mcg of Rh immune globulin is routinely administered at 26 to 30 weeks to all Rh-negative women without evidence of anti-D alloimmunization." Blood type is determined at the first prenatal visit, and Rh-negative women have an antibody screen drawn in the first and third trimesters.

(B) waits for information the antenatal dose does not require. The point is to prevent antibody formation before the immune system encounters the D antigen, which is why the dose is routine rather than conditional.
