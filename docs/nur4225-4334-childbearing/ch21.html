1. On postpartum day 1 the nurse palpates a fundus that is boggy and higher than expected. What are the two possible causes?
  • ✓Uterine atony or a distended bladder
  • BInfection or dehydration
  • CRetained placenta only
  • DNormal involution
Rationale

Table 21.1 gives the normal finding as a fundus that is "firm, midline; first 24 h at level of umbilicus; involutes ≈1 cm (1 fingerbreadth)/day," and the potential complication as "soft, boggy, higher than expected level: uterine atony or distended bladder."

Watch for

(C) names a cause of atony rather than the finding's differential. The value of holding both possibilities is that they call for different first actions — massage for one, voiding for the other.

2. The fundus is firm but deviated to the right of midline. What does this indicate, and what should the nurse do first?
  • AUterine atony; massage the fundus
  • ✓A distended bladder; assist the woman to void
  • CUterine rupture; notify the provider
  • DNormal variation; document and continue
Rationale

Table 21.1 gives "lateral deviation: distended bladder." The chapter explains the mechanism: "a full bladder causes the uterus to be displaced above the umbilicus and well to one side of midline in the abdomen. It also prevents the uterus from contracting normally."

Watch for

(A) treats every fundal abnormality as atony. A firm, deviated fundus is a bladder problem, and massaging it without emptying the bladder leaves the cause in place.

7. What does the chapter name as the two most important interventions for preventing excessive bleeding?
  • ✓Maintaining good uterine tone and preventing bladder distention
  • BAdministering intravenous fluids and oxytocic medications
  • CBed rest and ice packs
  • DFrequent vital signs and blood typing
Rationale

"The two most important interventions for preventing excessive bleeding are maintaining good uterine tone and preventing bladder distention."   • ADocument the small amount and reassess in an hour
  • ✓Always check for blood under the mother's buttocks as well as on the perineal pad, because blood can flow between the buttocks onto the linens and excessive bleeding can go undetected
  • CIncrease oral fluids
  • DRecheck the pulse in the other arm
Rationale

The Safety Alert states: "The nurse always checks for blood under the mother's buttocks as well as on the perineal pad.   • BThe brand of the pad only

14. Why is blood pressure not a reliable indicator of impending shock from early postpartum hemorrhage, and which indicators are better?
  • ✓Because compensatory mechanisms prevent a significant drop until 30% to 40% of blood volume is lost; respirations, pulse, skin condition, urinary output, and level of consciousness are more sensitive
  • BBecause postpartum blood pressure equipment is inaccurate
  • CBecause blood pressure always rises in hemorrhage
  • DBecause blood pressure is affected by breastfeeding
Rationale

"Blood pressure is not a reliable indicator of impending shock from early postpartum hemorrhage because compensatory mechanisms prevent a significant drop in blood pressure until the woman has lost 30% to 40% of her blood volume. (C) inverts the physiology. Table 21.1 does list hypotension as a sign of hemorrhage — it is simply a late one, which is why the other five indicators are what the nurse watches.

  • CRepositioning to Trendelenburg only

40. How does the chapter describe screening for postpartum depression?
  • ✓Routine screening is done in some facilities before discharge using a tool such as the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale, and screening should also be done after discharge, with pediatric providers routinely screening at the 1-, 2-, and 4-month well-child visits
  • BScreening is done only if the woman reports symptoms
  • CScreening occurs only at the 6-week postpartum visit
  • DScreening is not recommended
Rationale

"Routine screening for PPD is done in some facilities before discharge using a tool such as the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale," and "screening for PPD should also be done after discharge." Pediatric care providers "routinely perform maternal screening for PPD during infant follow-up at 1-, 2-, and 4-month well-child visits." A history of preconception or prenatal depression increases the risk.

Watch for

(B) makes screening contingent on disclosure from a population the chapter identifies as unlikely to raise concerns spontaneously.

41. A woman's birth ended in an unplanned cesarean, very different from her birth plan. She keeps returning to the story of her labor. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • AAs rumination requiring psychiatric referral
  • ✓As an expected need: many women need to review and reflect on their labor and birth experience, and when the experience differed from the birth plan, both partners may need to mourn the loss of their expectations before they can adjust to the reality
  • CAs a sign she is not bonding with the infant
  • DAs a complaint about care that should be redirected
Rationale

"Many women need to review and reflect on their labor and birth experience. Their partners may have similar needs. If their birth experience was different from their birth plan (e.g., induction, epidural anesthesia, cesarean birth), both partners may need to mourn the loss of their expectations before they can adjust to the reality of their actual birth experience."

Watch for

(D) shuts down the conversation the chapter says she needs, and being *unable or unwilling* to discuss the birth is the warning sign — not being willing to.

43. What is the couplet, or mother/baby, model of care?
  • ✓Nurses educated in both mother and infant care who function as primary nurses for both, even if the infant is kept in the nursery — a variation of rooming-in
  • BA model in which separate nurses care for the mother and the infant
  • CA model requiring the infant to remain in the nursery
  • DA model used only for high-risk dyads
Rationale

"Most birth facilities use the couplet or mother/baby model of care. Nurses in these settings have been educated in both mother and infant care and function as primary nurses for both the mother and the infant, even if the infant is kept in the nursery. This approach is a variation of rooming-in."

Watch for

(B) describes the arrangement couplet care replaced, and the phrase "even if the infant is kept in the nursery" is what makes the model about the nurse's assignment rather than the baby's location.

44. What does the chapter say about bedside reporting?
  • ✓It has been shown to improve client safety and satisfaction, because clients feel more involved in their plan of care and nurses can both visualize and communicate with the client at the time of report
  • BIt compromises privacy and is discouraged
  • CIt is used only on high-risk units

"Bedside reporting has been shown to improve client safety and satisfaction. Watch for

(D) confuses a communication practice with the record. The hand-off report itself covers an extensive list, from gravidity and parity through the assessment of initial parent-infant interaction.

45. What are the first steps in providing client-centered care when the woman arrives on the postpartum unit?
  • ✓Confirm her identity by checking her wristband, match the infant's identification number with the corresponding band on her wrist, and determine how she wishes to be addressed and her pronouns of choice, noting these in the record and plan of care
  • BComplete the physical assessment before speaking with her
  • CReview her chart and introduce the plan of care
  • DWait for the provider's orders
Rationale

"The first step in providing client-centered care is to confirm the woman's identity by checking her wristband. At the same time, the infant's identification number is matched with the corresponding band on the mother's wrist." And: "the nurse determines how the mother wishes to be addressed and her pronouns of choice and notes her preferences in the medical record and the nursing plan of care."

Watch for

(B) starts with the assessment and skips the identification step that protects both patients, which is why the chapter names it first.
