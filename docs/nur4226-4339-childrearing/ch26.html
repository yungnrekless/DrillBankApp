pediatric airway structure and respiratory assessmentgeneral
1. A nursing student asks why a small amount of airway edema causes so much more distress in an infant than in an adult. What is the correct explanation?
  • ✓If airway diameter decreases, resistance increases exponentially, and airway size is the dominant determinant of resistance
  • BResistance rises in direct proportion to the reduction in diameter
  • CChest wall tissue accounts for most airway resistance in infants
  • DInfants have fewer alveoli, which raises airway resistance directly
Rationale

The chapter states that "Resistance is determined primarily by airway size," that flow resistance in the airways is the most important of the three sources, and that while flow rate, gas viscosity, and airway length raise resistance proportionally, "If airway diameter decreases, resistance increases exponentially" (p. 884).

Watch for

(B) is the proportional relationship that holds for the other three determinants and not for diameter, which is exactly what makes a small change in caliber so consequential; chest wall tissue accounts for about 20% and lung tissue about 15% of resistance, so (C) inverts the ranking.

2. A nurse notes subcostal retractions in an infant with bronchiolitis. What does this finding indicate?
  • ✓A flattened diaphragm
  • BSevere upper airway obstruction
  • CReduced chest wall compliance
  • DPleural effusion
Rationale

The chapter states that subcostal retraction "indicates a flattened diaphragm," and reserves retractions extending to the supraclavicular areas and suprasternal notch for severe airway obstruction (pp. 884–885).

Watch for

(B) names the finding that belongs to a different retraction location, and the distinction matters because location grades severity; (C) reverses the developmental fact, since compliance is normally *high* in the newborn and infant "because of a more pliant (flexible) rib cage."

3. A child is brought in for a barking, brassy, nonproductive cough. Which conditions does the chapter associate with this specific cough quality?
  • ✓Croup and foreign body aspiration
  • BPertussis and measles
  • CPneumonia and cystic fibrosis
  • DAsthma and bronchiolitis
Rationale

The chapter states that "A brassy, nonproductive cough is part of the symptomatology of croup and foreign body aspiration," while a paroxysmal cough with an inspiratory whoop is typical of pertussis and a severe cough accompanies measles and cystic fibrosis (p. 885).

Watch for

(C) names the two conditions the chapter pairs with a severe cough rather than a brassy one; note also the chapter's caution that "Because there are no cough receptors in the alveoli, a cough may be absent in a child with pneumonia in the early stages."

5. A 12-year-old has a positive rapid streptococcal test and is prescribed oral penicillin. What should the nurse tell the family about why the full course matters?
  • ✓Children with this infection are at increased risk for rheumatic fever and glomerulonephritis
  • BCompleting the course confers lasting immunity to future streptococcal infections
  • CA follow-up throat culture is required after every course
  • DUntreated infection leads to adult rheumatoid arthritis
Rationale

The chapter states that streptococcal pharyngitis "is not in itself a serious disease, but affected children are at risk for serious sequelae," naming acute rheumatic fever appearing "in an average of 18 days" and acute glomerulonephritis appearing "in about 10 days" (p. 893).

Watch for

(B) and (D) are the distractors in the chapter's own review question, and both are simply false; note that the sequelae are not equivalent — penicillin prevents rheumatic fever but "does not prevent acute glomerulonephritis in susceptible children," though it may prevent spread of a nephrogenic strain to the family.

6. A child with streptococcal pharyngitis started penicillin yesterday morning and feels better. When may the child return to school?
  • ✓After a full 24-hour period of antibiotic therapy
  • BAfter 48 hours of antibiotic therapy
  • CAfter the 10-day course is complete
  • DAs soon as the fever has resolved
Rationale

The chapter states that "Children with streptococcal infection are noninfectious to others 24 hours after initiation of antibiotic therapy," and should not return to school or day care "until they have been taking antibiotics for a full 24-hour period" (p. 894).

Watch for

(C) keeps a well child home for the whole course, and (D) uses a symptom rather than the treatment clock; the family should also discard and replace the toothbrush after 24 hours of antibiotics.

7. A 2-year-old has a sore throat with nasal discharge, conjunctivitis, hoarseness, and a cough. How should the nurse interpret the likelihood of streptococcal infection?
  • ✓Suspicion is low, because these symptoms are more suggestive of a viral cause and the child is under 3 years old
  • BSuspicion is high, because sore throat plus fever is the classic presentation
  • CSuspicion is high, because conjunctivitis accompanies streptococcal infection in young children
  • DSuspicion cannot be estimated clinically and testing is mandatory in all cases
Rationale

Table 26.2 lists children under 3 years and symptoms more suggestive of a viral cause — nasal discharge, conjunctivitis, hoarseness, cough, diarrhea, mouth ulcers, stomatitis — as the situations in which suspicion is low; the chapter adds that "80% to 90% of all cases of acute pharyngitis are viral" (p. 893).

Watch for

(B) uses features the table does list as reasons to check, but ignores the two that argue against it here; the chapter's own instruction is to suspect strep "in children over the age of 2 years who have pharyngitis even if no exudate is present."

8. A child's rapid antigen test for group A streptococcus is negative, but the child has classic signs of the infection. What should the nurse anticipate?
  • ✓A confirmatory throat culture, because rapid test kits have questionable sensitivity
  • BNo further testing, because a negative rapid test excludes the infection
  • CEmpiric antibiotics without further testing
  • DRepeat rapid antigen testing in 24 hours
Rationale

The chapter states that rapid kits have "questionable sensitivity," so "a confirmatory throat culture is recommended in patients who have a negative test result with a rapid diagnostic test kit and classic signs of the infection are present" (p. 893).

Watch for

(B) treats a test the chapter calls insensitive as conclusive; note the converse caution too — because some children are carriers, "a positive culture or antigen test is not always conclusive evidence of active disease."

tonsillectomy and adenoidectomy caregeneral
9. A 5-year-old is 4 hours post-tonsillectomy. The nurse observes the sleeping child swallowing repeatedly. What should the nurse do?
  • ✓Notify the surgeon immediately, because continuous swallowing is the most obvious early sign of bleeding
  • BOffer the child a drink, since the swallowing indicates thirst
  • CDocument the finding and reassess in 1 hour
  • DReposition the child and continue routine observation
Rationale

The Nursing Alert states that "The most obvious early sign of bleeding is the child's continuous swallowing of the trickling blood. While the child is sleeping, note the frequency of swallowing. If continuous bleeding is suspected, notify the surgeon immediately" (p. 896).

Watch for

(B) and (C) both wait, and the chapter names the other hemorrhage signs to underline why waiting is wrong — tachycardia, pallor, frequent throat clearing, vomiting bright red blood, and restlessness, with "Decreasing blood pressure is a much later sign of shock."

10. A nurse is giving discharge instructions after a tonsillectomy and adenoidectomy. Which instructions are correct? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Observe for continuous swallowing
  • ✓Encourage sips of cool, clear liquids
  • ✓Observe for restlessness or difficulty breathing
  • ✓Administer an analgesic such as acetaminophen
  • EGive codeine elixir for pain at regular intervals
Rationale

These four are the keyed set in the chapter's own review question, and each is supported by the post-operative section — continuous swallowing as the earliest bleeding sign, cool clear fluids, restlessness among the hemorrhage and obstruction signs, and regular analgesia for at least the first 24 to 48 hours (p. 896).

Watch for

(E) is contraindicated by a Drug Alert — "Codeine is contraindicated in pediatric patients post tonsillectomy and adenoidectomy" — because ultrarapid metabolizers convert it to three to four times the expected morphine, which has caused hypopnea, apnea, and death; the other wrong option in the source's item is encouraging coughing.

11. The parent of a child 6 days post-tonsillectomy calls because she sees a cream-colored membrane on the tonsillar bed. What should the nurse tell her?
  • ✓This is an expected finding for 5 to 10 days after surgery
  • BThis indicates infection and the child should be seen today
  • CThis indicates bleeding and the child should go to the emergency department
  • DThis indicates the child has been drinking liquids that are too warm
Rationale

The chapter states that "A cream-colored membrane is often visible on the tonsillar bed postoperatively for 5 to 10 days postsurgery; reassure parents this is an expected finding" (p. 896).

Watch for

(B) and (C) escalate a normal finding, which is what the explicit instruction to reassure parents exists to prevent; also expected are dark brown old blood in emesis and between the teeth, objectionable mouth odor, and slight ear pain with low-grade fever.

tonsillectomy and adenoidectomy caregeneral
12. A child 2 days post-tonsillectomy asks for a milkshake. What should the nurse advise?
  • ✓Withhold milk products until clear fluids are retained, because they coat the mouth and throat and prompt throat clearing
  • BAllow it, since cold dairy products soothe the surgical site
  • CAllow it only if it is red or brown in color to mask any blood
  • DWithhold all oral intake until the tenth postoperative day
Rationale

The chapter directs that milk, ice cream, or pudding is withheld "until clear fluids are retained because milk products coat the mouth and throat, causing the child to clear the throat, which may initiate bleeding" (p. 896).

Watch for

(C) inverts a real rule — fluids with a red or brown color are *avoided*, precisely so fresh or old blood in emesis can be distinguished from the ingested liquid; citrus juice is also poorly tolerated, and soft foods begin on the first or second postoperative day.

13. A child is 8 days post-tonsillectomy and vomits bright red blood. What does the chapter say about this timing?
  • ✓Hemorrhage may occur up to 10 days after surgery as tissue sloughs during healing
  • BBleeding after 48 hours is always from a new source unrelated to the surgery
  • CBleeding this late indicates the child has an undiagnosed coagulopathy
  • DBleeding cannot occur after the first postoperative day
Rationale

The chapter states that "Hemorrhage may occur up to 10 days after surgery as a result of tissue sloughing from the healing process," and that most children resume normal activity within 1 to 2 weeks (p. 896).

Watch for

(D) and (B) both misplace the risk window, which is the reason discharge teaching must extend past the first days; the chapter also directs medical evaluation for persistent severe earache, fever, or cough.

14. A 3-month-old is irritable, cries constantly, refuses to nurse after 2 to 3 minutes, has rhinitis, and a rectal temperature of 101.8 degrees Fahrenheit. What should the nurse suspect?
  • ✓Acute otitis media
  • BOtitis media with effusion
  • CAcute otitis externa
  • DRespiratory syncytial virus infection
Rationale

The chapter describes infants with acute otitis media as irritable, holding or pulling at their ears, with a temperature as high as 104 degrees Fahrenheit common and loss of appetite because "sucking or chewing aggravates the pain" (pp. 898–901).

Watch for

(B) is excluded because in effusion "Severe pain or fever is usually absent... the child may not appear ill"; the brief feeding attempts are the specific cue here, since sucking worsens middle ear pain, and this is the chain the chapter's own review question tests.

otitis media and otitis externageneral
15. A nurse is using a pneumatic otoscope. Which finding indicates otitis media with effusion rather than acute otitis media?
  • ✓An immobile or orange-colored tympanic membrane without acute signs of illness
  • BModerate to severe bulging of the tympanic membrane
  • CIntense erythema of the tympanic membrane
  • DAcute onset of ear drainage not due to otitis externa
Rationale

The chapter states that "An immobile tympanic membrane or an orange-colored membrane indicates OME," while acute otitis media is diagnosed with moderate to severe bulging, acute onset of drainage, mild bulging with pain of less than 48 hours, or intense erythema (pp. 900–901).

Watch for

(B), (C), and (D) are all acute otitis media criteria; the consequence of the distinction is management, since "Antibiotics are not required for initial treatment of OME" while they are first-line for acute infection.

16. A 3-year-old is diagnosed with acute otitis media. Which prescription should the nurse expect?
  • ✓High-dose amoxicillin, 80 to 90 mg/kg/day divided twice daily, for 5 to 7 days
  • BHigh-dose amoxicillin for 10 days
  • CAntibiotic ear drops instilled three times daily
  • DAn oral decongestant with an antihistamine
Rationale

The chapter gives high-dose amoxicillin at 80 to 90 mg/kg/day divided twice daily "for 5 to 7 days with children 2 years and older, and 10 days with younger children, children with underlying medical conditions, craniofacial anomalies, tympanic membrane perforation or children with chronic otitis media" (p. 900).

Watch for

(B) applies the younger-child duration to a 3-year-old, which is the age tier this item tests; (C) and (D) are both ruled out, since "Antibiotic ear drops have no value in treating AOM" and "The use of steroids, decongestants, and antihistamines to treat AOM is not recommended."

17. A parent reports that her child's tympanostomy tube has fallen out 11 months after placement. What should the nurse advise?
  • ✓This is normal, requires no immediate intervention, and the practitioner should be notified
  • BThis is an emergency requiring same-day evaluation
  • CThis indicates the tube was placed incorrectly and will need replacement today
  • DThis is normal and the practitioner does not need to be told
Rationale

The chapter states that "The tympanostomy tube is eventually pushed out of the eardrum usually 8 to 18 months after tube placement," which "is normal and requires no immediate intervention, although they should notify the practitioner" (p. 901).

(D) drops the notification half of a two-part instruction, and (B) escalates a normal event; note also that water precautions are inconsistent in the literature, with "no need for water precautions unless the child develops recurrent drainage after swimming."

otitis media and otitis externageneral
18. An 8-year-old who swims daily has ear pain that worsens sharply when the nurse presses on the tragus. What is the likely diagnosis, and which treatment caution applies?
  • ✓Acute otitis externa, and the vinegar-and-alcohol prevention mixture must not be used if tympanostomy tubes are present
  • BAcute otitis media, and antibiotic ear drops should be started
  • CAcute otitis externa, and oral amoxicillin is first-line
  • DCholesteatoma, and surgical excision is required
Rationale

The chapter identifies pain "accentuated by manipulation of the pinna, especially pressure on the tragus" as the discriminating sign of otitis externa, most common in 5- to 14-year-olds and peaking in summer, and states that the 50:50 acetic acid and rubbing alcohol mixture "must not be used if tympanostomy tubes are present" (p. 902).

Watch for

(B) is the confusion the chapter anticipates, since children with either condition "pull at the ear"; note also that polymyxin-B, neomycin, and gentamicin drops should not be used if tympanostomy tubes are present because of ototoxicity risk.

croup syndromesgeneral
19. A 5-year-old developed a severe sore throat after going to bed, is now drooling with difficulty swallowing, has a temperature of 102.2 degrees Fahrenheit, clear breath sounds, no cough, and appears anxious and flushed. What is the most likely diagnosis?
  • ✓Acute epiglottitis
  • BAcute laryngotracheobronchitis
  • CAcute spasmodic laryngitis
  • DBacterial tracheitis
Rationale

The chapter names three predictive observations for epiglottitis — "absence of spontaneous cough, presence of drooling, and agitation" — and states that "The key differences between LTB and epiglottitis are the absence of cough, the presence of dysphagia, and the high degree of toxicity in children with epiglottitis" (pp. 903–904).

Watch for

(B) is excluded by the absent cough alone, since laryngotracheobronchitis is defined by a barking cough; the chapter's own mnemonic is that children with epiglottitis "usually look worse than they sound, in contrast to children with LTB, who sound worse than they look."

20. A nurse suspects epiglottitis in a 4-year-old. Which actions are appropriate? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Keep the child calm in a position of comfort with the parent present
  • ✓Summon personnel with advanced airway management skills
  • ✓Administer humidified oxygen by mask or blow-by to avoid further agitation
  • ✓Postpone diagnostic tests and invasive procedures until an airway is established
  • EVisualize the epiglottis with a tongue depressor to confirm the diagnosis
Rationale

The chapter directs that a lateral neck radiograph be accompanied by "Experienced personnel with advanced airway management skills," that oxygen be given by mask or blow-by "to avoid further agitation," and that "diagnostic tests and invasive procedures be postponed on the child with suspected epiglottitis until an airway has been established" (pp. 903–904).

Watch for

(E) is forbidden by a Nursing Alert — nurses "should not attempt to visualize the epiglottis directly with a tongue depressor or take a throat culture," and inspection is done only when immediate intubation or emergency tracheotomy can follow; this is the single most harmful option in the chapter.

croup syndromesgeneral
21. A 2-year-old has had 2 days of rhinitis and a low-grade fever and tonight developed a barking, seal-like cough with inspiratory stridor that worsens when he cries. He has no stridor at rest. What management does the chapter direct?
  • ✓Management at home, since mild croup without stridor at rest does not require hospital treatment
  • BImmediate nebulized racemic epinephrine
  • CImmediate intubation
  • DOral antibiotics for 10 days
Rationale

The chapter states that "Mild croup (no stridor at rest) is managed at home," with cool mist as a comfort measure, and reserves nebulized racemic epinephrine for moderate to severe cases (pp. 904–905).

Watch for

(B) escalates a mild case and commits the child to 3 to 4 hours of observation afterward; note the chapter's practical observation that "a ride in the car with the window down may help relieve symptoms, and the child often improves on the way to the emergency department due to exposure to cool air."

croup syndromesgeneral
22. A child with moderate croup receives nebulized racemic epinephrine in the emergency department and improves within 30 minutes. What should happen next?
  • ✓Observe the child for 3 to 4 hours for any visible signs of respiratory distress
  • BDischarge the child once the stridor has resolved
  • CRepeat the dose every 5 minutes until the cough stops
  • DAdmit the child for a minimum 48-hour observation
Rationale

The chapter states that "The patient who has received nebulized epinephrine for croup should be observed for 3 to 4 hours for any visible signs of respiratory distress," because although improvement appears within 30 minutes, "the effects were transient"; additional doses may be given every 20 to 30 minutes as needed (p. 905).

Watch for

(B) discharges a child whose improvement the chapter specifically calls transient, which is why the observation period exists; (C) compresses the repeat interval by a factor of four or more.

croup syndromesgeneral
23. A child with croup receives a single dose of oral dexamethasone. When should the nurse expect improvement?
  • ✓As early as 6 hours after administration, with continued improvement over 12 to 24 hours
  • BWithin 30 minutes
  • CWithin 2 hours
  • DNot until the second day of therapy
Rationale

The chapter states that a single dose of oral dexamethasone is "considered standard treatment for this condition" and that "The onset of action is clinically detectable as early as 6 hours after administration, with continued improvement over a period of 12 to 24 hours" (p. 905).

Watch for

(B) and (C) borrow the timing of nebulized epinephrine, whose peak effect is under 2 hours; the two drugs are given together precisely because their onsets differ, one immediate and transient, the other delayed and sustained.

croup syndromesgeneral
24. A 6-year-old with a preceding upper respiratory infection has a croupy cough, high fever, a toxic appearance, thick purulent tracheal secretions, no drooling, and has not responded to racemic epinephrine and corticosteroids. What is the most likely diagnosis?
  • ✓Bacterial tracheitis
  • BAcute epiglottitis
  • CAcute laryngotracheobronchitis
  • DAcute spasmodic laryngitis
Rationale

The chapter describes bacterial tracheitis at a mean age of 5 to 7 years with "a history of previous URI with croupy cough, stridor unaffected by position, toxicity, absence of drooling, respiratory distress, and high fever," adding that "A prominent manifestation is the production of thick, purulent tracheal secretions" and that manifestations resemble laryngotracheobronchitis "but are unresponsive to LTB therapy" (p. 906).

Watch for

(B) shares the toxicity and high fever, and the discriminator is drooling — present in epiglottitis, absent here; a croup that fails racemic epinephrine and steroids is this diagnosis rather than a worse case of the first one.

bronchiolitis and respiratory syncytial virusgeneral
25. An infant hospitalized with respiratory syncytial virus has an oxygen saturation of 92% on room air. What does the chapter direct?
  • ✓Continuous pulse oximetry and supplemental oxygen are not necessary at saturations of 90% or higher
  • BStart supplemental oxygen to keep saturation above 95%
  • CBegin continuous oximetry and titrate oxygen to 98%
  • DStart nebulized albuterol to improve oxygenation
Rationale

The chapter states that "Continuous pulse oximetry and supplemental oxygen are not necessary if oxygen saturations are 90% or higher," and lists oxygen saturation of 90% or higher among the Quality Patient Outcomes for bronchiolitis (p. 908).

Watch for

(D) is among the interventions the chapter rules out, since "Bronchodilators are not recommended and are rarely beneficial"; routine chest percussion and drainage, routine viral testing, and routine radiographs are likewise not recommended.

27. A nurse is planning isolation for an infant admitted with respiratory syncytial virus. What does the chapter direct?
  • ✓Contact and Standard Precautions, with some institutions adding Droplet Precautions
  • BAirborne Precautions in a negative-pressure room
  • CStandard Precautions only, since the virus does not survive on surfaces
  • DNo precautions, since reinfection confers immunity
Rationale

The chapter states that "Contact and Standard Precautions are used with hospitalized patients and some institutions use Droplet precautions in addition," and notes the virus "can survive for several hours on countertops, gloves, paper tissues, and cloth, and for 30 minutes on skin" (pp. 907–908).

Watch for

(B) overapplies airborne precautions, and the chapter rules it out directly — "There is no documentation of distant spread of RSV by small-particle aerosols (airborne transmission)"; transmission is by direct contact, "mainly as a result of inoculation from hand to eye, nose, or other mucous membranes."

30. A 7-year-old has had 2 weeks of malaise and a persistent cough, with patchy infiltrates on radiograph. Which organism is most likely, and what treatment does the chapter name?
  • ✓*Mycoplasma pneumoniae*, treated with erythromycin, azithromycin, or clarithromycin
  • B*Streptococcus pneumoniae*, treated with high-dose amoxicillin
  • CRespiratory syncytial virus, treated symptomatically
  • D*Staphylococcus aureus*, treated with vancomycin
Rationale

The chapter states that *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* "is the most common bacterial pathogen of community-acquired pneumonia in children 5 years of age or older," with an incubation of 2 to 3 weeks and a cough that may last several weeks, treated with erythromycin, azithromycin, and clarithromycin (p. 910).

Watch for

(B) names the most common bacterial pathogen overall in both children and adults, which is what makes it the strongest distractor; the age of the child is the discriminator, and erythromycin is named as the drug of choice for older children and adolescents because of its activity against this organism.

childhood pneumoniageneral
31. A 3-year-old with pneumonia develops abrupt worsening after a viral upper respiratory infection, appears ill with a high fever, and has lobar consolidation. How does this differ from viral pneumonia?
  • ✓Bacterial pneumonia has abrupt onset following a viral infection, high fever, an ill appearance, and lobar consolidation, and is treated with antibiotics
  • BViral pneumonia produces lobar consolidation while bacterial pneumonia produces patchy infiltrates
  • CBacterial pneumonia occurs more frequently than viral pneumonia in children
  • DThe two cannot be distinguished clinically or radiographically
Rationale

The chapter describes bacterial pneumonia as having an onset that "is abrupt and generally follows a viral infection that disturbs the natural defense mechanisms of the upper respiratory tract," while viral pneumonia shows "diffuse or patchy infiltration with a peribronchial distribution" and is treated symptomatically (pp. 909–910).

Watch for

(B) reverses the radiographic patterns, and (C) reverses the frequency, since viral pneumonias "occur more frequently than bacterial pneumonias and are seen in children of all age-groups."

32. A child with pneumonia complains of pain on one side of the chest. Which position does the chapter recommend?
  • ✓Lying on the affected side, which splints the chest and reduces pleural rubbing
  • BLying on the unaffected side, to allow the affected lung to expand
  • CSupine and flat, to equalize pressure
  • DProne, to improve posterior lung drainage

  • ✓Positive, because 5 mm or more is positive in children with immunosuppressive conditions or close contact with contagious tuberculosis
  • BNegative, because the threshold for any child is 10 mm
  • CNegative, because the threshold for a child under 4 years is 15 mm
