the three phases of adolescencegeneral
1. A 16-year-old shows the greatest push for emancipation, a peer-dominant orientation, and what the parents describe as the low point of their relationship with him. Which phase of adolescence does this describe?
  • AEarly adolescence, ages 11 to 14 years
  • ✓Middle adolescence, ages 15 to 17 years
  • CLate adolescence, ages 18 to 22 years
  • DPreadolescence, ending at the thirteenth birthday
Rationale

The three phases are early adolescence at 11 to 14 years, middle at 15 to 17 years, and late at 18 to 22 years. Early adolescence is dominated by the physical changes of puberty and the emotional reaction to them; middle adolescence brings the shift to a peer-dominant orientation, experimentation, the greatest push for emancipation, and the low point in the parent-child relationship; late adolescence brings full maturation, stable intimate relationships, and independent management of health care.

Watch for

(A) is chosen by anyone who associates conflict with the onset of adolescence. The turbulence peaks in the middle phase rather than at the beginning, when the adolescent's attention is still largely on the physical changes themselves.

3. A 12-year-old has recently become preoccupied with her changing body and emotionally reactive to comments about her appearance, but still relies mainly on her family rather than peers. Which phase does this fit?
  • ✓Early adolescence, in which the physical changes of puberty and the emotional reaction to them predominate
  • BMiddle adolescence, in which peer orientation predominates
  • CLate adolescence, in which intimate relationships stabilize
  • DSchool age, since peer orientation has not yet emerged
Rationale

Early adolescence, at 11 to 14 years, is characterized by the physical changes of puberty and the emotional reaction to them. The shift to a peer-dominant orientation belongs to middle adolescence, and stable intimate relationships with independent management of health care belong to late adolescence.

Watch for

(D) reads continued family orientation as evidence the adolescent phase has not begun. The marker of early adolescence is the pubertal change and the response to it, not the point at which peers displace parents — that displacement is the *next* phase.

neuroendocrine events of pubertygeneral
4. A nursing student asks what actually triggers puberty. Which explanation is correct?
  • ✓The pituitary gland and gonads are capable of mature function at any age; what changes at puberty is that the hypothalamus loses its negative sensitivity, releasing gonadotropin-releasing hormone from inhibition
  • BThe pituitary gland matures at puberty and becomes able to secrete gonadotropins for the first time
  • CThe gonads mature first and then signal the hypothalamus
  • DPuberty begins when the adrenal glands first produce androgens in adolescence
Rationale

"The pituitary gland and gonads are capable of mature function… at any age." What changes is that "the hypothalamus loses this negative sensitivity," which releases gonadotropin-releasing hormone from inhibition and begins the cascade.

Watch for

(B) describes maturation of a gland that was already competent, which is the intuitive but incorrect model. The change is the removal of an inhibition rather than the acquisition of a capability, and that distinction is what the chapter states in its own sentence.

5. A nurse is explaining the roles of follicle-stimulating hormone and luteinizing hormone in an adolescent girl. Which description is correct?
  • ✓Follicle-stimulating hormone drives follicle growth and estrogen production, while luteinizing hormone drives ovulation, corpus luteum formation, and progesterone production
  • BLuteinizing hormone drives follicle growth and estrogen, while follicle-stimulating hormone drives ovulation
  • CBoth hormones act only on the adrenal cortex in girls
  • DFollicle-stimulating hormone produces progesterone directly
Rationale

In girls, follicle-stimulating hormone stimulates follicle growth and estrogen production, while luteinizing hormone triggers ovulation, corpus luteum formation, and progesterone production.

Watch for

(B) swaps the two hormones, which is the commonest inversion in this material because the name "follicle-stimulating" is the only mnemonic anchoring either one. Luteinizing hormone is named for the corpus luteum it produces, which is the anchor for the other half.

6. A nurse is explaining the same two hormones in an adolescent boy. Which description is correct?
  • ✓Luteinizing hormone stimulates the Leydig cells to produce testosterone, and follicle-stimulating hormone, together with luteinizing hormone, drives sperm production
  • BFollicle-stimulating hormone stimulates the Leydig cells to produce testosterone
  • CLuteinizing hormone has no role in the male
  • DTestosterone is produced by the pituitary gland in response to gonadotropin-releasing hormone
Rationale

In boys, luteinizing hormone stimulates the Leydig cells to produce testosterone, and follicle-stimulating hormone acting with luteinizing hormone drives sperm production.

(B) is the mirror error of the previous item, carried across sexes. The same two hormones act in both sexes with different targets, so an item can test either sex and the distractor is always the other hormone's job.

7. A nurse is asked why girls typically stop growing earlier than boys. Which explanation does the chapter give?
  • ✓Estrogen stimulates skeletal growth at low levels and inhibits it at high levels, so rising estrogen eventually closes the growth process
  • BEstrogen inhibits skeletal growth at every concentration
  • CGirls produce less growth hormone than boys throughout puberty
  • DTestosterone has no effect on skeletal growth
Rationale

Estrogen stimulates skeletal growth at low levels and inhibits it at high levels. This dose-dependent reversal explains why girls stop growing sooner, and the chapter states it in a single sentence.

Watch for

(B) takes half the relationship and generalizes it, which loses the mechanism entirely — a hormone that only inhibited growth could not also have driven the growth spurt that preceded the inhibition.

8. A 12-year-old girl has just had her first menstrual period, and a 13-year-old boy has had a first ejaculation. Both families ask about fertility. Which response is correct?
  • ✓An adolescent should be considered potentially fertile with a first menstrual period or a first ejaculation
  • BGirls are not fertile for about a year after menarche, because ova are not yet mature enough to be released
  • CBoys are not fertile until viable sperm appear months after first ejaculation
  • DFertility cannot be assumed until Tanner stage 5 is reached
Rationale

The chapter states the clinical rule flatly: "an adolescent should be considered potentially fertile with a first menstrual period or a first ejaculation." It also notes that at menarche "ova still do not generally mature enough to be released," and that viable sperm follow first ejaculation.

Watch for

(B) and (C) both quote the chapter's own physiology correctly and draw the wrong clinical conclusion from it. The physiology is deliberately the distractor here: the counseling rule is set at the earliest possible point rather than at the average one, because an individual adolescent's timing cannot be known in advance.

9. An 11-year-old girl's mother reports that the first change she noticed was a small firm lump under one nipple. Which statement is correct?
  • ✓The first visible change in girls is nipple and areolar change with a breast bud, called thelarche; pubarche typically follows, although many girls have underarm or pubic hair before breast development
  • BPubic hair development always precedes breast development in girls
  • CA unilateral breast bud is abnormal and requires evaluation
  • DMenarche is the first sign of puberty in girls
Rationale

The first visible change in girls is nipple and areolar change with a breast bud, or thelarche. Pubarche "typically follows," but "many have strands of underarm and/or pubic hair before breast development."

Watch for

(B) is too absolute, and the chapter deliberately qualifies the sequence rather than fixing it. The usual order is thelarche then pubarche, but reversal is common enough that the book names it, so an option asserting an invariable order is wrong.

11. A 13-year-old girl and her mother are worried about a clear-to-white vaginal discharge that has been present for several months without itching, odor, or dysuria. What is the nurse's best response?
  • ✓Reassure them that physiologic leukorrhea is normal and a sign that her body is maturing
  • BObtain a culture and begin empiric treatment for candidiasis
  • CAdvise douching to reduce the discharge
  • DRefer for gynecologic evaluation of possible infection
Rationale

Girls or parents "may be concerned that this vaginal discharge is a sign of infection." The nursing response is reassurance that "the discharge is normal and a sign that their body is maturing."

Watch for

(B) and (D) both treat a normal maturational finding as pathology, and the stem gives the discriminators that rule infection out — no itching, odor, or dysuria. Physiologic leukorrhea is a reassurance item, not a workup item.

12. A 14-year-old boy is distressed by tender breast tissue that developed over the past several months. Which response is correct?
  • ✓Gynecomastia occurs in up to 70% of boys in midpuberty and usually disappears within 2 years, although it may persist in obese individuals; reassurance is appropriate

13. A nurse reviews thresholds for abnormal pubertal timing. Which statements are correct? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing

  • ✓Delayed puberty in girls includes no breast development by age 13

14. A 12-year-old girl has just experienced her peak height velocity. Her mother asks how much more she will grow. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓Peak height velocity in girls occurs at about 12 years, 6 to 12 months before menarche, and few girls grow more than 5 cm, about 2 inches, after menarche
  • BPeak height velocity follows menarche by about a year in girls
  • CMost girls gain another 15 cm after menarche
  • DGrowth in girls continues at the same rate until age 18
Rationale

Peak height velocity in girls occurs at about 12 years, 6 to 12 months before menarche, and "Few girls grow more than 5 cm (2 inches) after menarche." Growth in height usually ceases 2 to 2½ years after menarche.

Watch for

(B) inverts the order of the two events, which matters clinically because it changes what a parent should expect. Menarche is a late marker in the growth sequence rather than an early one, so a girl who has begun menstruating has already completed most of her linear growth.

  • ✓Peak height velocity in boys occurs at about 14 years, after testicular and penile growth and after axillary and mature pubic hair
  • BPeak height velocity in boys precedes all genital development
  • CPeak height velocity in boys and girls occurs at the same age
  • DBoys reach peak height velocity before menarche-equivalent maturation

Peak height velocity in boys occurs at about 14 years, after testicular and penile growth and after axillary and mature pubic hair.

(B) is the mirror error of the previous item applied to boys. In girls the growth spurt comes early in the sequence and precedes menarche; in boys it comes late, after the genital changes, which is why boys often appear to mature genitally before they visibly grow.

16. A nurse compares weight and height spurts in adolescent boys and girls. Which statement is correct?
  • ✓Peak weight velocity occurs about 6 months after peak height velocity in girls, whereas weight and height spurts occur simultaneously for boys
  • BPeak weight velocity precedes peak height velocity in girls
  • CWeight and height spurts occur simultaneously in girls and sequentially in boys
  • DWeight gain is not a measured component of the pubertal growth spurt

"Peak weight velocity occurs about 6 months after PHV in girls. In contrast, weight and height spurts occur simultaneously for boys."

(C) reverses which sex shows the lag. The practical consequence is that a girl who has just finished her height spurt is still expected to gain weight, and mistaking that expected gain for excess is a common source of unnecessary dieting at exactly the wrong age.

17. A 13-year-old suddenly needs longer pants but the same shirt size, and his parent wonders whether this is abnormal. Which changes reflect the expected adolescent growth sequence? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Leg length increases before trunk length, by about 6 to 9 months
  • ✓Shoulder and chest growth follows leg growth by about a year
  • ✓Heart rate decreases through adolescence while systolic blood pressure increases
  • ✓Boys gain more in red cell mass, hemoglobin, hematocrit, and serum iron than girls
  • ETrunk and shoulder growth precede leg growth, so shirt size changes first
Rationale

Leg length precedes trunk growth by 6 to 9 months, and shoulders and chest by about a year. Heart rate decreases while systolic blood pressure increases through adolescence, and boys gain more in red cell mass, hemoglobin, hematocrit, and serum iron because of greater muscle mass. Girls run a higher pulse and lower systolic pressure than boys.

(E) is the one option from the wrong side of the sort and reverses the sequence in the stem. The order is shoes, then pants, then shirts — a teenager outgrowing footwear and trouser length while shirts still fit is following the normal pattern, not deviating from it.

18. A 16-year-old can reason abstractly about hypothetical situations in class but makes an impulsive decision about drinking at a party. Which understanding does the chapter offer?
  • ✓Formal operational thought emerges at 11 to 14 years, but under stress, time pressure, sleep deprivation, or peer pressure adolescents abandon their rational thought processes, and most risky health decisions occur under exactly those conditions
  • BAn adolescent with formal operational thought will reason reliably in any setting
  • CFormal operational thought does not develop until age 18
  • DRisky decisions indicate the adolescent has not reached formal operations
Rationale

Formal operational thought emerges at 11 to 14 years, but under "stress, time pressure, sleep deprivation, or peer pressure," adolescents "abandon their rational thought processes," and most risky health decisions occur precisely under those conditions.

Watch for

(B) assumes capacity equals performance, which is the assumption behind a great deal of ineffective adolescent health education. The reasoning ability is genuinely present; the conditions under which risk decisions get made are the conditions that suspend it.

19. A 15-year-old with newly diagnosed diabetes refuses to use an insulin pump at school because "everyone will notice it." Which cognitive distortion does this illustrate?
  • ✓The imaginary audience — the belief that everybody will notice
  • BThe personal fable — the belief that it cannot happen to me
  • CFormal operational reasoning
  • DConcrete operational thinking
Rationale

The imaginary audience is the belief that "everybody will notice," and the chapter's own clinical example is the teen with diabetes refusing to use an insulin pump at school. The discriminator is the theme of being watched.

Watch for

(B) is the paired construct and the one most often substituted, because both involve a distorted sense of the self's importance. Being watched points to the imaginary audience; being exempt or unique points to the personal fable.

21. A nurse is told that adolescent egocentrism is uniformly harmful. Which response reflects the chapter?
  • ✓Narcissistic egocentrism may be an important developmental mechanism leading to positive mental health in mature adults; the harmful form is specifically feeling invincible to danger, while psychological invulnerability is associated with better mastery and coping skills
  • BAll forms of adolescent egocentrism predict delinquency and drug use
  • CAdolescent egocentrism has no relationship to later mental health
  • DPsychological invulnerability and invincibility to danger are the same construct
Rationale

Narcissistic egocentrism "may be an important developmental mechanism that leads to positive mental health among mature adults." The harmful form is invulnerability to danger specifically: "teens who feel invincible to danger have more delinquent behaviors and drug use, whereas teens with psychological invulnerability have better mastery and coping skills."

Watch for

(D) collapses the distinction the chapter draws, and it is the distinction that makes the finding useful. One form predicts risk behavior and the other predicts resilience, so treating them as one construct loses the clinical signal in both directions.

22. A nurse is assessing an adolescent's moral reasoning. Which statement reflects the chapter?
  • ✓Different levels of reasoning are used at different times and in different situations, and Gilligan's caring orientation is an equally valid alternative to Kohlberg's justice orientation, with both sexes capable of both
  • BAdolescents reason consistently at the postconventional level
  • CGilligan's caring orientation represents a lower stage than Kohlberg's justice orientation
  • DGirls reason from care and boys from justice, without overlap
Rationale

"Different levels of reasoning are used at different times and in different situations," so both conventional and principled reasoning coexist in adolescence. Gilligan's caring orientation is an equally valid alternative to Kohlberg's justice orientation rather than a lesser stage, and both sexes are capable of both.

(B) assigns adolescents cleanly to one level, which is tidier than the evidence supports. (D) makes the two orientations sex-specific, which the chapter denies explicitly.

23. A 15-year-old no longer confides in her parents, has developed an intense admiration for a teacher, and invests most of her emotional energy outside the family. Which type of autonomy does this describe?
  • ✓Emotional autonomy, which involves changes in close relationships
  • BBehavioral autonomy, which is the capacity to make independent decisions and follow through
  • CValue autonomy, which consolidates between ages 18 and 20
  • DCognitive autonomy
Rationale

Emotional autonomy involves changes in close relationships — no longer running to parents, developing crushes on other adults, and investing outside the family. Behavioral autonomy is the capacity to make independent decisions and follow through.

Watch for

(B) is the paired construct, and the discriminator is what is changing: relationships point to emotional autonomy, and decisions point to behavioral autonomy. The two develop on different timelines, so an adolescent can be well advanced in one and not the other.

24. A 19-year-old has begun articulating a personal value system that differs from her parents' in considered ways. When does the chapter say this typically consolidates?
  • ✓Relatively late in adolescence, between the ages of 18 and 20 years
  • BIn early adolescence, by age 13
  • CIn middle adolescence, between 15 and 17
  • DNot until the mid-twenties
Rationale

Behavioral independence expands earlier, but the personal value system consolidates "relatively late in adolescence, between the ages of 18 and 20 years."

(C) attaches value autonomy to the phase of greatest outward rebellion, which is when the adolescent most loudly asserts different values. Asserting a different position and holding a consolidated value system are different achievements, and the second arrives years after the first.

26. A 17-year-old attends religious services far less often than she did at 12, but describes her beliefs as more personally important than ever. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • ✓Adolescent beliefs become more oriented toward spiritual and ideological matters and less toward rituals, practice, and strict observance, with the emphasis shifting from attendance to belief; greater religiosity and spirituality are associated with protective, health-promoting behaviors
  • BDeclining attendance indicates loss of faith
  • CReligiosity has no documented relationship to adolescent health behaviors
  • DAdolescents typically increase ritual observance while abandoning belief

Adolescent beliefs become "more oriented toward spiritual and ideological matters" and less toward "rituals, practice, and the strict observance," with the emphasis shifting from attendance to belief. 27. A 16-year-old tells the nurse he has had sexual contact with male partners but identifies as heterosexual and reports attraction to girls. How should the nurse understand this?
  • ✓Sexual orientation has three dimensions — identity, attraction, and behavior — that need not agree with one another
  • BBehavior is the definitive indicator, so the adolescent's stated identity should be disregarded
  • CThe adolescent is confused and should be counseled toward a consistent identity
  • DIdentity always determines both attraction and behavior
Rationale

Sexual orientation has three dimensions: identity, or how one defines oneself; attraction, or who one is drawn to; and behavior, or who one has sex with. The chapter's own example has all three disagreeing.

Watch for

(B) infers orientation from behavior alone, which the chapter specifically rules out. The clinical consequence matters: a nurse who assumes identity from behavior will counsel the wrong risks and may damage the trust the confidentiality discussion was meant to establish.

28. A parent worries that their 14-year-old has close friendships but no romantic relationships. Which response reflects the chapter?
  • ✓Intimate relationships are distinct from sexual relationships and either can exist without the other; same-gender peer intimacy develops first, and opposite-gender intimacy comes relatively late
  • BAbsence of romantic relationships at 14 indicates delayed social development
  • CIntimacy and sexual activity develop together and cannot be separated
  • DOpposite-gender intimacy normally precedes same-gender friendship
Rationale

"Intimate relationships are distinct from sexual relationships," and one can exist without the other in either direction. Same-gender peer intimacy develops first, and opposite-gender intimacy comes "relatively late."

Watch for

(C) fuses the two, which is the assumption behind both parental worry and a good deal of adolescent risk. A teenager can be intimately connected without sexual activity, and sexually active without intimacy, and the chapter treats those as independent axes.

29. Parents ask the nurse about dating for their 13-year-old. Which guidance does the chapter give?
  • ✓Mixed-gender group activities are positive for young teens, and a moderate degree of dating with serious relationships delayed until late adolescence may be the ideal pattern; one-on-one dating with a lot of time alone in early adolescence may lead to sexual intimacy before the teen is ready
  • BEarly exclusive pair dating builds relationship skills and should be encouraged
  • CAll dating should be prohibited until age 18
  • DThe amount and type of dating has no bearing on outcomes
Rationale

"A moderate degree of dating, with serious relationships delayed until late adolescence, may be the ideal pattern." (C) is the protective extreme, and the chapter's recommendation is moderation rather than prohibition — some dating is part of the ideal pattern. The variable that matters is the format and the amount of unsupervised time alone, not whether dating occurs.

  • ✓Other accidental injuries
  • ✓Homicide
  • ✓Suicide
