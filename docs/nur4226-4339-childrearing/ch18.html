1. A 14-year-old boy has sudden severe left scrotal pain with nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain. He is afebrile with no dysuria. The cremasteric reflex is absent on the affected side. Which condition does this describe?
  • ✓Testicular torsion, indicated by sudden onset, gastrointestinal symptoms, absent cremasteric reflex, and the absence of fever or urinary symptoms
  • BEpididymitis, indicated by the scrotal pain and swelling
  • CVaricocele, indicated by the unilateral findings
  • DInguinal hernia
Rationale

Testicular torsion presents with sudden severe pain, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain, an absent cremasteric reflex, and no fever or urinary symptoms. Epididymitis is "slow and insidious," has no gastrointestinal symptoms, and presents with dysuria, fever, and pyuria.

Watch for

(B) is the paired diagnosis, and the chapter supplies the discriminator outright: the gastrointestinal symptoms belong to torsion. Onset speed and the cremasteric reflex are the other two, and this stem gives all three.

2. The same adolescent presents 3 hours after the onset of pain. What is the appropriate management?
  • ✓Manual detorsion may be attempted within 6 hours; beyond 6 hours, or after a failed manual attempt, prompt surgery is required
  • BSchedule an outpatient ultrasound for the following morning
  • CObserve for 24 hours with scrotal elevation and ice
  • DBegin oral antibiotics and reassess in 48 hours
Rationale

Under 6 hours, manual detorsion may be attempted; beyond 6 hours, or after a failed manual attempt, prompt surgery is indicated. The Nursing Alert calls for immediate medical evaluation of a red, painful, swollen scrotum.

Watch for

(B), (C), and (D) all introduce delay, and each is plausible as ordinary clinical caution. Torsion is the one condition in this chapter where scheduling anything is the wrong answer — the 6-hour window is the whole reason the evaluation is immediate.

3. A 16-year-old has a painless scrotal mass described as feeling like a bag of worms on the left. It becomes distended when he stands and decreases when he lies down, and the left testicle is smaller than the right. Which condition is this?
  • ✓Varicocele, the most common treatable cause of male-related impaired fertility
  • BTesticular torsion
  • CEpididymitis
  • DHydrocele
Rationale

A varicocele is typically left-sided and wormlike. Normally the left testicle is larger, but with a varicocele "the left is smaller." The mass "decreases in size when recumbent" and "becomes distended when upright," and it is "the most common treatable cause of male-related impaired fertility."

Watch for

(D) shares the painless-mass presentation. The two positional findings are the discriminator, and note that they run opposite to the usual description of a hernia — this mass is smaller lying down, not larger.

4. A parent asks why their son's scrotal pain warrants an emergency visit rather than a next-day appointment. Which explanation is correct?
  • ✓Testicular torsion is a surgical emergency in which delay beyond 6 hours changes the management from possible manual detorsion to prompt surgery, and a red, painful, swollen scrotum requires immediate medical evaluation
  • BScrotal pain is rarely urgent in adolescents
  • CThe urgency relates to the risk of transmitting infection
  • DEmergency evaluation is needed only if fever is present

5. A 15-year-old has never had a menstrual period, although she developed breasts at age 11. Which classification and first step apply?
  • ✓Primary amenorrhea, since there is no uterine bleeding by age 15 with secondary sex characteristics present and no menarche 4 years after thelarche; pregnancy should be ruled out first
  • BSecondary amenorrhea, since her cycles have stopped
  • CPrimary amenorrhea; begin with follicle-stimulating hormone and thyroid studies
  • DNormal variation requiring no evaluation until age 18
Rationale

Primary amenorrhea is defined as no secondary sex characteristics by 13, no uterine bleeding with secondary characteristics by 15, or no menarche 4 years after thelarche. "Pregnancy is the most common cause of secondary amenorrhea and should be ruled out in both types of amenorrhea even if the adolescent denies sexual activity."

Watch for

(C) identifies the classification correctly and then skips the first step. Pregnancy testing precedes hormonal workup in both types of amenorrhea, and the chapter specifies that this holds even when the adolescent denies sexual activity.

6. A 17-year-old who previously had regular cycles has had no period for 4 months. Which classification applies?
  • ✓Secondary amenorrhea, defined as absence for more than 3 cycles or irregular menses for 6 months
  • BPrimary amenorrhea, since she is over 15
  • CSecondary amenorrhea, defined as absence for more than 12 months
  • DOligomenorrhea, which is a separate diagnosis
Rationale

Secondary amenorrhea is the absence of menses for more than 3 cycles or irregular menses for 6 months. Primary amenorrhea applies to adolescents who have not yet established menses at all.

8. A 15-year-old has suprapubic cramping that begins with the onset of her menses and lasts about a day. A second 15-year-old has a dull ache radiating to her back and thighs with bloating and pelvic fullness. How should these be classified?
  • ✓The first has primary dysmenorrhea, which is prostaglandin-mediated and requires ovulatory cycles; the second has secondary dysmenorrhea, which reflects underlying pathology
  • BBoth have primary dysmenorrhea
  • CThe first has secondary dysmenorrhea and the second primary
  • DNeither pattern is dysmenorrhea
Rationale

Primary dysmenorrhea occurs in ovulatory cycles, is mediated by prostaglandin F2-alpha, and produces suprapubic cramping beginning at the onset of menses and lasting 8 to 48 hours. Secondary dysmenorrhea reflects an underlying pathology and produces dull aching that radiates to the back or thighs with bloating or pelvic fullness.

Watch for

(C) is the inversion. The discriminating features are the radiation and the bloating — cramping confined to the suprapubic area and timed to the onset of bleeding is primary, while aching that travels and is accompanied by fullness points to pathology.

9. An adolescent with primary dysmenorrhea asks when to take her ibuprofen. Which instruction is correct?
  • ✓Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs are most effective if started several days before menses or at least by the onset of bleeding
  • BTake the medication once cramping becomes uncomfortable
  • CTake the medication only after the first full day of bleeding
  • DBegin oral contraceptives instead, since nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs are ineffective
Rationale

Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs are "most effective if started several days before menses or at least by the onset of bleeding." The escalation is scripted: try a different drug in the class first, and only after a 6-month unsuccessful trial of a second agent do oral contraceptives enter.

Watch for

(B) is how most people take an analgesic and it defeats the mechanism. These drugs work by blocking prostaglandin production rather than by relieving established pain, so starting after the cramping begins means the prostaglandins responsible are already circulating.

10. A 16-year-old has irregular heavy bleeding, and an extensive workup has found no pathologic cause. Which term applies?
  • ✓Dysfunctional uterine bleeding, which is abnormal uterine bleeding without a pathogenic cause and is diagnosed only after ruling out all other causes
  • BThe two terms are synonymous
  • CAbnormal uterine bleeding, which by definition excludes an identified cause
  • DPrimary dysmenorrhea
Rationale

Dysfunctional uterine bleeding is abnormal uterine bleeding without a pathogenic cause. Abnormal uterine bleeding is "most commonly caused by anovulation," and the dysfunctional designation "is made only after ruling out all other causes."

Watch for

(B) treats the two as interchangeable, which is the commonest error with this pair. Abnormal uterine bleeding is the umbrella finding; the dysfunctional label is a diagnosis of exclusion reached only after the workup in this stem has come back negative.

12. A 17-year-old reports irritability, bloating, and breast tenderness that begin after ovulation, worsen in the week before her period, and are gone by the time her period ends. Which condition does this describe?
  • ✓Premenstrual syndrome, which begins at ovulation, recurs in the luteal phase, and disappears by the end of menstruation
  • BPremenstrual dysphoric disorder, which is diagnosed on this symptom pattern alone
  • CGeneralized anxiety disorder, since the symptoms include irritability
  • DSymptoms present throughout the month, which is characteristic of premenstrual syndrome
Rationale

Premenstrual syndrome starts at ovulation, recurs in the luteal phase, and disappears by the end of menstruation. Premenstrual dysphoric disorder is the severe variant, affects 2% to 6%, and is a distinct DSM-5 diagnosis. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors are first-line pharmacologic therapy for both.

Watch for

(D) states the opposite of the defining feature and is worth recognizing as a distractor pattern: symptoms that persist all month are not premenstrual syndrome, because the cyclic resolution is what makes the diagnosis.

14. A 17-year-old has a thin, white-gray discharge with a fishy odor. Microscopy shows clue cells, the pH is 5.0, and the whiff test is positive. Which infection is this, and does her partner need treatment?
  • ✓Bacterial vaginosis; treatment of sexual partners is not recommended routinely
  • BTrichomoniasis; the partner must be treated to prevent recurrence
  • CCandidiasis; the partner does not require treatment
  • DBacterial vaginosis; the partner must be treated to prevent recurrence
Rationale

Bacterial vaginosis produces a thin, white, gray, or milky discharge with a fishy odor, clue cells, a pH of 4.5 or above, and a positive whiff test. For bacterial vaginosis, "treatment of sexual partners is not recommended routinely."

Watch for

(D) has the right organism and the wrong partner rule, which is the half of this item that changes what the nurse does. Partner treatment is required for trichomoniasis, where the organisms "harbor in the urethra or prostate," and not routinely for bacterial vaginosis.

15. A 16-year-old has a yellow-green frothy malodorous discharge, a vaginal pH above 5.0, and strawberry spots on the cervix. Which infection is this?
  • ABacterial vaginosis
  • ✓Trichomoniasis, diagnosed by protozoa on a saline wet mount, with partner treatment required
  • CCandidiasis, diagnosed by pseudohyphae on potassium hydroxide preparation
  • DChlamydia
Rationale

Trichomoniasis produces a yellow-green, frothy, malodorous discharge with strawberry spots and a pH above 5.0, with protozoa visible on a saline wet mount. Partner treatment is required because trichomonads "harbor in the urethra or prostate."

(C) names the third member of this trio, and the three discharges are the most swappable set in the chapter. 17. An adolescent being treated for a vaginal yeast infection asks whether she can use tampons during her period while finishing the medication. Which response is correct?
  • ✓Do not use tampons, because the medication will be absorbed by the tampon, and complete the full course even during menstruation
  • BTampons are acceptable and do not affect the medication
  • CStop the medication during menstruation and resume afterward
  • DSwitch to an oral agent whenever menstruation begins
Rationale

Tampons should not be used during treatment because "the medication will be absorbed by the tampon," and the course is completed even during menstruation. A first suspected infection warrants seeing a provider; subsequent episodes may be self-treated with over-the-counter preparations.

18. A sexually active 17-year-old is asymptomatic and asks why she should be screened for chlamydia. Which response is correct?
  • ✓Chlamydia is often silent and highly destructive, and its worst outcome is pelvic inflammatory disease leading to infertility, ectopic pregnancy, and chronic pelvic pain
  • BChlamydia reliably produces a noticeable discharge in women
  • CScreening is unnecessary in the absence of symptoms
  • DChlamydia produces symptoms in women but not in men
Rationale

Chlamydia is "often silent and highly destructive," and its worst outcome is pelvic inflammatory disease leading to infertility, ectopic pregnancy, and chronic pelvic pain. Gonorrhea shows the opposite pattern between the sexes: women are often asymptomatic, with a greenish-yellow purulent discharge when symptomatic, while men usually have symptoms.

Watch for

(D) inverts the sex pattern that applies to gonorrhea and misapplies it to chlamydia. The reason asymptomatic screening exists is that the damage accrues silently, so waiting for symptoms means waiting until the tubal injury has occurred.

  • BTreat gonorrhea alone and test for chlamydia in 3 months
  • CTreat chlamydia only if the patient is symptomatic

The CDC "recommends concomitant treatment for chlamydia because coinfection is common." For chlamydia, azithromycin is preferred "when compliance is a problem because only one dose is needed."

22. A 16-year-old develops lower abdominal pain a few days after her period ended and is diagnosed with pelvic inflammatory disease. Which statements reflect the chapter?
  • ✓Ascending infection is facilitated just after menses because the cervical os is slightly open, the cervical mucus barrier is absent, and menstrual blood is an excellent growth medium; acute care includes bed rest in semi-Fowler position and few pelvic examinations during the acute phase
  • BOnset just after menses argues against the diagnosis
  • CFrequent pelvic examinations during the acute phase are needed to monitor progress
  • DAmbulation is encouraged during the acute phase to prevent adhesions
Rationale

Three factors facilitate ascent just after menses: "the cervical os is slightly open, the cervical mucus barrier is absent, and menstrual blood is an excellent medium for growth." Acute management includes bed rest in a semi-Fowler position, few pelvic examinations during the acute phase, and follow-up endocervical cultures as a test of cure.

(B) treats the timing as evidence against the diagnosis when it is a diagnostic clue in favor of it. (C) increases exactly the examinations the chapter says to limit while the infection is acute.

23. A 17-year-old has a single nontender shallow genital ulcer that appeared about 2 weeks after a sexual encounter. Two months later he develops a rash that includes his palms and soles. Which infection does this describe?
  • ✓Syphilis, with a painless chancre at 14 to 21 days and a secondary rash at 6 weeks to a few months that includes the palms and soles
  • BHerpes simplex virus, with a primary outbreak followed by recurrence
  • CHuman papillomavirus, with condylomata acuminata
  • DChancroid
Rationale

The syphilitic chancre appears at 14 to 21 days as a "nontender, shallow ulcer." Secondary syphilis appears at 6 weeks to a few months with a rash "including the palms and soles," which is the classic identifier, along with condylomata lata — "broad, painless, pink-gray, wartlike" lesions.

Watch for

(C) names a lesion with a confusingly similar name: condylomata *acuminata* are the warts of human papillomavirus, while condylomata *lata* belong to secondary syphilis. (B) would be painful and vesicular rather than a single painless ulcer.

26. A 19-year-old has a first herpes outbreak with lesions plus fever, chills, malaise, headache, and lymphadenopathy lasting about 2 weeks. A second patient has local tingling followed by lesions lasting 9 days without systemic symptoms. How do these differ?
  • ✓The first is a primary infection, which lasts 2 to 3 weeks with systemic symptoms; the second is a recurrence, which is local only with prodromal tingling and lasts 8 to 10 days, with systemic symptoms usually absent

  • DSystemic symptoms indicate bacterial superinfection rather than primary herpes

27. A 16-year-old presents with amenorrhea and nausea, and the nurse suspects pregnancy. Which approach is correct?
  • ✓Do not test for pregnancy without the adolescent's knowledge, and before testing discuss what she will do if the test is positive
  • BOrder the test with routine laboratory work without raising it, to avoid causing anxiety
  • CNotify the parents before testing so they can be present for the result

Pregnancy testing is not done without the adolescent's knowledge, and before testing the nurse should "discuss what she will do if the test is positive." (D) postpones a conversation that shapes how the result lands, and the chapter places it deliberately before the test rather than after. sexual assaultgeneral
31. A 15-year-old discloses consensual sexual contact with a 20-year-old partner. What is the correct classification and action?
  • ✓This is statutory rape — consensual contact by someone 18 or older with a person under the age of consent — and all statutory rape requires reporting to child protective services or local law enforcement
  • BNo report is required, since the contact was consensual
  • CThis is acquaintance rape, and reporting is at the adolescent's discretion
  • DReporting is required only if the adolescent requests it
Rationale

Statutory rape is consensual contact by someone 18 years or older with a person under the age of consent. All statutory rape "require[s] reporting to child protective services or local law enforcement." Rape is forced contact, and acquaintance or date rape occurs between parties who know each other.

Watch for

(B) treats consent as decisive, and consent is built into the definition of statutory rape rather than excluding it. That is what makes this category counterintuitive: the act is consensual by definition and mandatory to report anyway.

(B) honors an understandable request at the cost of evidence that cannot be recovered afterward. The nurse's task is to explain why rather than simply to refuse — which is why the two reassurance messages come first.

  • DAvoidant restrictive food intake disorder
Rationale

Table 18.4 contrasts the two profiles. 35. A severely malnourished 15-year-old with anorexia nervosa is admitted and refeeding is begun. What is the greatest danger and its mechanism?
  • ✓Refeeding syndrome, with the risk of heart failure greatest in the first 2 weeks, caused by phosphate shifting from extracellular to intracellular; prevention is slower refeeding, replacing phosphorus, and avoiding high sodium
  • BRefeeding syndrome, caused by excess potassium intake, greatest in the second month
  • CImmediate aspiration, which is the principal risk of refeeding
  • DHyperglycemia, which is the principal danger of refeeding
Rationale

The risk of heart failure is greatest in the first 2 weeks of refeeding, with a risk window of 2 to 3 weeks for patients about 10% below ideal body weight. The cause is phosphate shifting from extracellular to intracellular. Prevention is slower refeeding, replacing phosphorus, and avoiding high sodium, with monitoring of electrolytes, edema, and signs of congestive heart failure.

Watch for

(B) has the right syndrome with the wrong electrolyte and the wrong window. Phosphate is the mechanism, and the danger is front-loaded into the first 2 weeks — which is counterintuitive, because that is the period in which the patient appears to be improving.

40. An adolescent who used cocaine sleeps for an extended period and then reports lack of energy, irritability, appetite changes, and irregular sleep. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • ✓The long sleep after a high is the crash and should not be confused with physical withdrawal; withdrawal itself presents as lack of energy and motivation, irritability, appetite changes, psychomotor delay, and irregular sleep
  • BThe long sleep is the defining feature of physical withdrawal
  • CStimulants produce strong physical dependence with a dramatic withdrawal syndrome
  • DThese symptoms indicate a primary depressive disorder unrelated to substance use
Rationale

Cocaine withdrawal presents as "lack of energy and motivation, irritability, appetite changes, psychomotor delay, irregular sleep." The crash — a long period of sleep after a high — "should not be confused" with physical withdrawal. Stimulants generally produce strong psychologic but not strong physical dependence.

Watch for

(C) expects the dramatic physical withdrawal associated with other drug classes, and stimulants do not produce it. The withdrawal here looks like depression rather than like a physiologic crisis, which is why it is easy to misattribute.

43. A 17-year-old who has been severely depressed for months appears suddenly cheerful and calm, and tells the nurse in confidence that he has a plan to end his life. Which two actions are correct?
  • ✓Recognize that sudden improvement after deep depression may mean the adolescent has decided and found the means; confidentiality cannot be honored for self-destructive behavior, so the suicidal behavior is reported to the family and other professionals, and the adolescent is told that this will be done
  • BAccept the improvement as genuine recovery and honor the confidentiality he requested
  • CReport the disclosure without informing him, to preserve the therapeutic relationship
  • DHonor confidentiality unless he attempts suicide
Rationale

"It is a mistake to be lulled into a false sense of security when an adolescent's depression is apparently relieved. The improvement in attitude may mean that the adolescent has made the decision and found the means to carry out the threat." And: "In the case of self-destructive behaviors, confidentiality cannot be honored. Suicidal behavior is reported to the family and other professionals, and adolescents are informed that this will be done."

Watch for

(C) gets the reporting right and omits the second half — the adolescent is told the report will be made, which is what keeps the earlier confidentiality discussion honest. (B) misses both halves, and this confidentiality exception reverses the rule taught throughout Chapter 17, which is precisely what makes it a reliable trap.
