national health promotion initiatives for childrenscreen time and media guidance
1. A mother of a 12-month-old says she lets the baby watch cartoons on a tablet for about 20 minutes while she cooks, and asks whether that is acceptable. Based on the 2016 AAP media guidelines, how should the nurse respond?
  • ATwenty minutes daily is within the recommended limit for this age
  • ✓No screen time is recommended before 18 months of age, except for video calling with a grandparent or loved one
  • CScreen time is acceptable at this age as long as the content is educational
  • DScreen time should be avoided entirely until the child is 5 years old
Rationale

The AAP 2016 guidelines quoted on p. 2 state that "For infants less than 18 months of age, no screen time is recommended except for video calling with a grandparent or loved one." The guidelines deliberately "shift the importance from what is on the screen to who is viewing the information with the young child."

Watch for

(C) is the reflex pick because "educational content" is the compromise parents expect a nurse to offer, and it is exactly the framing the 2016 revision abandoned. Under 18 months the recommendation is about the interaction, not the content.

Table 1.5 defines the measure as the percentage of children 2 years of age who had the listed series — four DTaP, three IPV, one MMR, three HiB, three Hep B, one VZV, four PCV, one Hep A, two or three rotavirus, and two influenza vaccines — "by their second birthday."
