severe acute malnutrition and protein-energy deficiencygeneral
1. A 3-year-old seen in a refugee clinic has thin, wasted extremities, a prominent abdomen, and scaly, dry, depigmented skin. The child was weaned from the breast when a younger sibling was born. Which form of severe acute malnutrition does this describe, and what is the deficit?
  • ✓Kwashiorkor, primarily a deficiency of protein with an adequate supply of calories
  • BMarasmus, general malnutrition of both calories and protein
  • CMarasmic kwashiorkor, in which stunted growth occurs without edema
  • DKwashiorkor, a deficiency of both calories and protein
Rationale

Kwashiorkor is "primarily a deficiency of protein with an adequate supply of calories." It develops "in the first child, usually between 1 and 4 years of age, when weaned from the breast after the second child is born," and presents with "thin, wasted extremities and a prominent abdomen from edema (ascites)" and skin that is "scaly and dry" with depigmentation. The word itself means "the sickness the older child gets when the next baby is born."

Watch for

(D) attaches the correct name to marasmus's deficit, which is the commonest error because both are called malnutrition and the calorie-versus-protein split is the only thing separating them. (C) names a real entity, but marasmic kwashiorkor requires edema, severe wasting, and stunted growth together.

severe acute malnutrition and protein-energy deficiencygeneral
2. A 4-month-old in the same clinic shows gradual wasting and atrophy of body tissues, especially subcutaneous fat, with no edema, after breastfeeding was unsuccessful. Which form of severe acute malnutrition is this?
  • AKwashiorkor, from protein deficiency with adequate calories
  • ✓Marasmus, from general malnutrition of both calories and protein
  • CSecondary lactase deficiency from intestinal damage
  • DFailure to thrive from defective utilization
Rationale

Marasmus "results from general malnutrition of both calories and protein" and is "characterized by gradual wasting and atrophy of body tissues, especially of subcutaneous fat." It may appear "in infants as young as 3 months of age if breastfeeding is unsuccessful," and it carries no edema.

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error of the previous item. Absence of edema is the discriminating finding at the bedside, and the age here — months rather than years — points the same way.

3. A child with kwashiorkor is admitted. Which findings should the nurse expect? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Edema that masks severe muscular atrophy, making the child appear less debilitated than he or she actually is
  • ✓Increased susceptibility to infection from protein deficiency
  • ✓Blindness from severe lack of vitamin A
  • DAbsence of edema, with wasting of subcutaneous fat as the dominant finding
  • ✓Anemia and fatty infiltration of the liver
Rationale

In kwashiorkor the edema "often masks severe muscular atrophy making the child appear less debilitated than he or she actually is." The chapter also lists blindness from severe lack of vitamin A, deficiencies of iron, calcium, and zinc, a blunted immune response, fatty liver infiltration, and anemia. "Protein deficiency increases the child's susceptibility to infection, which eventually results in death."

(D) is the one option from the wrong side of the sort, and it is marasmus's defining appearance. It pulls because the word malnutrition summons a wasted image, and kwashiorkor's edema hides exactly that — which is also why these children are undertriaged.

severe acute malnutrition and protein-energy deficiencygeneral
4. A nurse is teaching about the causes of severe acute malnutrition worldwide. Which statement is accurate?
  • ALack of food is always the primary cause, so increasing food supply is the only meaningful intervention
  • ✓Lack of food is not always the primary cause; in many developing and underdeveloped nations diarrhea is a major factor
  • CSevere acute malnutrition does not occur in the United States, where food supplies are ample
  • DKwashiorkor in the United States is most often caused by excessive whole cow's milk intake
Rationale

"Lack of food is not always the primary cause of malnutrition. In many developing and underdeveloped nations, diarrhea (gastroenteritis) is a major factor." In the United States "SAM may occur despite ample food supplies," and the chapter names a rice beverage diet as a domestic cause of kwashiorkor, since that drink "contains only 0.13 g of protein per ounce (compared with the 0.5 g found in human milk and infant formulas)."

Watch for

(C) is the reflex assumption that makes this chapter's United States cases invisible; the book lists cystic fibrosis, renal disease, cancer, bone marrow transplantation, HIV, inborn errors of metabolism, and prolonged untreated anorexia nervosa among domestic causes.

5. A parent asks why food allergies seem to appear in babies rather than later in childhood. Which explanation does the chapter give?
  • ✓The immature intestinal tract is more permeable to proteins than the mature tract, increasing the likelihood of an immune response
  • BInfants produce more immunoglobulin E than older children at every age
  • CInfants lack the enzymes needed to digest most food proteins
  • DBreast milk sensitizes the infant to common allergens during the first months of life
Rationale

Food allergies "are most common during infancy because the immature intestinal tract is more permeable to proteins than the mature intestinal tract thus increasing the likelihood of an immune response."

Watch for

(C) substitutes an enzyme problem for an immune one, which is the same confusion that runs through the cow's milk allergy and lactose intolerance pair later in this chapter. (D) inverts the recommendation, which is "exclusive breastfeeding for at least 4 to 6 months."

6. Both parents of a newborn have documented allergies. What risk should the nurse convey?
  • AThe infant's risk is the same as that of the general population
  • BThe infant has a 50% or greater risk, the same figure as with one affected parent
  • ✓The infant has up to a 100% risk, and some atopic infants can be identified at birth from elevated immunoglobulin E in umbilical cord blood
  • DThe infant has roughly a 25% risk, following an autosomal recessive pattern
Rationale

"Children who have one parent with allergy have a 50% or greater risk"; "Children who have both parents with allergy have up to a 100% risk." Some atopic infants "can be identified at birth from elevated levels of IgE in umbilical cord blood." Atopy is defined as "allergy with a hereditary tendency."

Watch for

(B) is the one-parent figure, and it is picked by anyone carrying a single number for family history. The chapter gives two, and the two-parent figure is double it.

food allergy recognition and anaphylaxis managementgeneral
7. A 2-year-old with a known peanut allergy begins wheezing with decreased air movement and dyspnea about 10 minutes after a meal. There are no hives, rash, or flushing. What should the nurse conclude?
  • AThis is an asthma exacerbation, since anaphylaxis always begins with skin signs
  • ✓This may be anaphylaxis; in children who died of food anaphylaxis the reactions often did not begin with skin signs but mimicked an acute asthma attack
  • CThis is too early to be anaphylaxis, which begins 2 to 48 hours after ingestion
  • DThis is oral allergy syndrome, which produces wheezing without skin involvement
Rationale

In the fatal cases the chapter describes, "Onset of the reactions occurred shortly after ingestion (5 to 30 minutes). In most of these children, the reactions did not begin with skin signs, such as hives, red rash, and flushing, but rather mimicked an acute asthma attack (e.g., wheezing, decreased air movement in airways, dyspnea)."

Watch for

(A) is the assumption behind these deaths, and it is why the chapter reports them: waiting for hives before treating is what made the reactions fatal. (C) applies the delayed-allergy window of 2 to 48 hours to an immediate reaction. (D) misdescribes oral allergy syndrome, which is "edema and pruritus involving the lips, tongue, palate, and throat."

8. A child with a known food allergy is being assessed after an accidental ingestion. Which findings indicate that intramuscular epinephrine should be given? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Itching sensation or tightness in the throat, hoarseness, difficulty swallowing
  • ✓A barky cough, wheezing, dyspnea, or cyanosis
  • ✓Mild cardiac dysrhythmia or mild hypotension
  • DAn isolated area of localized itching where the food touched the skin
  • ✓Severe bradycardia, hypotension, or loss of consciousness
Rationale

The Nursing Alert lists four indications for intramuscular epinephrine, any one of which qualifies: throat itching or tightness with hoarseness and difficulty swallowing; a barky cough, wheezing, dyspnea, cyanosis, or respiratory arrest; mild cardiac dysrhythmia or mild hypotension; and severe bradycardia, hypotension, cardiac arrest, or loss of consciousness.

Watch for

(D) is the one option from the wrong side of the sort, and it is where antihistamines belong: diphenhydramine and cetirizine "are effect[ive] for cutaneous and nasal symptoms but not for airway symptoms." Note that even mild hypotension is on the epinephrine list, which is a lower threshold than most learners set.

9. A 4-year-old treated with epinephrine for food anaphylaxis improves within minutes, and the parents ask to take the child home. What should the nurse explain?
  • ✓A biphasic reaction — apparent recovery followed by acute recurrence of symptoms — is reported in as many as 35% of children, so continued close observation is needed
  • BDischarge is appropriate once symptoms resolve, because epinephrine prevents recurrence
  • CA dose of diphenhydramine at home will prevent any recurrence of airway symptoms
  • DRecurrence can happen only if the child ingests the allergen again
Rationale

A biphasic reaction is "an immediate response to treatment, apparent recovery, and then acute recurrence of symptoms," reported "in as many as 35% of children." Children with severe food allergies should also "wear a medical identification bracelet and have an injectable epinephrine cartridge (EpiPen) readily available," plus a written emergency treatment plan.

Watch for

(C) offers an antihistamine for exactly the symptoms it does not treat: diphenhydramine and cetirizine are effective for cutaneous and nasal symptoms "but not for airway symptoms."

food allergy diagnosis and prevention evidencegeneral
10. A family asks which test will confirm their child's suspected food allergy. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓The gold standard is the double-blind, placebo-controlled food challenge; skin prick and serum immunoglobulin E testing are adjuncts and singly should not be used for diagnosis
  • BA serum immunoglobulin E level is definitive and is the recommended first test
  • CThe atopy patch test establishes the diagnosis when skin prick testing is equivocal
  • DThe intradermal test is the gold standard for food allergy in children
Rationale

"The gold standard is the double-blind, placebo-controlled food challenge." Skin prick and serum IgE "may be used as an adjunct... but singly should not be used for diagnosis," and "The atopy patch test, intradermal test, and serum IgE test are not recommended for establishing a diagnosis."

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick because a blood test feels more objective than a supervised feeding, and it is one of the three tests the chapter names explicitly as not recommended for establishing a diagnosis.

11. An expectant mother with a strong family history of allergy asks what she can do to prevent food allergy in her baby. Which statements reflect the chapter's evidence? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓There is no evidence that maternal avoidance of common food allergens during pregnancy or lactation prevents food allergies in children
  • ✓There is no evidence that soy formulas prevent allergic disease in infants and children
  • ✓Delaying the introduction of highly allergenic foods past 4 to 6 months of age may not be as protective as previously believed
  • DProlonged or complete avoidance of foods is recommended, since lack of exposure to antigens is protective
  • ✓Exclusive breastfeeding for at least 4 to 6 months is recommended
Rationale

The chapter states each of the three negatives directly and recommends "exclusive breastfeeding for at least 4 to 6 months." It adds that "The prolonged or complete avoidance of foods to prevent food allergy is not recommended, and there is some evidence that lack of exposure to antigens may be detrimental."

Watch for

(D) is the one option from the wrong side of the sort, and it inverts the evidence precisely: lack of exposure may be detrimental rather than protective. That reversal is what most families, and much older teaching, still hold.

food allergy diagnosis and prevention evidencegeneral
12. A 3-year-old has been on a strict milk-elimination diet for a confirmed allergy. What ongoing care does the chapter direct?
  • ✓An annual nutritional assessment, because dietary restriction of milk may lead to calcium, vitamin D, calorie, and protein deficiencies
  • BNo additional monitoring, since elimination diets are nutritionally complete by definition
  • CAnnual skin prick testing to confirm that the allergy persists
  • DPermanent avoidance of the food in every form, since cooking does not change tolerance
Rationale

Children with food allergies "are at risk for inadequate nutrient intake and growth failure," and should have "an annual nutritional assessment." "Dietary restrictions of milk may lead to calcium, vitamin D, calorie, and protein deficiencies," and eliminating wheat "may result in inadequate intake of the B vitamins, iron, and calories."

Watch for

(D) contradicts the chapter's note that children may tolerate previously allergenic foods "when these foods are extensively heated as in muffins or breads." It is the reflex answer because the word allergy reads as absolute.

cow's milk allergy versus lactose intolerancegeneral
13. A 3-month-old formula-fed infant has diarrhea, bloody stools, eczema, and pallor, and is otherwise healthy. Which condition should the nurse suspect first?
  • ✓Cow's milk allergy, which may be manifested within the first 4 months of life
  • BPrimary lactase deficiency, which usually manifests after 4 or 5 years of age
  • CCongenital lactase deficiency, which is the most common lactose intolerance in infancy
  • DColic, which is defined by bloody stools and eczema
Rationale

Cow's milk allergy "may be manifested within the first 4 months of life," with signs appearing "within 45 minutes of milk ingestion or after several days." Box 11.2 lists diarrhea, bloody stools, and rectal bleeding among gastrointestinal signs, and eczema and "Pallor (from anemia secondary to chronic blood loss in gastrointestinal tract)" among the others. The presentation is protean: "colic, diarrhea, vomiting, GI bleeding, gastroesophageal reflux, chronic constipation, or sleeplessness in an otherwise healthy infant."

Watch for

(C) mislabels congenital lactase deficiency, which is "extremely rare." Primary late-onset deficiency is the most common type and does not appear in infancy at all.

cow's milk allergy versus lactose intolerancegeneral
14. An infant with confirmed cow's milk allergy needs a formula change. Which choice reflects the chapter's guidance, and what caution accompanies it?
  • AA soy-based formula, which is safe because soy protein is unrelated to cow's milk protein
  • ✓A casein hydrolysate formula such as Pregestimil, Nutramigen, or Alimentum, noting that approximately 50% of infants sensitive to cow's milk protein also react to soy-based formulas
  • CA reduced-lactose formula, since lactose is the offending component
  • DWhole cow's milk diluted with water, to reduce the protein load
Rationale

Treatment is a change to "a casein hydrolysate milk formula (Pregestimil, Nutramigen, or Alimentum) in which the protein has been broken down into its amino acids through enzymatic hydrolysis." The AAP recommends extensively hydrolyzed formulas, and "approximately 50% of infants who are sensitive to cow's milk protein will also demonstrate a sensitivity to soy-based formulas." Amino acid-based alternatives exist but cost is a major consideration.

Watch for

(A) is the substitution practitioners often reach for first because of expense, and the chapter says so — but half of these infants react to soy as well, so calling it safe is the error. (C) treats a protein allergy as a sugar intolerance, which is the central confusion of this topic.

cow's milk allergy versus lactose intolerancegeneral
15. A parent says their 6-year-old is "allergic to milk" because dairy causes bloating, flatulence, and diarrhea about an hour after eating it. Which statement distinguishes lactose intolerance from cow's milk allergy?
  • ALactose intolerance is an immune reaction to milk protein and can produce anaphylaxis
  • ✓Lactose intolerance is a deficiency of the enzyme lactase needed to digest milk sugar, and it will not manifest as an anaphylactic-type reaction
  • CCow's milk allergy produces symptoms only after several days, so an hour is too soon for it
  • DThe two are the same condition described at different ages
Rationale

Lactose intolerance "encompasses at least four different conditions that involve a deficiency of the enzyme lactase, which is needed for the hydrolysis or digestion of lactose in the small intestine," with symptoms appearing "within 30 minutes to several hours of lactose consumption." The chapter names the key discrimination outright: "An important distinction between lactose intolerance and food allergy is that lactose intolerance will not manifest as an anaphylactic-type reaction."

Watch for

(A) inverts the definition, and it is why this distinction earns a question: families use the word allergy for both conditions, and only one of them can kill. (C) misstates cow's milk allergy timing, which runs from 45 minutes to several days.

cow's milk allergy versus lactose intolerancegeneral
16. A preterm infant born at 32 weeks of gestation has lactose malabsorption. Which type of lactase deficiency does this represent?
  • ACongenital lactase deficiency, which requires a lifelong lactose-free diet
  • BPrimary, or late-onset, lactase deficiency
  • ✓Developmental lactase deficiency, the relative deficiency observed in preterm infants of less than 34 weeks of gestation
  • DSecondary lactase deficiency following intestinal damage
Rationale

Developmental lactase deficiency is "the relative lactase deficiency observed in preterm infants of less than 34 weeks of gestation." Congenital deficiency is extremely rare and requires "a lifelong lactose-free or extremely reduced lactose diet"; primary deficiency is the most common type and appears after 4 or 5 years; secondary deficiency follows intestinal damage from conditions such as celiac disease, kwashiorkor, giardiasis, HIV, or rotavirus.

Watch for

(A) is chosen because present-from-birth sounds like congenital. Congenital lactase deficiency appears in a newborn "soon after birth when the newborn has consumed lactose-containing milk" and is permanent; prematurity has its own named category, and it is relative rather than absolute.

cow's milk allergy versus lactose intolerancegeneral
17. A school-age child with lactose intolerance has been placed on a completely dairy-free diet by the family. What should the nurse advise?
  • AContinue complete elimination, since any amount of dairy will produce symptoms
  • ✓Decrease rather than eliminate dairy, because dairy avoidance contributes to reduced bone mineral density and osteoporosis later in life
  • CReplace all dairy with soy formula, which supplies equivalent calcium
  • DAdd lactase tablets only if symptoms later develop on the dairy-free diet
Rationale

"Some advocate decreasing amounts of dairy products rather than total elimination, especially in small children," because "dairy avoidance in children and adolescents with lactose intolerance will contribute to reduced bone mineral density and osteoporosis later in life." The chapter adds that "dietary lactose enhances calcium absorption and... lactose-free diets may negatively affect bone mineralization." Calcium and vitamin D should be supplemented.

Watch for

(A) is the intuitive management and is the one the chapter argues against. The harm from total avoidance is skeletal and appears years later, so it is invisible at the moment the decision is made.

cow's milk allergy versus lactose intolerancegeneral
18. A family asks how to keep some dairy in their lactose-intolerant child's diet. Which strategy set does the chapter support?
  • ✓Most people tolerate about 1 cup of milk per day, milk is better tolerated with meals than alone, and fresh plain yogurt is often tolerated because its inactive lactase is activated by the temperature and pH of the duodenum
  • BMilk is best tolerated on an empty stomach, when intestinal transit is fastest
  • CFrozen and flavored yogurts are tolerated better than fresh plain yogurt
  • DHard cheeses should be avoided, since they carry the highest lactose content
Rationale

"Most people can tolerate small amounts of lactose (approximately 1 cup of milk per day [12 g])." "Milk taken at meals may be better tolerated than when taken alone." Yogurt "contains inactive lactase enzyme which is activated by the temperature and pH of the duodenum," substituting for endogenous lactase, and "Fresh, plain yogurt may be tolerated better than frozen or flavored yogurt." Hard cheeses, lactase-treated dairy products, and lactase tablets are also useful.

Watch for

(C) inverts the yogurt guidance, and it pulls because frozen yogurt feels like the more processed and therefore more treated product. The chapter says the opposite, and the mechanism explains why: freezing does not activate the enzyme, the duodenum does.

failure to thrive in the infantgeneral
19. A 9-month-old's weight has fallen from the 50th to the 4th percentile over four months. Which statement supports a diagnosis of failure to thrive?
  • ✓Growth parameters that drop more than 2 percentiles from baseline, or are persistently below the third to fifth percentiles, meet the diagnostic criteria
  • BA single weight below the 50th percentile is sufficient for the diagnosis
  • CHeight for age is the most accurate indicator of undernutrition
  • DThe diagnosis requires a weight below the first percentile
Rationale

Failure to thrive is diagnosed on growth parameters that "(1) drop more than 2 percentiles from baseline, (2) are persistently below the third to fifth percentiles, or (3) are less than the 80th percentile of median weight for height." "Weight for length is reported to be a more accurate indicator of undernutrition," and it is "the finding of a pattern of persistent deviation from established growth parameters" that matters.

Watch for

(C) names the wrong ratio — weight for length, not height for age, is the more accurate indicator. (B) mistakes a below-average measurement for a deviation, and no single measurement makes this diagnosis.

failure to thrive in the infantgeneral
20. A 2-year-old has a weight below the fifth percentile with length at the tenth percentile. A second 2-year-old has both weight and length below the fifth percentile. How should the nurse interpret these two patterns?
  • AThe first child has chronic failure to thrive and the second has acute failure to thrive
  • ✓The first child has acute failure to thrive and the second has chronic failure to thrive, indicating ongoing malnutrition
  • CBoth patterns indicate acute failure to thrive
  • DNeither pattern meets diagnostic criteria without an accompanying developmental delay
Rationale

"If FTT is acute, the weight, but not the length/height, is below accepted standards (usually the 5th percentile). If FTT is chronic, both weight and length/height are low, indicating ongoing malnutrition."

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error, and it is easy to make because chronic intuitively sounds like the more selective finding. Chronicity actually shows up as the broader deficit, because linear growth takes far longer to fall away than weight does.

failure to thrive in the infantgeneral
21. A nurse reviews the causes of failure to thrive. Which statement reflects the chapter?
  • ✓The four pathophysiologic categories are inadequate caloric intake, inadequate absorption, increased metabolism, and defective utilization, and the primary etiology is inadequate caloric intake regardless of the cause
  • BThe organic and nonorganic categories remain the preferred classification
  • CIncreased metabolism is the primary etiology in the majority of cases
  • DPoverty is a minor contributor in developed countries
Rationale

The four pathophysiologic categories replaced the older organic and nonorganic split, now considered "too simplistic." "However, the primary etiology of FTT is inadequate caloric intake regardless of the cause." The chapter also states that "poverty is believed to be the greatest risk factor for FTT in developed and developing countries."

Watch for

(D) is the reflex assumption that poverty belongs to the developing world, and the chapter names it as the greatest risk factor in both settings. (B) keeps a classification the chapter has explicitly retired.

failure to thrive in the infantgeneral
22. An 11-month-old admitted with failure to thrive is beginning nutritional rehabilitation. Which plan reflects the chapter's goals?
  • AStandard 20 kcal/oz formula at maintenance volumes, with unrestricted juice to increase total intake
  • ✓Higher-calorie formula at 24 kcal/oz, calories adequate for catch-up growth at 2 to 3 times the average rate of weight gain for corrected age, multivitamins including zinc and iron, and restricted juice until adequate weight gain is achieved
  • C30 kcal/oz formula, which is the standard infant density for catch-up growth
  • DRapid advancement without monitoring, since refeeding syndrome occurs only in adults
Rationale

Catch-up growth requires "2 to 3 times the average rate of weight gain for the child's corrected age," using "higher-calorie formulas (24 kcal/oz)" for infants, with multivitamin supplementation "including zinc and iron." "Fruit juice is restricted in children with FTT until adequate weight gain has been achieved," then limited to "no more than 4 oz/day."

Watch for

(C) applies the 30-kcal/oz density meant for children 1 to 6 years to an infant, which is the commonest swap in this pair of numbers. (A) adds unrestricted juice, which the chapter names as a cause of inadequate caloric intake in the first place. (D) dismisses refeeding syndrome, which the chapter says to watch for when malnutrition is severe.

  • ✓Suspected child abuse or neglect

infantile colicgeneral
24. A 6-week-old cries inconsolably for about four hours most evenings, five days a week, and has done so for a month. Weight gain has tracked steadily along the 50th percentile. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • ✓Colic, characterized by crying greater than 3 hours a day, more than 3 days per week, for more than 3 weeks; despite the behavioral indications of pain the infant with colic gains weight and usually thrives
  • BFailure to thrive, since prolonged crying implies inadequate intake
  • CCow's milk allergy, which the crying pattern alone confirms
  • DColic, which the normal weight gain excludes
Rationale

Colic "can be characterized by crying (1) greater than 3 hours a day, (2) for more than 3 days per week, and (3) for more than 3 weeks," and symptoms "may increase in the late afternoon or evening." "Despite the obvious behavioral indications of pain, the infant with colic gains weight and usually thrives." Colic is also "more common in younger infants under the age of 3 months."

Watch for

(D) inverts the relationship: normal weight gain is characteristic of colic rather than against it, and it is the finding that separates colic from a problem causing failure to thrive. (B) is the reflex leap from crying to not eating, which this growth curve contradicts.

infantile colicgeneral
25. Parents of a colicky 8-week-old ask how long this will last and whether it will harm their child. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓Colic is self-limiting and usually resolves as the infant matures, generally around 12 to 16 weeks of age, with no evidence of a residual effect on older children
  • BColic typically persists until the first birthday and is associated with later behavioral disorders
  • CColic resolves only with dietary treatment and continues indefinitely without it
  • DAn organic cause is identified in most infants evaluated for excessive crying
Rationale

"Colic is self-limiting and usually resolves as the infant matures, generally around 12 to 16 weeks of age," and "There is no evidence of a residual effect of colic on older children, except perhaps a strained parent-child relationship." Colic affects "15% to 20% of all infants," yet "an organic cause may be identified in less than 5% of infants seen by practitioners for excessive crying."

Watch for

(D) badly overstates how often a cause is found — under 5% — and it pulls because a symptom this dramatic feels as though it must have a findable explanation. That expectation is what drives families through repeated unproductive workups.

infantile colicgeneral
26. Parents of a colicky infant are exhausted, and the mother says she must be doing something wrong. What is the most important nursing intervention?
  • ✓Reassure the parents that they are not to blame for their infant's discomfort, and address coping with the crying
  • BBegin simethicone, which resolves symptoms in most infants
  • CAdvise an immediate switch to soy formula, which eliminates colic
  • DInstruct the parents to leave the infant to cry until the episode passes each evening
Rationale

"One important nursing intervention is to reassure parents they are not to blame for their infant's discomfort." The chapter goes further: "Helping caregivers understand the nature of the infant's crying and methods to effectively cope may be more important than finding a specific cause or treatment for colic." It also warns that "colicky infants may be at increased risk for being shaken by their caregivers and experiencing traumatic brain injury," and that excessive crying raises the risk of abusive head trauma and of maternal and paternal depression.

Watch for

(B) overstates simethicone, which "may help relieve the symptoms" while "in most controlled studies no medications completely resolved the symptoms." The Nursing Alert is blunt: "there is no evidence to support one remedy that will relieve symptoms in every infant."

infantile colicgeneral
27. Cow's milk sensitivity is suspected in a breastfed colicky infant. Which instruction is correct?
  • ✓The mother should follow a milk-free diet for a minimum of 3 to 5 days, and should know that some nondairy creamers contain calcium caseinate, a cow's milk protein
  • BThe mother should wean to a soy formula, which carries no risk of sensitivity
  • CThe mother should eliminate milk for 24 hours, which is long enough to judge the response
  • DNo dietary trial is warranted, since colic and cow's milk allergy do not overlap
Rationale

If cow's milk sensitivity is suspected, "breastfeeding mothers should follow a milk-free diet for a minimum of 3 to 5 days," and "some nondairy creamers may contain calcium caseinate, a cow's milk protein." Soy formulas are often prescribed, but "10% to 14% of those infants will develop a sensitivity to soy protein as well."

Watch for

(D) is contradicted by the overlap the chapter reports — "Infants with CMA symptoms have a high rate of colic (44%)" — which is exactly why a milk-free trial is a legitimate colic intervention. (B) treats soy as risk-free, which this chapter denies twice, here and under cow's milk allergy.

infant sleep disturbances and their managementgeneral
28. A parent describes a 4-year-old who sleepwalks and has episodes of sleep terrors, and a 9-month-old who cannot fall asleep without prolonged rocking. How does the chapter classify these two problems?
  • ABoth are dyssomnias
  • ✓The 4-year-old's episodes are parasomnias, and the infant's difficulty falling asleep is a dyssomnia
  • CThe 4-year-old's episodes are dyssomnias, and the infant's difficulty is a parasomnia
  • DBoth are parasomnias, which occur in children 3 to 8 years old
Rationale

Dyssomnias are problems in which "the child has trouble either falling or staying asleep at night or has difficulty staying awake during the day." Parasomnias are "confusional arousals, sleepwalking, sleep terrors, nightmares, and rhythmic movement disorders," which "typically occur in children 3 to 8 years old."

Watch for

(C) is the inversion, and the two words are close enough that the pair has to be held by content rather than by prefix: trouble sleeping is a dyssomnia, events during sleep are parasomnias.

infant sleep disturbances and their managementgeneral
29. A 9-month-old who has slept through the night for months now wakes abruptly and cries. A second 9-month-old falls asleep in a rocking chair each evening, is carried to bed, and cries on waking until the rocking is repeated. How should the nurse advise each family?
  • ABoth families should let the child cry until falling asleep
  • ✓The first is developmental night crying, so reassure the parents that the phase is temporary and keep checks brief; the second is trained night crying, so put the child in the bed awake and do not resume the usual routine
  • CThe first is trained night crying and the second is developmental night crying
  • DBoth children should be fed on waking to re-establish the sleep cycle
Rationale

Table 11.1 describes developmental night crying in a child "ages 6-12 months with undisturbed nighttime sleep" who "now awakens abruptly," managed by reassuring parents "that this phase is temporary," entering the room immediately but keeping reassurances brief. Trained night crying occurs when a child "typically falls asleep in place other than own bed... and is brought to own bed while asleep; on awakening, cries until usual routine is instituted," managed by putting the child "in own bed when awake" and checking at progressively longer intervals without resuming the routine.

Watch for

(C) is the inversion of the pair. The discriminator is where the child fell asleep: a child moved after falling asleep elsewhere has learned an association, while a previously good sleeper who wakes abruptly has not. (A) applies advice the chapter names as "difficult to implement and inappropriate for certain conditions."

  • ✓Place infants in their crib while still awake, and use the bed for sleeping only, not for play

  • CKeep the infant in the parents' bed for the first year to build security

Rationale

"The best way to prevent sleep problems is to encourage parents to establish bedtime routines that do not foster problematic patterns. One of the most constructive routines consists of placing infants in their crib while still awake." When infants fall asleep elsewhere and are transferred, "they awaken in unfamiliar surroundings and may be unable to fall back to sleep until the routine is repeated." The chapter adds that "the bed should be used for sleeping only, not for play."

Watch for

(B) is precisely the pattern that produces trained night crying, and it is the reflex answer because a sleeping infant transfers more quietly tonight. (D) contradicts the management of nighttime feeding — "Offer no bottles in bed. Put to bed awake" — and creates caries risk on top of it.

32. A 3-month-old is found dead in the crib and sudden infant death syndrome is suspected. What should the nurse understand about the diagnosis?
  • ✓It is the sudden death of an infant younger than 1 year that remains unexplained after a complete postmortem examination including a death-scene investigation and case-history review, and the autopsy typically cannot distinguish it from intentional suffocation
  • BThe autopsy alone establishes the diagnosis definitively
  • CIt can be diagnosed at the scene when no signs of trauma are present
  • DIt is defined only in infants younger than 6 months, which is when 95% of cases occur
Rationale

SIDS is "the sudden death of an infant younger than 1 year of age that remains unexplained after a complete postmortem examination (autopsy), including an investigation of the death scene and a review of the case history." The autopsy is essential to identify natural explanations "and to identify a death that was the result of child abuse," but "The autopsy typically cannot distinguish between SIDS and intentional suffocation."

Watch for

(B) is the assumption that makes the scene investigation look optional, and the chapter is explicit that postmortem findings in SIDS and in accidental or intentional suffocation "are practically the same." (D) confuses the statistic that 95% of cases occur by 6 months with the age boundary of the definition, which is 1 year.

34. A pregnant woman who smokes asks what she can change to reduce her baby's risk of sudden infant death syndrome. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓The incidence is approximately 3 times greater among infants whose mothers smoked during pregnancy, and the risk grows progressively as daily cigarette use increases
  • BOnly postnatal smoke exposure matters, so quitting after delivery is sufficient
  • CSmoking affects birth weight but has no established relationship to sudden infant death syndrome
  • DSleeping with the infant on a sofa offsets the smoking risk by allowing closer monitoring
Rationale

"The incidence of SIDS is approximately 3 times greater among infants whose mothers smoked during pregnancy, and risk of death is progressively greater as daily cigarette use increases." (D) inverts one of the chapter's sharpest statements: sleeping on a sofa carries "a twofold increase in accidental suffocation or strangulation" compared with other locations, and cosleeping risk is higher still when the adult smokes. Two risk factors here compound rather than offset.

35. A parent delays immunizations, believing that vaccines and sleep apnea both cause sudden infant death syndrome. Which response reflects the chapter?

(C) is the half most clinicians still carry, because home apnea monitoring was built on it. (B) is the conclusion parents reach, and the reason the chapter states the negative so plainly: the CHIME study found apnea and bradycardia "in all groups of infants studied," including healthy term infants, and "Many infants experience apnea and bradycardia in each of these groups, yet do not die."
