childhood obesity screening and classificationgeneral
1. A 9-year-old girl's body mass index falls at the 91st percentile for her age and sex. How should the nurse classify this?
  • ✓Overweight, which is a body mass index at the 85th to 94th percentile
  • BObesity, which is a body mass index at or above the 95th percentile
  • CNormal weight, since the value is below the 95th percentile
  • DSevere obesity, requiring immediate referral for bariatric evaluation
Rationale

Overweight is a body mass index at the "85th to 94th percentile," age- and gender-specific; obesity is a body mass index "at or above the 95th percentile for children of the same age and sex." Body mass index is "the most accurate method for screening children and adolescents for obesity," calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared.

Watch for

(B) names the adjacent band, and these two percentile ranges are the single most swappable pair of numbers in the chapter. Note the wording that decides borderline cases: obesity is "at or above" the 95th percentile, so a child exactly at the 95th percentile is obese rather than overweight.

2. A 12-year-old competitive swimmer has a body mass index at the 96th percentile but very low measured body fat. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • ✓A subset of adolescents such as athletes may have a high body mass index because of increased muscle mass rather than fat mass, and clinical judgment is needed
  • BThe body mass index is diagnostic and the child should be treated for obesity
  • CBody mass index in children uses fixed cut-points, so athletic status is irrelevant
  • DBody mass index is not a useful screening tool in any child
Rationale

"A subset of adolescents (e.g., athletes) may have a high BMI because of increased muscle mass rather than fat mass. Clinical judgment is needed." Body mass index remains "currently considered the best method to assess weight in children and adolescents," but in children it "varies to accommodate age- and gender-specific changes in growth," unlike the fixed adult definitions.

Watch for

(C) imports the adult framework, where "BMI definitions are fixed measures without regard for sex and age." Pediatric body mass index is interpreted by percentile precisely because the same raw number means different things at different ages.

3. A parent of an obese 10-year-old asks whether a thyroid problem or a slow metabolism explains the weight. Which response reflects the chapter?
  • ✓Fewer than 5% of childhood obesity is attributable to an underlying disease, and there is little evidence to support a relationship between obesity and low metabolism; energy imbalance is the usual explanation
  • BEndocrine disease accounts for the majority of childhood obesity
  • CA low basal metabolic rate is the most common identified cause
  • DNo endocrine condition ever causes childhood obesity
Rationale

Fewer than 5% of childhood obesity has an underlying disease, and there is "little evidence to support a relationship between obesity and low metabolism." Hypothyroidism, Cushing-type states, and hypothalamic damage do occur but are the minority.

Watch for

(C) is the explanation families most often arrive with, and the chapter addresses it directly. (D) overcorrects — organic causes exist and are worth evaluating when the history suggests them; they are simply not where the answer usually lies.

4. A child with hypotonia, short stature, hypogonadism, almond-shaped eyes, and relentless food seeking is being evaluated for obesity. Which syndrome does this suggest, and what does it imply for management?
  • ✓Prader-Willi syndrome; these children lack the internal mechanism that regulates satiety and go to great lengths to obtain food, so environmental control of food access is required
  • BPrader-Willi syndrome; appetite counseling and portion education are sufficient
  • CCushing syndrome; corticosteroid excess explains the food seeking
  • DHypothyroidism; the food seeking resolves with hormone replacement
Rationale

Prader-Willi syndrome presents with hypotonia, hyperphagia, short stature, hypogonadism, and almond-shaped eyes. The functional key is that these children "lack the internal mechanism that regulates satiety and as a result go to great lengths to obtain food."

Watch for

(B) names the right syndrome and the wrong intervention, which is the more consequential half of the item. Counseling a child to recognize fullness cannot work when the satiety mechanism itself is absent; the environment has to supply the limit the physiology does not.

5. An asymptomatic obese 11-year-old is being evaluated. Which findings are recognized complications of childhood obesity? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Insulin resistance syndrome, which develops before hyperinsulinemia, obesity, hypertension, and dyslipidemia individually appear
  • ✓Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, the most common liver disease in children
  • ✓Obstructive sleep apnea, reported in 20% to 30% of obese children and adolescents
  • ✓Slipped capital femoral epiphysis and Blount disease
  • EA low incidence of bullying, since peer groups accommodate weight differences at this age
Rationale

"The incidence of metabolic syndrome was 30% in obese children," and insulin resistance syndrome — hyperinsulinemia, obesity, hypertension, and dyslipidemia — "develops before any of these conditions develops." Between 10% and 25% of obese youth are diagnosed with nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, "the most common liver disease in children." Obstructive sleep apnea "is reported in 20% to 30%." Musculoskeletal complications include slipped capital femoral epiphysis and Blount disease.

Watch for

(E) is the one option from the wrong side of the sort and inverts the chapter: "overweight children are more frequently the victims of bullying compared with children of normal weight," including "name calling, teasing, threats, physical harm, rejection, rumors, and sexual harassment."

6. An obese 13-year-old has a normal alanine aminotransferase and feels well. The parent concludes the liver is unaffected. Which response is correct?
  • ✓Liver enzymes in nonalcoholic fatty liver disease are either normal or slightly elevated, and people with nonalcoholic steatohepatitis may feel healthy and show no outward signs of liver disease, so a normal enzyme does not exclude it
  • BA normal alanine aminotransferase reliably excludes fatty liver disease
  • CFatty liver disease in children always produces jaundice
  • DNonalcoholic fatty liver disease does not progress in children
Rationale

In nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, alanine and aspartate aminotransferase are "either normal or slightly elevated," and "People with NASH may feel healthy and show no outward signs of liver disease." Approximately 25% of pediatric cases progress to nonalcoholic steatohepatitis, in which fatty infiltration "causes liver inflammation… and may cause scarring," and may progress to "cirrhosis and end-stage liver disease" requiring transplantation. Diagnosis is by ultrasound, computed tomography, or liver biopsy; treatment is weight loss.

Watch for

(B) is the inference the laboratory value invites, and it is why the chapter states the negative. A normal enzyme in a child with the risk factor is not reassurance; it is a reason to image rather than to stop looking.

7. An obese 13-year-old reports hip pain and has begun walking with a limp. Which complication should the nurse suspect?
  • ✓Slipped capital femoral epiphysis, in which the cartilage plate at the top of the femur slips out of place; it is the most common hip disorder among young teenagers
  • BBlount disease, an overgrowth of the medial proximal tibial metaphysis
  • CObstructive sleep apnea referred pain
  • DNonalcoholic steatohepatitis with referred pain to the hip
Rationale

Slipped capital femoral epiphysis is "the most common hip disorder among young teenagers," occurring when "the cartilage plate (epiphysis) at the top of the femur slips out of place." Blount disease is a different entity: "overgrowth of the medial aspect of the proximal tibial metaphysis that causes the lower leg to angle inward (tibia vara)," with "a dose-response relationship between BMI and Blount disease."

Watch for

(B) is the paired musculoskeletal complication and is distinguished by site: the hip points to slipped capital femoral epiphysis, and inward bowing of the lower leg points to Blount disease. Both are obesity complications, so the presenting location is the whole discriminator.

8. A 10-year-old with obesity bows inward at the lower legs below the knees. Which complication does this suggest?
  • ASlipped capital femoral epiphysis
  • ✓Blount disease, an overgrowth of the medial aspect of the proximal tibial metaphysis causing the lower leg to angle inward, known as tibia vara
  • CA normal variant requiring no referral
  • DRickets from vitamin D deficiency, which obesity does not affect

Blount disease is "overgrowth of the medial aspect of the proximal tibial metaphysis that causes the lower leg to angle inward (tibia vara)," and the chapter notes "a dose-response relationship between BMI and Blount disease." Orthopedic referral is the named pathway for it.

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error of the previous item. Both conditions share obesity as the driver and differ only in which joint is affected, so an item on this pair always turns on the anatomic site named in the stem.

9. A nurse is counseling the family of an obese 10-year-old about diet. Which approach reflects the chapter?
  • ✓Focus dietary counseling on improving the nutritional quality of the diet rather than on dietary restriction, and advise the child to avoid fad diets
  • BPrescribe a specific calorie restriction and weigh the child weekly
  • CRecommend a commercially available fad diet, since adherence is higher
  • DRestrict the child's food at home while allowing free choice elsewhere
Rationale

"Dietary counseling focuses on improving the nutritional quality of the diet rather than on dietary restriction. Children should avoid fad diets." The recommended profile is "no trans fats, low saturated fat, moderate total fat (≤30%), low sodium, and at least nine servings of fruits and vegetables," with high-fiber foods promoted and refined starches and sugars avoided.

Watch for

(D) splits the environment in a way the chapter argues against; the emphasis is on parents taking "charge of family meals and the home food environment" so that "healthy choices are readily available," with meals eaten together "away from distractions such as television."

10. A nurse wants to increase physical activity in an obese 11-year-old. Which intervention does the chapter identify as most effective?
  • ✓Limiting sedentary screen time behaviors, which is the most effective way to encourage physical activity, targeting 60 minutes or more of activity per day and less than 2 hours of screen time
  • BEnrolling the child in a competitive sports league regardless of interest
  • CBeginning a supervised weight-training program
  • DSetting a daily pedometer target and no other change
Rationale

"Limiting sedentary screen time behaviors… is the most effective way to encourage physical activity." The paired figures are 60 minutes or more of physical activity per day and less than 2 hours of screen time, with a daily target of 10,000 to 15,000 steps.

Watch for

(C) is addressed separately and with an age restriction, as the next item shows. (D) names a real target but adopts it in isolation; the pedometer measures the activity that removing screen time makes room for.

11. Parents of a prepubertal 9-year-old ask about starting weight training to reduce body fat. Which response is correct?
  • ✓Weight training increases basal metabolic rate and replaces fat mass with muscle mass, but it is not generally recommended for prepubertal children until they have reached physical and skeletal maturity; increased outdoor playtime is the appropriate alternative
  • BWeight training is the first-line activity intervention at any age
  • CWeight training is contraindicated at every age in childhood
  • DPrepubertal children gain muscle mass more efficiently than adolescents
Rationale

Weight training "increases basal metabolic rate and replaces fat mass with muscle mass" but is "not generally recommended for prepubertal children until they have reached physical and skeletal maturity." For this age group, increased outdoor playtime is the recommended approach. Chapter 15 gives the underlying reason: school-age muscles are "still functionally immature… and they are more readily injured by overuse."

Watch for

(C) overcorrects into a blanket prohibition; the restriction is developmental rather than permanent, and it lifts at physical and skeletal maturity.

12. A 14-year-old is brought in by a parent who insists the adolescent begin a weight-loss program. The adolescent is uninterested and says the visit was not their idea. What should the nurse do first?
  • ✓Explore the reasons behind the desire to lose weight, recognizing that it may be wiser to defer treatment until the family is ready
  • BBegin a structured diet and exercise plan at this visit, since delay worsens outcomes
  • CRefer immediately for bariatric surgical evaluation
  • DCounsel the adolescent privately that the parent's concern should be disregarded
Rationale

The Nursing Alert makes motivation the pivot: explore "the reasons behind the desire to lose weight." And on timing, "It may be wiser to defer treatment until the family is ready." Treatment of obesity "is difficult," and "the average individual only loses about 5% to 10% of his or her weight with available therapies," with lost weight "frequently regained in a year or two."

Watch for

(B) starts a plan with no one committed to carrying it out, which is what the deferral guidance exists to prevent. Beginning and failing is not neutral — it adds a documented failure to a child already at risk for "a negative self-image and low body satisfaction that persists into adulthood."

13. A severely obese 16-year-old is being considered for bariatric surgery. Which criteria apply? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Attainment of Tanner stage 4 or 95% of adult linear growth
  • ✓A body mass index of 35 or above with severe comorbidity
  • ✓A body mass index of 40 or above with less severe comorbidity
  • ✓Avoidance of pregnancy for at least 18 months postoperatively
  • ECompletion of at least 5 years of documented dietary failure before consideration
Rationale

The bariatric surgery criteria are attainment of Tanner stage 4 or 95% of adult linear growth; a body mass index of 35 or above with severe comorbidity, or 40 or above with less severe comorbidity; and avoidance of pregnancy for at least 18 months postoperatively.

Watch for

(E) is the one option from the wrong side of the sort — no such duration requirement appears in the criteria. Note the relationship among the real ones, which is counterintuitive and therefore testable: the *higher* body mass index threshold pairs with the *milder* comorbidity, because either severe disease or extreme weight alone can justify the procedure.

dental disorders in the school-age childgeneral
14. A parent asks which is worse for a child's teeth — a large soda with dinner or a lollipop eaten between meals. Which response reflects the chapter?
  • ✓Sucrose has been consistently implicated as the most cariogenic, and the damage depends on form and timing, so sticky or chewy candy and lollipops ingested between meals are worse than a larger quantity of sugar taken with a meal
  • BThe total quantity of sugar is the only factor that matters
  • CLiquid sugars are more cariogenic than sticky candies in all circumstances
  • DTiming of sugar intake has no effect on caries risk
Rationale

"Sucrose has been consistently implicated as the most cariogenic," and the damage depends on form and timing — sticky or sour chewy candy and lollipops, "ingested between meals." Conversely, "saliva, some foods, and chewing gum after a meal" help neutralize acid.

Watch for

(B) is the intuitive metric and it is the weaker answer here. Quantity matters less than how long the sugar stays in contact with the tooth and whether the meal's own saliva response is available to neutralize the acid.

15. A nurse is teaching about caries risk in a 7-year-old whose permanent incisors have just erupted. Which statement is accurate?
  • ✓Newly erupted teeth are more susceptible to decay than those that have been erupted for 2 years or longer, because they have not yet acquired sufficient surface minerals
  • BNewly erupted permanent teeth are highly resistant to decay
  • CSusceptibility to caries is unrelated to how recently a tooth erupted
  • DPermanent teeth are not at risk for caries until adolescence
Rationale

Newly erupted teeth are "more susceptible to decay than those that have been erupted for 2 years or longer" because they have not "acquired sufficient surface minerals." This is why middle childhood, when the permanent teeth erupt, is the peak susceptibility window, and more than half of US children have dental caries.

Watch for

(B) assumes the permanent dentition arrives fully protected, which is the opposite of the case and is why this period carries the risk it does.

16. A child has inflamed, bleeding gums. Radiographs show loss of connective tissue and bone in the supporting structures. How should the nurse describe this?
  • AGingivitis, which is simple inflammation of the gums
  • ✓Periodontitis, which is inflammation plus loss of connective tissue and bone in the supporting structures
  • CAcute necrotizing ulcerative gingivitis, also called trench mouth, which is common in this age group
  • DNormal eruption-related inflammation requiring no intervention
Rationale

Gingivitis is "simple inflammation of the gums." Periodontitis is inflammation plus "loss of connective tissue and bone in the supporting structures." Acute necrotizing ulcerative gingivitis, or trench mouth, is a distinct entity and is uncommon.

Watch for

(A) names the milder condition on the same spectrum, and bone loss is the single finding that separates them. (C) attaches a memorable name to an uncommon condition, which is the reason it stays in memory out of proportion to how often it is the answer.

17. A 9-year-old has a permanent central incisor knocked out during a soccer game. Which actions should the nurse take? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Hold the tooth by the crown rather than the root
  • ✓Rinse the tooth gently under running water or saline, with the sink stoppered
  • CScrub the root surface thoroughly to remove debris before reinsertion
  • ✓Reinsert the tooth into the socket and transport the child immediately
  • ✓Understand that reimplantation within 1 hour gives the best chance of success
Rationale

Hold an avulsed permanent tooth "by the crown," rinse it gently under running water or saline with the sink stoppered, do not scrub, reinsert it into the socket, and transport immediately. Reimplantation "within 1 hour" gives the best chance of success. An unsuccessfully reimplanted tooth is retained 6 months to 12 years.

Watch for

(C) is the one option from the wrong side of the sort, and it is the step that destroys the tooth — scrubbing removes the periodontal ligament cells that reattachment depends on. Note also the exception that a distractor may exploit: avulsed *primary* teeth are usually not reimplanted.

enuresisgeneral
18. A 6-year-old wets the bed about three nights a week and has done so continuously for the past 5 months. Which statement is correct?
  • ✓This meets the diagnostic criteria for enuresis, which require occurrence at least twice a week for at least 3 months in a child at least 5 years of age
  • BThis does not meet criteria, because the child must be at least 7 years old
  • CThis does not meet criteria, because the events must occur nightly
  • DEnuresis cannot be diagnosed before puberty
Rationale

Enuresis requires occurrence at least twice a week for at least 3 months, in a child at least 5 years old. Prevalence falls with age: 20% at age 5, 10% at age 8, and 3% at age 12. It is more common in boys, and the risk reaches up to 77% when both parents were enuretic.

Watch for

(B) substitutes the wrong minimum age. These three parameters — twice a week, 3 months, age 5 — pair with encopresis's three, which are once a month, 3 months, and age 4, and the six numbers get cross-swapped more than any other set in the chapter.

enuresisgeneral
19. A nurse is calculating the expected functional bladder capacity of a 6-year-old. Which figure is correct?
  • ✓8 ounces, using the formula of age in years plus 2 ounces
  • B10 ounces, using age plus 4 ounces
  • C6 ounces, equal to the child's age in ounces
  • D12 ounces, which is the adult capacity
Rationale

Normal functional bladder capacity is age plus 2 ounces, so a 6-year-old's capacity is 8 ounces, or 237 ml. Separately, 10 to 12 ounces — 300 to 350 ml — is the volume sufficient to hold a night's urine.

Watch for

(B) uses the common distractor formula of age plus 4. (D) borrows the volume needed to hold a night's urine and presents it as the child's current capacity, which is in fact the gap the treatment aims to close.

20. Parents of an 8-year-old with enuresis ask about the urine alarm. Which teaching is accurate?
  • ✓The alarm requires at least 6 to 8 weeks to be effective, children may relapse once they stop using it, and relapse is treated by reinstituting the alarm
  • BThe alarm works within a few nights or not at all
  • CThe alarm is a second-line option after medication has failed
  • DRelapse after alarm use means the treatment should not be repeated
Rationale

The urine alarm needs "at least 6 to 8 weeks," children "may relapse once they stop using it," and relapse is treated by reinstituting the alarm. It is inexpensive and free of side effects, and drug therapy is explicitly second-line, with imipramine third-line and cardiotoxic at high doses. Successful treatment is defined as 7 to 28 consecutive dry nights.

Watch for

(B) sets an expectation that guarantees the family abandons an effective treatment before it has had time to work, which is the practical reason the 6-to-8-week figure matters. (C) inverts the treatment order.

21. A provider is considering desmopressin for a child with enuresis. Which statement reflects current recommendations?
  • ✓The tablet form is used and can produce a response in up to 70% of children, but the nasal spray caused hyponatremia and seizures and is no longer recommended; the drug must be tapered off rather than stopped abruptly
  • BThe nasal spray is preferred because of more reliable absorption
  • CDesmopressin may be discontinued abruptly once dryness is achieved
  • DDesmopressin is first-line therapy ahead of the urine alarm
Rationale

Desmopressin response can reach 70%, but the nasal spray "caused hyponatremia and seizures and is no longer recommended," and the drug "must [be] tapered off," since abrupt discontinuation carries a high relapse rate.

(B) names the route that was withdrawn for causing seizures, and it pulls because a nasal spray sounds gentler than a tablet in a child. (D) promotes a second-line drug over the first-line behavioral treatment.

22. A frustrated parent says they have begun making their enuretic 7-year-old wash his own sheets every morning as a consequence. What should the nurse advise?
  • ✓Punishment must not be used to correct enuresis, and parents need reassurance that bed-wetting does not represent willful misbehavior; the child may participate in changing linens as part of self-care, but not as a penalty
  • BRequiring the child to launder the sheets is an appropriate natural consequence

"It is imperative that punishment not be used to correct enuresis," and parents "need reassurance that the bed-wetting does not represent willful misbehavior."

23. A 6-year-old has been soiling his underwear several times a week for 4 months. Examination reveals loose stool leakage and small amounts of hard stool in the rectum. What does this pattern suggest, and what is the first treatment?
  • ✓Leakage around an impaction; the first treatment is disimpaction, and customary dosages are usually insufficient
  • BInfectious diarrhea; the first treatment is an antidiarrheal agent
  • CIrritable bowel syndrome; the first treatment is dietary fiber alone
  • DNormal variation requiring no treatment
Rationale

Loose leakage together with "small amounts of hard stool… suggests leakage around an impaction." The first treatment is disimpaction, and "customary dosages are usually insufficient."

Watch for

(B) treats the most visible finding — loose stool — as the problem, and an antidiarrheal would worsen the impaction that is producing it. The stem's combination of loose leakage *and* palpable hard stool is the signature that separates overflow from true diarrhea.

24. A parent reports that their 5-year-old stiffens, stands in a corner with straight legs and a red face, and "does a little dance" before soiling. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • ✓Retentive posturing, which looks like effort to defecate but is in fact withholding
  • BEffective straining that indicates constipation is resolving
  • CA seizure equivalent requiring neurologic evaluation
  • DNormal toileting behavior for the age
Rationale

Retentive posturing presents as "Stiffening, standing in a corner with straight legs and a bright red face, 'doing a little dance,' crawling, or hiding." A parent who reports the child "straining" may in fact be describing withholding.

Watch for

(B) is the parent's usual reading of the behavior, and it points the assessment in the opposite direction from the truth. The child is working to keep stool in, not to pass it, which is why the history taken at face value can be misleading.

25. A nurse suspects encopresis in a 7-year-old but the family has not raised it. What is the best approach, and what are the diagnostic criteria?
  • ✓Ask directly about soiling, since parents and children are reluctant to volunteer the information; encopresis requires occurrence at least once a month for at least 3 months in a child at least 4 years of age
  • BWait for the family to raise the topic, to avoid embarrassing the child
  • CAsk directly; the criteria are twice a week for 3 months in a child at least 5 years old
  • DAsk the child alone, without informing the parents
Rationale

"Because the parents and child are reluctant to volunteer information, direct questioning about the soiling is more successful." Encopresis requires occurrence at least once a month for at least 3 months in a child at least 4 years of age. The toileting routine is 10 to 15 minutes after meals, for 10 minutes.

Watch for

(C) has the right approach and imports enuresis's criteria — twice a week, age 5 — onto encopresis. Those two criteria sets are adjacent in the chapter and differ in both frequency and minimum age, which is exactly what makes them swappable.

26. A 9-year-old is being evaluated for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder. Which elements are required by the DSM-5 criteria? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓At least six symptoms of inattention or hyperactivity-impulsivity
  • ✓Symptoms present for at least 6 months
  • ✓Onset of symptoms before age 12 years
  • ✓Symptoms present in two or more settings
  • EOnset of symptoms before age 7 years
Rationale

The criteria require six symptoms — five suffice at age 17 years and older — present for at least 6 months, with onset before age 12 years, in two or more settings. Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder affects about 11% of school-age children, and a parent with the condition has a more than 50% chance of having a child with it.

Watch for

(E) is the one option from the wrong side of the sort, and it is the superseded DSM-IV threshold. It remains the classic distractor precisely because it was correct under the previous edition, so a learner who studied from older material will select it confidently.

attention-deficit/hyperactivity disordergeneral
27. A 5-year-old has been diagnosed with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder. Which first-line treatment does the chapter specify for this age?
  • ✓Behavior therapy first, with methylphenidate only if behavior interventions fail and the disturbance is moderate to severe
  • BStimulant medication first, with behavior therapy added if medication is insufficient
  • CMedication and behavior therapy started simultaneously in all cases
  • DNo treatment until the child reaches school age
Rationale

The treatment algorithm is age-banded. At ages 4 to 5, behavior therapy comes first, with methylphenidate only if behavior interventions fail and the disturbance is moderate to severe. At ages 6 to 11, medication and/or behavior therapy, "preferably both." At ages 12 to 18, medication with the adolescent's assent and/or behavior therapy.

Watch for

(B) applies the school-age approach to a preschooler, which is the distinction this algorithm exists to draw. Note the assent requirement that appears only in the oldest band — the adolescent's agreement is part of the criterion, not a courtesy.

28. A child with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder also has a motor tic disorder, and a stimulant is being considered. Which concern applies?
  • ✓Stimulants may exaggerate tics, so they are avoided when tics or a family history of Tourette syndrome are present
  • BStimulants reliably suppress tics and are therefore preferred
  • CTics are a contraindication to all attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder treatment
  • DTics and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder never coexist
Rationale

Stimulants "may exaggerate tics," so they are avoided when tics or a family history of Tourette syndrome are present. This is the one hard contraindication in the attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder section, and it links directly to the tic-disorder content later in the chapter.

Watch for

(C) converts a medication-specific caution into a blanket bar on treatment. Behavior therapy remains available and is first-line at several ages regardless, so the presence of tics changes the agent rather than removing the option of treatment.

29. A child taking a stimulant for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder has lost appetite and is having difficulty falling asleep. Which management strategies apply? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Give the medication with or after meals to manage appetite loss
  • ✓Offer evening snacks and small frequent meals
  • ✓Give the medication early in the day and use the minimal effective dose for sleeplessness
  • DAdd caffeinated beverages to enhance the medication's effect
  • ✓Monitor growth, since long-term dextroamphetamine use is associated with growth suppression
Rationale

For appetite loss, give the medication "with or after meals," with evening snacks and small frequent meals. For sleeplessness, give it "early in the day" at the minimal effective dose. Growth suppression is associated with long-term dextroamphetamine use, so growth is monitored. Insulin requirements may change.

(D) is the one option from the wrong side of the sort and reverses the interaction: "Caffeine decreases efficacy." It pulls because caffeine is itself a stimulant, so combining the two sounds additive.

37. A 10-year-old has had motor tics but no vocal tics for the past 14 months, with no secondary cause identified. Which classification applies?
  • ATourette syndrome, since the duration exceeds 12 months
  • ✓A chronic, or persistent, tic disorder, since Tourette syndrome requires both motor and vocal tics

  • DNo tic disorder, since vocal tics are absent
Rationale

A chronic or persistent tic disorder is defined by duration of more than 1 year. Tourette syndrome requires both motor and vocal tics, onset before 18 years, duration of more than 12 months, and no secondary cause. Tic disorders affect about 6 per 1000 children, with a 3:1 male predominance.

(A) applies the duration criterion alone and ignores the requirement that both tic types be present, which is the single feature separating Tourette syndrome from a chronic tic disorder. Duration is satisfied here; the symptom profile is not.

41. A child who witnessed a traumatic event appeared numb and detached about 2 weeks afterward, and the parents say he seemed unaffected. Two months later he is tearful, irritable, and clearly struggling. How should the nurse interpret these two phases?
  • ✓The second phase, at about 2 weeks, is numbness and denial, during which severe depression, aggression, or psychosis can develop without support; the third phase, at 2 to 3 months, is when the child appears to be getting worse when actually he or she is getting better
  • BThe child deteriorated because the trauma was inadequately treated in the first weeks
  • CThe apparent recovery at 2 weeks indicates the trauma had no lasting effect
  • DDistress emerging at 2 months indicates a new, unrelated stressor
Rationale

Posttraumatic stress in children runs in phases of 1 to 2 hours, about 2 weeks, and 2 to 3 months. The second phase is numbness and denial, in which the child "appears to have produced no impression," and it is when "severe depression, aggression, or psychosis" can develop without support. In the third phase the child "appears to be getting worse, when actually he or she is getting better."

Watch for

(C) reads the numb phase as resilience, which is the more dangerous of the two misreadings because it withdraws support precisely when it is most needed. Reading the third phase as deterioration is the mirror error, and it can prompt intervention changes at the point where recovery has begun.
