  • ✓Persistent, localized pain or limping
  • ✓Prolonged, unexplained fever
  • ✓Frequent headaches, often with vomiting

Rationale

Box 29.1 lists an unusual mass or swelling, unexplained paleness and loss of energy, easy bruising, persistent localized pain or limping, prolonged unexplained fever or illness, frequent headaches often with vomiting, sudden eye or vision changes, and unexplained rapid weight loss (p. 1078).

2. A parent reports that her 5-year-old has a swollen lymph node. What additional findings would make this concerning for malignancy?
  • ✓Fever for more than 1 week, recent weight loss, or an abnormal chest radiograph
  • BAny palpable node larger than 1 cm
  • CTenderness of the node on palpation
  • DA node that has been present for 3 days
Rationale

The chapter states that "enlarged, firm lymph nodes in a child with fever for more than 1 week, a recent history of weight loss, or an abnormal chest x-ray film may indicate a serious disease and should be evaluated further" (p. 1079).

Watch for

(B) and (C) each treat a single node characteristic as sufficient, and a swollen node alone is not the cancer cue in this chapter — all three of the named qualifiers do the work, which is why they are stated together.

3. A parent brings in a photograph showing a white glow in her toddler's pupil instead of the normal red reflex. What should the nurse recognize?
  • ✓Leukocoria, which can be a sign of retinoblastoma
  • BA normal photographic artifact requiring no follow-up
  • CCataract, which is unrelated to malignancy
  • DStrabismus, which is the most common presenting sign of eye tumors
Rationale

The chapter describes leukocoria or white eye reflex as a yellow or whitish glow in the pupil, as opposed to the normal red pupillary reflex, noted in photographs, and states it can be a sign of retinoblastoma (pp. 1079, 1102).

Watch for

(D) names the second most common sign rather than the first — strabismus results from poor fixation of the visually impaired eye — and (B) dismisses a finding whose "fleeting nature... often results in a delayed diagnosis," which is precisely why a photograph matters.

4. A nurse is explaining childhood cancer to a parent who asks what she could have done to prevent it. What is accurate?
  • ✓There are no generally recognized preventive measures for childhood cancer, because lifestyle risk factors drive adult rather than childhood cancer
  • BDietary changes in early childhood substantially reduce risk
  • CAvoiding all environmental exposures prevents most childhood cancers
  • DChildhood cancer is the leading cause of death in children ages 1 to 15
Rationale

The chapter states that lifestyle risk factors drive adult cancer rather than childhood cancer, and that there are no generally recognized preventive measures for childhood cancer (pp. 1072–1073).

Watch for

(D) overstates a real statistic, since cancer is the leading cause of death *from disease* in children ages 1 to 15 rather than the leading cause of death overall; the distinction matters when counseling families, and so does the fact that nothing they did or failed to do caused this.

6. A nurse notes swelling and a lack of blood return at a peripheral intravenous site during a chemotherapy infusion. What should the nurse do first?
  • ✓Stop the infusion immediately
  • BSlow the infusion and apply a warm compress
  • CFlush the line with saline to confirm patency
  • DNotify the practitioner before changing anything
Rationale

The chapter directs stopping the chemotherapy infusion immediately at the first sign of infiltration (p. 1076).

Watch for

(C) pushes more vesicant into the tissue while testing a line that has already failed; note that the action differs for a suspected hypersensitivity reaction, where the nurse discontinues the drug, flushes and maintains the line with saline, and monitors vital signs — infiltration means stop, hypersensitivity means stop and keep the line open.

7. A child is scheduled for chemotherapy known to cause nausea. When should antiemetics be given?
  • ✓On a schedule, before chemotherapy and around the clock for at least 24 hours after
  • BAs needed, once nausea develops
  • COnly if vomiting occurs more than three times
  • DAfter the infusion is complete, to avoid drug interactions
Rationale

The chapter directs giving antiemetics on a schedule, before chemotherapy and around the clock for at least 24 hours after, rather than as needed, with the rationale of preventing anticipatory nausea (p. 1081).

Watch for

(B) is standard practice for most symptoms and is specifically wrong here, because once anticipatory nausea is established it becomes conditioned and far harder to treat; note also that nausea and vomiting are usually not indications for stopping the chemotherapy itself.

8. A child receiving vincristine has not had a bowel movement in 4 days. What should the nurse recommend?
  • ✓A stool softener, increased activity, and a laxative such as polyethylene glycol
  • BIncreased dietary fiber as the primary intervention
  • CA rectal suppository for prompt relief
  • DFluid restriction to firm the stool
Rationale

The chapter states that "Increased fiber may not be effective because the increased bulk tends to increase fecal distention and discomfort without producing the necessary mechanical stimulation," directing stool softeners, activity, and laxatives such as polyethylene glycol instead (p. 1082).

Watch for

(C) is contraindicated, since rectal temperatures and suppositories are avoided in the child with cancer — an instruction that appears three separate times in this chapter; (B) is the intuitive constipation answer and the one the chapter specifically rules out for this cause.

9. A child beginning induction for acute lymphoblastic leukemia develops flank pain, muscle cramps, and tetany. Which laboratory pattern should the nurse expect? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Hyperuricemia
  • ✓Hyperphosphatemia
  • ✓Hyperkalemia
  • ✓Hypocalcemia
  • EHypercalcemia
Rationale

The chapter defines tumor lysis syndrome by "hyperuricemia, hypocalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, and hyperkalemia," resulting from rapid release of intracellular contents during lysis of malignant cells, typically during the initial treatment period (p. 1078).

Watch for

(E) uses the pattern against the reader — three of the four values go up and calcium is the outlier that goes down, so an option offering the calcium in the same direction as the rest is the designed error.

pediatric oncologic emergenciesgeneral
10. A child with tumor lysis syndrome is at risk for acute renal failure. Which intervention addresses the mechanism?
  • ✓Aggressive intravenous fluids with allopurinol or rasburicase to reduce uric acid
  • BFluid restriction to reduce renal workload
  • CPotassium supplementation to correct the electrolyte shift
  • DCalcium restriction to prevent stone formation
Rationale

The chapter states that "The crystallization of uric acid that can occur with hyperuricemia can lead to acute renal failure," and directs frequent serum chemistries and urine pH, strict intake and output, aggressive intravenous fluids, and allopurinol, noting that hyperuricemia "is now effectively treated with recombinant urate oxidase, or rasburicase," which converts uric acid to the more soluble allantoin (p. 1078).

Watch for

(C) would worsen an already elevated potassium, and (B) removes the very intervention that clears the uric acid load; risk factors for this syndrome are a high white cell count at diagnosis, large tumor burden, chemosensitivity, and a high proliferative rate.

11. A child newly diagnosed with leukemia has a white blood cell count of 130,000/mm3 with respiratory distress and confusion. What is this, and what is the mechanism?
  • ✓Hyperleukocytosis, in which capillary obstruction and microinfarction produce organ dysfunction
  • BTumor lysis syndrome, in which electrolyte shifts produce neurologic change
  • CSuperior vena cava syndrome, in which a mediastinal mass obstructs venous return
  • DDisseminated intravascular coagulation, in which microthrombi consume clotting factors
Rationale

The chapter defines hyperleukocytosis as "a peripheral white blood cell count greater than 100,000/mm3, [which] can lead to capillary obstruction, microinfarction, and organ dysfunction," with respiratory distress, cyanosis, and neurologic changes (p. 1078).

Watch for

(B) is the other early-treatment emergency in the same patient and is a metabolic rather than a rheologic problem; the distinguishing feature here is the cell count itself, and management is rapid cytoreduction with hydration, urinary alkalinization, and allopurinol.

12. A child with Hodgkin lymphoma develops cyanosis of the face and neck, facial and upper extremity edema, and distended neck and chest veins. What is the priority?
  • ✓Airway protection and alleviation of respiratory distress
  • BImmediate lumbar puncture to assess for central nervous system involvement
  • CElevation of the lower extremities to improve venous return
  • DFluid bolus to improve perfusion
Rationale

The chapter describes superior vena cava syndrome from space-occupying chest lesions, "especially from Hodgkin disease and NHL," presenting with cyanosis of the face, neck, and upper chest, facial and upper extremity edema, and distended neck and chest veins, managed by "Airway protection and alleviation of respiratory distress" (p. 1078).

Watch for

(D) treats the distention as hypovolemia when the problem is obstructed return; note that the second leading cause of this syndrome is thrombotic complications of implantable intravenous devices, so a child with a port belongs on this differential too.

13. A child receiving chemotherapy has a white blood cell count of 1000/mm3, with 7% neutrophils and 7% bands. What is the absolute neutrophil count, and what does it mean?
  • ✓140/mm3, which places the child at high risk for overwhelming infection
  • B1400/mm3, which is mildly reduced but not high risk
  • C70/mm3, since only segmented neutrophils are counted
  • D140/mm3, which is within normal limits during chemotherapy
Rationale

The chapter's guideline directs determining the total percentage of neutrophils including polys or segs and bands, then multiplying the white cell count by that percentage — so 7% plus 7% is 14%, and 0.14 times 1000 is 140/mm3; the at-risk child is the one with fever and a count below 500/mm3 (p. 1079).

Watch for

(C) drops the bands, which the calculation explicitly includes; (D) normalizes a value more than threefold below the risk threshold. Note the clinical trap in the same paragraph — the usual signs of inflammation may be absent, but "fever will occur," so fever is the reliable sign precisely when redness and pus are not.

14. A child with an absolute neutrophil count of 300/mm3 is being cared for at home. Which instructions are correct? Select all that apply.

  • ✓Report fever promptly, since it may be the only sign of infection

The chapter advises cautious isolation from crowded areas when the count falls below 500/mm3, notes that fever occurs even when other signs do not, states that siblings and family members "can receive live MMR and varicella vaccine without risk to the child who is immunosuppressed," and substitutes acetaminophen for aspirin (pp. 1079–1080, 1084).

(E) over-applies the live-vaccine rule — the restriction is on the patient receiving them, not on the household, and isolating the child from a recently vaccinated sibling imposes a separation the chapter does not require.

15. A child receiving chemotherapy has a platelet count of 18,000/mm3. Which finding should the nurse expect, and which action is appropriate?
  • ✓Spontaneous gum or nose bleeding may occur, and pressure is applied at the site without disturbing clot formation
  • BSpontaneous bleeding is not expected until the count falls below 5000/mm3
  • CProphylactic platelet transfusion should be given regardless of bleeding
  • DRectal temperatures should be taken to avoid oral mucosal trauma
Rationale

The chapter states that severe spontaneous internal hemorrhage "usually does not occur until the platelet count is 20,000/mm3 or less," that spontaneous gum or nose bleeding appears below that level, and that for the most common bleeds — epistaxis and gingival bleeding — "applying pressure at the site without disturbing clot formation is the general rule" (pp. 1079–1080).

Watch for

(D) reverses a rule stated three times in this chapter, since the rectal area is prone to ulceration and rectal temperatures and suppositories are avoided; (C) over-treats, since platelet transfusions "are generally reserved for active bleeding episodes that do not respond to local treatment."

16. A child with cancer has a platelet count of 85,000/mm3 and wants to ride his bicycle. What should the nurse advise?
  • ✓Avoid activities that might cause injury or bleeding while the count is below 100,000/mm3
  • BBicycle riding is safe at any platelet count above 20,000/mm3
  • CAll activity should be restricted until the count exceeds 150,000/mm3
  • DActivity restriction applies only to contact sports at any count
Rationale

The chapter directs that children with low platelet counts, usually below 100,000/mm3, "should avoid activities that might cause injury or bleeding, such as riding bicycles or skateboards, skating, climbing trees or playground equipment, and contact sports," and that once the count rises "these restrictions are not necessary" (p. 1080).

Watch for

(B) applies the spontaneous-bleeding threshold to an activity decision, and the chapter deliberately keeps four platelet numbers apart for four different decisions — 20,000 for spontaneous bleeding, 100,000 for activity, 40,000 with granulocytes of 500 for toothbrushing, and transfusion reserved for refractory active bleeding.

17. A child with cancer develops stomatitis. Which oral care measure does the chapter recommend?
  • ✓A soft sponge toothbrush, avoiding lemon glycerin swabs and hydrogen peroxide
  • BViscous lidocaine for a young child before meals
  • CHydrogen peroxide rinses to debride the ulcerations
  • DLemon glycerin swabs to stimulate salivation

18. A 4-year-old newly diagnosed with acute lymphoblastic leukemia presents with pallor, recurrent infections, and petechiae. What accounts for all three?
  • ✓Bone marrow infiltration producing anemia, infection from neutropenia, and bleeding from decreased platelet production
  • BDirect organ infiltration by leukemic cells in the skin and lungs
  • CA paraneoplastic autoimmune process
  • DSide effects of induction chemotherapy, which has not yet begun
Rationale

The chapter names three main consequences of bone marrow infiltration: "(1) anemia from decreased erythrocytes, (2) infection from neutropenia, and (3) bleeding from decreased platelet production" (p. 1086).

Watch for

(D) attributes the presentation to treatment that has not started, which is a common misreading when the findings resemble chemotherapy toxicity; sorting each presenting sign into one of the three categories is what makes this material tractable.

acute leukemia in childrengeneral
19. A child with acute lymphoblastic leukemia is entering the phase that begins immediately after diagnosis. What defines it?
  • ✓Induction, lasting 4 to 5 weeks, using corticosteroids, vincristine, and L-asparaginase, aiming for less than 5% blasts in the marrow
  • BMaintenance, lasting 2 to 3 years, using daily mercaptopurine and weekly methotrexate
  • CConsolidation, using cyclophosphamide, cytarabine, and mercaptopurine
  • DDelayed intensification, repeating the induction drugs at higher doses
Rationale

The chapter states that induction begins almost immediately after diagnosis and "lasts for 4 to 5 weeks," that "The principal drugs are the corticosteroids (dexamethasone or prednisone), vincristine, and L-asparaginase," and that complete remission is "the presence of less than 5% blast cells in the bone marrow and no detectable leukemia in extramedullary sites" (pp. 1086–1087).

Watch for

(B) names the final phase, which runs 2 to 3 years; note that the period immediately after remission is critical, because myelosuppression leaves "the body defenseless against invading organisms... and susceptible to spontaneous hemorrhage."

20. Why does every child with acute lymphoblastic leukemia receive central nervous system prophylaxis?
  • ✓Leukemic cells in the central nervous system are protected by the blood-brain barrier from systemic chemotherapy
  • BAll children have detectable central nervous system disease at diagnosis
  • CCranial radiation is required in every case
  • DThe central nervous system is the most common site of the primary tumor
Rationale

The chapter states that because leukemic cells in the central nervous system are protected by the blood-brain barrier, "all children receive CNS prophylactic therapy," delivered as intrathecal chemotherapy plus central nervous system–directed systemic chemotherapy, with cranial radiation reserved for those at highest risk of relapse (pp. 1086–1087).

Watch for

(C) generalizes a treatment the chapter restricts to the highest-risk group; note that the central nervous system and the testes are the two named "sanctuaries" for leukemic cells requiring specific therapy.

  • BMissing occasional doses has no measurable effect on outcome
  • CAdherence matters only during induction
  • DDoses may be doubled to make up for missed ones

22. A nurse is comparing acute lymphoblastic and acute myeloid leukemia. Which statement is accurate?
  • ✓There is no established clinical staging system for acute myeloid leukemia, which is considered disseminated at diagnosis, and its treatment is more intense and shorter
  • BAcute myeloid leukemia uses the same induction-through-maintenance sequence as lymphoblastic disease
  • CAcute myeloid leukemia has a higher 5-year survival than lymphoblastic disease
  • DCentral nervous system irradiation is routine in acute myeloid leukemia
Rationale

The chapter states that "There is no established clinical staging system for AML; it is considered disseminated at diagnosis," that "Treatment for AML is generally more intense and of shorter duration than treatment for ALL," with maintenance not used in most regimens, and that "CNS irradiation is not usually used in AML treatment" (p. 1087).

Watch for

(C) reverses the prognosis, since almost 80% of children with lymphoblastic disease live 5 years or longer while myeloid disease carries 68% 5-year survival under 15 years; note also the two distinctive myeloid skin findings, leukemia cutis in blue or purple and blue-green chloromas around the eyes.

23. A lymph node biopsy in an adolescent shows Reed-Sternberg cells. What does this establish?
  • ✓Hodgkin lymphoma, since these cells are absent in the other lymphomas
  • BNon-Hodgkin lymphoma, since these cells are characteristic of it
  • CEither lymphoma, since the cells appear in both
  • DLeukemia with nodal involvement
Rationale

The chapter states that "The presence of Hodgkin and Reed-Sternberg cells is considered diagnostic of Hodgkin disease because it is absent in the other lymphomas" (p. 1089).

Watch for

(B) reverses the finding, and the consequence is more than nomenclature — the staging system used for Hodgkin lymphoma "is not used for NHL," so the diagnosis determines how the disease is staged as well as what it is called.

  • ✓Non-Hodgkin lymphoma is more common in children overall, while Hodgkin disease shows a striking increase in ages 15 to 19
  • BHodgkin disease is more common in children than non-Hodgkin lymphoma at every age
  • CBoth peak in the preschool years
  • DHodgkin disease is most common before 5 years of age

The chapter states that "In children NHL is more common than Hodgkin disease," and that "Hodgkin disease is extremely rare before 5 years of age, [with] a striking increase in children ages 15 to 19" (pp. 1088–1089).

(D) inverts the age distribution exactly — the rarity before 5 and the adolescent spike are what make age itself a useful discriminator when a lymphoma is suspected.

25. An adolescent with a mediastinal lymphoma develops facial swelling and dyspnea that worsen when lying flat. What should the nurse anticipate?
  • ✓Superior vena cava syndrome, requiring airway protection
  • BTumor lysis syndrome, requiring rasburicase
  • CSpinal cord compression, requiring magnetic resonance imaging
  • DDisseminated intravascular coagulation, requiring heparin

(C) is the other structural emergency in this chapter, but its initial manifestation is back pain rather than facial swelling, and magnetic resonance imaging is its gold standard; the positional worsening here points to venous obstruction.

26. A child with a lymphoma develops back pain, extremity weakness, and loss of bladder control. What is the priority action?
  • ✓Obtain magnetic resonance imaging, which is the gold standard for diagnosing spinal cord compression
  • BBegin physical therapy for the weakness
  • CObtain a lumbar puncture to assess for malignant cells
  • DSchedule a bone scan for the following week
Rationale

The chapter names back pain as "a common initial manifestation" of spinal cord compression, with sensation change, extremity weakness, loss of bowel and bladder function, and respiratory insufficiency, and states that "MRI is the gold standard for diagnosis," with high-dose steroids and emergent radiation or laminectomy if indicated (p. 1078).

Watch for

(D) treats an emergency as routine follow-up, and the neurologic deficits here are time-critical; note that a lumbar puncture is also avoided in the presence of increased intracranial pressure elsewhere in this chapter.

childhood brain tumorsgeneral
27. A 6-year-old has headaches worst on awakening and vomiting unrelated to feeding, with ataxia and nystagmus. Which tumor location does this suggest?
  • ✓Infratentorial, which accounts for about 60% of pediatric brain tumors
  • BSupratentorial, which typically presents with seizures
  • CSpinal, which presents with back pain
  • DThe location cannot be inferred from symptoms
Rationale

The chapter states that "the most common symptoms of infratentorial brain tumors are headache, especially on awakening, and vomiting that is not related to feeding," with cerebellar involvement producing nystagmus, ataxia, dysarthria, and dysmetria, and that about 60% of pediatric tumors are infratentorial (p. 1092).

Watch for

(B) names the supratentorial picture, which "more commonly include seizures, personality or behavioral changes, visual disturbances, and hemiparesis"; the location matters because it determines the postoperative positioning order.

28. A child has just returned from resection of an infratentorial brain tumor. How should the nurse position the child?
  • ✓Flat, on either side, but not on the operative side
  • BWith the head elevated above the level of the heart
  • CIn Trendelenburg position to promote venous return
  • DProne with the head turned to the operative side
Rationale

31. A child with a suspected brain tumor and signs of increased intracranial pressure is scheduled for diagnostic testing. Which test does the chapter identify as the gold standard, and which is avoided?
  • ✓Magnetic resonance imaging is the gold standard, and lumbar puncture is avoided because of the danger of brainstem herniation
  • BComputed tomography is the gold standard, and magnetic resonance imaging is avoided
  • CLumbar puncture is the gold standard, and imaging is avoided
  • DElectroencephalography is the gold standard, and imaging is avoided
Rationale

The chapter states that "The gold standard diagnostic procedure is MRI," with computed tomography used in urgent cases when magnetic resonance imaging is unavailable, and that "In the presence of increased ICP, LP is avoided due to the danger of possible brainstem" herniation (p. 1092).

32. A 2-year-old has a firm, nontender, irregular abdominal mass that crosses the midline. Which tumor does this suggest?
  • ✓Neuroblastoma
  • BWilms tumor
  • CRhabdomyosarcoma
  • DHepatoblastoma
Rationale

The chapter states that with abdominal tumors "the most common presenting sign is a firm, nontender, irregular mass in the abdomen that crosses the midline (in contrast to Wilms tumor, which is usually confined to one side)" (p. 1096).

(B) is the confusable partner, and midline crossing is the single discriminator the chapter gives explicitly; the median age also differs, about 19 months for neuroblastoma and 44 months for unilateral Wilms tumor.

33. A child is being evaluated for suspected neuroblastoma. Which laboratory study does the chapter name?
  • ✓Urinary catecholamine metabolites, vanillylmandelic acid and homovanillic acid
  • BSerum alpha-fetoprotein
  • CUrinary protein and creatinine ratio
  • DSerum renin
Rationale

The chapter states that because neuroblastomas excrete epinephrine and norepinephrine, "urinary excretion of catecholamine metabolites (vanillylmandelic acid [VMA] and homovanillic acid [HVA]) is measured before therapy," and these markers monitor response and detect relapse (p. 1096).

Watch for

(D) belongs to Wilms tumor, where hypertension arises from excess renin secretion; each tumor has its own biochemical signature, which is another way the two abdominal masses separate.

34. A 3-year-old is admitted with a suspected Wilms tumor. Which nursing action is essential?
  • ✓Do not palpate the abdomen, and post a sign on the bed
  • BPalpate the abdomen once each shift to track the mass size
  • CMeasure abdominal girth every 2 hours with deep palpation
  • DEncourage the child to lie prone to reduce pressure on the mass
Rationale

The chapter states that "the most important" preoperative concern is "not to palpate the tumor unless absolutely necessary because manipulation of the mass may cause dissemination of cancer cells to adjacent and distant sites," and a Nursing Alert directs posting a sign on the bed reading "Do not palpate abdomen" (p. 1100).

Watch for

(B) and (C) are both ordinary nursing assessments that are specifically harmful here, which is what makes this the chapter's clearest single nursing action; careful bathing and handling also matter in preventing trauma to the tumor site.

  • ✓Gastrointestinal activity, because vincristine-induced adynamic ileus can cause intestinal obstruction
  • BNeurologic status, because of the risk of intracranial hemorrhage
