1. A 5-month-old is at a well-child visit and the parent asks whether the weight gain is on track. Which statement reflects the expected pattern?
  • ✓Birth weight should have at least doubled by now
  • BBirth weight should have at least tripled by now
  • CBirth weight should have increased by about 50%
  • DBirth weight should have increased by about 33%
Rationale

Infants gain 680 g (1.5 lb) per month until age 5 months, "when the birth weight has at least doubled." The average 6-month weight is 7.26 kg (16 lb). Tripling is the 1-year landmark, not the 5-month one.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick because doubling and tripling are taught together and tripling is the more memorable figure; it belongs to 12 months. (C) is the length landmark and (D) the head-circumference landmark, both also at 1 year.

2. A 12-month-old is measured at the well-child visit. Which set of findings is expected at this age?
  • AWeight doubled, birth length increased by 33%, chest circumference still well below head circumference
  • ✓Weight tripled, birth length increased by almost 50%, chest circumference approximately equal to head circumference
  • CWeight tripled, birth length increased by 33%, chest circumference exceeding head circumference by several centimeters
  • DWeight doubled, birth length increased by almost 50%, chest circumference approximately equal to head circumference
Rationale

"By 1 year of age the infant's birth weight has tripled, to an average of 9.75 kg (21.5 lb)," "birth length has increased by almost 50%," and "The chest circumference approximately equals head circumference by the end of the first year." Head circumference increases by almost 33% over the same period, which is the figure (C) misattributes to length.

Watch for

(A) carries the 5-month doubling figure forward to 12 months, which is the mirror error of the previous item. (C) keeps the correct weight and moves the chest past the head a year early; the two are approximately equal at 1 year, and the chest exceeds the head only afterward.

3. A nurse palpates an open anterior fontanel and a closed posterior fontanel in a 4-month-old. What is the correct interpretation?
  • ✓Both findings are expected at this age
  • BThe open anterior fontanel is abnormal and requires evaluation
  • CThe closed posterior fontanel is premature and warrants referral
  • DBoth fontanels should have closed by 8 weeks
Rationale

The posterior fontanel fuses by 6 to 8 weeks of age and the anterior fontanel closes by 12 to 18 months, the average age being 14 months. At 4 months the posterior is expected to be closed and the anterior still open.

Watch for

(D) is the pick of a learner who remembers the 6-to-8-week figure but attaches it to both fontanels; only the posterior closes that early, and the anterior has most of a year left.

4. At a 4-month well-child visit the nurse notes that the infant's eyes do not consistently work together to fix on a single image. Why does this finding warrant prompt referral rather than watchful waiting?
  • ✓Binocular vision should be well established by 4 months, and lack of it results in strabismus, which must be detected early to prevent permanent blindness
  • BBinocular vision is not expected until 12 months, so referral mainly prevents unnecessary parental anxiety
  • CIt signals a need for corrective lenses, which are most effective when fitted before 6 months
  • DIt indicates that depth perception has failed to develop, which is irreversible after 9 months
Rationale

Binocularity, "the fixation of two ocular images into one cerebral picture (fusion)," "begins to develop by 6 weeks of age and should be well established by age 4 months." The chapter states the consequence directly: "Lack of binocular vision results in strabismus and must be detected early to prevent permanent blindness."

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick for anyone who groups all visual maturation into the second half of the first year; the 4-month deadline is exactly what makes this finding actionable at this visit rather than the next.

5. A 9-month-old is being assessed and the nurse finds that the eyes lag behind when the head is rotated to one side. What does this finding indicate?
  • AA normal finding that persists throughout infancy
  • ✓An abnormal finding that may indicate a neurologic problem
  • CA normal finding indicating intact binocular vision
  • DAn expected finding until depth perception is fully established
Rationale

The doll's eye reflex, in which "eyes lag behind if head is rotated to one side," is present in the newborn and disappears at 6 to 12 weeks. "The presence of this reflex at any other time in childhood is abnormal and may indicate a neurologic problem."

Watch for

(A) is the pull for a learner who knows the reflex is normal and stops there. Normal in the newborn and abnormal afterward is the whole content of this item, and the same shape recurs with the primitive reflexes generally.

6. A 5-month-old is brought in with a third episode of otitis media since birth. Which anatomic explanation should the nurse give the parent?
  • ✓The short, straight eustachian tube communicates closely with the ear, allowing infection to ascend from the pharynx to the middle ear
  • BThe eustachian tube is long and narrow in infancy, so secretions pool and cannot drain
  • CThe infant overproduces immunoglobulin A in the mucosal lining, and the resulting inflammation obstructs the tube
  • DAirway lumens are large relative to lung size, so organisms are carried upward more readily
Rationale

"The short, straight eustachian tube closely communicates with the ear, allowing infection to ascend from the pharynx to the middle ear." The chapter adds that inability to produce immunoglobulin A in the mucosal lining "provides less protection."

Watch for

(B) is the adult picture read backward into the infant and is the commonest inversion here; long and narrow is what the tube becomes with growth, not what it is. (C) inverts the immunoglobulin fact, which is underproduction, and (D) inverts the airway fact, which is that lumens remain small relative to lung size, "maintaining high resistance."

7. A 4-month-old who has been exclusively breastfed has a mildly low hemoglobin. The parent asks whether they should have been giving more iron. Which explanation is correct?
  • ✓Physiologic anemia at this age results from fetal hemoglobin shortening red blood cell survival, and its occurrence is not affected by an adequate iron supply
  • BPhysiologic anemia at this age is caused by inadequate iron intake and is prevented by iron supplementation
  • CPhysiologic anemia is caused by occult gastrointestinal blood loss and is prevented by avoiding cow's milk
  • DPhysiologic anemia does not occur before 12 months of age
Rationale

Fetal hemoglobin is present up to the first 5 months and "results in a shortened survival of red blood cells (RBCs)," producing physiologic anemia at 3 to 6 months of age. The chapter states the discriminating fact in one sentence: "The occurrence of physiologic anemia is not affected by an adequate iron supply."

Watch for

(B) is the reflex answer because nearly every other anemia in pediatrics is an iron story. This one is not, and the book says so explicitly — which is why it is worth a question on its own.

8. A 9-month-old has been drinking whole cow's milk since 7 months of age and now has iron deficiency anemia. Which statement correctly distinguishes this from physiologic anemia?
  • AThis anemia is unaffected by iron intake, whereas physiologic anemia responds to iron supplementation
  • ✓This anemia is worsened by whole cow's milk, possibly through occult gastrointestinal blood loss, whereas physiologic anemia arises from fetal hemoglobin and is unaffected by iron supply
  • CBoth arise from depletion of maternal iron stores at 5 to 6 months and are managed identically
  • DPhysiologic anemia occurs at 9 to 12 months and iron deficiency anemia at 3 to 6 months
Rationale

Whole cow's milk "can cause iron deficiency anemia in infants, possibly as a result of occult gastrointestinal blood loss," and is "deficient in iron, zinc, and vitamin C." Physiologic anemia occurs at 3 to 6 months from shortened red cell survival and "is not affected by an adequate iron supply."

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error of the previous item, attaching the iron-independence to the wrong anemia. (D) swaps the two age windows, which works on anyone who memorized both numbers without their owners.

9. A nurse is teaching new graduates why infants dehydrate faster than adults. Which statements from the chapter support that vulnerability? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓At birth 75% of the infant's body weight is water
  • ✓The high proportion of extracellular fluid predisposes the infant to more rapid loss of total body fluid
  • ✓Complete maturity of the kidney does not occur until the latter half of the second year
  • DInfant urine is concentrated at a high specific gravity, which conserves free water
  • ✓Extracellular fluid falls from 40% at term to 20% in adulthood

The chapter gives 75% body water at birth, extracellular fluid falling "from 40% at term to 20% in adulthood," the ECF proportion as the mechanism — it "predisposes the infant to more rapid loss of total body fluid and, consequently, dehydration" — and renal immaturity persisting into the latter half of the second year. 10. An 11-month-old is observed picking up a raisin neatly between the thumb and the tip of the index finger. How should the nurse document this?
  • APalmar grasp, which is expected at this age
  • BCrude pincer grasp, which is expected at 8 to 9 months
  • ✓Neat pincer grasp, which is expected at 11 months
  • DVoluntary grasp, first achieved at 5 months
Rationale

The palmar grasp uses the whole hand; the pincer grasp uses "the thumb and index finger," appearing crude at 8 to 9 months and neat at 11 months. By 10 months the pincer grasp is "sufficiently established to enable infants to pick up a raisin."

Watch for

(B) is the near-miss, and it pulls because both grasps use the same two digits; crude versus neat is the whole distinction, and neat is the 11-month one. (D) names a real 5-month milestone — being "able to voluntarily grasp an object" — which is three grasp stages earlier.

11. A parent reports that their 5-month-old has started rolling from the abdomen to the back but not the other way. Which response is correct?
  • ✓This is the expected sequence; rolling from back to abdomen typically follows at about 6 months
  • BThis is reversed; infants roll from back to abdomen first, at about 5 months
  • CThis is delayed; both directions should be achieved by 4 months
  • DThis suggests a persistent asymmetric tonic neck reflex and needs evaluation
Rationale

Infants roll from abdomen to back at 5 months and from back to abdomen at 6 months. The asymmetric tonic neck reflex "persists from birth to 3 months" and "prevents the infant from rolling over," so it is long gone by 5 months.

Watch for

(B) is the inversion this pair reliably produces, and it pulls because rolling off the back feels like the easier direction to picture. Abdomen to back comes first.

12. A 9-month-old moves across the floor on hands and knees with the abdomen off the floor. Which term does the chapter use for this?
  • ACrawling, because the infant is propelling forward
  • ✓Creeping, because the abdomen is off the floor and the infant is on hands and knees
  • CCruising, because the infant is moving independently across a surface
  • DCrawling, because creeping requires holding onto furniture
Rationale

"Crawling (propelling forward with belly on floor) progresses to creeping on hands and knees (with belly off floor) by 9 months." The book uses the two terms precisely and distractors swap them.

Watch for

(A) is ordinary everyday usage, which runs exactly backward from the book's: what most people call crawling is what the book calls creeping. (D) invents a furniture criterion the chapter never attaches to creeping.

13. A 12-month-old has never pulled to a standing position. What is the nurse's correct action?
  • AReassure the parent, since independent standing is not expected until 15 months
  • ✓Refer for further evaluation for possible developmental dysplasia of the hip
  • CRecommend a walker to strengthen the legs
  • DReassure the parent, since bearing full weight on the legs is not expected until 12 months
Rationale

"An infant who does not pull to a standing position by 11 to 12 months of age should be further evaluated for possible developmental dysplasia of the hip." Infants stand holding furniture and pull to standing at 9 months, and walk holding furniture at 11 months.

Watch for

(C) is the intuitive intervention and is wrong twice over: "walkers do not enhance walking and coordination, and they are dangerous if tipped over or placed near the top of stairs," and recommending one substitutes a device for the referral this finding requires. (D) misstates the age for full weight bearing with assistance, which is 6 to 7 months.

Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Labyrinth righting reflex, appearing at 2 months
  • ✓Neck righting reflex, appearing at 3 months
  • CDoll's eye reflex, appearing at 6 to 12 weeks
  • ✓Landau reflex, appearing at 6 to 8 months

15. Parents of a 2-month-old describe feeding, bathing, and dressing the infant on a strict schedule, and ask whether meeting every physical need is enough to raise a secure child. Which response reflects Erikson's phase I?
  • AProviding food, warmth, and shelter is by itself adequate for developing a sense of trust
  • ✓The crucial element is the quality of the parent-child relationship and the care received; provision of food, warmth, and shelter by itself is inadequate
  • CTrust is determined by the quantity of food taken and the length of sucking at each feeding
  • DTrust cannot be assessed until the toddler years, when autonomy emerges
Rationale

"The crucial element for the achievement of this task is the quality of both the relationship between the parent (or caregiver) and child and the care the infant receives. The provision of food, warmth, and shelter by itself is inadequate." The chapter adds that "the degree of mothering skill, the quantity of food, or the length of sucking does not determine the quality of the experience. Rather, it is the total quality of the interpersonal relationship."

Watch for

(C) is the reflex pick because the oral stage makes feeding feel like the entire mechanism; the book names quantity of food and length of sucking specifically as the things that do not determine quality.

16. A parent says they anticipate every need and never let their 5-month-old fuss for even a moment. Which concern should the nurse raise?
  • ANone; anticipating all needs before they are signaled is the ideal way to build trust
  • ✓If parents always meet needs before the infant signals readiness, the infant never learns to test the ability to control the environment
  • CMistrust develops only from prolonged delay in meeting needs, so this approach is protective
  • DThis will cause the infant to skip the oral stage and progress early to autonomy
Rationale

"Mistrust can result from either too much or too little frustration. If parents always meet their children's needs before the children signal their readiness, infants will never learn to test their ability to control the environment." Prolonged delay produces mistrust from the other direction, "therefore consistency of care is essential."

Watch for

(C) states the half of the rule nearly everyone holds, and that is why this item exists: mistrust comes from too much frustration and from too little, and only the prolonged-delay half is intuitive.

17. A 3-month-old shows anticipation before feeding, becomes bored when left alone, and shows no separation anxiety unless the caregiver's routine differs from the usual. In Piaget's stage II, primary circular reactions, what is the infant learning from?
  • ✓The type of interaction between objects or individuals rather than from the object itself
  • BThe object itself rather than from the interaction surrounding it
  • CSymbolic representation, which allows thought about an object without experiencing it
  • DIntentional coordination of two separate schemas to reach a goal
Rationale

Table 10.1 gives stage II, 1 to 4 months, as "Learns from type of interaction between objects or individual rather than from object itself," and lists anticipation before feeding, boredom when left alone, and "Shows no separation anxiety unless caregiver's skill differs from usual routine" among its behaviors.

(B) is the stage IV answer, and the chapter contrasts the two stages in a single sentence precisely because this is the inversion learners make. (D) describes stage IV's coordination of secondary schemas, which is five months away.

18. An 11-month-old moves a pillow aside to reach a toy hidden behind it, then puts the toy into a container. In Piaget's stage IV, coordination of secondary schemas, what is the infant learning from?
  • AThe type of interaction between objects or individuals
  • ✓The object itself, with the beginning of intellectual reasoning
  • CRepetitious use of reflexes establishing a pattern of experiences
  • DDeferred imitation of an action observed hours earlier
Rationale

In stage IV, 9 to 12 months, infants "learn from the object itself. This is in contrast to the second stage, in which infants learn from the type of interaction between objects or individuals." The stage brings "beginning of intellectual reasoning," actively searching for a hidden object, and distinguishing end products from their means so as to remove barriers.

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error of the previous item. (C) is stage I, use of reflexes, and it is the answer chosen when "reflexes" is remembered as the Piaget keyword for infancy generally rather than for the first month specifically.

19. A nurse explains why attachment to a specific caregiver cannot be complete in the first weeks of life. Which two achievements does the chapter name as its prerequisites?
  • ✓The ability to discriminate the mother from other individuals, and the achievement of object permanence
  • BThe social smile, and the ability to self-soothe
  • CBinocular vision, and the ability to localize sound
  • DSymbolic thought, and the emergence of expressive language
Rationale

Attachment requires "(1) the ability to discriminate the mother from other individuals and (2) the achievement of object permanence." Object permanence develops at approximately 9 to 10 months of age, which is why the earliest weeks bring responses "indiscriminately to anyone."

Watch for

(C) names two real infant milestones, which is what makes it attractive. They are sensory achievements; attachment is built on cognitive ones.

infant attachment, separation anxiety, and stranger fearvision
20. A 7-month-old cries as soon as the parent walks out of the examination room and settles when the parent returns. Which phenomenon is this, and what makes it possible?
  • ✓Separation anxiety, which develops between 4 and 8 months as object permanence makes the parent's absence knowable
  • BStranger fear, which develops between 6 and 8 months as the infant discriminates familiar from unfamiliar people
  • CReactive attachment disorder, which appears when comfort seeking repeatedly fails
  • DSeparation anxiety, which does not develop until 11 to 12 months
Rationale

Separation anxiety develops between 4 and 8 months, when "the infant progresses through the first stage of separation-individuation" and object permanence makes the absent parent still real. By 11 to 12 months infants "are able to anticipate her imminent departure by watching her behaviors, and they begin to protest before she leaves" — which is a later refinement, not the onset.

Watch for

(B) is the companion phenomenon with a heavily overlapping age window, which is exactly why the two are confused. It is triggered by a stranger's presence, not by the parent's absence. (D) mistakes the anticipatory stage for the onset.

21. The same 7-month-old cries and turns away when an unfamiliar nurse approaches, even while sitting on the parent's lap. Which phenomenon is this, and what does it require?
  • ASeparation anxiety, which requires object permanence
  • ✓Stranger fear, which requires the ability to discriminate between familiar and unfamiliar people
  • CDisinhibited social engagement disorder, which is marked by loss of stranger wariness
  • DSpoiled child syndrome, which results from inconsistent limit setting
Rationale

"Between ages 6 and 8 months fear of strangers and stranger anxiety become prominent and are related to infants' ability to discriminate between familiar and unfamiliar people," shown as "clinging to the parent, crying, and turning away from strangers."

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error of the previous item — the parent has not left, so nothing here is a separation. (C) inverts the behavior entirely, describing an infant who approaches unfamiliar adults with no suspicion at all.

22. A nurse must assess a 7-month-old who is sitting on a parent's lap and watching warily. Which approach is best?
  • AStand at the bedside, smile broadly, and extend both arms to pick the infant up
  • ✓Speak softly, meet the child at eye level, keep a safe distance, and avoid sudden intrusive gestures
  • CTake the infant from the parent quickly, so the assessment is over sooner
  • DMove the infant to an infant seat away from the parent to reduce clinging
Rationale

The chapter directs the stranger, including the nurse, to "talk softly; meet the child at eye level (to appear smaller); maintain a safe distance from the infant; and avoid sudden, intrusive gestures, such as holding the arms out and smiling broadly." It also names proximity as a factor: being "on parent's lap rather than in infant seat" is less stressful.

Watch for

(A) is the reflex friendly approach, and the book names both of its components — outstretched arms and a broad smile — as the specific things to avoid. (D) removes the single factor most protective against the distress being assessed.

23. A toddler in foster care approaches every unfamiliar adult in the waiting room without hesitation, shows no wariness of strangers, and has poor impulse control. Which pattern does this describe?
  • AReactive attachment disorder, marked by failure to seek and respond to comfort when distressed
  • ✓Disinhibited social engagement disorder, marked by inappropriate approach to unfamiliar adults and lack of suspicion of strangers
  • CNormal stranger fear for the age
  • DSeparation anxiety that has failed to resolve
Rationale

Children with disinhibited social engagement disorder may exhibit "inappropriate approach to unfamiliar adults, lack of suspicion of strangers, and poor impulse control." Reactive attachment disorder is the withdrawn, inhibited picture: "not being cuddly with parents, failing to seek and respond to comfort when distressed, minimal social and emotional reciprocity."

Watch for

(A) is the reflex pick because reactive attachment disorder is by far the better known of the two names, so it gets applied to any attachment problem. It describes the opposite behavior — withdrawn rather than indiscriminate.

24. A parent of a 12-month-old worries that the child says only four words. What should the nurse explain?
  • ✓A 12-month-old says 3 to 5 words with meaning and may understand as many as 100, so comprehension normally runs far ahead of speech
  • BA 12-month-old should have a vocabulary of at least 20 spoken words, so evaluation is warranted
  • CExpressive and receptive language develop at the same rate, so understanding is also limited to about four words
  • DSpeech is not expected until 18 months, so no comparison can be made yet
Rationale

At 12 months the infant "can say 3 to 5 words with meaning and may understand as many as 100 words." Expressive language is the "ability to make thoughts, ideas, and desires known to others"; receptive language is the "ability to understand the words being spoken."

Watch for

(C) is the inversion that worries parents most, because it treats the spoken word count as a measure of comprehension. Receptive language far outpaces expressive at this age, and the gap in this stem is the expected one.

25. A 10-month-old does not comprehend "no," does not turn toward voices, and produces few sounds. Beyond a language referral, what does the chapter direct the nurse to consider?
  • ✓Careful evaluation for potential hearing loss
  • BReassurance, since comprehension of "no" is not expected until 18 months
  • CImmediate evaluation for reactive attachment disorder
  • DRestriction of screen media, which is the usual cause of expressive delay at this age
Rationale

Infants comprehend "no" and obey simple commands with gestures at 9 to 10 months, and "delays in achieving milestones are carefully evaluated for potential hearing loss." A 10-month-old who does not turn toward voices has failed a hearing milestone as well as a language one.

Watch for

(D) is attractive because the chapter really does discourage screen media, but it gives that guidance as a stimulation issue — media "does not provide infants with appropriate sensory stimulation and does not increase language skills" — not as a diagnosis to pursue. Hearing loss is the named consideration.

26. A parent of a 2-month-old has begun spooning rice cereal and reports that the baby "pushes it right back out with the tongue." How should the nurse interpret this?
  • AThe infant dislikes the taste, and a different first cereal should be tried
  • ✓The extrusion reflex is still strong, which is one reason solids are not recommended before 4 to 6 months
  • CThe infant has a swallowing disorder and needs referral
  • DThe infant is refusing food deliberately, which is itself a sign of readiness for self-feeding
Rationale

"The extrusion (protrusion) reflex is strong and often causes food to be pushed out of the mouth" in the early months, and "The addition of solid foods before 4 to 6 months of age is not recommended." Early introduction "before 4 months of life is correlated with obesity later in life."

Watch for

(D) inverts the chapter's own point: young infants "are unable to deliberately push food away or avoid feeding," so tongue thrust at 2 months is a reflex, not a preference.

27. A 5-month-old no longer thrusts the tongue when a spoon is offered, sits with support, turns the head away when full, and has begun tooth eruption. What do these findings indicate?
  • ✓Readiness for the introduction of solid foods
  • BThat solids should still be withheld until 12 months, when table foods are introduced
  • CReadiness for whole cow's milk
  • DThat weaning from the breast or bottle should be completed now
Rationale

Readiness at 4 to 6 months is marked by a gastrointestinal tract that "has matured sufficiently," tooth eruption that "facilitates biting and chewing," disappearance of the extrusion reflex, head control permitting sitting with support and turning away to refuse, and voluntary grasp allowing self-feeding.

Watch for

(B) is the mirror error of the previous item, carrying the "not before 4 to 6 months" rule past its own endpoint; 12 months is when well-cooked table foods may be served, not when solids begin. (C) confuses readiness for solids with the separate 12-month gate on whole cow's milk.

28. A nurse teaches a parent how to start solids for a 5-month-old. Which instruction is correct?
  • ABegin with strained meats, and introduce a new food each day to build variety quickly
  • ✓Begin with iron-fortified rice cereal for its high iron content and low allergenic potential, and introduce only one new food every 5 to 7 days
  • CBegin with a mixed-grain cereal, and introduce one new food every 24 hours
  • DBegin with fruit juice offered in a bottle, to supply vitamin C
Rationale

Iron-fortified infant cereal is given first "because of its high iron content (7 mg/3 tbsp of prepared dry cereal)," and rice is "usually suggested as an initial food because of its easy digestibility and low allergenic potential." "Only one solid is introduced every 5 to 7 days so that a reaction to a particular food can be distinguished." Meats typically come last, after fruits and vegetables.

Watch for

(C) keeps a cereal start but collapses the spacing interval, which is the half of the rule that exists to make a reaction attributable; a daily change makes any reaction untraceable. It also starts with a grain combination, which the chapter says to avoid when introducing a new grain.

29. A parent of a 7-month-old asks how much of a new vegetable to offer, and whether it can replace a milk feeding. Which response is correct?
  • ✓About 1 tbsp per year of age, and solids should not be perceived as a substitute for milk until the child is older than 12 months
  • BAbout one-quarter cup per feeding, and solids may replace one milk feeding per day from 6 months
  • CAs much as the infant will willingly take, and solids should replace milk gradually from 7 months
  • DAbout 1 tbsp per month of age, and solids should have replaced milk by 9 months
Rationale

"1 tbsp per year of age" is an adequate portion, and "solids should not be perceived as a substitute for milk until the child is older than 12 months" — at this age they are for "taste and chewing experience." Smaller portions also avoid the "clean your plate" habit "known to contribute to overeating in later life."

Watch for

(C) is the reflex answer of a parent who reads appetite as the guide, and it also inverts the chapter's point that solids do not replace milk in the first year. (D) keeps the correct unit and swaps the multiplier from years to months, which at 7 months would be seven times the recommended portion.

30. A parent of a 9-month-old asks which finger foods are safe and when the bottle should go. Which response reflects the chapter?
  • AGrapes and raw carrot sticks are appropriate finger foods, and the nighttime feeding should be the first one dropped
  • ✓Junior and finger foods are appropriate except grapes; raw fruits with seeds, vegetables, and nuts are hazardous; and the nighttime feeding is usually the last to be discontinued
  • COnly pureed foods are safe until 12 months, and weaning should then be completed abruptly
  • DHard candy and teething biscuits are safe because they dissolve, and weaning should not begin before 12 to 14 months
Rationale

By 8 to 9 months junior foods and finger foods are offered "(except grapes)," and "Raw fruits with seeds, vegetables, and nuts are hazardous." Weaning "should be gradual by replacing one bottle-feeding or breastfeeding at a time," and "The nighttime feeding is usually the last feeding to be discontinued."

Watch for

(A) inverts the weaning order, and dropping the nighttime feeding first is the intuitive error because that is the feeding parents most want to end. (D) is wrong on both counts: hard candy is listed as a choking hazard, and teething biscuits carry their own caution "because large chunks may be broken off."

31. A mother who is exclusively breastfeeding her 2-week-old asks whether the baby needs any supplement. Which response is correct?
  • ANo supplement is needed, because human milk is the most desirable complete diet for the first 6 months
  • ✓All infants, including those exclusively breastfed, should receive 400 international units of vitamin D daily beginning in the first few days of life
  • CVitamin D is needed only by formula-fed infants taking less than 1 L per day
