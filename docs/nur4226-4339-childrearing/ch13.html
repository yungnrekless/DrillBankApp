preschooler growth and gross motor refinementgeneral
1. A parent asks what to expect of their 4-year-old's growth over the coming year. Which statement reflects the chapter?
  • ✓The rate of physical growth slows and stabilizes; weight increases about 2 to 3 kg (4.5 to 6.5 lb) per year and height 6.5 to 9 cm (2.5 to 3.5 inches) per year, with the legs rather than the trunk increasing in length
  • BGrowth accelerates during the preschool years, with weight gain of about 5 kg per year
  • CThe trunk rather than the legs accounts for most of the height gain in this period
  • DWeight doubles between ages 3 and 5 years
Rationale

"The rate of physical growth slows and stabilizes during the preschool years," with weight gain of "approximately 2 to 3 kg (4.5 to 6.5 lb) per year" and "a yearly increase of 6.5 to 9 cm (2.5 to 3.5 inches)." "The legs of a preschooler, rather than the trunk, increase in length." Average weights are 14.5 kg at 3 years, 16.5 kg at 4 years, and 18.5 kg at 5 years.

Watch for

(B) inverts the chapter's opening statement, and it pulls because preschoolers visibly lengthen and lose the toddler's squat, potbellied look for a build that is "slender but sturdy, graceful, agile, and posturally erect." That change is in proportion, not in rate. (D) would require a gain roughly three times the stated annual figure.

2. A 4-year-old at a well-child visit skips and hops proficiently on one foot and catches a ball reliably. Which assessment is correct?
  • ✓Expected for 4 years; skipping on alternate feet and jumping rope belong to 5 years
  • BDelayed; skipping on alternate feet is expected at 4 years
  • CAdvanced; hopping on one foot is the 5-year milestone
  • DExpected, but catching a ball reliably indicates unusually advanced coordination for the age
Rationale

At 3 years the child "rides a tricycle, walks on tiptoe, balances on one foot for a few seconds, and broad jumps." At 4 years the child "skips and hops proficiently on one foot and catches a ball reliably." At 5 years the child "skips on alternate feet, jumps rope, and begins to skate and swim." Motor development in this period "consists of increases in strength and refinement of previously learned skills."

Watch for

(B) moves the 5-year skill back a year, and it pulls because the word "skips" appears in both descriptions. The discriminator is one foot at 4 years versus alternate feet at 5.

3. Parents of a 5-year-old ask about enrolling the child in a formalized, competitive training program for a sport. Which response reflects the chapter?
  • ✓Muscle development and bone growth are still far from mature and overexertion can injure delicate tissues; the most important aspect of organized play at this age is that the activity be developmentally appropriate and occur in a nonthreatening, fun, and safe environment, with readiness determined individually
  • BFormalized training should begin as early as possible to maximize athletic potential
  • COrganized sports should be avoided entirely until school age, since preschoolers cannot follow rules
  • DPhysical activity should be limited to quiet indoor play, because preschoolers are prone to falls
Rationale

"Muscle development and bone growth are still far from mature. Excessive activity and overexertion can injure delicate tissues." Whether young children should begin formalized training "is controversial," and "readiness must be determined individually." "The most important aspect of organized play for preschoolers is that the activity is developmentally appropriate and occurs in a nonthreatening, fun, and safe environment." Children at this age "benefit from free play and exposure to a variety of physical activities."

Watch for

(D) inverts the injury picture for this age: "Because of improved gross and fine motor skills, coordination, and balance, preschoolers are less prone to falls than toddlers," and motor activity levels "continue to be high" and are therefore encouraged.

4. A 5-year-old's examination reveals that a permanent tooth is erupting, the child writes consistently with the right hand, and the child cannot yet manage the small print in a workbook. How should the nurse interpret these findings?
  • ✓All are expected at 5 years; eruption of permanent dentition may begin, handedness is established with about 90% right-handed, and the child is not ready for concentrated close work because of slight farsightedness and still-unrefined eye-hand coordination
  • BPermanent dentition should not begin until 7 years, so dental referral is needed
  • CDifficulty with small print indicates a visual defect requiring correction
  • DEstablished handedness at 5 years is premature and should not be reinforced
Rationale

Table 13.1 gives for age 5 years: "eruption of permanent dentition may begin"; "handedness is established (about 90% are right-handed)"; and the child is "not ready for concentrated close work or small print because of slight farsightedness and still unrefined eye-hand coordination."

(C) reads a normal developmental limitation as pathology, and it is the reading that sends children for unnecessary vision workups. The farsightedness named here is the expected state at this age, not a defect.

5. A 3-year-old is handed a crayon and shown a model. Which performance is expected?
  • ✓Copies a circle and imitates a cross and vertical and horizontal lines, but cannot draw a complete stick figure
  • BCopies a square and traces a cross and diamond
  • CCopies a diamond and triangle and adds seven to nine parts to a stick figure
  • DCan only scribble, and cannot name what has been drawn
Rationale

At 3 years the child "copies a circle and imitates a cross and vertical and horizontal lines," holds the writing instrument "with the fingers rather than the fist," scribbles but "can name what has been drawn," and cannot draw a complete stick figure. At 4 to 5 years the child "can trace a cross and copy a square"; the triangle and diamond are "usually the last geometric figures to be mastered," at 5 to 6 years.

Watch for

(B) is the 4-year-old's performance and (C) the 5-year-old's. The distractors swap the verb rather than the shape, and the verb is the whole discriminator: copying works from a finished model, imitating follows a demonstration, and tracing follows an outline — with the same shape moving through all three across two years.

6. A 4-year-old draws a stick figure with a head, a body, and legs — three parts in all. How should the nurse document this?
  • ✓Expected; adding three parts to a stick figure is the 4-year milestone, and seven to nine parts belongs to 5 years
  • BDelayed; seven to nine parts is expected at 4 years

7. A nurse uses a child's drawings during assessment. Which statements reflect the chapter's guidance on children's art?
  • ✓Children progress through placement, shape, design, and pictorial stages; two united diagrams make a combine and three or more an aggregate; and because drawings before age 6 are strikingly similar universally while later drawings are shaped by culture and environment, nurses need to consider children's backgrounds when interpreting drawings
  • BDrawings are a precise and validated measure of intelligence and personality
  • CThe pictorial stage, in which designs are recognizable as familiar objects, appears at 3 years
  • DA combine is three or more united diagrams and an aggregate is two
Rationale

Children "advance through four distinguishable stages": placement at 15 months, shape at 3 years, design immediately after, and pictorial at 4 to 5 years, "in which their designs are recognizable as familiar objects." "When two diagrams are united, the resulting design is called a combine"; "Three or more united diagrams produce an aggregate." Drawings before age 6 years "are strikingly similar universally," after which "cultural and environmental influences... shape much of what children draw," so nurses "need to consider children's backgrounds when interpreting drawings."

Watch for

(B) overstates evidence the chapter deliberately qualifies: drawing is used to assess intelligence, personality development, and psychosocial adjustment, but "the precise value of using drawing to measure such concepts is still a nebulous science." (D) reverses combine and aggregate.

initiative versus guilt in the preschoolergeneral
8. A 4-year-old energetically tries to pour milk for the whole family, spills it across the table, and then becomes tearful and says "I'm bad." Which psychosocial conflict does this illustrate?
  • AAutonomy versus shame and doubt
  • ✓Initiative versus guilt
  • CIndustry versus inferiority
  • DTrust versus mistrust
Rationale

"The chief psychosocial task of this period is acquiring a sense of initiative." Children this age "play, work, and live to the fullest and feel a real sense of accomplishment and satisfaction in their activities," and the conflict "arises when children overstep the limits of their ability and inquiry and experience guilt for not having behaved appropriately."

Watch for

(A) is the toddler's conflict, and it pulls because shame and guilt feel like one emotion. The discriminator sits in the stem: a child who attempted something beyond their ability and then feels bad about it is showing guilt, which is the preschooler's half of the pair.

9. A 5-year-old whose father died suddenly tells the nurse, "I got mad at Daddy last week and wished he would go away. Then he died." What is the essential nursing response?
  • ✓Clarify for the child that wishes cannot make events occur
  • BReassure the child that everyone has angry thoughts, without addressing the wish itself
  • CTell the child the thought was wrong and should not be repeated
  • DRedirect the conversation, since discussing the death will increase the child's distress
Rationale

"A particularly stressful thought is wishing one's parent dead." The contest normally resolves when the child "strongly identifies with the same-sex parent and peers during the school years," but if that parent dies before identification is complete, "the preschooler can be overwhelmed with guilt for having wished and therefore 'caused' the death." The chapter states the intervention directly: "Clarifying for children that wishes cannot make events occur is essential in helping them overcome their guilt and anxiety."

Watch for

(B) offers general reassurance and leaves the child's actual belief — that the wish caused the death — completely untouched, which is what magical thinking requires the nurse to name explicitly. (C) confirms the guilt rather than relieving it.

10. A parent asks why verbal warnings now work with their 4-year-old when they did nothing for their 2-year-old. Which explanation does the chapter give?
  • ✓Verbal enforcement of limits is much more effective in this age group than with toddlers; the preschooler still needs close supervision but is much more aware of danger and can listen and obey in most instances
  • BPreschoolers understand why a behavior is unacceptable, so explanation alone is now sufficient
  • CPreschoolers have completed superego development and no longer need parental limits
  • DThe change reflects the end of negativism rather than any change in comprehension
Rationale

"Verbal enforcement of limits is much more effective in this age-group than with toddlers." The preschooler "still needs close supervision but is much more aware of danger and can listen and obey in most instances." Development of the superego, or conscience, "starts toward the end of the toddler years and is a major task for preschoolers."

Watch for

(B) overstates comprehension. Children this age "are generally unable to understand why something is acceptable or unacceptable" and are "aware of appropriate behavior primarily through punishment or reward," relying "almost completely on parental principles for developing their own moral judgment."

11. A 4-year-old boy announces that he is going to marry his mother and has been unusually hostile toward his father. His parents are alarmed. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓This reflects the oedipal stage, and the conflict normally resolves through identification with the same-sex parent; Table 13.1 records that the 4-year-old identifies strongly with the opposite-sex parent while the 5-year-old identifies strongly with the same-sex parent
  • BThis indicates a disturbance in gender identification and warrants referral
  • CThis is expected at 2 years and is delayed at 4
  • DGirls show the same pattern but fear castration rather than experiencing penis envy
Rationale

Freud "described this goal in psychosexual terms and labeled the period the oedipal, or phallic, stage," in which the boy "wishes that his father were dead so he could marry his mother (Oedipus complex)." The resolution is "identification with the same-sex parent." Table 13.1 records that the 4-year-old "identifies strongly with parent of opposite sex" while the 5-year-old "strongly identifies with parent of same sex, especially boys with their fathers."

Watch for

(D) reverses the girls' side of the pair. Girls "do not fear castration; rather they experience penis envy"; it is the boy whose guilt about his feelings toward his father "makes him fear the same punishment of mutilation, resulting in the castration complex."

12. A 3-year-old and a 4-year-old are both described as preoperational. Which statement places each more precisely?
  • ✓The 3-year-old is in the preconceptual phase, ages 2 to 4 years, and the 4-year-old is in the phase of intuitive thought, ages 4 to 7 years
  • BThe 3-year-old is in the phase of intuitive thought and the 4-year-old in the preconceptual phase
  • CThe 3-year-old is sensorimotor and the 4-year-old preoperational
  • DThe 4-year-old has entered concrete operational thought
Rationale

The preoperational phase "covers the age span from 2 to 7 years and is divided into two stages: the preconceptual phase, ages 2 to 4 years, and the phase of intuitive thought, ages 4 to 7 years." Table 13.1 tags the 3-year-old as "in preconceptual phase" and the 4-year-old as "in phase of intuitive thought." The main transition across the two is "the shift from totally egocentric thought to social awareness and the ability to consider other viewpoints."

Watch for

(D) jumps a whole period; concrete operational thought belongs to the school-age child. Note that calling a 3-year-old preoperational would not be wrong — the sub-stage is the finer discriminator, and it is what the table supplies.

13. A 4-year-old whose newborn brother died of sudden infant death syndrome tells the nurse he had wished the baby would "go back." Which characteristic of preoperational thought does this illustrate?
  • ✓Magical thinking — because of egocentrism and transductive reasoning, preschoolers believe that thoughts are all powerful
  • BAnimism — ascribing lifelike qualities to inanimate objects
  • CInability to conserve — judging everything according to one dimension
  • DEgocentric speech — assuming everyone thinks as they do
Rationale

"Because of their egocentrism and transductive reasoning, they believe that thoughts are all powerful," which leaves preschoolers "feeling guilty and responsible for bad thoughts that may coincide with the occurrence of a wished event." The chapter's own example is a child who wishes a new sibling dead: if the sibling dies, "young children think their wish caused the death."

Watch for

(B) is the adjacent characteristic and the one this pair is most often swapped with. Both arise from egocentrism, and the object of the stem tells you which: a thought or a wish points to magical thinking, a thing points to animism.

14. A 4-year-old refuses to use the toilet after watching a television commercial in which the toilet bowl turns into a monster. Which characteristic does this illustrate?
  • AMagical thinking — the belief that thoughts are all powerful
  • ✓Animism — ascribing lifelike qualities to inanimate objects
  • CTransductive reasoning — reasoning from one particular to another
  • DLiteral belief in the power of words
Rationale

Animism, "ascribing lifelike qualities to inanimate objects," is what "helps explain why children fear objects." The chapter gives this exact example: a child "may refuse to use the toilet after watching a television commercial in which the toilet bowl is portrayed as turning into a monster."

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error of the previous item. Nothing here turns on the child's own thoughts causing an event; the fear is of the object itself, which is what makes it animism.

15. A hospitalized 4-year-old repeatedly asks when her mother is coming. Which response is most appropriate?
  • ✓"Your mother will visit you after you finish your lunch."
  • B"Your mother will be here at two o'clock."
  • C"Your mother will be here tomorrow afternoon."
  • D"Your mother will come back in about three hours."
Rationale

Preschoolers interpret time "according to their own frame of reference" — "A long time means until Christmas." The chapter directs explaining time by event and gives this sentence verbatim as its example, advising against "yesterday," "tomorrow," "next week," or "Tuesday." Associating time "with usual expected daily occurrences" helps children learn temporal relationships "while increasing their trust in others' predictions."

Watch for

(B) and (D) both use clock time, which a preschooler cannot convert into waiting. (C) uses a calendar word the chapter names specifically as one to avoid; it sounds concrete to an adult and carries no meaning for the child.

16. A 5-year-old overhears a nurse describe another child as "a bad patient." Later the 5-year-old says she must be a bad girl too. Which principle applies, and what is the correct practice?
  • ✓Preschoolers believe in the power of words and accept their meaning literally, so the act should be labeled rather than the child, as in "That was a bad thing to do"
  • BPreschoolers understand figurative language by 5 years, so the comment carries no risk
  • CThe child should be told she misunderstood, and the matter dropped
  • DPreschoolers cannot generalize a comment made about another child to themselves
Rationale

"Preschoolers believe in the power of words and accept their meaning literally" — calling a child "bad" means, in the child's mind, that they are bad. The chapter's nursing translation is to label the act rather than the child: "That was a bad thing to do."

Watch for

(D) underestimates precisely what this age does most. A child in the preconceptual stage is "able to imagine an event without actually experiencing it," which is why "Seeing someone hurt is sufficient for realizing what the hurt must be like," and why a preschooler watching another child get an injection "may become upset, almost as if he or she received the injection."

17. A 4-year-old who has just received an intramuscular injection becomes distressed and insists on a bandage, though there is no further bleeding. What is the nurse's best action?
  • ✓Apply the bandage, because preschoolers have poorly defined body boundaries and fear that all their blood and insides can leak out if the skin is broken
  • BExplain that the bleeding has stopped, so a bandage is unnecessary
  • CApply the bandage, but explain that it is only for decoration
  • DDistract the child, since the request reflects attention seeking
Rationale

Preschoolers "have poorly defined body boundaries and little knowledge of their internal anatomy. Intrusive experiences are frightening, especially those that disrupt the integrity of the skin (e.g., injections and surgery). They fear that all their blood and 'insides' can leak out if the skin is 'broken.' Therefore preschoolers may believe it is critical to use bandages after an injury."

Watch for

(B) is the factually correct and developmentally useless answer, and it is the one an adult reaches for. The child's concern is not bleeding but body integrity, and no explanation of hemostasis touches it.

preschooler body image, fears, and the fear of annihilationgeneral
18. A 4-year-old screams and clings to the side of the bathtub when the drain is opened. Which explanation is correct, and what is a useful intervention?
  • ✓Fear of annihilation, arising from poorly defined body boundaries and the inability to understand concepts of size, so the child cannot grasp that the body is too large to disappear down the drain; letting the child bathe a doll allows the child to observe that large objects cannot go down the drain
  • BOrdinary stranger fear, which resolves without intervention
  • CA sensory processing difficulty requiring referral
  • DDeliberate bath-time resistance that should be ignored
Rationale

Fear of annihilation is "peculiar to this age." Because of "poorly defined body boundaries and improved cognitive abilities," children develop "concerns related to loss of body parts, such as their body going down the drain," and because they "cannot understand concepts of size, they cannot understand that their body is too large to disappear down the drain." The chapter's own remedy is "letting the child bathe a doll so that the child can observe that large objects cannot go down the drain," which reconstructs the experience "without involving the child directly as the victim."

Watch for

(B) names a fear the preschooler has largely outgrown — preschoolers "have overcome much of their anxiety associated with strangers and the fear of separation of earlier years." This age brings the greatest number and variety of fears, but they are different fears.

19. A 5-year-old is terrified of dogs. Which approaches does the chapter support? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Gradually introducing the child to the experience by watching other children play with a dog
  • ✓Actively involving the child in finding practical methods to deal with the frightening experience
  • ✓Having others demonstrate fearlessness, allowing the child to progress at their own rate
  • DPlacing the child's hand on a calm dog, so the child learns there is nothing to fear
  • ✓Keeping a dim night-light in the bedroom, for the child's separate fear of the dark
Rationale

"The best way to help children overcome their fears is by actively involving them in finding practical methods to deal with frightening experiences." Desensitization means "Exposing children to the feared object in a safe situation"; children afraid of dogs "should never be forced to approach or touch one, but they may be gradually introduced to the experience by watching other children play with the animal." Modeling — "having others demonstrate fearlessness" — "can be effective if children are allowed to progress at their own rate." A dim night-light is the chapter's own example for fear of the dark.

Watch for

(D) is the one option from the wrong side of the sort, and it is forced exposure rather than desensitization. It pulls because the contact looks like the logical endpoint of a graded approach; the chapter says such children "should never be forced to approach or touch one."

20. Parents of a 4-year-old are exasperated by the child's many fears and ask whether something is wrong. Which response reflects the chapter?
  • ✓The greatest number and variety of real and imagined fears are present during the preschool years and usually by 5 or 6 years children relinquish many of them; parents should also know that when they demonstrate their own fears, those concerns are communicated to the child and tend to be long-lasting and difficult to dispel
  • BFears at this age indicate an anxiety disorder and warrant referral now
  • CFears peak in the school-age years, so this is early and unusual
  • DParents' own fears have no bearing on a preschooler's fears
Rationale

"The greatest number and variety of real and imagined fears are present during the preschool years," typically of "the dark, being left alone (especially at bedtime), animals (particularly large dogs and snakes), ghosts, sexual matters (castration), and objects or persons associated with pain." "Usually by 5 or 6 years of age, children relinquish many of their fears." And: "When parents demonstrate their fears, these concerns are communicated to the children. Such fears tend to be long-lasting and difficult to dispel."

Watch for

(B) medicalizes a normative peak. Professional help is indicated only "when children experience severe fears that disrupt family life," which this stem does not describe.

22. Four preschoolers in a playroom are all building with blocks in the same area, talking to one another and borrowing pieces, but with no assigned roles, rules, or shared goal. Which type of play is this?
  • AParallel play
  • ✓Associative play — group play in similar or identical activities but without rigid organization or rules
  • CCooperative play, with assigned roles and a shared goal
  • DSolitary play
Rationale

The preschooler's play is associative, defined as "group play in similar or identical activities but without rigid organization or rules." (C) is the next stage, and it pulls because the children are plainly interacting. The absence of rules, organization, and assigned roles is the cue; a stem describing organized, goal-directed team play has moved to the school-age child.

23. A preschool teacher plans a holiday art session for a class of 3-year-olds. Which planning rule does the chapter give?
  • ✓One simple project per year of age — a 3-year-old usually has the patience to decorate three eggs but will become bored and restless with more
  • BAs many projects as the session allows, since preschoolers have high activity levels
  • COne project per session, regardless of age
  • DProjects should be avoided at 3 years, since fine motor skills are insufficient
Rationale

(B) confuses activity level with attention span. Preschool motor activity is high, but this rule is about how many discrete projects a child can see through, which scales with age rather than with energy.

24. Parents of a 4-year-old with an imaginary companion ask for guidance. Which responses reflect the chapter? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓The child's fantasy is a sign of health that helps differentiate between make-believe and reality
  • ✓Imaginary companions usually appear between ages 2½ and 3 years and last until the child enters school
  • ✓Parents may acknowledge the companion by name and agree to simple requests, such as setting an extra place at the table
  • DParents should discourage the companion, since it delays the child's grasp of reality
  • ✓If the child blames the companion for a mess, parents should state clearly that the child is the only person they see and is therefore responsible for cleaning up
Rationale

Parents "should be reassured that the child's fantasy is a sign of health that helps differentiate between make-believe and reality." Imaginary companions "usually appear between ages 2½ and 3 years and, for the most part, last until the child enters school." Parents may acknowledge the companion by name and "even agree to simple requests such as setting an extra place at the table," but "should not allow the child to use the playmate to avoid punishment or responsibility"; if the child blames the companion, "the parents need to clearly state that the child is the only person they see, and therefore the child is responsible for cleaning up."

Watch for

(D) is the one option from the wrong side of the sort. The correct guidance has two halves — acknowledge the companion, and refuse to let it carry the blame — and the common wrong answers supply only one: either bare reassurance with no limit, or the instruction here to discourage the companion altogether.

25. Parents ask the nurse whether their 5-year-old is ready for kindergarten. Which response reflects the chapter?
  • ✓There are no absolute indicators for school readiness; it is influenced by social, emotional, and physical development, health status, ability and desire to learn, life experiences, family environment, and parental support — and the child's social maturation, especially attention span, is as important as academic readiness
  • BA specific readiness test score is the definitive indicator

Rationale

(D) is the metric parents usually apply, and the chapter deliberately balances it against social maturation. (B) contradicts "there are no absolute indicators" outright, and it pulls because a test result feels like the objective answer a nurse ought to be able to give.

26. A school offers both readiness testing and developmental screening. How should the nurse explain the difference?
  • ✓Readiness testing focuses on evaluation of skill acquisition, while developmental screening focuses on the potential to learn and identifies children who may benefit from further diagnostic testing
  • BReadiness testing focuses on the potential to learn, while developmental screening evaluates skill acquisition
  • CThe two terms describe the same process
  • DDevelopmental screening is diagnostic, and readiness testing is a screening tool
Rationale

Readiness testing "focuses on evaluation of skill acquisition"; developmental screening "focuses on the potential to learn," addresses "cognitive (especially language), social, and physical milestones," and identifies "children who may benefit from further diagnostic testing." Use "varies by state and local district," and "there is controversy over the most beneficial form of evaluation."

Watch for

(B) is the straight inversion of the pair, and it is the only thing separating them. The cue words carry the whole distinction: skill belongs to readiness testing, potential belongs to developmental screening.

school readiness and preparing the preschooler for schoolgeneral
27. A parent asks how to handle their 4-year-old's first day of preschool. Which guidance reflects the chapter?
  • ✓Present the idea as exciting and pleasurable beforehand; behave confidently on the day; introduce the child to the teacher and facility; and if remaining for part of the first day, be available to the child but inconspicuous
  • BLeave without saying goodbye, to avoid triggering tears
  • CStay with the child throughout the entire first week, remaining actively involved in the activities
  • DBegin with a full-day schedule, so the child adjusts to the routine immediately
Rationale

Before school, parents "should present the idea as exciting and pleasurable," talking about painting, blocks, swings, and outdoor equipment so children can "fantasize about the forthcoming event in a positive manner." On the first day "parents should behave confidently. Such behavior requires parents to have resolved their own feelings regarding the experience."
