toddler growth parameters and sensory developmentgeneral
1. A healthy 2-year-old weighs 12 kg and measures 86.6 cm. The parent asks what to expect over the next year. Which statement reflects the chapter's growth landmarks?
  • ✓Birth weight is quadrupled by 2½ years old, and adult height is about twice the child's height at 2 years of age
  • BBirth weight is tripled by 2 years old, and adult height is about three times the height at 2 years
  • CBirth weight is quadrupled by 12 months, and growth during toddlerhood accelerates over infancy
  • DWeight gain during toddlerhood averages 4 to 6 kg per year
Rationale

"The birth weight is quadrupled by [2½] years old," and "adult height is about twice the child's height at 2 years of age." Weight gain runs "1.8 to 2.7 kg (4 to 6 lb) per year" and height "7.5 cm (3 inches) per year," mainly in elongation of the legs rather than the trunk. The chapter opens by saying that "Physical growth slows considerably during toddlerhood," and the growth curve should be "steplike rather than linear (straight)."

Watch for

(C) borrows infancy's tripling landmark, moves it earlier, and inverts the chapter's opening statement — growth slows sharply in toddlerhood rather than accelerating. (D) swaps pounds for kilograms in the annual gain, which would be more than double the true figure.

2. A 15-month-old's chest circumference now slightly exceeds head circumference, and the anterior fontanel is no longer palpable. How should the nurse interpret these findings?
  • ✓Both are expected; head circumference equals chest circumference at 1 to 2 years, and the anterior fontanel closes between 12 and 18 months
  • BThe closed anterior fontanel is premature and requires evaluation
  • CChest circumference should not exceed head circumference until 3 years of age
  • DThe anterior fontanel normally closes by 8 weeks, so this closure is late
Rationale

Head circumference is "equal to chest circumference by 1 to 2 years of age," with a total second-year increase of "2.5 cm (1 inch)." The anterior fontanel "closes between 12 and 18 months of age."

Watch for

(D) is the posterior fontanel's timing, which is 6 to 8 weeks; attaching that figure to the anterior fontanel is the commonest error carried forward from the infant chapter, where both numbers are taught together.

3. A 3-year-old's vision screening shows acuity of 20/40, and the nurse notes a persistent inward deviation of one eye. What are the correct interpretations?
  • ✓The acuity is acceptable for the toddler years, but persistent strabismus should receive professional attention as early as possible to prevent amblyopia
  • BBoth findings are normal and require only routine rescreening
  • CThe acuity is abnormal, and the strabismus is an expected finding at this age
  • DThe acuity is acceptable, and strabismus resolves spontaneously by school age
Rationale

"Visual acuity of 20/40 is considered acceptable during the toddler years." Full binocular vision is developed by this stage, and "any evidence of persistent strabismus should receive professional attention as early as possible to prevent amblyopia."

Watch for

(C) reverses both halves, and it pulls because 20/40 sounds impaired by adult standards while a wandering eye looks like something toddlers grow out of. The chapter accepts the first finding and refuses to wait on the second.

toddler gross and fine motor sequencegeneral
4. A 30-month-old climbs stairs placing one foot per step and jumps down from a low step. How should the nurse document this?
  • ADelayed; alternate footing on stairs is expected by 18 months
  • ✓Expected; climbing stairs with alternate footing and jumping from a chair or step appear by the end of the second year into 30 months
  • CAdvanced; alternate footing is not expected until 4 years of age
  • DExpected, but jumping from a step indicates poor safety awareness requiring referral
Rationale

Toddlers walk up and down stairs at 2 years, and by the end of the second year can "stand on one foot, walk on tiptoe, and climb stairs with alternate footing," and jump "from chair or step." They walk alone with "a wide stance for extra balance" at 12 to 13 months, try to run and fall easily at 18 months, and at 2½ years "jump using both feet, stand on one foot for a second or two, and manage a few steps on tiptoe."

Watch for

(A) pulls the skill nearly a year early, and 18 months is a memorable age because it carries running; the two milestones are a full year apart in the book's sequence.

5. A 24-month-old builds a tower of six blocks and imitates a circular stroke with a crayon. Which assessment is correct?
  • ✓Expected for 24 months; a tower of six or seven blocks and imitation of a circular stroke and a vertical line belong to this age
  • BDelayed; a tower of eight or more blocks is expected at 24 months
  • CAdvanced; six blocks is the 30-month milestone
  • DDelayed; copying a circle is expected at 24 months
Rationale

Toddlers build a tower of three or four blocks at 18 months, six or seven at 24 months, and eight or more at 30 months. At 24 months they "imitate a circular stroke and a vertical line"; copying a circle and imitating a cross come at the end of toddlerhood.

Watch for

(D) confuses imitating with copying, which the book separates by roughly a year. Imitation follows a demonstration; copying works from a finished model alone, and it is the harder task.

6. A 2-year-old insists on putting on their own shoes, refuses all help, and shouts "me do" throughout the visit. Which developmental task does this represent?
  • ATrust versus mistrust, with grasping and biting as the social modality
  • ✓Autonomy versus shame and doubt, with holding on and letting go as the newly gained modality
  • CInitiative versus guilt, with intrusion as the social modality
  • DIndustry versus inferiority, expressed through completing tasks
Rationale

"The developmental task of toddlerhood is acquiring a sense of autonomy while overcoming a sense of doubt and shame," and "the toddler has the newly gained modality of holding on and letting go," evident "in how the toddler uses the hands, mouth, eyes, and, eventually, sphincters when toilet training is begun."

Watch for

(A) belongs to infancy and (C) to the preschooler, and the three run in sequence, so they are routinely attached to the wrong age. Note the prerequisite the chapter names: "If the need for basic trust has been satisfied, they are ready to give up dependence for control, independence, and autonomy."

8. A parent asks why a 2-year-old needs firm limits when "he's only little." Which reasoning does the chapter give?
  • ✓Without appropriate limits on acceptable versus unacceptable behavior, children have no guidelines for establishing the end-points of their ability to control
  • BLimits are primarily for the parent's convenience and can be deferred until the preschool years
  • CLimits prevent autonomy from developing, which is what reduces negativism
  • DLimits are needed because toddlers cannot yet experience shame or doubt
Rationale

The toddler's doubt is compounded by shame and by "a fear that they will exceed their own capacity for manipulating the environment. The latter fear is a basis for instituting limit setting and consistent discipline at this age. Without appropriate limits on what is acceptable versus unacceptable behavior, children have no guidelines for establishing the end-points of their ability to control."

Watch for

(C) inverts the purpose entirely: limits are what make autonomy safe to exercise, not what suppresses it. That inversion is why parents who fear stifling a child abandon limits at exactly the age they are most needed.

9. A parent says their 2-year-old answers "no" to everything, including things the child clearly wants, and asks how to stop the defiance. Which response is best?
  • ✓The negativism is not an expression of being stubborn or disrespectful but a necessary assertion of control; reduce the opportunities for a negative answer by stating what will happen rather than asking
  • BConfront each refusal consistently, so the child learns that refusing is unacceptable
  • CGive in to the refusals, since resisting them prolongs the phase
  • DThe behavior indicates a failure of trust during infancy and warrants referral
Rationale

"The negativism is not an expression of being stubborn or disrespectful, but a necessary assertion of control." The technique is "reducing the opportunities for a 'no' answer": rather than asking whether the child wants to go to sleep now, "tell the child when it will be time to go to sleep (preferably within a specific time frame, such as 'after reading a story')." Real choices between two acceptable options work, and "if their response is negative, parents should make the choice for the child."

Watch for

(C) is what many exhausted parents do, and the chapter names the cost: giving in "further threatens the child's search for acceptable methods of interacting with others." (B) turns every interaction into a contest, which is the opposite of reducing the openings for refusal.

10. A 2-year-old admitted to the hospital begins wetting the bed and demanding a bottle, having given up both months ago. Which explanation does the chapter give?
  • ✓Ritualism provides comfort, and without the familiar routines the child has little opportunity to exert autonomy, so dependency and regression occur
  • BThe child has developed a urinary tract infection and the behavior is physiologic
  • CThe child is deliberately manipulating the staff for attention
  • DThe child has permanently regressed to the trust versus mistrust stage
Rationale

Ritualism is "the need to maintain sameness and reliability," which "provides a sense of comfort. Toddlers can venture out with security when they know that familiar people, places, and routines still exist." The chapter draws the conclusion directly: "One can easily understand why change, such as hospitalization, represents such a threat to these children. Without the comfortable rituals, they have little opportunity to exert autonomy. Consequently, dependency and regression occur."

Watch for

(C) treats regression as a strategy, which the chapter contradicts: "the loss of newly acquired achievements is frightening and threatening" to the child rather than rewarding. The management is to ignore the regression while praising existing appropriate behavior.

11. A 16-month-old watches a nurse hide a toy under a blanket, then watches it moved to a second hiding place. The child searches only where it was hidden first. Which stage does this represent?
  • ✓Stage V, tertiary circular reactions, in which the child can find hidden objects but only in the first location
  • BStage VI, in which the child searches for an object through several hiding places
  • CStage IV, in which object permanence has not yet begun
  • DPreoperational thought, in which object permanence is complete
Rationale

Table 12.1 gives stage V, tertiary circular reactions, at 13 to 18 months: "Can find hidden objects, but only in first location," alongside "Active experimentation to achieve previously unattainable goals" and the realization that "'out of sight' is not out of reach; opens doors and drawers to find objects."

Watch for

(B) is the next stage, and the age gap is only a few months, which is what makes the pair swappable. Searching through several hiding places requires awareness of object permanence "regardless of number of invisible displacements," which is stage VI at 19 to 24 months.

12. A 22-month-old watches the same sequence and searches the first hiding place, then the second, then a third location entirely. Which stage does this represent?
  • AStage V, tertiary circular reactions
  • ✓Stage VI, invention of new means through mental combinations, with awareness of object permanence regardless of the number of invisible displacements
  • CStage IV, coordination of secondary schemas
  • DConcrete operational thought
Rationale

Stage VI, at 19 to 24 months, brings "Awareness of object permanence regardless of number of invisible displacements," so the child "Searches for object through several hiding places." Its other listed behaviors are domestic mimicry, referring to self by name, parallel play with awareness of ownership, and concern with a ritualistic, routinized schedule.

Watch for

(A) is the mirror error of the previous item. The discriminator is not whether the child searches but whether the search survives being wrong the first time.

13. A 3-year-old refuses to eat mashed potatoes because they are white, though the child accepts their taste and smell without complaint. Which characteristic of preoperational thought does this illustrate?
  • ✓Centration, focusing on one aspect rather than considering all possible alternatives
  • BTransductive reasoning, reasoning from the particular to the particular
  • CAnimism, attributing lifelike qualities to inanimate objects
  • DIrreversibility, the inability to undo or reverse actions initiated physically
Rationale

Box 12.1 gives this example verbatim under centration: the "Child refuses to eat a food because of its color, even though its taste and smell are acceptable."

Watch for

(B) is the neighbouring food example in the same box and names a different error. A child who "refuses to eat a food because something previously eaten did not taste good" is reasoning from one particular to another. Here nothing previously eaten is at issue; one attribute has crowded out all the rest.

14. A 3-year-old whose bed was moved to the other side of the bedroom refuses to sleep there, and later scolds the stairs for making her fall. Which two characteristics do these behaviors illustrate?
  • ✓Global organization and animism
  • BCentration and magical thinking
  • CEgocentrism and irreversibility
  • DTransductive reasoning and inability to conserve
Rationale

Box 12.1 defines global organization as the "Belief that a change in any one part of the whole changes the entire whole," illustrated when a "Child refuses to sleep in room because location of bed is changed." Animism is "Attributing lifelike qualities to inanimate objects," illustrated when a "Child scolds stairs for making child fall down."

Watch for

(B) is the commonest substitution, because centration also produces a refusal and magical thinking also assigns intent to the world. Centration is about attending to one attribute of a thing; global organization is about the entire whole being spoiled by a single change.

15. A parent, frustrated after a spill, tells their 3-year-old "you are a bad boy." Which concern should the nurse raise?
  • ✓Magical thinking makes the label literal; calling children bad because they did something wrong makes children feel as though they are bad
  • BThe child will not understand the word, so no harm is done
  • CThe label is effective discipline at this age, because moral reasoning is preconventional
  • DThe child will reverse the statement independently, since irreversibility resolves by age 3
Rationale

Box 12.1 gives magical thinking as "Believing that thoughts are all-powerful and can cause events," and its example includes that "Calling children 'bad' because they did something wrong makes children feel as though they are bad." The chapter warns elsewhere that negative labels about the body "are internalized and can last a lifetime."

Watch for

(C) borrows a true fact and misapplies it. Moral development at this age is preconventional, so "whether an action is good or bad depends on whether it results in reward or punishment" — which is a reason to make the consequence of the act clear, not a licence to label the child.

16. A 2-year-old who received a shock from an electrical outlet is now seen poking and inspecting every other outlet in the house, while avoiding the one that shocked him. What does this tell the nurse about safety planning?
  • AThe child has learned the lesson, so the remaining outlets can be left uncovered
  • ✓The child cannot transfer information from one situation to another, which leaves toddlers particularly vulnerable to injury; the hazard must be removed rather than the judgment expected
  • CThe child is deliberately testing the parents' limits and needs a time-out
  • DAvoiding the original outlet proves that memory is fully developed at this stage
Rationale

After such a shock, "they will adamantly poke and inspect every other outlet... This inability to transfer information leaves toddlers particularly vulnerable to injuries. However, traces of memory are evident because they usually avoid the outlet where the shock occurred." The same principle governs containers: a toddler "does not perceive a difference between that toy receptacle and the garbage pail or laundry basket," so "the forbidden object, such as the garbage pail, should be placed out of reach."

Watch for

(A) is exactly the inference the avoided outlet invites, and it is the dangerous one — the memory is specific to that outlet and generalizes to nothing. Expecting toddlers "to judge which receptacles are permissible to explore and which are not is inappropriate for this age-group."

17. A 2-year-old screams and struggles violently during an otoscopic examination and while an axillary temperature is taken. Which explanation is correct?
  • AThe procedures are painful, and analgesia should be offered before repeating them
  • ✓Body integrity is poorly understood in young children and intrusive experiences are threatening; the procedure does not hurt but represents an intrusion into personal space
  • CThe behavior indicates a previous traumatic hospitalization

Rationale

"Body integrity is poorly understood in young children and... intrusive experiences are threatening. 18. During a well-child visit a 2-year-old goes readily to the nurse, shows no distress when the parent leaves the room, and approaches every adult in the hallway. What should the nurse conclude?
  • AThe child is unusually well adjusted and the parent should be praised
  • ✓Indiscriminate friendliness toward strangers and lack of anxiety during separation from parents may be reasons for concern

  • DThis indicates the child has achieved autonomy ahead of schedule
Rationale

"Indiscriminate friendliness toward strangers and lack of anxiety during separation from parents may be reasons for concern." (A) is the reading almost every observer makes, because an easy child looks like a secure one. The chapter reverses it, and the same pattern appears in Chapter 11 as disinhibited social engagement disorder.

20. A toddler in the playroom repeatedly wanders a short distance from the parent and then returns to touch them before wandering off again. Which process does this represent, and what does it require of the parent?
  • ✓Rapprochement, in which the child moves away and returns for reassurance; if the parent's response is inappropriate, the toddler may experience insecurity and confusion
  • BIndividuation, in which the child emerges from symbiotic fusion with the mother
  • CSeparation anxiety, requiring the parent to keep the child physically close at all times
  • DNegativism, requiring the parent to reduce the opportunities for refusal
Rationale

Rapprochement is when "the child moves away and returns for reassurance. If the mother's response to the toddler is inappropriate, the toddler may experience insecurity and confusion." Separation is "the children's emergence from a symbiotic fusion with the mother"; individuation is "those achievements that mark children's assumption of their individual characteristics in the environment."

Watch for

(B) swaps the two definitions — emerging from symbiotic fusion is separation, not individuation. (C) prescribes the infant's remedy for a toddler, where "Verbal and visual reassurance from the parent gradually replaces some of the previous need to be physically close for comfort."

21. A 2-year-old uses about 300 words in two- and three-word sentences, and a visiting grandparent understands roughly two-thirds of what the child says. How should the nurse interpret this?
  • ✓Expected; a 2-year-old has approximately 300 words, and approximately 65% of the speech is understandable
  • BDelayed; a 2-year-old should have about 900 words and be fully intelligible
  • CAdvanced; 300 words is the 3-year milestone
  • DConcerning; intelligibility below 90% at 2 years warrants a speech referral
Rationale

At 1 year the toddler has "about 4" words, with "about 25% of the vocalizations are intelligible." At 2 years there are "approximately 300" words, with "approximately 65% of the speech is understandable." By 3 years the child "acquires five or six new words daily." Throughout, "the ability to understand speech is much greater than the number of words the child can say."

Watch for

(D) applies an adult standard for intelligibility, which is the standard most families apply. The book's expectation at 2 years is roughly two-thirds understandable, which is exactly what this child shows.

22. Two 2-year-olds in a waiting room sit side by side, each absorbed in a separate toy, glancing at each other occasionally but not interacting. A parent worries that the children are not socializing. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓This is parallel play — the toddler plays alongside, not with, other children — and it is the expected progression from the solitary play of infancy
  • BThis is solitary play and indicates delayed social development
  • CThis is associative play and is advanced for the age
  • DToddlers should be engaging in cooperative play by 2 years, so this warrants assessment
Rationale

"The solitary play of infancy progresses to parallel play: the toddler plays alongside, not with, other children." The chapter adds that "Imitation is one of the most distinguishing characteristics of play."

(D) applies a much later stage to a 2-year-old, and it is the reflex worry because side-by-side play looks like a failure to connect. Playing alongside is the developmental achievement here, not a shortfall.

23. Parents of a 20-month-old ask about tablet use and which toys to buy. Which guidance reflects the chapter?
  • ✓Digital media may be introduced between 18 and 24 months but should be viewed together with parents; simple toys that foster creative thinking are preferred over passive battery-operated ones, and the most educational toy is the one that fosters adult-child interaction in supportive, unconditional play
  • BUnlimited screen time is acceptable at this age provided the programming is educational
  • CScreen media of every kind, including video-chatting, are prohibited until age 2
  • DBattery-operated and mechanical toys are preferred because they hold attention longer
Rationale

Screen media are discouraged "other than video-chatting for children younger than 18 months"; children 18 to 24 months "can be introduced to digital media consisting of high-quality programming/apps but should be viewed together with parents." Over age 2, total media time should be limited to "1 hour or less" of quality programming per day. The chapter prefers "simple toys that foster creative thinking (such as blocks, dolls, and clay), rather than passive toys that the child observes," and states that "The most educational toy is the one that fosters the interaction of an adult with a child in supportive, unconditional play. Toys are never substitutes for the attention of devoted caregivers."

Watch for

(C) overstates the under-18-month rule by dropping the video-chatting exception the chapter deliberately carves out, and that exception is the easiest part of the guidance to over-remember away.

24. A parent wants to begin toilet training their 19-month-old, noting that the child now stays dry for a couple of hours. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓Voluntary sphincter control is physiologically possible between 18 and 24 months, but physiologic and psychologic readiness is not complete until 24 to 30 months
  • BSphincter control is not physiologically possible until 3 years of age
  • CReadiness is complete once the child can stay dry for 2 hours, so training should begin immediately
  • DTraining should have begun at 12 months, which is the recommended starting age
Rationale

"Voluntary control of the anal and urethral sphincters is achieved sometime after the child is walking, probably between ages 18 and 24 months," but "complex psychophysiologic factors are required for readiness. The child must be able to recognize the urge to let go and hold on and be able to communicate this sensation to the parent." Physiologic and psychologic readiness "is not complete until ages 24 to 30 months."

Watch for

(C) treats one readiness marker as the whole assessment. Being "dry for at least 2 hours during the day" is one of three markers, alongside awareness of the urge to void or stool and interest in or motivation to use the toilet. (D) quotes the 1920s practice the chapter cites as history, not as recommendation.

25. A nurse assesses a 26-month-old for toilet training readiness. Which findings indicate readiness? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing

The Nursing Care Guidelines assess readiness across four domains. Physical readiness includes the "Ability to stay dry for 2 hours; decreased number of wet diapers; waking dry from nap." (D) is the one option from the wrong side of the sort, and it names two of the exact stressors the parental-readiness domain says must be absent. It pulls because readiness is normally assessed on the child alone, and this chapter makes the family's circumstances part of the criteria.

  • BBladder training is accomplished first because urination is more frequent, giving more practice opportunities

27. A nurse teaches a parent how to conduct toilet training practice sessions. Which instructions are correct?
  • ✓Use a freestanding potty chair so the child can plant the feet firmly on the floor, which facilitates defecation; limit practice sessions to 5 to 8 minutes; and stay with the child
  • BSeat the child on the adult toilet with the feet dangling, and leave the child alone for 20 minutes to concentrate
  • CKeep practice sessions to at least 20 minutes so the child has time to produce a result
  • DDress the child in overalls with back fasteners, so that accidents are less likely
Rationale

A freestanding potty chair "allows children a feeling of security," and "Planting the feet firmly on the floor also facilitates defecation." With a regular toilet, "Placing a small bench under the feet helps stabilize the child's position," or the child may sit facing the toilet tank for added support. "Practice sessions should be limited to 5 to 8 minutes, and a parent should stay with the child." The child should be dressed in easily removed clothing.

Watch for

(D) inverts the clothing guidance for a plausible reason, and it defeats a readiness skill the chapter lists — the "Fine motor skills to remove clothing." (C) more than doubles the session limit, and long sessions are how training turns into a contest of wills.

28. A 4-year-old girl who has been reliably dry during the day still wets the bed most nights, and her parents ask whether something is wrong. Which response is accurate?
  • ✓Nighttime control normally takes several months to years after daytime training begins, because the sleep cycle must mature; bedwetting is normal in girls up to age 4 and in boys up to age 5, and children without nighttime dryness by age 6 are likely to require intervention
  • BNighttime dryness should follow daytime dryness within a few weeks, so evaluation is needed now
  • CBedwetting is normal in both girls and boys until age 8
  • DThe child should be woken every 2 hours through the night until dryness is achieved
Rationale

Nighttime control "normally takes several months to years after daytime training begins... because the sleep cycle needs to mature so the child can awake in time to urinate." "Bedwetting is normal in girls up to age 4 years old and in boys up to age 5 years," and children without nighttime dryness "by the age of 6 years old are likely to require intervention."

Watch for

(B) treats the two as one continuous process, and it is the assumption behind most premature referrals. Daytime dryness is the training target; nighttime dryness waits on the maturation of sleep, which no amount of training accelerates.
