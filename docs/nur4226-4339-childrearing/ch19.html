Select all that apply · scored all or nothing

  • ✓Educate others about the child's condition
  • CPursue every available treatment until a cure is achieved
  • ✓Cope with ongoing stress and periodic crises
  • EEliminate the family's financial burden before discharge
Rationale

Box 19.2 lists eight adaptive tasks from Canam, among them "Meet the developmental needs of other family members," "Educate others about the child's condition," and "Cope with ongoing stress and periodic crises" (p. 592); achieving a cure and eliminating financial burden appear nowhere in the box.

(C) and (E) pull because they sound like what a good parent would want, but the box's tasks are all adaptation to the condition rather than escape from it, and (A) and (B) are the two real members most often dropped from recall because they point away from the ill child.

2. The parents of a child with spina bifida ask the nurse which upcoming events are likely to be hardest on them, so they can prepare. Drawing on the chapter's anticipated stress points, which event should the nurse name?
  • ✓The start of schooling, particularly if placement will not be in a regular class
  • BThe next scheduled surgical revision of the shunt
  • CAn unplanned admission for a urinary tract infection
  • DA relapse requiring a change in the bracing regimen
Rationale

Box 19.3's seven anticipated parental stress points are developmental and life-stage transitions — diagnosis, developmental milestones, start of schooling, reaching the ultimate attainment, adolescence, future placement, and death of the child (p. 592); the box specifically notes that placement outside a regular class is "particularly stressful" (p. 592).

Watch for

(B), (C), and (D) are all medical events, and medical events are not on this list even though the chapter discusses all of them elsewhere; the reflex is to equate stress with acute illness rather than with transition.

3. A nurse is comparing two families. The first cares for a ventilator-dependent child but has extended family nearby, stable income, and a strong marriage. The second cares for a child with well-controlled asthma but is a single parent working two jobs with no nearby relatives. Which family should the nurse identify as being at greater risk for poor adjustment?
  • ✓The second family, because the functional burden weighs demands against the family's resources
  • BThe first family, because the severity of the condition predicts the level of adjustment
  • CThe first family, because technology dependence always exceeds a family's coping capacity
  • DNeither, because adjustment cannot be predicted before the family is assessed in the home
Rationale

The chapter states directly that "The level of adjustment is significantly influenced by the functional burden on the family" and gives essentially this comparison, noting that a high-technology family with many resources "may adjust more successfully" than a family with "a less serious condition and few resources to counterbalance" (p. 596).

Watch for

(B) is the intuitive answer and the one the chapter names and rejects in the same sentence — "it is easy to assume that families of children with the most severe illnesses or disabilities would have the poorest adjustment" (p. 596); severity is one part of the picture, not the predictor.

4. The 9-year-old sister of a child with a chronic condition tells her mother she does not want her brother coming along to her friend's sleepover. The mother asks the nurse whether she should insist. What should the nurse advise?
  • ✓Respect the sister's reluctance to include her brother in the activity
  • BRequire the sister to include her brother so she does not learn to exclude him
  • CExplain to the sister that her brother's condition means he needs her more than her friends do
  • DCancel the sleepover so that neither child feels singled out
Rationale

Box 19.4 lists "Respect a sibling's reluctance to be with or to include the child with special needs in activities" among the ways to promote healthy sibling relationships (p. 593), alongside the instruction to let siblings settle their own differences.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex answer because forcing inclusion looks like fairness, but this box repeatedly protects the well sibling's own life; (C) compounds the error by making the sister responsible for her brother's needs, which the same box caps by telling parents to "Limit caregiving responsibilities" (p. 593).

  • ✓An approach behavior indicating movement toward adjustment
  • BAn avoidance behavior indicating maladaptation to the crisis

6. At the same visit, the mother of a different child with cystic fibrosis describes in detail the college she has already chosen for her daughter, but cannot say what the current treatment plan is. The nurse should interpret this as:

The second has stopped giving the prescribed enzymes, saying that healing is in God's hands now. How should the nurse classify these two statements?

  • BBoth are approach behaviors, because religion sustains hope in this chapter

8. A nurse observes three avoidance behaviors in a parent during one admission and is deciding whether to document the family as being at risk. What should most influence that judgment?
  • ✓The family's available supports and resources
  • BThe total number of avoidance behaviors observed
  • CWhether any approach behaviors were also observed
  • DHow long the child has carried the diagnosis
Rationale

The chapter refuses to let avoidance behaviors be scored by counting, stating that "several avoidance behaviors in an emotionally healthy family may denote significantly less risk ... than an equal number of avoidance behaviors in an individual who has few available supports" (p. 594).

(B) is the reflex because a checklist invites tallying, and the chapter anticipates exactly that; the same count means different things in a supported family and an unsupported one.

10. The mother of a 6-year-old with a seizure disorder carries him to the bathroom although he can walk, has never disciplined him, and says she cannot bear to see him frustrated. Which parental reaction does this describe?
  • ✓Overprotection
  • BRejection
  • CDenial
  • DGradual acceptance
Rationale

The chapter defines overprotection as parents who "fear letting the child achieve any new skill, avoid all discipline, and cater to every desire to prevent frustration" (p. 595).

Watch for

(D) is the near miss, because both overprotection and gradual acceptance involve a parent structuring the child's life; the discriminator is the single word realistic, since gradual acceptance places "necessary and realistic restrictions" while overprotection places none.

11. A different mother of a 6-year-old with a seizure disorder expects him to dress himself and carry his plate to the sink, gives him the same bedtime as his sister, and has enrolled him in a swim class with a helmet policy. Which parental reaction does this describe?
  • ✓Gradual acceptance
  • BOverprotection
  • CDenial
  • DRejection
Rationale

Gradual acceptance is defined as parents who "place necessary and realistic restrictions on the child, encourage self-care activities, and promote reasonable physical and social abilities" (p. 595), and it is the only one of the four reactions the chapter treats as adaptive.

Watch for

(C) is the mirror error of the previous item — a reader who has just keyed overprotection looks for the opposite extreme and lands on denial, reading the ordinary expectations as a parent acting "as if the disorder does not exist" (p. 595); the helmet policy is what rules that out.

12. A father brings his daughter to every appointment, keeps her immaculately groomed, and manages her tube feedings without error, but does not speak to her during the visit and criticizes her sharply when she drops a toy. The nurse should recognize this pattern as:
  • ✓Rejection, which may coexist with adequate physical care

  • CDenial, because the father is avoiding emotional acknowledgment of the disorder

The chapter defines rejection as parents who "detach themselves emotionally from the child but usually provide adequate physical care or constantly nag and scold the child" (p. 595) — and this father shows both faces the definition names.

16. The mother of a child with a complex chronic condition tells the nurse she has stopped giving her son chores and no longer disciplines him, because it seems unfair to hold him to the same standard as his sisters. Which response is best?

Expectation directs the parent to "Apply the same family rules to the child with a complex chronic illness as to the well siblings or peers," with the child "disciplined ... expected to fulfill household responsibilities, and attend school in accordance with abilities" (p. 601); the chapter adds that "Resentment and hostility can arise among siblings if different standards are applied to each child" (p. 604).

23. While the physician explains the diagnosis, a mother begins to cry. The nurse wants to convey support. Which action is most consistent with the chapter's guidance?
  • ✓Have tissues available and let the mother continue expressing her feelings
  • BPlace a hand on the mother's shoulder and tell her everything will be all right
  • COffer to step out so the parents can have privacy with their emotions
  • DSuggest the session be rescheduled until the mother is composed
Rationale

The chapter directs that emotional support be offered "by having tissues available if a family member cries and demonstrating through facial and body language that indeed this is a difficult and painful period" (p. 599), and warns that touch "can prematurely terminate free expression of feelings, especially when combined with statements, such as" everything will be all right (p. 599).

(B) is the single most reflexive comforting gesture in nursing and this chapter names precisely that combination as the thing that shuts a parent down; touch is not automatically therapeutic here, and the chapter also directs awareness of cultural issues regarding touching.

  • CThe parents are given time alone to absorb what they have heard

25. The parents of a child newly diagnosed with a life-threatening illness ask when palliative care would begin. What should the nurse tell them?
  • ✓It begins at diagnosis and continues regardless of whether the child receives treatment directed at the disease
  • BIt begins when curative treatment has been exhausted
  • CIt begins when the child's life expectancy is judged to be under 6 months
  • DIt begins when the family consents to a do-not-attempt-resuscitation order
Rationale

Both definitions the chapter gives start at diagnosis — palliative care runs "from diagnosis through death," and the World Health Organization definition states "It begins when illness is diagnosed, and continues regardless of whether or not a child receives treatment directed at the disease" (p. 606).
