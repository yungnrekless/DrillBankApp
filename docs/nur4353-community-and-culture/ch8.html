exposure pathwaythe five-element chain
1. For an environmental exposure to result in harm, which five elements must all be in place and connected?
  • AAgent, host, environment, dose, and route
  • ✓Source, environmental medium, receptor population, route of exposure, and dose
  • CToxicology, epidemiology, risk assessment, risk communication, and monitoring
  • DPoint source, nonpoint source, medium, host, and standard
Rationale

The five-element exposure pathway is a source of harm, an environmental medium for transport, a receptor population, a route of exposure, and an adequate dose. All five must be present and connected for harm to occur.

Watch for

Option A recycles the epidemiological triangle (agent, host, environment) plus two extra terms — a plausible-sounding but different list from the exposure pathway.

2. A community health nurse wants to interrupt an ongoing environmental exposure. According to the chapter, what is required?
  • AAll five links in the exposure pathway must be addressed simultaneously
  • ✓Breaking any single link in the chain interrupts the harm
  • COnly the source and the dose need to be addressed; the other three are administrative
  • DThe receptor population must be relocated before any other action is effective
Rationale

Breaking any one of the five links — removing the source, blocking the medium, protecting the receptor, cutting off the route, or reducing the dose — interrupts the harm. This is the conceptual basis for nursing intervention.

Watch for

An option implying all five links must be addressed at once misses the point of the chain concept.

10. A nurse is assessing a home built in 1965 with old, well-maintained, fully intact lead-based paint on the walls. What is the most accurate statement about the risk?

  • ✓Intact lead poses no risk; the danger arises when it flakes or turns to dust
  • CThere is no risk because the home predates 1978
  • DThe risk is limited to drinking water in homes of this age
Rationale

Intact lead poses no risk; the risk comes when it flakes or turns to dust on surfaces or soil. Homes built before 1978 (when lead was banned in household paint) are the flag for risk, particularly when the paint is disturbed, as during renovation.

36. How does the chapter define environmental justice (environmental equity)?
  • AThe principle that all populations are exposed to environmental hazards equally
  • ✓The framework addressing the fact that certain environmental health risks disproportionately affect poor people and people of color
  • CA legal requirement that all industrial permits be reviewed by a community board
  • DA federal program that relocates residents away from hazardous waste sites
Rationale

Environmental justice, also called environmental equity, addresses the fact that certain environmental health risks disproportionately affect poor people and people of color. It is grounded in the Environmental Justice Act (1993) and Executive Order 12898 (1994).

Watch for

Framing environmental justice as "everyone is exposed equally" directly contradicts the chapter's point about disproportionate burden.
