US health care systemprimary care vs. primary health care
1. A nurse is asked to distinguish "primary care" from "primary health care" for a class of new students. Which statement correctly captures the difference?
  • APrimary care is population-focused; primary health care is individual-focused
  • ✓Primary care is an individual's first point of contact with the health system; primary health care is essential care made universally accessible to a community with its full participation
  • CThe two terms are interchangeable synonyms for the same delivery model
  • DPrimary care refers to hospital-based care; primary health care refers to outpatient care
Rationale

Primary care is the individual's first point of contact with the health system, provided by physicians, NPs, CNSs, PAs, and CNMs. Primary health care (PHC) is essential health care made universally accessible to individuals and families in the community, with their full participation, at an affordable cost — a population/community-level model.

Watch for

The two terms are one word apart and are the most commonly swapped pair on this blueprint line. If the stem says "individual" or "first contact," it is primary care; if it says "community," "universal access," or "participation," it is primary health care.

2. Which model of care traces its origin to the Declaration of Alma-Ata (1978)?
  • APrimary care
  • ✓Primary health care
  • CManaged care
  • DPublic health surveillance
Rationale

Primary health care emerged from the 1978 WHO Declaration of Alma-Ata, which stated that health is a human right and introduced the PHC model along with the role of community health workers and traditional healers.

Watch for

Primary care is a clinical delivery model with no Alma-Ata origin. Any stem naming Alma-Ata is pointing to primary health care.

3. A community health nurse works with local residents to design and deliver essential health services that are affordable and universally accessible, with the community's full participation in identifying its own priorities. This best exemplifies which concept?
  • APrimary care
  • ✓Primary health care
  • CManaged care
  • DPublic health assurance
Rationale

This scenario describes primary health care: essential care made universally accessible to individuals and families in the community, with their full participation, at an affordable cost — the Alma-Ata model.

Watch for

"Community participation" and "universal access" are the scenario's tell. A first-contact clinic visit would be primary care instead.

8. Within US health care spending, which statement correctly ranks the shares of expenditure?
  • ✓The largest share goes to acute care, with physician services next; public health receives the smallest share
  • BThe largest share goes to public health, with acute care next
  • CPhysician services receive the largest share, followed by prescription drugs
  • DAcute care and public health receive roughly equal shares
Rationale

The largest share of expenditures goes to acute care, with physician services next; public health receives the smallest share. Other cost drivers include prescription drugs, technology, chronic/degenerative disease care, and the aging baby-boomer population swelling Medicare enrollment.

Watch for

Public health is consistently the smallest slice of the pie — a distractor that puts public health spending on par with or above acute care inverts this.

9. The US access problem is often described as a "two-class system." Which group is specifically identified as falling into the gap between the two classes?
  • AMedicare beneficiaries
  • ✓The working poor, who earn too much for public funds but cannot afford private insurance
  • CUndocumented immigrants only
  • DRetirees on fixed incomes
Rationale

The US is described as a two-class system — private and public. Those with insurance or ability to pay get superior care, while the working poor (who earn too much for public funds but can't afford insurance) and undocumented immigrants get lower-quality care.

Watch for

Undocumented immigrants are also named as falling into the lower-quality-care group, but the working poor are the group defined specifically by being caught between the two classes on the basis of income.

10. How did the number of uninsured Americans change between 2012 and 2016?
  • AIt rose from 27.6 million to 48 million
  • ✓It fell from 48 million (pre-ACA) to 27.6 million
  • CIt remained essentially unchanged at approximately 40 million
  • DIt fell to zero following full ACA implementation
Rationale

Uninsured Americans fell from 48 million in 2012 (pre-ACA) to 27.6 million in 2016. The uninsured receive less preventive care, are diagnosed at more advanced stages, and receive less treatment.

16. At the state level, which statement correctly describes the state health department's role and authority?
  • AStates administer Medicare and Medicaid directly, independent of CMS
  • ✓State health departments handle licensing, health statistics, and communicable disease control, and hold police power to protect public health
  • CState health departments deliver most direct clinical services, bypassing local health departments
  • DStates are the level at which USPHS agencies are organized
Rationale

State health departments handle licensing, health statistics, communicable disease control, and coordinate local health; states hold police power to protect public health. The local health department is where services actually reach the community, with functions varying widely by locality.

Watch for

Direct service delivery to the community is a local, not state, function — states coordinate and regulate, localities deliver. ---

18. How do the Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) and Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) compare?
  • AThe MDGs (17 goals, 2000–2015) were succeeded by the SDGs (8 goals, 2015–), with SDG 3 focused on health
  • ✓The MDGs (8 goals, 2000–2015) were succeeded by the SDGs (17 goals, 2015–); SDG 3 is Good Health and Well-Being
  • CThe MDGs and SDGs ran concurrently, each covering different UN member states
  • DThe SDGs (8 goals) preceded the MDGs (17 goals)
Rationale

The Millennium Development Goals were 8 UN goals spanning 2000–2015. The Sustainable Development Goals succeeded them with 17 goals from 2015 onward; SDG 3 is Good Health and Well-Being.

Watch for

The goal counts are easy to swap — 8 belongs to the MDGs, 17 to the SDGs, and the SDGs came after, not before.
