1. How does the ANA/NASN (2017) definition characterize school nursing?
  • ✓A specialty practice focused on providing health care and illness prevention to school-age children, with the goal of facilitating their participation in educational opportunities

School nursing is defined as a specialty practice focused on providing health care and illness prevention to school-age children, with the goal of facilitating their participation in educational opportunities (ANA & NASN, 2017).

school nursing foundationsdefinition and scope
2. School nursing is best described as which of the following?
  • ✓Population focused, serving children, families, staff, and teachers in the school
  • BFocused solely on individual students with chronic illness
  • CLimited to students enrolled in special education programs
  • DA service that ends once a child reaches adolescence
Rationale

School nursing is population focused: the population is all children, families, staff, and teachers in the school setting, not only individual students.

federal legislationIEP vs. IHP
12. Which statement correctly distinguishes the IEP from the IHP?
  • AThe IEP addresses health care coordination; the IHP addresses classroom accommodations
  • ✓The IEP addresses educational accommodations; the IHP addresses health care needs
  • CThe IEP and IHP are two names for the same document
  • DThe IHP is required only for children over age 18

IEP = educational accommodations; IHP = health care needs met in balance with the school experience. IDEA requires both for children with special needs.

federal legislationFERPA, HIPAA, and confidentiality
13. In the school setting, a student's health record is protected primarily under which law?
  • AHIPAA
  • ✓FERPA
  • CThe ADA
  • DIDEIA
Rationale

FERPA protects the privacy of student education records, and in schools, student health records fall under FERPA's protection. HIPAA protects health information generally but is not the operative law for school health records.

Watch for

HIPAA is the plausible distractor because it is the health-information law students recognize, but FERPA is the one that governs records in schools.

federal legislationFERPA, HIPAA, and confidentiality
14. A teacher, without written authorization, asks the school nurse to see a student's confidential health record to better understand the student's needs. What should the nurse do?
  • AShare the record, since teachers are part of the educational team
  • BShare only a summary, since teachers have a legitimate educational interest
  • ✓Decline to release the record; protected information cannot be released to anyone, including teachers or other health personnel, without authorization
  • DShare the record only if the student's parent is unreachable
Rationale

The school nurse must maintain confidentiality. Protected information cannot be released to anyone, including teachers or other health personnel, without authorization — this is the FERPA principle in practice.

Watch for

"Teachers have a legitimate educational interest" sounds reasonable but is not how the chapter frames the confidentiality rule; authorization is required regardless of who is asking. ---

levels of preventionapplying the three levels in schools
18. Which level of prevention represents the largest responsibility for the school nurse?
  • APrimary prevention
  • ✓Secondary prevention
  • CTertiary prevention
  • DQuaternary prevention
Rationale

Secondary prevention — early intervention for children who need health care, including caring for ill or injured students and employees, screening and assessing children, and referral to appropriate health agencies — is the largest responsibility for the school nurse.

Watch for

A stem asking which level occupies most of the school nurse's work is testing this fact specifically; primary (education/vaccination) and tertiary (managing existing conditions) are the distractors.

19. A school nurse conducts vision and hearing screenings and refers a student with a suspected hearing deficit to an audiologist. This is an example of which level of prevention?
  • APrimary
  • ✓Secondary
  • CTertiary
  • DHealth promotion, not a level of prevention

Secondary prevention includes screening and assessing children and referring them to appropriate health agencies.
