federal disaster frameworkFEMA's role and the escalation ladder
13. Place the disaster response escalation ladder in the correct order, from where response first begins to the point at which the National Response Framework may take effect.
  • AState → county/city → local → federal
  • ✓Local → county/city → state → federal
  • CFederal → state → county/city → local
  • DCounty/city → local → federal → state
Rationale

The first level of response occurs at the local level (fire, law enforcement, public health, emergency services). If needs exceed local resources, the county or city emergency management agency coordinates through an emergency operations center. When state resources are overwhelmed, governors may request federal assistance under a presidential declaration, and if the event is an incident of national significance, the NRF may take effect.

16. Under disaster triage, which criteria must BOTH be met for a victim to receive the highest priority?
  • AA life-threatening injury alone, regardless of prognosis
  • ✓A life-threatening injury and a high probability of survival once stabilized
  • CAny injury requiring more than 60 minutes of care
  • DBeing the first casualty found at the scene
Rationale

Highest priority requires both a life-threatening injury and a high probability of survival once stabilized. This survival clause is what distinguishes disaster triage from ED triage.

23. First responders at a disaster scene work for hours on end without rest, refuse breaks, and fight needed sleep. Which community stress phase does this describe, and what is the associated danger?

  • ✓Heroic phase; exhausted, overworked responders present a danger to themselves and the community served
  • CDisillusionment phase; the danger is despair among survivors

The heroic phase involves an overwhelming need to help others survive; responders work without thought of their own needs and refuse rest breaks. 31. When making a family disaster plan, how many meeting places should a family choose, and what is required of the emergency contact person?
  • AOne meeting place inside the home; the contact person should be a trusted neighbor
  • ✓Two meeting places — one right outside the home and one outside the neighborhood; the contact person should be out of the area
  • CThree meeting places spread across the community; the contact person should be a local relative
  • DOne meeting place outside the neighborhood; no contact person is needed if cell phones are charged
Rationale

Families should choose two meeting places — one right outside the home for a sudden emergency such as a fire, and another outside the neighborhood in case they cannot return home or must evacuate. The contact person should be out of the area, because it may be easier to call long distance or text if local phone lines are overloaded.

Watch for

"A trusted neighbor" is a plausible-sounding but incorrect substitute for an out-of-area contact — the rationale is specifically that local lines may be overloaded while long-distance lines still work.

responsethe first goal of response and biological incidents
36. In a biological incident where an agent has been released without warning, who does the chapter identify as effectively "first on the scene," and why?
  • AFire and EMS personnel, because they arrive within minutes of any incident
  • BLaw enforcement, because they secure the area before health responders enter
  • ✓Health care professionals, because symptoms may not appear for days or weeks after exposure, by which time victims have already sought a health evaluation
  • DPublic health surveillance systems, because they detect agents in real time
Rationale

An unannounced biological release may go unnoticed, and victims may have left the area of exposure long before the act is recognized. The first signs may not be apparent for days or weeks, until victims become ill and seek a health evaluation — in this case, health care professionals are considered the "first on the scene."

Watch for

This inverts the usual assumption that fire or EMS are always first on scene; for biological incidents specifically, delayed symptom onset shifts that role to health care professionals encountering ill patients later.
