payment mechanismsretrospective vs. prospective reimbursement (organizations)
1. A home health agency is paid based on its cost per patient-day, with the rate determined only after services have already been delivered. Which reimbursement method is this?
  • AProspective payment system
  • ✓Retrospective reimbursement
  • CCapitation
  • DPreferred provider arrangement
Rationale

Retrospective reimbursement is the traditional method of paying organizations: fees are set after services are delivered, based on cost or charges. It encourages cost shifting and shows little regard for cost.

Watch for

The "per patient-day" detail sounds like a fixed rate, but what makes it retrospective is that the rate is not fixed until after the service is rendered.

payment mechanismsretrospective vs. prospective reimbursement (organizations)
2. What is the central risk created by retrospective reimbursement of organizations?
  • AUnder-serving patients to stay within a fixed budget
  • ✓Cost shifting — charging one payer more to cover losses incurred elsewhere
  • CDenying care to patients who cannot pay a capitated fee
  • DRestricting referrals through a gatekeeper
Rationale

Because retrospective reimbursement pays organizations on cost or charges after the fact, it encourages inflating prices and cost shifting rather than cost control.

Watch for

Under-serving to stay in budget is the risk of prospective payment, the opposite method — an easy inversion.

payment mechanismsretrospective vs. prospective reimbursement (organizations)
3. A hospital is paid a fixed amount per diagnosis category, determined in advance, regardless of the hospital's actual cost of care. Which method is this, and what risk does it carry?
  • ARetrospective reimbursement; risk of cost shifting
  • ✓Prospective payment system; risk of under-serving and compromised quality
  • CFee-for-service; risk of inflated billing
  • DCapitation; risk of denied referrals
Rationale

Prospective payment systems set the reimbursement rate before care is delivered. Medicare's hospital PPS pays a fixed amount per diagnosis category (DRG) regardless of actual cost, which encourages staying within budget but risks under-serving patients and compromising quality.

payment mechanismsretrospective vs. prospective reimbursement (organizations)
4. Which classification system is the basis of Medicare's prospective payment system for hospitals, and when was it established?
  • ARBRVS, established 1991
  • ✓DRGs, established 1983
  • CCPT codes, established 1965
  • DUCR schedules, established 1997
Rationale

Diagnosis-related groups (DRGs) classify hospital payment into set amounts per diagnosis category, established by the amended Social Security Act (PL 98-21) in 1983 — Medicare's prospective payment method for hospitals.

Watch for

RBRVS is a real Medicare payment reform, but it applies to physician reimbursement (1989/1991), not to the hospital PPS.

payment mechanismspaying practitioners (fee-for-service, preferred provider, capitation)
5. Under fee-for-service payment, when is the rate for the service determined, and on what basis?
  • ABefore care is delivered, based on a negotiated discount
  • ✓After the practitioner delivers the service, based on usual, customary, and reasonable (UCR) charges the practitioner sets
  • CBefore care is delivered, based on a preset per-member fee
  • DAfter care is delivered, based on a diagnosis-related group
Rationale

Fee-for-service is the traditional method of paying practitioners: the practitioner sets the cost, delivers the service, and then bills a third-party payer based on UCR charges. It is retrospective in nature.

payment mechanismspaying practitioners (fee-for-service, preferred provider, capitation)
6. A managed care organization pays a primary care practitioner a fixed amount per enrolled member each month, regardless of how many services that member actually uses. Which payment method is this?
  • AFee-for-service
  • BPreferred provider arrangement
  • ✓Capitation
  • DRetrospective reimbursement
Rationale

Capitation pays a practitioner a preset, negotiated fee per member (per member per month), agreed before services are delivered, regardless of the volume of care used. It is typically used by managed care organizations.

payment mechanismspaying practitioners (fee-for-service, preferred provider, capitation)
7. In a preferred provider arrangement, when is the practitioner's reimbursement rate set relative to care delivery?
  • AAfter care is delivered, based on the practitioner's own charges
  • ✓Before care is delivered, negotiated by the third-party payer, with reimbursement capped per patient
  • CBefore care is delivered, but only as a per-member-per-month fee
  • DThere is no set rate; payment is fully at the payer's discretion
Rationale

Preferred provider payment, like prospective payment, has the third-party payer negotiate the rate before care is delivered and cap reimbursement per patient.

9. The resource-based relative value scale (RBRVS) was established by which legislation, and when did Medicare adopt it?
  • AThe Balanced Budget Act of 1997; adopted 1998
  • ✓The Omnibus Reconciliation Act of 1989; adopted by Medicare in 1991
  • CThe Social Security Act of 1965; adopted 1966
  • DThe amended Social Security Act (PL 98-21); adopted 1983
Rationale

RBRVS was established by the Omnibus Reconciliation Act of 1989 and adopted by Medicare in 1991; it reimburses physicians based on the service provided and the resources required.

Watch for

PL 98-21 and 1983 belong to DRGs, not RBRVS — the two prospective-payment reforms are easy to cross-assign.

payment mechanismspaying practitioners (fee-for-service, preferred provider, capitation)
10. The Balanced Budget Act of 1997 changed reimbursement for nurse practitioners and clinical nurse specialists in what way?
  • AIt granted them full parity with physician rates under Medicare Part A
  • ✓It granted them third-party reimbursement under Medicare Part B at 85% of physician rates for the same service
  • CIt capitated all NP/CNS services under Medicaid
  • DIt excluded NPs and CNSs from Medicare reimbursement entirely
Rationale

Effective January 1, 1998, the Balanced Budget Act of 1997 granted NPs and CNSs third-party reimbursement for Medicare Part B services at 85% of physician rates for the same service.

Watch for

Part B (medical/provider services), not Part A (hospital insurance), is where this reimbursement applies. ---

public insurance programsMedicare vs. Medicaid
11. A 68-year-old with no history of disability is asking who pays for her hospitalization. Which federal program covers her, and under what title of the Social Security Act?
  • AMedicaid, Title XIX
  • ✓Medicare, Title XVIII
  • CCHIP, Balanced Budget Act of 1997
  • DMedicaid, Title XVIII
Rationale

Medicare (Title XVIII, Social Security Act of 1965) is federal insurance for people 65 and older, the permanently disabled, and those with ALS or end-stage renal disease (ESRD). Age alone, without a means test, qualifies her.

public insurance programsMedicare vs. Medicaid
12. A low-income family with dependent children needs coverage but no member is 65 or disabled. Which program applies, and what determines eligibility?
  • AMedicare, based on age
  • ✓Medicaid, based on means testing (income and assets)
  • CCHIP, based on Social Security Act Title XVIII
  • DMedicare Part C, based on income
Rationale

Medicaid (Title XIX, Social Security Act of 1965) is joint federal/state social insurance for the poor, aged, blind, disabled, and families with dependent children, with eligibility determined by means testing.

Watch for

The chapter's own trap: if the stem says "65-year-old," think Medicare; if it says "low-income family" or "needy," think Medicaid. Age/disability-based and means-tested are not interchangeable qualifying criteria.

13. Which statements correctly describe Medicare? Select all that apply.

14. Which statements correctly describe Medicaid? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing

Medicaid is a joint federal/state social insurance program financed by federal and state general revenues, with means-tested eligibility, and it pays for more than half of all long-term care spending.

Medicaid is classified as social insurance (assistance), not straight "insurance" — that classification belongs to Medicare.

19. A nursing student sees "CHIP" referenced in both a community health economics context and an earlier chapter on the community-as-client model. Which statement correctly distinguishes the two uses?

managed careHMO vs. PPO
20. A managed care enrollee must select a primary care provider who authorizes referrals to specialists, and the practice is paid a preset amount per enrolled member. Which model is this?
  • APreferred provider organization (PPO)
  • ✓Health maintenance organization (HMO)
  • CFee-for-service indemnity plan
  • DProspective payment system
Rationale

HMOs are prepaid plans in which enrollees use a defined network and typically need a primary care gatekeeper for referrals; they emphasize prevention and are often paid via capitation.

managed careHMO vs. PPO
21. An enrollee can see any provider in a network of "preferred providers" who accept negotiated, discounted rates, and can go out-of-network at a higher cost, without needing a referral. Which model is this?
  • AHealth maintenance organization (HMO)
  • ✓Preferred provider organization (PPO)
  • CCapitation-only plan
  • DDiagnosis-related group plan
Rationale

A PPO is a network of preferred providers who accept negotiated discounted rates; enrollees may go out of network at higher cost, and there is no strict gatekeeper.

managed careHMO vs. PPO
22. Which of the following correctly distinguishes HMOs from PPOs? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓HMOs typically require a primary care gatekeeper for referrals; PPOs do not
  • ✓HMOs are commonly paid via capitation; PPOs rely on negotiated discounted rates
  • ✓PPOs offer more provider choice and flexibility than HMOs, generally at higher cost
  • DHMOs allow unrestricted out-of-network care at the same cost as in-network care

Rationale

HMOs use a gatekeeper model with capitated payment and the tightest network; PPOs offer looser networks with negotiated discounts, more choice, and higher cost, but no strict gatekeeper. Both fall under managed care.

Unrestricted out-of-network access at no added cost describes neither model — even PPOs charge more for out-of-network care.

access and disparitiesthe uninsured and the working poor
27. More than 80% of the uninsured belong to which group, and why do they remain uninsured?
  • ARetired seniors who are not yet enrolled in Medicare
  • ✓Working families — the "working poor" — who earn too much for Medicaid but cannot afford private coverage
  • CUndocumented immigrants ineligible for any federal program
  • DCollege students who opted out of parental coverage
Rationale

More than 80% of the uninsured were in working families (income below 400% of poverty), most with one or more full-time workers — the "working poor," who earn too much to qualify for Medicaid but cannot afford private insurance.

Watch for

This is the same earn-too-much-for-Medicaid gap that CHIP is designed to close for children — the adult working-poor population has no equivalent program named in this chapter.
