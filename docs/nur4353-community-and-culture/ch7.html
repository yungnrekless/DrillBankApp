  • ARace and ethnicity are always identical for members of the same racial group
  • ✓Individuals may be of the same race but of different cultures
  • CCulture is a purely biological concept
  • DEthnicity determines race

The chapter states explicitly that individuals may be of the same race but of different cultures, using African Americans — who may trace heritage to Africa, the Caribbean, North America, or elsewhere — as a heterogeneous group that should not be viewed as culturally and racially homogeneous.

culture, race, and ethnicitythe three-way distinction
2. Which definition matches "culture" as the chapter defines it?
  • AA biological variation within population groups based on physical markers derived from genetic ancestry
  • ✓A set of beliefs, values, and assumptions about life that is widely held among a group of people and transmitted across generations
  • CThe shared feeling of peoplehood among a group of individuals
  • DThe degree of variation represented among populations based on lifestyle, ethnicity, and race
Rationale

Culture is a set of beliefs, values, and assumptions about life widely held among a group and transmitted across generations (Leininger). Option A is race, C is ethnicity, and D is cultural diversity.

Watch for

All four terms — culture, race, ethnicity, cultural diversity — are commonly confused. Race is biological and not learned; ethnicity is about shared identity/peoplehood; culture is the learned, transmitted set of beliefs and values.

5. A nurse instructs Asian American patients on taking antituberculin medication. The patients smilingly respond "yes, yes," and the nurse assumes they understand and will comply. A week later the medications have not been taken. What is the best explanation and correction, per the chapter's worked scenario?
  • AThe patients were non-adherent due to low health literacy; the nurse should simplify the vocabulary used
  • ✓Acceptance and avoidance of confrontation with authority figures are valued in Asian American culture; the nurse should have the patients repeat the instructions back and discuss the cultural meaning of TB
  • CThe patients did not speak English well enough to understand; the nurse should have used a translated pamphlet only
  • DThe patients were testing the nurse's authority; the nurse should escalate to a supervisor

6. A nurse notices that a patient from a certain cultural group appears uncomfortable when the nurse stands close during an exam, while a patient from a different cultural group appears offended when the nurse maintains distance. What does the chapter say this illustrates?
  • ✓Most cultural groups have spatial preferences, and the nurse may offend the patient by using a distance that conflicts with the patient's cultural comfort zone
  • BPersonal space preferences are identical across cultures and any discomfort reflects individual anxiety
  • CSpatial preference is dictated entirely by the patient's socioeconomic status
  • DNurses should always maintain at least a 9-inch distance regardless of culture
Rationale

Personal space is the physical area individuals need to feel comfortable, and most cultural groups have spatial preferences — one community may be comfortable at a 9-inch distance while another finds that same distance threatening and overly aggressive.

33. A nurse believes her own cultural standards are the correct measure by which all other groups' behavior should be judged, but she has not acted on this belief in patient care. Which term applies?
  • ACultural imposition
  • ✓Ethnocentrism
  • CRacism
  • DStereotyping
Rationale

Ethnocentrism is a type of cultural prejudice at the population level — the belief that one's own group determines the standards for behavior by which all other groups are to be judged. It remains a belief until acted upon.

Watch for

If the nurse instead overrode the patient's values and forced her own standards into the plan of care, that added ACT would make it cultural imposition, not ethnocentrism. Belief alone = ethnocentrism; belief plus the act of imposing = cultural imposition.
