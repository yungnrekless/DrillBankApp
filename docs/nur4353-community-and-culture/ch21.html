2. What is health risk appraisal?
  • AAssigning a diagnosis based on presenting symptoms
  • ✓The process of assessing for the presence of specific factors associated with an increased likelihood of an illness or unhealthy event
  • CA one-time screening required before Medicaid eligibility
  • DThe process of ranking families by socioeconomic status
Rationale

Health risk appraisal is the process of assessing for the presence of specific factors in each risk category identified as associated with an increased likelihood of an illness (such as cancer) or an unhealthy event (such as an automobile accident).

Watch for

The appraisal method produces an individual score, but that score is examined as a whole within the family — it also appraises risks likely to be experienced by other family members.

5. A family has a history of cardiovascular disease. Several members also smoke, and the family lives in a community that is unsupportive of exercise and low-fat diets. How does the chapter characterize the combined effect of these risks?
  • AThe risks are independent and do not interact
  • ✓The combined effect of accumulated risks is often greater than the sum of the individual effects
  • COnly the biological risk is clinically significant
  • DBehavioral risks cancel out biological risks over time
Rationale

This is the chapter's worked cardiovascular example of the synergy principle: "Although single risk factors can influence outcomes, the combined effect of accumulated risks is often greater than the sum of the individual effects." Smoking is more likely if other family members also smoke, and diet/exercise are shaped by family and societal norms.

Watch for

Treating accumulated risks as merely additive understates the chapter's point — the effect is synergistic, not simply summed.

family health risk conceptslife events and transitions
6. A family experiences the anticipated birth of a grandchild. According to the chapter, how does this differ from an unexpected event such as sudden job loss?
  • AThere is no meaningful difference — both are equally stressful
  • ✓The normative event allows anticipatory preparation, which can increase coping and decrease stress and negative outcomes; the non-normative event allows little or no time to prepare
  • CNormative events are always more stressful because they involve more people
  • DNon-normative events are always positive
Rationale

Normative (anticipated) events let families identify needed resources, make plans, learn new skills, and prepare — anticipatory preparation increases coping ability and decreases stress. Non-normative (unexpected) events leave little or no time to prepare and can result in increased stress, crisis, or dysfunction.

9. Which statements reflect Murdaugh's principles of risk tolerance? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Voluntarily assumed risks, such as overeating, are tolerated better than those imposed by others

family assessment toolsgenogram and ecomap
11. What does a genogram show, and what does it reveal?
  • AThe family's interactions with outside groups and organizations
  • ✓A drawing of the family unit across several generations, using circles, squares, and connecting lines, allowing patterns such as recurring illness to be quickly assessed
  • CA numeric score of the family's total accumulated health risk
  • DA record of the family's insurance and financial eligibility status
Rationale

A genogram is a drawing that shows the family unit of immediate interest across several generations using circles, squares, and connecting lines. Patterns can be quickly assessed and guide the interviewer toward areas needing further exploration — the Graham family genogram revealed repeated hypertension, type 2 diabetes, cancer, and hypercholesterolemia.

Watch for

Option A describes the ecomap, not the genogram — the genogram looks inward across generations, the ecomap looks outward to the community.

Square = male, circle = female, an X through either shape = a death, a solid horizontal line = marriage, a solid vertical line = offspring/children, and a broken horizontal line = divorce or separation.

14. What does an ecomap represent, and what do its conventions show? Select all that apply.

16. What does a home visit offer that a clinic visit does not? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓A more accurate assessment of family structure and behavior in the home environment
  • ✓Opportunities to identify barriers and supports for reaching family health goals
  • ✓The ability to modify interventions directly to match the family's resources
  • DA guarantee of lower total cost of care than clinic-based services
  • ✓Contribution to the family's sense of control and active participation in its own health needs
Rationale

Home visits give a more accurate assessment of family structure, environment, and behavior; identify barriers and supports; let the nurse modify interventions to match resources; and contribute to the family's sense of control and active participation. Cost is actually the major disadvantage, not a guaranteed savings.

Watch for

The chapter is explicit that it is not always the least costly service that is the most effective — cost and effectiveness are separate considerations.

  • DRefer the case to law enforcement immediately
Rationale

"In some instances, the nurse will be mandated to persist in requesting a home visit because of legal obligations, such as follow-up of certain communicable and infectious diseases." TB follow-up is the chapter's own example of a required visit.

Ordinarily the nurse leaves the door open for future contact rather than pressing the issue — the exception is legally mandated communicable-disease follow-up, which this scenario is.

home visitswhen a family refuses a visit, and personal safety
29. According to the chapter's guidance on personal safety, what should the nurse do if a reasonable question exists about the safety of making a visit?
  • AMake the visit but request an escort
  • BMake the visit only during daylight hours
  • ✓The nurse should not make the visit
  • DProceed as planned since home visits are generally safe
Rationale

"If a reasonable question exists about the safety of making a visit, the nurse should not make the visit." Home visits are generally safe, but the possibility of violence exists, so the nurse must use caution and good judgment.

Watch for

Precautions like escorts, pairs, uniforms, and sign-out logs are used for known high-risk situations — but they do not override the flat rule that a genuine safety question means the visit is not made.

  • ARequired, nurse-controlled, and formal/investigatory
  • ✓Voluntary, with easier entry for the nurse, patient-controlled interaction, and a more informal tone

Voluntary visits (requested by the patient) allow easier entry for the nurse, are patient-controlled, have a more informal tone, and typically involve mutual discussion of future visit frequency. The breastfeeding-assistance request is the chapter's own example.

home visitsvoluntary versus required visits
31. A family member has been diagnosed with tuberculosis, and the nurse must verify the patient is taking medication regularly. How would this visit typically be characterized?
  • AVoluntary, patient-controlled, and informal
  • ✓Required, with more difficult entry, nurse-controlled interaction, a more formal/investigatory tone, and possibly distorted nurse–patient communication
  • CVoluntary, because the patient's health is at stake
  • DRequired, but otherwise identical in tone and control to a voluntary visit
Rationale

Required visits (often legally mandated) involve more difficult entry, nurse-controlled interaction, a more formal and investigatory tone with distorted communication, and there may be no mutual discussion of future visit frequency. TB medication verification is the chapter's own example, alongside COVID-19 quarantine follow-up.

Assuming all home visits share the same tone ignores the chapter's explicit contrast — required visits are structurally different from voluntary ones, not just more serious versions of the same thing. ---

32. What is the stated premise of contracting with families?

  • ✓Family control — when the family has legitimate control, its ability to make healthful choices is increased
  • CContracting is a legally binding document required for all home visits

"The premise of contracting is family control. It is assumed that when the family has legitimate control, its ability to make healthful choices is increased." Contracting involves a shift in responsibility and control toward a shared effort by patient and professional.

33. Which statement correctly distinguishes a contingency contract from a noncontingency contract?
  • ✓A contingency contract states a specific reward after completion of the patient's portion; a noncontingency contract does not specify rewards, relying on the implied reward of reaching the goals
  • BA contingency contract is always written; a noncontingency contract is always verbal
  • CA noncontingency contract offers a larger reward than a contingency contract
  • DContingency and noncontingency contracts are interchangeable terms for the same agreement
Rationale

A contingency contract states a specific reward for the patient after completion of the patient's portion of the contract. A noncontingency contract does not specify rewards — the implied rewards are the positive consequences of reaching the goals specified in the contract.

34. For family health risk reduction, who should be involved in the contract?
  • AOnly the family member with the identified health problem
  • BThe head of household only, since that person can make decisions for the family
  • ✓All responsible and appropriate members of the family, because the goal requires a total family system effort and change
  • DOnly the nurse and the primary care provider, with the family notified afterward
Rationale

"For family health risk reduction, it is essential that the contract be made with all responsible and appropriate members of the family. Involving only one individual is not sufficient if the goal is family health risk reduction, which requires a total family system effort and change."

Watch for

If meeting with the entire family at once is not possible, each family member can still review the contract, give input, and sign it — the requirement is inclusion, not necessarily a single joint meeting.
