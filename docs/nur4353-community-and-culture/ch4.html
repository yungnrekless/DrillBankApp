1. A state health department issues regulations implementing a new infection-control statute. Which branch of government is exercising its health-policy role here?
  • ALegislative
  • ✓Executive
  • CJudicial
  • DAdministrative-judicial hybrid
Rationale

4. A state's nurse practice act was passed by the legislature, but the Board of Nursing licenses RNs and revokes licenses for violations. Which branch does the Board of Nursing belong to?

  • ✓Executive, because it is an administrative/regulatory agency

  • AJudicial law, based on precedent
  • BLegislative/statute law
  • ✓Constitutional law, through the state's police power
  • DCommon law duty to warn
Rationale

The legal basis for quarantine/isolation is constitutional law, specifically the state's police power — the state's authority to protect the health, safety, and welfare of its citizens, even overriding individual rights when the public is genuinely threatened, provided the action is necessary and reasonable.

  • BA federal agency's authority to enforce criminal law

government and lawprofessional negligence and malpractice
18. Which four elements must a patient prove, all together, to recover damages for professional negligence (malpractice)? Select all that apply.
Select all that apply · scored all or nothing
  • ✓Duty
  • ✓Breach
  • ✓Causation
  • ✓Damages
  • EIntent to harm
Rationale

To recover damages the patient must prove all four elements: duty (the nurse owed a duty to the patient), breach (the nurse failed to act as a reasonable, prudent nurse would), causation (that failure led to the injury), and damages (the injury supports a monetary claim). Missing any one element defeats the claim.

Watch for

Malpractice is negligence, not an intentional tort — intent to harm is not one of the four required elements.

19. A nurse fails to reassess a patient's pain per protocol (a clear deviation from the reasonable, prudent nurse standard), but the patient's outcome was identical to what it would have been with proper reassessment, and the patient suffered no injury or financial loss. Can the patient successfully sue for malpractice?
  • AYes, because duty and breach are both clearly established
  • ✓No, because causation and damages are missing even though duty and breach are present
  • CYes, automatically, once any breach of the standard of care is shown
  • DNo, because there was no duty owed in this scenario
Rationale

All four elements — duty, breach, causation, and damages — must be present. Here duty and breach are established, but there is no injury (damages) and nothing the breach can be shown to have caused (causation), so the claim fails.

Watch for

A clear breach feels like enough on its own, but breach without resulting, compensable harm defeats the claim — this is the scenario version of "missing any one of the four D's."

21. A hospital-employed nurse commits a negligent act while working within the scope of her employment. Under respondeat superior, who may be held responsible?
  • AOnly the nurse individually
  • BOnly the employer, since the nurse was following employer direction
  • ✓The employer, along with the individual nurse
  • DNeither party, since the employer did not act directly
Rationale

Under respondeat superior, when a nurse is employed and acting within the scope of employment, the employer is responsible for the nurse's negligent actions along with the individual nurse, because the employer directed the function and is usually better able to pay.
