cell cycle specificity in cancer chemotherapyspecific versus nonspecific action
1. A combination regimen pairs a drug that is cytotoxic only while DNA is being synthesized with a second drug that kills cells at any point in the cycle. How should the nurse classify the first drug?
  • ✓A cell cycle–specific drug, because it is cytotoxic during a specific phase of the cellular growth cycle
  • BA cell cycle–nonspecific drug, because it is cytotoxic in any phase of the cellular growth cycle
  • CA cell cycle–specific drug, because it is the more potent of the two agents
  • DA cell cycle–nonspecific drug, because anything acting on DNA acts throughout the cycle
Rationale

Cell cycle–specific denotes antineoplastic drugs that are cytotoxic during a specific phase of the cellular growth cycle; the antimetabolites are the example, working primarily in the S phase during which DNA synthesis is most active. Chapter 45 covers the cell cycle–specific drugs and Chapter 46 the nonspecific ones, and the chapter states that split outright on p. 706.

Watch for

(C) reaches the right label by the wrong reasoning and will carry a learner to the wrong answer on the mirror item. The book defeats potency reasoning itself: regardless of the cell cycle characteristics of a drug, it is more effective on rapidly growing tumors. Specific and nonspecific describe when a drug kills, not how hard.

2. The second drug in that regimen is described as cytotoxic in any phase of the cellular growth cycle. What does that tell the nurse?
  • ✓It is cell cycle–nonspecific, and being phase-indifferent does not make it the stronger of the two drugs
  • BIt is cell cycle–specific, since acting in every phase means it must be targeted to each one
  • CIt is cell cycle–nonspecific and therefore more effective than the phase-limited drug
  • DIt is cell cycle–nonspecific and is therefore reserved for slowly growing tumors
Rationale

Antineoplastic drugs that are cytotoxic in any phase of the cycle are cell cycle–nonspecific. The chapter adds that drugs may have actions falling into both categories, and that regardless of the cell cycle characteristics of a drug, it is more effective on rapidly growing tumors (p. 706).

Watch for

(C) is the mirror error of the previous item, now attaching potency to the other label. (D) inverts the chapter's one comparative claim, which is that rapid growth predicts response for both categories alike.

3. A patient asks why chemotherapy must be repeated in cycles rather than given once until every cancer cell is dead. Which explanation is grounded in the cell growth cycle?
  • ✓Cancer cells sitting in the resting phase are not susceptible to cell cycle–specific drugs, so any single dose misses them
  • BCancer cells develop resistance to the drug within hours of the first dose
  • CThe drugs are cleared from the body too quickly to reach all of the cells at once
  • DRepeated dosing is required to keep the tumor's doubling time from shortening
Rationale

Table 45.5 states that cancer cells in the G0 resting phase are not susceptible to the toxic effects of cell cycle–specific drugs, and p. 704 describes G0 cells as considered out of the cell cycle. That escape hatch is the rationale for combination regimens and for the existence of a separate cell cycle–nonspecific class in Chapter 46.

Watch for

(B) is the reflex pick because acquired resistance is the familiar explanation for an incomplete kill. The chapter's stated reason is phase location, not resistance. When a stem asks why chemotherapy fails to eradicate every cell, the resting phase is the answer.

4. A student lists the phases of the cell growth cycle as G1, S, G2, prophase, and metaphase. Which correction should the nurse make?
  • ✓The five phases are G0, G1, S, G2, and M; prophase and metaphase are subphases within M
  • BThe five phases are correct, but prophase and metaphase come before G1 rather than after G2
  • CThe list is accurate as given, since mitosis is subdivided rather than named as a phase
  • DThe five phases are G0, G1, S, G2, and telophase
Rationale

Table 45.5 gives five phases: G0 resting, G1 first gap or postmitotic, S DNA synthesis, G2 second gap or premitotic, and M mitosis. M is divided into four subphases — prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase — after which the cell divides into two daughter cells.

Watch for

(D) is the same level swap with a different subphase promoted, which is why it survives a quick scan. Any option that lists a mitotic subphase alongside G1 and S has collapsed two levels of the outline into one.

5. A pathology report describes a tumor with a high percentage of its cells in mitosis at any given time and a short doubling time. Which conclusion is supported?
  • ✓The tumor has a high growth fraction, and its short doubling time makes it difficult to cure because of rapid regrowth
  • BThe tumor has a high mitotic index, which is another name for the same measurement as growth fraction
  • CThe short doubling time predicts a better cure rate, since rapidly dividing tumors respond to chemotherapy
  • DThe tumor is largely in the resting phase and will respond poorly to any chemotherapy
Rationale

Growth fraction is the percentage of cells in mitosis at any given time, and p. 704 states that tumors with shorter doubling times are often difficult to cure because of rapid regrowth. Chemotherapy is most effective in a rapidly dividing or highly proliferative tumor, so rapid proliferation cuts both ways.

Watch for

(B) treats growth fraction and mitotic index as synonyms because both count dividing cells. Growth fraction is a percentage; the mitotic index is the actual number of cells per unit, usually 1000 cells, undergoing mitosis. Doubling time is neither of those — it is a clock.

cell cycle–specific drug classes and mechanismsmechanism by class
7. Methotrexate is started, and the nurse explains that it belongs to the antimetabolites. Which statement describes how this class works?
  • ✓It is an antagonist analogue that interferes with DNA synthesis, working primarily in the S phase
  • BIt is an agonist analogue that supplies the folic acid a rapidly dividing tumor demands
  • CIt acts in any phase of the cycle by cross-linking DNA strands directly
  • DIt halts mitosis during metaphase by producing nonfunctional microtubules
Rationale

Antimetabolites are cell cycle–specific analogues that antagonize folic acid, purines, or pyrimidines, and they work primarily in the S phase during which DNA synthesis is most active (p. 709). An analogue may have agonist or antagonist activity, and an antagonist analogue is also known as an antimetabolite.

Watch for

(B) collapses analogue and antimetabolite into synonyms and then picks the wrong half. Every antimetabolite is an analogue, but not every analogue is an antimetabolite — the antimetabolite is specifically the antagonist kind. (D) is the taxane mechanism borrowed from a class two pages later.

8. A patient receives vincristine. By which mechanism do the vinca alkaloids kill dividing cells?
  • ✓They bind to tubulin and prevent the assembly of microtubules, so the mitotic spindle dissolves
  • BThey cause the formation of nonfunctional microtubules, which halts mitosis during metaphase
  • CThey inhibit topoisomerase II, which causes breaks in DNA strands
  • DThey convert asparagine to aspartic acid so the cell cannot synthesize protein
Rationale

The vinca alkaloids are plant-derived mitotic inhibitors that bind tubulin and prevent the assembly of microtubules, dissolving the mitotic spindle. The mitotic inhibitors as a group all work shortly before or during mitosis (p. 712).

Watch for

(B) is the taxane mechanism, and the two classes swap constantly because both are plant-derived drugs acting on microtubules around mitosis. The cue is that vincas prevent microtubules from being built while taxanes build them wrong.

9. The same patient is later switched to paclitaxel. How does its action on microtubules differ from vincristine's?
  • ✓Paclitaxel causes the formation of nonfunctional microtubules, which halts mitosis during metaphase
  • BPaclitaxel binds tubulin and prevents microtubules from assembling at all
  • CPaclitaxel leaves microtubules intact and instead binds the DNA–topoisomerase I complex
  • DPaclitaxel acts in the S phase rather than around mitosis
Rationale

The taxanes cause the formation of nonfunctional microtubules, which halts mitosis during metaphase (p. 712). Both taxanes and vincas are mitotic inhibitors reaching the same endpoint — a cell that cannot complete mitosis — by opposite routes.

Watch for

(B) is the mirror error of the previous item. Prevented assembly is the vinca mechanism; a built but nonfunctional spindle is the taxane one. The name cue is that vincas come from periwinkle and taxanes carry the "-taxel" stem from the yew.

10. A patient with metastatic colorectal cancer is to receive irinotecan. Which description of this drug's class is accurate?
  • ✓It is a camptothecin that binds the DNA–topoisomerase I complex in the S phase, retarding religation and producing a DNA strand break
  • BIt is a derivative of epipodophyllotoxin that inhibits topoisomerase II in late S and G2
  • CIt is a vinca alkaloid that dissolves the mitotic spindle during metaphase
  • DIt is an antineoplastic enzyme approved exclusively for acute lymphocytic leukemia
Rationale

The camptothecins — topotecan and irinotecan — inhibit proper DNA function in the S phase by binding to the DNA–topoisomerase I complex, retarding religation and producing a DNA strand break (p. 714).

Watch for

(B) names the other topoisomerase, and a single numeral is the whole distinction between the two classes. Topoisomerase I means the camptothecins in S phase; topoisomerase II means the epipodophyllotoxins in late S and G2.

11. A patient with testicular cancer receives etoposide. Which statement about this drug is accurate?
  • ✓It is a derivative of epipodophyllotoxin that inhibits topoisomerase II, causing breaks in DNA strands, and acts in late S and G2
  • BIt is a camptothecin that binds the DNA–topoisomerase I complex during the S phase
  • CIt is an alkylating drug and therefore cell cycle–nonspecific
  • DIt depletes circulating asparagine so that leukemic cells cannot synthesize protein
Rationale

Etoposide and teniposide are derivatives of epipodophyllotoxin and act by inhibiting the enzyme topoisomerase II, which causes breaks in DNA strands (p. 712).

Watch for

(B) is the mirror error of the previous item, with the numeral flipped. Note also that the book files the topoisomerase II inhibitors under the mitotic-inhibitor heading on p. 712 while listing them separately in its key points, so an option that calls etoposide a mitotic inhibitor is not thereby wrong.

12. A child with acute lymphocytic leukemia is receiving pegaspargase. Beyond the usual chemotherapy toxicities, which assessment is specific to this drug class?
  • ✓Monitor blood glucose and for abdominal pain, because impaired pancreatic function can produce hyperglycemia and severe or fatal pancreatitis
  • BMonitor for peripheral neuropathy, because this class is the most significant neurotoxin among the cytotoxic drugs
  • CMonitor for delayed diarrhea beginning 2 to 10 days after the infusion
  • DMonitor the palms and soles for hand-foot syndrome
Rationale

The antineoplastic enzymes asparaginase and pegaspargase are approved exclusively for the treatment of acute lymphocytic leukemia. They convert asparagine to aspartic acid and ammonia, so leukemic cells are then unable to synthesize the asparagine required. Their distinguishing toxicity is impaired pancreatic function, with hyperglycemia and severe or fatal pancreatitis (p. 715).

Watch for

(B) is vincristine's signature toxicity and (C) is irinotecan's. Both pull because they are the chapter's other two drug-specific assessments, and a learner who has memorized "chemotherapy has drug-specific toxicities" without pairing each to its drug picks whichever sounds most serious.

chemotherapy-induced myelosuppression and nadirnaming the condition and the moment
13. A patient who received chemotherapy 14 days ago calls the clinic. Applying the concept of the nadir, when is this patient at greatest risk for infection?
  • ✓Now, because the nadir normally occurs approximately 10 to 28 days after dosing
  • BWithin the first 24 hours after the infusion, when drug levels peak
  • CNot until the next cycle begins and the doses accumulate
  • DOnly once a fever has actually developed
Rationale

The nadir is the lowest point in any fluctuating value over time — for example, the lowest white blood cell count measured after the count has been depressed by chemotherapy. It normally occurs approximately 10 to 28 days after dosing, and anticipating it guides the timing of prophylactic antibiotics and hematopoietic growth factors (p. 707).

Watch for

(B) assumes toxicity tracks the infusion, which is true for acute reactions and false for marrow toxicity. The chapter puts the window at 10 to 28 days, so the patient feels best at the point of greatest danger.

chemotherapy-induced myelosuppression and nadirnaming the condition and the moment
14. The same patient's counts return with a low white blood cell count, a low hemoglobin, and a low platelet count all at once. Which term names the condition producing all three?
  • ✓Myelosuppression, the suppression of bone marrow function
  • BNadir, since the nadir is what makes all three counts fall
  • CTumor lysis syndrome
  • DA paraneoplastic syndrome
Rationale

Myelosuppression, also known as bone marrow suppression, results from destruction of the rapidly dividing cellular precursors of white blood cells, red blood cells, and platelets, and leads to leukopenia, anemia, and thrombocytopenia (p. 707).

Watch for

(B) is the mirror error of the previous item. Myelosuppression is the condition and the nadir is the moment, which is why the key-terms box prints them side by side. "Why are all three cell lines low" wants myelosuppression; "when is the risk greatest" wants nadir.

16. A patient with severe bone marrow suppression has documented fatigue, altered body image from alopecia, impaired mobility, and a white blood cell count near its nadir. Which problem takes priority?
  • ✓Risk for infection
  • BFatigue
  • CAltered body image
  • DImpaired physical mobility
Rationale

All four are documented in this chapter, which is what makes the distractors strong, but the physiologic threat to life wins. Here that is infection, because myelosuppression has cost the patient the ability to mount a defense. Because fever is a principal early sign of infection, an oral or axillary temperature is taken at least every 4 hours (p. 720).

(B) pulls because fatigue is the most commonly reported chemotherapy symptom and the chapter names it directly as an effect of anemia. Priority items in this chapter resolve to the physiologic threat unless the scenario supplies active bleeding or an airway or circulation problem.

17. A patient's absolute neutrophil count returns at 420 cells/mm3 on the morning the next chemotherapy cycle is due. What does the nurse anticipate?
  • ✓The dose will be held, because an ANC below 500 is severe neutropenia and carries a high risk for infection
  • BThe dose will proceed as scheduled, since 420 falls within the normal range of 1500 to 8000 cells/mm3
  • CThe dose will proceed with an added antibiotic, since the count only reflects marrow recovery
  • DA platelet transfusion will be given first, since this value reflects the bleeding risk
Rationale

A normal absolute neutrophil count is 1500 to 8000 cells/mm3, severe neutropenia is less than 500, and Box 45.2 places an ANC of 500 or lower at high risk for infection. Chemotherapy is held below 500 (p. 708).

Watch for

(D) reads any low chemotherapy value as a platelet problem, which is a common reflex because bleeding precautions are the most visible nursing intervention in this chapter. The ANC reports neutrophils; the separate bleeding threshold is a platelet count under 100,000/mm3, with some guidelines using 50,000.

18. During an infusion of a chemotherapy drug with vesicant potential, the nurse notes swelling and blanching at the intravenous site. What is the first action?
  • ✓Stop the infusion and leave the intravenous cannula in place
  • BApply cold compresses and elevate the extremity
  • CInject the prescribed antidote through the catheter
  • DGive subcutaneous epinephrine at the site
Rationale

The drug is usually discontinued immediately and the intravenous cannula is left in place for possible use of antidotes through the cannula (p. 721). Extravasation is the leakage of an intravenously administered medication into the tissue space surrounding the vein, and it is a serious complication of chemotherapy.

Watch for

(C) is the strong distractor because it is eventually correct — just not first, and it depends on the cannula still being there. Stop the exposure before treating it.

19. Two chemotherapy drugs infiltrate on the same unit. One is classified as an irritant and the other as a vesicant. What distinguishes the vesicant?

These drugs may be irritants, irritating the intravenous site and vein, or vesicants, causing cell death with extravasation and necrosis with ulcerations (p. 718). Only vesicant extravasation carries emergency status.

20. Vincristine has extravasated, and the protocol directs hyaluronidase 150 units/mL by multiple subcutaneous injections into the site. What else does Table 45.8 specify?

  • BCold compresses, since cooling limits the spread of the drug through the tissue

Rationale

Table 45.8 covers etoposide, teniposide, vinblastine, and vincristine, and specifies hyaluronidase 1 to 6 mL by multiple subcutaneous injections into the site, repeated over the next few hours, plus warm compresses. 21. A pharmacy technician asks whether vincristine can be sent up for intrathecal administration, since cytarabine on the same unit is given by that route. What is the nurse's response?
  • ✓Vincristine is for intravenous use only and is fatal if given by other routes; several deaths have been reported after inadvertent intrathecal administration
  • BVincristine may be given intrathecally provided it is diluted in 25 to 50 mL first
  • CEither drug may be given intrathecally as long as it is dispensed in a syringe
  • DIntrathecal vincristine is acceptable when the goal is to treat central nervous system disease
Rationale

Every vincristine product carries the warning that it is for intravenous use only and fatal if given by other routes. The safeguards are to dilute the dose in 25 to 50 mL and never dispense it in a syringe. Intrathecal is a legitimate route in this same chapter for cytarabine, which may be given intravenously, subcutaneously, or intrathecally (pp. 712 and 713).

Watch for

(D) pulls because the intrathecal route exists precisely to deliver chemotherapy to the central nervous system and is correct for other drugs on the same unit. The axis of this question is route, not indication. (B) inverts the safeguard: intrathecal drugs are not normally dispensed in a minibag, so the 25 to 50 mL dilution is what makes the fatal error harder to commit.

tumor lysis syndrome and uric acid prophylaxisrecognizing the syndrome
23. A patient begins induction chemotherapy for acute leukemia. Which pattern of laboratory changes is consistent with tumor lysis syndrome?
  • ✓Elevated potassium, elevated phosphate, elevated uric acid, and reduced calcium
  • BElevated potassium, phosphate, uric acid, and calcium
  • CReduced potassium, phosphate, and uric acid with elevated calcium
  • DElevated calcium and uric acid with a normal potassium
Rationale

The key term defines tumor lysis syndrome by excessive cellular waste products and electrolytes including uric acid, phosphate, and potassium, and by reduced serum calcium levels. The chapter names hyperphosphatemia, hyperkalemia, and hypocalcemia, and calls it a common postchemotherapy condition often associated with induction chemotherapy for rapidly growing hematologic malignancies (pp. 701 and 710).

Watch for

(B) flips calcium, and it is the intuitive reading because cells bursting suggests everything spills upward at once. Calcium is the single value that falls, and the key term prints it as the discriminator. Note also the stage cue in the stem: this is an induction-phase event in a rapidly growing hematologic malignancy, not a maintenance-phase event in a slow-growing solid tumor.

tumor lysis syndrome and uric acid prophylaxisthe two rescues and what each rescues from
24. Allopurinol is ordered before induction chemotherapy begins. A student asks whether it is an antineoplastic drug. What is the nurse's response?
  • ✓No; it inhibits xanthine oxidase to reduce uric acid levels, and although it is chemically similar to purines it does not disrupt DNA synthesis
  • BYes; it is a purine antimetabolite that antagonizes DNA synthesis in the S phase
  • CNo; it is an enzyme that degrades the uric acid already circulating in the blood
  • DYes; it slows the rate of cell kill so that waste products are released gradually
Rationale

Allopurinol and rasburicase are both indicated for the hyperuricemia associated with tumor lysis syndrome and are usually given in anticipation of the condition rather than in response to it. Allopurinol inhibits xanthine oxidase, which reduces serum and urinary levels of uric acid, and it is also used commonly in oral form to treat gout (p. 709).

Watch for

(B) is exactly the distractor the chapter pre-empts. Allopurinol looks like a purine, so it reads as a purine antagonist and therefore an antimetabolite. The text states plainly that it does not disrupt DNA synthesis, which means it has no direct antineoplastic action at all.

Antiemetics are given before chemotherapy (p. 706). Anticancer drugs cause nausea and vomiting by stimulating the cells of the chemoreceptor trigger zone, and the chapter's own review question places the antiemetic 30 to 60 minutes before the dose.
