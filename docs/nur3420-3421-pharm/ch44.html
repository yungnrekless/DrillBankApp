inflammation and the arachidonic acid pathwayhow injury becomes inflammation
1. A patient sprains an ankle, and within hours the joint is red, swollen, warm, and painful. The patient asks how a twisted ligament turns into all of that. Which sequence describes what happened?
  • ✓Arachidonic acid was released from phospholipids in cell membranes and metabolized in either the prostaglandin or the leukotriene pathway
  • BArachidonic acid was synthesized in the preoptic hypothalamic region and carried in the blood to the injured joint
  • CLeukotrienes in the joint were converted into arachidonic acid by cyclooxygenase
  • DUric acid crystals were released from the ligament and drew leukocytes to the joint
Rationale

Arachidonic acid is released from phospholipids in cell membranes in response to a triggering event such as an injury, and is then metabolized in either the prostaglandin pathway or the leukotriene pathway, both branches of the arachidonic acid pathway. Both branches lead to inflammation, edema, headache, and other pain.

Watch for

(C) reverses the pathway, and it is the intuitive reversal because leukotrienes are the more familiar word. Arachidonic acid is the precursor, not a product, and leukotrienes come from lipoxygenase rather than cyclooxygenase.

8. A patient with rheumatoid arthritis and a history of peptic ulcer disease is switched from naproxen to celecoxib. What is the rationale for the change?
  • ✓Celecoxib specifically inhibits COX-2 and theoretically has limited or no effect on COX-1, which reduces the prevalence of gastrointestinal ulceration
  • BCelecoxib inhibits both COX-1 and COX-2 more completely, which shortens the duration of exposure
  • CCelecoxib is not an NSAID, so the class contraindications do not apply
  • DCelecoxib provides antiplatelet protection that naproxen lacks
Rationale

The COX-2 inhibitors work by specifically inhibiting the COX-2 form of COX and theoretically have limited or no effect on COX-1. Previous NSAIDs nonspecifically inhibited both. That greater enzyme specificity allows the beneficial antiinflammatory effects while reducing the prevalence of adverse effects such as gastrointestinal ulceration.

Watch for

(D) inverts a real fact. COX-2 inhibitors have little effect on platelet function, which is a benefit for bleeding risk and a liability if the patient needed antiplatelet action.

12. A patient asks why one small aspirin a day prevents clots when a full-strength ibuprofen does not. Which explanation is correct?
  • ✓Aspirin is uniquely an irreversible inhibitor of COX-1 in the platelets themselves, which reduces the formation of thromboxane A2
  • BAspirin reversibly inhibits COX-2 in the platelets, which reduces thromboxane A2
  • CAspirin increases prostacyclin production in the platelets
  • DAspirin thins the blood by lowering the number of circulating platelets
Rationale

Aspirin has the unique property among NSAIDs of being an irreversible inhibitor of COX-1 receptors within the platelets themselves, which results in reduced formation of thromboxane A2, a substance that normally promotes platelet aggregation.

Watch for

(D) is the lay explanation patients arrive with, and it is wrong in a way that matters: aspirin disables the platelets it meets rather than reducing their number, which is why the effect outlasts the drug.

The pharmacokinetics table gives allopurinol an onset of 1 to 2 weeks and colchicine an onset of 12 hours.
