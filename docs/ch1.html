1. According to the IOM (1988), how is public health defined?
  • AThe branch of medicine concerned with preventing disease in individual patients
  • ✓What we, as a society, do collectively to assure the conditions in which people can be healthy
  • CThe organized delivery of primary care to underserved populations
  • DA government program that provides health insurance to low-income populations
Rationale

The IOM's 1988 definition is "what we, as a society, do collectively to assure the conditions in which people can be healthy."

Watch for

Option A describes clinical medicine's focus on the individual, which is precisely what public health is not — the unit of concern is the collective, not the single patient.

  • ✓Primary prevention; reduces incidence
  • BSecondary prevention; reduces prevalence

levels of preventiondefinitions and the book's PHN examples
12. A PHN develops toxin screenings for migrant workers exposed to pesticides and refers those who screen positive for treatment. Which level of prevention is this, and why?
  • APrimary prevention, because it targets a population before disease develops
  • ✓Secondary prevention, because it involves early detection and treatment
  • CTertiary prevention, because it addresses an occupational hazard
  • DPrimary prevention, because screening prevents pesticide exposure
Rationale

Secondary prevention stops the progress of disease through early detection and treatment, reducing prevalence and chronicity. The migrant-worker toxin-screening example is the book's illustration of secondary prevention.

Watch for

Screening happening across a whole population (migrant workers) makes this feel primary or population-level "prevention," but the level is set by what the nurse is doing — early detection — not by the setting. Screening is the classic secondary cue even at the population level.

levels of preventiondefinitions and the book's PHN examples
14. Which statement correctly matches a level of prevention to the outcome it is meant to reduce?
  • APrimary prevention reduces prevalence and chronicity
  • BSecondary prevention reduces incidence
  • ✓Primary prevention reduces incidence; secondary prevention reduces prevalence and chronicity
  • DTertiary prevention reduces incidence
Rationale

Primary prevention promotes health and prevents disease development, reducing incidence. Secondary prevention stops disease progress through early detection and treatment, reducing prevalence and chronicity.

Watch for

Swapping "incidence" and "prevalence/chronicity" between primary and secondary is the easiest way this pairing gets inverted on an exam.

  • CToxin screening is actually tertiary prevention because it involves referral for treatment

22. Which statement correctly describes the unit of focus and goal of community-oriented nursing practice?

  • ✓The community/population; preventing disease and disability and creating conditions for health via community diagnosis, surveillance, and monitoring
  • CIndividual patients enrolled in a managed care plan; comprehensive coordinated services
  • DThe community/population; providing acute and chronic care in community settings
Rationale

community-oriented vs. community-based nursingthe confusable pair
23. A student nurse says, "Community-based and community-oriented nursing both mean the same thing — nursing that happens somewhere in the community." How should this be corrected?
  • AThe terms are in fact interchangeable and the distinction is not meaningful
  • ✓"Based" refers to a setting where individual/family care happens; "oriented" refers to the whole population as the unit of care
  • C"Oriented" refers to a setting where individual/family care happens; "based" refers to the whole population as the unit of care
  • D"Based" refers to acute care only, while "oriented" refers to chronic care only
Rationale

The chapter's tested distinction is that "based" is about where — a setting in the community where individual/family care happens — while "oriented" is about whom — the population as the unit of care.

Watch for

Option C is the exact inversion of the correct mapping and is the version most likely to appear as a tempting distractor.

27. A nursing instructor asks, "What is the priority intervention for a public health nurse planning activities for a community?" Absent a scenario describing an existing disease, what does the chapter say the default answer should be?
  • ATertiary prevention, because it addresses the most severe cases
  • BSecondary prevention, because early detection has the highest yield
  • ✓Primary prevention, because selecting activities should prioritize it
  • DWhichever level the community requests through its representatives
Rationale

One of the eight principles states that primary prevention is the priority in selecting activities. The chapter notes that if a stem asks for the priority intervention, the answer defaults to primary prevention unless the scenario clearly describes an existing disease.
